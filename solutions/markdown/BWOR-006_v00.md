# Operations Research Model: Factory Production Planning

## 1. Decision Variables

Let $x_{ijk}$ denote the quantity of product $j$ processed on machine $i$ at stage $k$.

Since there are two stages (A and B) and multiple machine options per stage, we define the variables based on the specific machine used for each stage. Let the indices be:
- Product $j \in \{I, II, III\}$
- Stage $k \in \{A, B\}$
- Machine $i$ depends on stage and product feasibility.

To ensure conservation of flow (units entering stage A must equal units exiting stage A and entering stage B, and final output must be sold), it is more efficient to define decision variables as the **total quantity of each product produced and sold**, while using auxiliary variables or direct allocation if costs vary by machine.

However, because **processing costs vary by machine** and **capacity constraints are per machine**, we must track how much of each product is processed on which specific machine.

Let:
- $a_{ij}$ = Quantity of product $j$ processed on machine $A_i$ (for $i \in \{1, 2\}$)
- $b_{ij}$ = Quantity of product $j$ processed on machine $B_i$ (for $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$)

**Feasible Combinations based on Problem Statement:**

**Stage A:**
- Product I: Can use $A_1$ or $A_2$.
- Product II: Can use $A_1$ or $A_2$.
- Product III: Can only use $A_2$.

**Stage B:**
- Product I: Can use $B_1$, $B_2$, or $B_3$.
- Product II: Can only use $B_1$.
- Product III: Can only use $B_2$.

**Defined Decision Variables:**

| Variable | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| $a_{11}$ | Units of Product I processed on $A_1$ |
| $a_{12}$ | Units of Product II processed on $A_1$ |
| $a_{21}$ | Units of Product I processed on $A_2$ |
| $a_{22}$ | Units of Product II processed on $A_2$ |
| $a_{23}$ | Units of Product III processed on $A_2$ |
| $b_{11}$ | Units of Product I processed on $B_1$ |
| $b_{21}$ | Units of Product II processed on $B_1$ |
| $b_{12}$ | Units of Product I processed on $B_2$ |
| $b_{32}$ | Units of Product III processed on $B_2$ |
| $b_{13}$ | Units of Product I processed on $B_3$ |

*Note: Variables representing infeasible routes (e.g., Product III on $A_1$) are fixed to 0 and omitted.*

## 2. Objective Function

**Goal:** Maximize Total Profit.

$$ \text{Profit} = \text{Total Revenue} - \text{Total Processing Cost} - \text{Total Raw Material Cost} $$

Let:
- $P_j$ = Selling price per unit of product $j$.
- $r_j$ = Raw material cost per unit of product $j$.
- $c_{ik}$ = Processing cost per hour on machine $i$ in stage $k$.
- $t_{ijk}$ = Processing time (hours) per unit of product $j$ on machine $i$ in stage $k$.

The total quantity of product $j$ produced (and sold) is the sum of units processed on all valid Stage A machines for that product (which must equal the sum of units processed on all valid Stage B machines). Let $Q_j$ be the total quantity of product $j$.

$$ Q_I = a_{11} + a_{21} = b_{11} + b_{12} + b_{13} $$
$$ Q_{II} = a_{12} + a_{22} = b_{21} $$
$$ Q_{III} = a_{23} = b_{32} $$

**Objective Function:**

$$ \text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{j \in \{I, II, III\}} Q_j (P_j - r_j) - \sum_{\text{valid } i,j} a_{ij} (t_{Aij} \cdot c_{Ai}) - \sum_{\text{valid } i,j} b_{ij} (t_{Bij} \cdot c_{Bi}) $$

Substituting the specific values from Table 1-18:

**Revenue - Raw Material Cost (Contribution Margin per unit):**
- Product I: $1.25 - 0.25 = 1.00$
- Product II: $2.00 - 0.35 = 1.65$
- Product III: $2.80 - 0.50 = 2.30$

**Processing Costs (Cost = Hours $\times$ Cost per Hour):**

*Stage A Costs:*
- $A_1$ (Cost $0.05/h$):
  - Prod I: $5 \times 0.05 = 0.25$
  - Prod II: $10 \times 0.05 = 0.50$
- $A_2$ (Cost $0.03/h$):
  - Prod I: $7 \times 0.03 = 0.21$
  - Prod II: $9 \times 0.03 = 0.27$
  - Prod III: $12 \times 0.03 = 0.36$

*Stage B Costs:*
- $B_1$ (Cost $0.06/h$):
  - Prod I: $6 \times 0.06 = 0.36$
  - Prod II: $8 \times 0.06 = 0.48$
- $B_2$ (Cost $0.11/h$):
  - Prod I: $4 \times 0.11 = 0.44$
  - Prod III: $11 \times 0.11 = 1.21$
- $B_3$ (Cost $0.05/h$):
  - Prod I: $7 \times 0.05 = 0.35$

**Final Objective Function:**

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Max } Z = & \quad 1.00 Q_I + 1.65 Q_{II} + 2.30 Q_{III} \\
& - (0.25 a_{11} + 0.50 a_{12}) \\
& - (0.21 a_{21} + 0.27 a_{22} + 0.36 a_{23}) \\
& - (0.36 b_{11} + 0.48 b_{21}) \\
& - (0.44 b_{12} + 1.21 b_{32}) \\
& - (0.35 b_{13})
\end{aligned}
$$

*Subject to the flow balance constraints (defined in Section 3) linking $Q_j$ to the $a$ and $b$ variables.*

Alternatively, substituting $Q$ directly into the objective to make it a pure function of decision variables $a, b$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Max } Z = & \quad (1.00 - 0.25 - 0.36) a_{11} + (1.00 - 0.21 - 0.44) a_{21} + (1.00 - 0.21 - 0.35) a_{21\_via\_B3?} \dots
\end{aligned}
$$
*Note: This direct substitution is complex because the Stage B cost depends on which B machine is chosen, which is independent of which A machine was chosen, except that the total quantity must match. It is mathematically cleaner to keep $Q_j$ as auxiliary variables or simply state the objective in terms of $Q$ and the cost summations.*

**Recommended Formulation for Solver:**

$$
\text{Max } Z = 1.00 Q_I + 1.65 Q_{II} + 2.30 Q_{III} - \sum_{i,j} c^A_{ij} a_{ij} - \sum_{i,j} c^B_{ij} b_{ij}
$$

Where $c^A_{ij}$ and $c^B_{ij}$ are the pre-calculated processing costs per unit listed above.

## 3. Operational Constraints

### A. Machine Capacity Constraints
Total processing time used on each machine must not exceed available hours.

**Stage A Machines:**
1.  **Machine $A_1$** (Capacity: 6,000 h)
    $$ 5 a_{11} + 10 a_{12} \le 6000 $$
2.  **Machine $A_2$** (Capacity: 10,000 h)
    $$ 7 a_{21} + 9 a_{22} + 12 a_{23} \le 10000 $$

**Stage B Machines:**
3.  **Machine $B_1$** (Capacity: 4,000 h)
    $$ 6 b_{11} + 8 b_{21} \le 4000 $$
4.  **Machine $B_2$** (Capacity: 7,000 h)
    $$ 4 b_{12} + 11 b_{32} \le 7000 $$
5.  **Machine $B_3$** (Capacity: 4,000 h)
    $$ 7 b_{13} \le 4000 $$

### B. Flow Balance Constraints (Conservation of Mass)
The quantity of product $j$ processed in Stage A must equal the quantity processed in Stage B (and ultimately sold).

1.  **Product I:**
    $$ a_{11} + a_{21} = b_{11} + b_{12} + b_{13} = Q_I $$
2.  **Product II:**
    $$ a_{12} + a_{22} = b_{21} = Q_{II} $$
3.  **Product III:**
    $$ a_{23} = b_{32} = Q_{III} $$

### C. Non-negativity Constraints
All production quantities must be non-negative.

$$ a_{ij} \ge 0, \quad b_{ij} \ge 0, \quad Q_j \ge 0 \quad \forall i, j $$
