### 1. Decision Variables

Let $x_{ij}$ be the amount (in kg) of Grade $i$ base liquor used to produce Brand $j$ liquor per day.

*   **Indices:**
    *   $i \in \{I, II, III\}$: Represents the three grades of base liquor.
    *   $j \in \{R, Y, B\}$: Represents the three brands (Red, Yellow, Blue).

*   **Variables:**
    *   $x_{I,R}, x_{II,R}, x_{III,R}$: Amount of Grade I, II, III used for Red.
    *   $x_{I,Y}, x_{II,Y}, x_{III,Y}$: Amount of Grade I, II, III used for Yellow.
    *   $x_{I,B}, x_{II,B}, x_{III,B}$: Amount of Grade I, II, III used for Blue.

Let $P_j$ be the total production quantity of Brand $j$, where $P_j = \sum_{i} x_{ij}$.

### 2. Objective Function

The problem involves **lexicographic optimization** with three prioritized conditions. We define the objectives in order of priority:

**Priority 1 (Hard Constraint/Goal):** Strictly follow specified blending proportions.
*   *Note:* In standard linear programming, "strictly follow" combined with the specific inequalities given in the table (e.g., $< 10\%$) usually implies that these are **hard operational constraints** that must be satisfied for any feasible solution, rather than soft goals to be optimized. If they were soft goals, we would minimize deviation. However, given the phrasing "must strictly follow," we treat the blending ratios as **hard constraints** (see Section 3). If interpreted as a goal to minimize deviation from target ratios, the problem would require target values which are not explicitly provided as single points (only bounds). Therefore, the standard interpretation is that the *feasible region* is defined by these ratios.
*   *Correction based on "Prioritized Conditions":* In Goal Programming contexts, "Priority 1" often means minimizing the deviation from a target. However, no specific target percentages are given, only bounds ($<, >$). Therefore, Priority 1 effectively restricts the feasible space.
*   **Priority 2 (Maximize Profit):** Maximize total profit.
*   **Priority 3 (Minimize/Maximize Quantity):** Produce at least 2,000 kg of Red brand. This is a lower bound constraint.

Since Priority 1 and 3 are essentially feasibility/limit conditions defined by inequalities, the primary **optimization objective** to be solved is **Maximizing Profit**, subject to the strict blending ratios and production limits.

**Objective Function (Maximize Profit):**
$$ \text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{j \in \{R, Y, B\}} \left( \text{Price}_j \cdot P_j - \sum_{i \in \{I, II, III\}} \text{Cost}_i \cdot x_{ij} \right) $$

Substituting the values from the tables:
*   Price: $R=5.5, Y=5.0, B=4.8$
*   Cost: $I=6.0, II=4.5, III=3.0$

$$ Z = (5.5 P_R - 6.0 x_{I,R} - 4.5 x_{II,R} - 3.0 x_{III,R}) + (5.0 P_Y - 6.0 x_{I,Y} - 4.5 x_{II,Y} - 3.0 x_{III,Y}) + (4.8 P_B - 6.0 x_{I,B} - 4.5 x_{II,B} - 3.0 x_{III,B}) $$

Or expanded by variable:
$$ Z = \sum_{i,j} (\text{Price}_j - \text{Cost}_i) x_{ij} $$
$$ Z = (-0.5 x_{I,R} + 1.0 x_{II,R} + 2.5 x_{III,R}) + (-1.0 x_{I,Y} + 0.5 x_{II,Y} + 2.0 x_{III,Y}) + (-1.2 x_{I,B} + 0.3 x_{II,B} + 1.8 x_{III,B}) $$

### 3. Operational Constraints

**A. Supply Constraints (Raw Material Availability)**
The total usage of each grade cannot exceed its daily supply.
1.  **Grade I:** $x_{I,R} + x_{I,Y} + x_{I,B} \le 1500$
2.  **Grade II:** $x_{II,R} + x_{II,Y} + x_{II,B} \le 2000$
3.  **Grade III:** $x_{III,R} + x_{III,Y} + x_{III,B} \le 1000$

**B. Blending Ratio Constraints (Strictly Followed)**
These constraints define the composition of each brand based on the total production $P_j$. Note that inequalities involving percentages must be linearized by multiplying by $P_j$.

*For Red Brand ($P_R = x_{I,R} + x_{II,R} + x_{III,R}$):*
1.  **Grade III < 10%:**
    $$ x_{III,R} < 0.10 P_R \implies x_{III,R} < 0.10(x_{I,R} + x_{II,R} + x_{III,R}) $$
    $$ 0.9 x_{III,R} - 0.1 x_{I,R} - 0.1 x_{II,R} < 0 $$
    *(In LP, strict inequalities are often treated as $\le$ if the boundary is not optimal, or handled via epsilon. We will use $\le$ for modeling feasibility bounds).*
    $$ 0.1 x_{I,R} + 0.1 x_{II,R} - 0.9 x_{III,R} \ge 0 $$

2.  **Grade I > 50%:**
    $$ x_{I,R} > 0.50 P_R \implies x_{I,R} > 0.50(x_{I,R} + x_{II,R} + x_{III,R}) $$
    $$ 0.5 x_{I,R} - 0.5 x_{II,R} - 0.5 x_{III,R} > 0 $$
    $$ 0.5 x_{I,R} - 0.5 x_{II,R} - 0.5 x_{III,R} \ge 0 $$

*For Yellow Brand ($P_Y = x_{I,Y} + x_{II,Y} + x_{III,Y}$):*
1.  **Grade III < 70%:**
    $$ x_{III,Y} < 0.70 P_Y \implies 0.3 x_{III,Y} - 0.7 x_{I,Y} - 0.7 x_{II,Y} < 0 $$
    $$ 0.7 x_{I,Y} + 0.7 x_{II,Y} - 0.3 x_{III,Y} \ge 0 $$

2.  **Grade I > 20%:**
    $$ x_{I,Y} > 0.20 P_Y \implies 0.8 x_{I,Y} - 0.2 x_{II,Y} - 0.2 x_{III,Y} > 0 $$
    $$ 0.8 x_{I,Y} - 0.2 x_{II,Y} - 0.2 x_{III,Y} \ge 0 $$

*For Blue Brand ($P_B = x_{I,B} + x_{II,B} + x_{III,B}$):*
1.  **Grade III < 50%:**
    $$ x_{III,B} < 0.50 P_B \implies 0.5 x_{III,B} - 0.5 x_{I,B} - 0.5 x_{II,B} < 0 $$
    $$ 0.5 x_{I,B} + 0.5 x_{II,B} - 0.5 x_{III,B} \ge 0 $$

2.  **Grade I > 10%:**
    $$ x_{I,B} > 0.10 P_B \implies 0.9 x_{I,B} - 0.1 x_{II,B} - 0.1 x_{III,B} > 0 $$
    $$ 0.9 x_{I,B} - 0.1 x_{II,B} - 0.1 x_{III,B} \ge 0 $$

**C. Production Quantity Constraints**
1.  **Red Brand Minimum Production:**
    $$ P_R = x_{I,R} + x_{II,R} + x_{III,R} \ge 2000 $$
2.  **Non-negativity:**
    $$ x_{ij} \ge 0 \quad \forall i, j $$
    *(Implicitly, if a brand is not produced, $P_j=0$. However, the constraints above allow $P_j=0$ trivially. If a brand is produced, the ratios apply. To avoid division by zero issues in interpretation, these are modeled as linear inequalities as shown.)*
