## Model Type Declaration
**Model Type:** MILP (Mixed Integer Linear Programming)

**Objective Identification:**
The problem asks to "minimize the total cost." The total cost is defined as the **incremental cost** relative to the current state (all workshops in the Urban area).
- **Savings:** Relocating workshops saves money (negative cost).
- **Transportation Increase:** Relocating workshops increases transport costs (positive cost).
- **Net Objective:** Minimize (Total Increased Transportation Cost - Total Relocation Savings).
- **Unit Check:** Savings are in `10,000 yuan/year`. Transportation costs are in `yuan/t` and `t/year`, so the product is `yuan/year`.
- **Normalization:** To ensure unit consistency, all monetary values must be converted to the same base unit. I will use **yuan/year** as the base unit.
    - Savings parameters from Table C-18 must be multiplied by 10,000.
    - Transportation parameters are already in yuan.

**Cost Inventory (Mandatory Pre-Modeling Step):**
1.  **Savings (Table C-18):**
    *   A->α: 100 (10k) = 1,000,000 yuan
    *   A->β: 100 (10k) = 1,000,000 yuan
    *   B->α: 150 (10k) = 1,500,000 yuan
    *   B->β: 200 (10k) = 2,000,000 yuan
    *   C->α: 100 (10k) = 1,000,000 yuan
    *   C->β: 150 (10k) = 1,500,000 yuan
    *   D->α: 200 (10k) = 2,000,000 yuan
    *   D->β: 150 (10k) = 1,500,000 yuan
    *   E->α: 50 (10k) = 500,000 yuan

    *   $C_{AC}=1000, C_{AD}=1500, C_{BC}=1400, C_{BD}=1200, C_{CE}=2000, C_{DE}=700$. Others 0.

### Parameters

    *   Non-zero values: $C_{AC}=C_{CA}=1000$, $C_{AD}=C_{DA}=1500$, $C_{BC}=C_{CB}=1400$, $C_{BD}=C_{DB}=1200$, $C_{CE}=C_{EC}=2000$, $C_{DE}=C_{ED}=700$.

## 2. Decision Variables

Let $x_{i,l}$ be a binary variable:
$$
x_{i,l} = 
\begin{cases} 
1 & \text{if workshop } i \text{ is located in location } l \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\quad \forall i \in W, l \in L
$$

Let $y_{ik}$ be a binary variable representing the relative location relationship for transportation cost calculation. However, since the transport cost depends on the *pair* of locations, it is more efficient to use auxiliary variables or direct expansion. Given the small size (5 workshops), we can use a standard formulation with auxiliary binary variables $z_{i,k,j,l}$ indicating if $i$ is in $j$ and $k$ is in $l$, OR simply expand the cost term using the linearity of expectation/sums if we define the cost contribution per pair.

A cleaner approach for MILP:
Define $z_{i,k,j,l}$ as binary variables? No, that's too many variables ($5 \times 4 \times 3 \times 2$).
Better approach: Since $C_{ik}$ is fixed, the cost between $i$ and $k$ is determined by their locations $j$ and $l$.
Let's define the objective using the $x$ variables directly. The term for cost between $i$ and $k$ is $C_{ik} \sum_{j \in L} \sum_{l \in L} D_{jl} x_{i,j} x_{k,l}$.
This is **quadratic** (product of binary variables). To linearize, we introduce auxiliary variables.

Let $w_{i,k,j,l}$ be a binary variable:
$$
w_{i,k,j,l} = 
\begin{cases} 
1 & \text{if workshop } i \text{ is in } j \text{ AND workshop } k \text{ is in } l \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\quad \forall i \neq k \in W, j,l \in L
$$

**Domain:**
$x_{i,l} \in \{0,1\}$
$w_{i,k,j,l} \in \{0,1\}$

## 3. Objective Function

Minimize Total Net Cost = (Total Increased Transportation Cost) - (Total Savings)

Note: The "baseline" transportation cost (all in Urban) is constant and does not affect the optimization decision, so we minimize the *change*.
Change in transport cost for pair $(i,k)$ = $C_{ik} (D_{loc(i), loc(k)} - D_{U,U})$.
However, it is simpler to calculate the total new transport cost and subtract the savings, then subtract the constant baseline transport cost?
Actually, the problem says "minimize total cost". Usually, this implies minimizing the *net* impact.
Let's define the objective as:
$$ \text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{i \neq k} \sum_{j \in L} \sum_{l \in L} C_{ik} D_{jl} w_{i,k,j,l} - \sum_{i \in W} \sum_{l \in L} S_{i,l} x_{i,l} $$
*Wait*, $C_{ik}$ is symmetric. The sum over $i \neq k$ counts each pair twice (i,k and k,i).
Standard practice: Sum over $i < k$.
Also, $D_{jl}$ is the cost from $j$ to $l$. Since transport is bidirectional in volume terms (usually $C_{ik}$ implies flow between them, and cost is per ton), we assume the cost is incurred for the flow. If $C_{ik}$ is the total annual volume exchanged, the cost is $C_{ik} \times D_{loc(i), loc(k)}$.
Since $D_{jl}$ is defined "from j to l", and $C_{ik}$ is "between i and k", we assume the cost is symmetric or we sum over directed pairs. Given $D_{jl}$ values: $D_{\alpha U} = 1300$ and $D_{U \alpha} = 1300$ (symmetric). $D_{\alpha \beta} = 1400, D_{\beta \alpha} = 1400$ (symmetric). The matrix is symmetric.
So, Cost$_{ik} = C_{ik} D_{loc(i), loc(k)}$.

Objective:
$$ \text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{i < k, i,k \in W} C_{ik} \sum_{j \in L} \sum_{l \in L} D_{jl} w_{i,k,j,l} - \sum_{i \in W} \sum_{l \in L} S_{i,l} x_{i,l} $$

*Note on Baseline:* The problem asks to minimize total cost *of the plan*. The savings are relative to the urban location. The transportation costs are absolute.
If we keep all in Urban, Savings = 0, Transport Cost = $\sum C_{ik} D_{UU}$.
If we move, Savings > 0, Transport Cost changes.
The objective above calculates (New Transport Cost - Savings).
Does this correctly reflect "Total Cost"?
Total Cost = (Fixed Costs) + (Transport Cost) - (Savings).
Since Fixed Costs and Baseline Transport Cost are constants, minimizing (New Transport Cost - Savings) is equivalent to minimizing the Net Change in Total Cost. This is the standard interpretation.

## 4. Operational Constraints

### 1. Location Assignment Constraints
Each workshop must be located in exactly one location.
$$ \sum_{l \in L} x_{i,l} = 1 \quad \forall i \in W $$

### 2. Capacity Constraints
No more than 3 workshops in any location.
$$ \sum_{i \in W} x_{i,l} \le 3 \quad \forall l \in L $$

### 3. Auxiliary Variable Linking Constraints
The variable $w_{i,k,j,l}$ must be 1 if and only if $x_{i,j}=1$ and $x_{k,l}=1$.
Since $x$ are binary, we can use the following linear constraints:
$$ w_{i,k,j,l} \le x_{i,j} \quad \forall i \neq k \in W, j,l \in L $$
$$ w_{i,k,j,l} \le x_{k,l} \quad \forall i \neq k \in W, j,l \in L $$
$$ w_{i,k,j,l} \ge x_{i,j} + x_{k,l} - 1 \quad \forall i \neq k \in W, j,l \in L $$
$$ w_{i,k,j,l} \ge 0 \quad \forall i \neq k \in W, j,l \in L $$

### 4. Symmetry/Consistency of w
For a specific pair $(i,k)$, the location is uniquely determined. Thus, for fixed $i,k$, the sum of all $w_{i,k,j,l}$ must be 1.
$$ \sum_{j \in L} \sum_{l \in L} w_{i,k,j,l} = 1 \quad \forall i < k \in W $$
*(Note: This constraint is actually redundant if the linking constraints and assignment constraints are tight, but it helps solver performance. Actually, it's not redundant. If $x_{i,j}=1$ and $x_{k,l}=1$, then $w_{i,k,j,l} \ge 1$ and $w_{i,k,j,l} \le 1$, so $w=1$. For any other $j',l'$, $w \le 0$, so $w=0$. The sum is naturally 1. So this constraint is redundant but harmless. I will omit it to keep the model minimal, relying on the linking constraints.)*

Wait, there is a subtlety. The linking constraints ensure $w \le x$ and $w \ge x+x-1$.
If $x_{i,j}=1$, then for any $l$, $w_{i,k,j,l} \le x_{k,l}$.
If $x_{k,l}=1$, then $w_{i,k,j,l} \le 1$.
Also $w_{i,k,j,l} \ge 1 + 1 - 1 = 1$.
So $w_{i,k,j,l}=1$.
For any other $l' \neq l$, $x_{k,l'}=0$, so $w_{i,k,j,l'} \le 0 \implies w=0$.
So the logic holds.

### 5. Non-negativity
All variables are binary, so non-negativity is implied.

## 6. Final Model Summary

**Model Type:** MILP

**Index Sets:**
*   $W = \{A, B, C, D, E\}$
*   $L = \{\alpha, \beta, U\}$
*   $P = \{(i,k) \mid i,k \in W, i < k\}$

**Parameters:**
*   $S_{i,l}$: Savings (yuan/year) as listed in Table C-18 (multiplied by 10,000).
*   $C_{ik}$: Volume (t/year) as listed in Table C-19.
*   $D_{jl}$: Rate (yuan/t) as listed in Table C-20.

**Variables:**
*   $x_{i,l} \in \{0,1\} \quad \forall i \in W, l \in L$
*   $w_{i,k,j,l} \in \{0,1\} \quad \forall (i,k) \in P, j,l \in L$

**Objective Function:**
$$ \text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{(i,k) \in P} C_{ik} \sum_{j \in L} \sum_{l \in L} D_{jl} w_{i,k,j,l} - \sum_{i \in W} \sum_{l \in L} S_{i,l} x_{i,l} $$

**Constraints:**
1.  **Assignment:**
    $$ \sum_{l \in L} x_{i,l} = 1 \quad \forall i \in W $$
2.  **Capacity:**
    $$ \sum_{i \in W} x_{i,l} \le 3 \quad \forall l \in L $$
3.  **Linking (i in j):**
    $$ w_{i,k,j,l} \le x_{i,j} \quad \forall (i,k) \in P, j,l \in L $$
4.  **Linking (k in l):**
    $$ w_{i,k,j,l} \le x_{k,l} \quad \forall (i,k) \in P, j,l \in L $$
5.  **Linking (AND condition):**
    $$ w_{i,k,j,l} \ge x_{i,j} + x_{k,l} - 1 \quad \forall (i,k) \in P, j,l \in L $$
6.  **Bounds:**
    $$ x_{i,l} \ge 0, \quad w_{i,k,j,l} \ge 0 $$
    *(Implicitly $\le 1$ due to binary domain)*
