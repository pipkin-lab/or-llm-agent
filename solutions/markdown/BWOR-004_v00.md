### 1. Decision Variables

Let $t$ denote the month, where $t \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$.

*   **$R_t$**: Number of regular (skilled) workers available at the beginning of month $t$, before hiring/attrition adjustments for production capacity calculation (Note: Standard formulation usually defines state at end of month or start of month. Let's define state variables clearly).
    *   Let $W_t$ be the number of skilled workers working in month $t$.
    *   Let $H_t$ be the number of new trainees hired at the beginning of month $t$.
*   **$X_t$**: Number of units produced in month $t$.
*   **$I_t$**: Ending inventory of units at the end of month $t$.
*   **$Y_t$**: Number of skilled workers available at the beginning of month $t$ (before attrition, after previous month's trainees have graduated). *Correction for clarity*: It is easier to define the workforce evolution directly.

**Refined Decision Variables:**

1.  **$W_t$**: Number of skilled workers employed in month $t$.
2.  **$H_t$**: Number of trainees hired at the start of month $t$.
3.  **$X_t$**: Production quantity in month $t$ (units).
4.  **$I_t$**: Ending inventory in month $t$ (units).

*Note: The number of skilled workers in month $t+1$ depends on $W_t$, $H_t$, and attrition. We will express the workforce balance as a constraint.*

### 2. Objective Function

**Goal**: Maximize Total Profit (Revenue - Costs).

**Revenue**:
$$ \text{Revenue}_t = 240 X_t $$
*Note: In a make-to-stock model with fixed demand, we usually assume all demand is met from production + inventory. The "Revenue" is generated when goods are sold. Since the problem asks to maximize "total revenue" but provides cost structures, the standard OR interpretation is to **Maximize Net Profit**. If strict "Revenue" maximization were intended, one would produce/sell as much as possible, but constraints limit this. Given the cost details, the objective is implicitly **Maximize Total Profit**.*

**Costs**:
1.  **Raw Materials**: $150 X_t$
2.  **Labor Cost (Skilled)**: $2000 W_t$
3.  **Labor Cost (Trainees)**: $800 H_t$
4.  **Training Opportunity Cost**: Training consumes 50 hours of a skilled worker's time. This reduces production capacity. It is handled in the capacity constraint, not as a direct dollar cost in the objective, unless we value the skilled worker's time at their opportunity cost (production value). However, standard formulation handles this via the capacity constraint.
5.  **Inventory Holding Cost**: $10 I_t$ (for $t=1$ to $5$, and potentially $t=6$ if ending inventory is non-zero, though the constraint says $I_6 \le 100$. Usually, holding cost applies to the inventory held *during* the month or at the end. Let's assume cost applies to ending inventory $I_t$ for $t=1..5$ and potentially $I_6$ if we strictly follow "storage cost... per month". The problem says "if garments are not delivered... incur... per month". This implies cost is incurred on the inventory held. Let's apply $10 I_t$ for $t=1 \dots 6$).

**Objective Function:**
$$ \text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{t=1}^{6} \left( 240 X_t - 150 X_t - 2000 W_t - 800 H_t - 10 I_t \right) $$
Simplifying the unit contribution:
$$ \text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{t=1}^{6} \left( 90 X_t - 2000 W_t - 800 H_t - 10 I_t \right) $$

### 3. Operational Constraints

**A. Workforce Dynamics**

1.  **Initial Workforce**:
    At the beginning of January ($t=1$), there are 80 skilled workers.
    $$ W_1 = 80 + \text{Trainees from Dec} $$
    Since we start the year fresh, assume no trainees from previous year.
    $$ W_1 = 80 $$
    *(Note: The 2% attrition usually applies to the workforce existing at the start of the month. Let's define the transition carefully.)*

    Let's define the state more precisely for the constraints:
    *   $W_t$: Skilled workers working in month $t$.
    *   $H_t$: Trainees hired in month $t$.
    *   Trainees hired in month $t$ become skilled workers in month $t+1$.
    *   Attrition: 2% of skilled workers leave. This typically applies to the workers present at the beginning of the month.

    **Workforce Balance Constraint for $t=1$ to $5$**:
    The number of skilled workers available to work in month $t+1$ is determined by the skilled workers in month $t$ (minus attrition) plus the trainees who completed training in month $t$.
    
    *Attrition Logic*: "2% of skilled workers... leave". Do they leave at the start or end? "At the beginning of January... 80 workers". "Each month, 2% ... leave". Usually, attrition is modeled as a fraction of the current workforce leaving at the end of the period or start of the next. Let's assume attrition happens at the end of month $t$ from the pool $W_t$.
    
    $$ W_{t+1} = W_t (1 - 0.02) + H_t $$
    $$ W_{t+1} = 0.98 W_t + H_t $$
    
    For $t=1$:
    $$ W_1 = 80 $$
    
    For $t=2, 3, 4, 5$:
    $$ W_{t} = 0.98 W_{t-1} + H_{t-1} $$
    
    For $t=6$:
    $$ W_6 = 0.98 W_5 + H_5 $$
    *(Note: $H_6$ would become skilled in July, which is outside the planning horizon, so they don't affect $W_6$ production capacity, but they incur cost and consume training capacity in month 6. Do we hire in June? Yes, if it helps production in June? No, trainees cannot produce in June. They only become skilled in July. So hiring in June ($H_6$) only incurs cost and consumes training hours in June, providing no production benefit in the 6-month horizon. Thus, optimally, $H_6 = 0$. We can explicitly state $H_6 = 0$ or let the optimizer decide, but logically it's 0.)*

**B. Production Capacity Constraints**

Each unit requires 4 hours of skilled labor.
Each trainee requires 50 hours of skilled labor for training.
Total available skilled labor hours in month $t$: $160 W_t$.

$$ 4 X_t + 50 H_t \le 160 W_t $$
$$ \text{or} \quad X_t \le 40 W_t - 12.5 H_t $$

**C. Inventory Balance Constraints**

Demand $D_t$:
$D_1=3000, D_2=3600, D_3=4000, D_4=4600, D_5=4800, D_6=5000$.

Initial Inventory $I_0 = 400$.

$$ I_t = I_{t-1} + X_t - D_t $$

Subject to:
$$ I_t \ge 0 \quad \forall t \in \{1, \dots, 6\} $$
$$ I_6 \le 100 $$

**D. Non-negativity and Integer Constraints**

*   Workers and trainees must be integers (or large enough to treat as continuous, but typically integers in workforce planning).
*   Production and inventory are integers (or continuous if scales are large, but units are discrete).

$$ W_t, H_t, X_t, I_t \ge 0 $$
$$ W_t, H_t, X_t, I_t \in \mathbb{Z}^+ \quad (\text{or } \mathbb{R}^+ \text{ if relaxation is accepted}) $$

**E. Specific Month Constraints Summary**

1.  **Workforce**:
    *   $W_1 = 80$
    *   $W_t = 0.98 W_{t-1} + H_{t-1}$ for $t=2,3,4,5,6$
    *   $H_6 = 0$ (Optimality condition, or strictly $H_t \ge 0$)

2.  **Capacity**:
    *   $4 X_t + 50 H_t \le 160 W_t$ for $t=1,\dots,6$

3.  **Inventory**:
    *   $I_1 = 400 + X_1 - 3000$
    *   $I_t = I_{t-1} + X_t - D_t$ for $t=2,\dots,6$
    *   $I_t \ge 0$ for all $t$
    *   $I_6 \le 100$

4.  **Objective**:
    *   Maximize $\sum_{t=1}^{6} (90 X_t - 2000 W_t - 800 H_t - 10 I_t)$
