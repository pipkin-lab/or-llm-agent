### MANDATORY COST INVENTORY (PRE-MODELING STEP)

**Objective Identification Gate:**
The problem asks to "maximize profit".
- **Profit Definition:** (Total Revenue from Sales) - (Total Cost of Purchases).
- **Parameters Extracted:**
    - **Initial Inventory ($I_0$):** 500 units.
    - **Warehouse Capacity ($C$):** 1,000 units.
    - **Buying Prices ($b_k$):**
        - Month 1: 10
        - Month 2: 9
        - Month 3: 11
        - Month 4: 15
    - **Selling Prices ($s_k$):**
        - Month 1: 12
        - Month 2: 9
        - Month 3: 13
        - Month 4: 17
- **Cost/Price Assignment Check:**
    - We have 4 months, 4 buying prices, 4 selling prices.
    - Buying prices are costs associated with the purchase variable $x_k$.
    - Selling prices are revenues associated with the sales variable $y_k$.
    - All objective parameters are explicitly provided in Table 7-6. No `[NOT_PROVIDED]` values.

**Model Type Declaration:**
- **Type:** MILP (Mixed Integer Linear Programming)
- **Reasoning:** The decision variables (units purchased, units sold, inventory levels) represent discrete physical units (products). The problem states "product quantities are in integers". The objective and constraints are linear.

---

### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
- $K = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$: Set of months (January to April).

**Parameters:**
- $I_0 = 500$: Initial inventory at the start of Month 1 (units).
- $C = 1,000$: Maximum warehouse capacity (units).
- $b_k$: Buying price per unit in month $k$ (currency/unit).
    - $b_1 = 10$
    - $b_2 = 9$
    - $b_3 = 11$
    - $b_4 = 15$
- $s_k$: Selling price per unit in month $k$ (currency/unit).
    - $s_1 = 12$
    - $s_2 = 9$
    - $s_3 = 13$
    - $s_4 = 17$

---

### 2. Decision Variables

Let the variables be defined for each month $k \in K$:

1.  $x_k$: Number of units **purchased** in month $k$.
    - Domain: $x_k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ (Non-negative integers)
    - *Note:* Goods purchased in month $k$ are available for sale starting in month $k+1$. Therefore, $x_4$ represents purchases made in April. Since the planning horizon ends in April, these goods do not contribute to sales revenue within the horizon. However, the problem asks for a plan over 4 months. Usually, in such finite-horizon problems, purchasing in the final period is only beneficial if there is a salvage value or if the "plan" implies holding them. But typically, "maximize profit over the next 4 months" implies realizing cash flow within those 4 months. Purchasing in Month 4 yields no sales revenue in Months 1-4. Thus, $x_4$ will naturally be 0 in the optimal solution unless there is a terminal value, which is not stated. We include it for completeness of the "purchase decision" but it will be zero.

2.  $y_k$: Number of units **sold** in month $k$.
    - Domain: $y_k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ (Non-negative integers)

3.  $I_k$: Inventory level at the **end** of month $k$.
    - Domain: $I_k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ (Non-negative integers)
    - *Timing Note:* The problem states "If the store decides to purchase goods in a given month, those goods will only be available starting the following month."
    - This implies the inventory balance for month $k$ depends on:
        - Inventory at the start of month $k$ (which is $I_{k-1}$, with $I_0 = 500$).
        - Purchases made in month $k-1$ ($x_{k-1}$) become available during month $k$.
        - Sales made in month $k$ ($y_k$) are deducted from available stock.
    - Therefore, the inventory balance equation is:
      $$I_k = I_{k-1} + x_{k-1} - y_k$$
      For $k=1$: $I_1 = I_0 + x_0 - y_1$. Since no purchases are made before month 1, $x_0 = 0$. So, $I_1 = 500 - y_1$.
      For $k=2$: $I_2 = I_1 + x_1 - y_2$.
      For $k=3$: $I_3 = I_2 + x_2 - y_3$.
      For $k=4$: $I_4 = I_3 + x_3 - y_4$.

---

### 3. Objective Function

Maximize total profit (Total Revenue - Total Cost of Purchases).

$$ \text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{k=1}^{4} (s_k \cdot y_k) - \sum_{k=1}^{4} (b_k \cdot x_k) $$

Substituting the values:
$$ Z = (12y_1 + 9y_2 + 13y_3 + 17y_4) - (10x_1 + 9x_2 + 11x_3 + 15x_4) $$

*Note on $x_4$:* Since $x_4$ does not add to inventory available for sale in months 1-4 (it arrives for Month 5), it generates cost but no revenue within the horizon. The solver will set $x_4 = 0$ to maximize profit.

---

### 4. Operational Constraints

**1. Inventory Balance Constraints:**
For each month $k$, the ending inventory is determined by the starting inventory, incoming goods (from previous month's purchase), and outgoing goods (current month's sales).

- **Month 1:**
  $$ I_1 = I_0 + x_0 - y_1 $$
  Given $I_0 = 500$ and $x_0 = 0$:
  $$ I_1 = 500 - y_1 $$

- **Month 2:**
  $$ I_2 = I_1 + x_1 - y_2 $$

- **Month 3:**
  $$ I_3 = I_2 + x_2 - y_3 $$

- **Month 4:**
  $$ I_4 = I_3 + x_3 - y_4 $$

**2. Warehouse Capacity Constraints:**
The inventory at the end of each month cannot exceed the warehouse capacity $C = 1,000$. Also, inventory cannot be negative (handled by variable domains, but good to state).

- $$ I_1 \le 1000 $$
- $$ I_2 \le 1000 $$
- $$ I_3 \le 1000 $$
- $$ I_4 \le 1000 $$

*Implicit Constraint:* The store can only sell what is currently in the warehouse. The balance equation $I_k = I_{k-1} + x_{k-1} - y_k$ combined with $I_k \ge 0$ ensures that $y_k \le I_{k-1} + x_{k-1}$.

**3. Non-negativity and Integrality Constraints:**
- $x_k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ for $k = 1, 2, 3, 4$
- $y_k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ for $k = 1, 2, 3, 4$
- $I_k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ for $k = 1, 2, 3, 4$

### 5. Feasibility Sanity Check & Logic Verification

1.  **Supply vs Demand:** This is an inventory management problem, not a fixed supply/demand matching problem. The "demand" is met by sales $y_k$, which is a decision variable bounded by available inventory.
2.  **Timing Check:**
    - Buy in Month 1 ($x_1$) $\rightarrow$ Available in Month 2.
    - Buy in Month 2 ($x_2$) $\rightarrow$ Available in Month 3.
    - Buy in Month 3 ($x_3$) $\rightarrow$ Available in Month 4.
    - Buy in Month 4 ($x_4$) $\rightarrow$ Available in Month 5 (outside horizon, so effectively useless for profit within the 4 months).
3.  **Initial Stock:** 500 units available in Month 1.
4.  **Capacity:** 1000 units.
    - Max possible inventory after Month 1 sale: If we buy 1000 in Month 1, it arrives Month 2.
    - Let's check if capacity is binding.
    - $I_1 = 500 - y_1$. Max $I_1 = 500$ (if $y_1=0$).
    - $I_2 = I_1 + x_1 - y_2$. Max $I_2 = 500 + 1000 - 0 = 1500$? No, $x_1$ is limited by the fact that we can only buy enough to fill the warehouse *if* we consider the purchase happens and then stock up. Wait, the constraint is $I_k \le 1000$.
    - So, $I_2 \le 1000$.
    - This implies $x_1$ cannot be arbitrarily large.
5.  **Profitability Check (to ensure model direction is correct):**
    - Month 1: Buy 10, Sell 12. Profit margin 2. But goods bought in M1 arrive M2. Goods in M1 are initial stock (cost sunk or historical).
    - Month 2: Buy 9, Sell 9. Margin 0.
    - Month 3: Buy 11, Sell 13. Margin 2.
    - Month 4: Buy 15, Sell 17. Margin 2.
    
    *Strategic Insight for Verification:*
    - Initial 500 units: Sell in M1 (price 12) or hold?
    - If sell in M1: Revenue $500 \times 12 = 6000$.
    - If hold to M2: Price 9. Worse.
    - If hold to M3: Price 13. Better than M1.
    - If hold to M4: Price 17. Best.
    - Capacity is 1000. We have 500. We can store up to 500 more? No, we can store up to 1000 total. So we can hold the 500.
    - Can we buy more to sell in M4?
    - Buy in M3 ($b_3=11$), sell in M4 ($s_4=17$). Profit 6/unit.
    - Buy in M2 ($b_2=9$), sell in M4 ($s_4=17$). Profit 8/unit.
    - Buy in M1 ($b_1=10$), sell in M4 ($s_4=17$). Profit 7/unit.
    - Buy in M4 ($b_4=15$), sell in M4 ($s_4=17$)? No, buy in M4 arrives M5. So cannot sell in M4.
    - So, optimal strategy likely involves buying in M2 and M3 to sell in M4, and deciding what to do with initial 500.
    - Initial 500: Sell in M4 for 17? Or sell earlier to free up capacity?
    - Capacity is 1000.
    - If we hold initial 500 to M4, we have 500 space left.
    - We can buy in M2 (arrives M3) or M3 (arrives M4).
    - If we buy in M2, it arrives M3. We can hold it to M4.
    - Let's say we buy $x_2$. It arrives M3. $I_3 = I_2 + x_2 - y_3$.
    - If we don't sell in M1, M2, M3, $I_1=500, I_2=500+x_1, I_3=500+x_1+x_2, I_4=500+x_1+x_2+x_3$.
    - Constraint $I_4 \le 1000$.
    - To maximize profit, we want to sell at highest price (M4, $s=17$).
    - We want to buy at lowest price.
    - Cheapest buy price is M2 ($b=9$). Next is M1 ($b=10$).
    - If we buy in M2, it arrives M3. We can hold to M4.
    - If we buy in M1, it arrives M2. We can hold to M4.
    - Since $b_2 < b_1$, we prefer buying in M2 over M1 if possible.
    - However, buying in M2 requires capacity in M3 and M4.
    - Buying in M1 requires capacity in M2, M3, M4.
    - Since capacity is the same, and holding cost is 0, we prefer the cheapest source.
    - Source M2 is cheapest. So we should maximize $x_2$?
    - $x_2$ arrives M3. $I_3 = I_2 + x_2 - y_3$.
    - If we sell nothing in M1, M2, M3: $I_1=500, I_2=500, I_3=500+x_2, I_4=500+x_2+x_3$.
    - Max $I_4 = 1000$.
    - So $x_2 + x_3 \le 500$.
    - We want to minimize cost. $b_2=9, b_3=11$.
    - So we should prioritize $x_2$.
    - Set $x_2 = 500, x_3 = 0$.
    - Then $I_4 = 1000$.
    - Sell all 1000 in M4.
    - Revenue: $1000 \times 17 = 17000$.
    - Cost: $500 \times 9 = 4500$.
    - Profit: $12500$.
    - What about selling some earlier?
    - If we sell 500 in M1 (price 12):
      - $I_1 = 0$.
      - Buy 1000 in M2? Arrives M3.
      - $I_2 = 0$.
      - $I_3 = 1000$.
      - Sell 0 in M3.
      - $I_4 = 1000$.
      - Sell 1000 in M4.
      - Revenue: $500 \times 12 + 1000 \times 17 = 6000 + 17000 = 23000$.
      - Cost: $1000 \times 9 = 9000$.
      - Profit: $14000$.
    - This is better.
    - Can we do better?
    - Buy in M1? $b_1=10$. More expensive than M2.
    - So, sell initial stock in M1 to free up capacity? No, selling initial stock *reduces* inventory, allowing us to *buy more* in later months?
    - Wait. Capacity is on *inventory*.
    - If I keep 500 in M1, I have 500 space left.
    - If I sell 500 in M1, I have 1000 space left.
    - Buying in M2 fills space in M3/M4.
    - So yes, selling early (at a loss relative to M4 price, but freeing capacity for cheaper bought goods) is beneficial if the arbitrage profit (Sell Price - Buy Price) on the *new* goods is higher than the opportunity cost of the *old* goods.
    - Old goods opportunity cost: Sell in M4 (17) vs Sell in M1 (12). Loss of 5 per unit.
    - New goods gain: Buy in M2 (9), Sell in M4 (17). Gain of 8 per unit.
    - Net gain per unit of capacity freed: $8 - 5 = 3$.
    - So yes, sell initial stock in M1, buy max in M2, sell in M4.
    
    The model formulation correctly captures this trade-off. The solver will find this.

### Final Model Summary

**Model Type:** MILP

**Sets:**
$K = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$

**Parameters:**
$I_0 = 500$
$C = 1000$
$b = [10, 9, 11, 15]$
$s = [12, 9, 13, 17]$

**Variables:**
$x_k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ (Purchases in month $k$)
$y_k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ (Sales in month $k$)
$I_k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ (End-of-month inventory)

**Objective:**
$$ \text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{k=1}^4 s_k y_k - \sum_{k=1}^4 b_k x_k $$

**Constraints:**
1.  **Inventory Balance:**
    - $I_1 = 500 - y_1$
    - $I_2 = I_1 + x_1 - y_2$
    - $I_3 = I_2 + x_2 - y_3$
    - $I_4 = I_3 + x_3 - y_4$
2.  **Capacity:**
    - $I_1 \le 1000$
    - $I_2 \le 1000$
    - $I_3 \le 1000$
    - $I_4 \le 1000$
3.  **Non-negativity/Integrality:**
    - $x_k, y_k, I_k \ge 0$ and integer for all $k$.
