Here is the Operations Research model for the hospital nurse scheduling problem involving full-time and contract nurses.

### 1. Decision Variables

Let $i$ index the 6 possible shift start times:
*   $i=1$: 2:00
*   $i=2$: 6:00
*   $i=3$: 10:00
*   $i=4$: 14:00
*   $i=5$: 18:00
*   $i=6$: 22:00

We define two sets of non-negative integer decision variables for each shift start time $i$:

*   $x_i$: The number of **full-time** nurses assigned to the shift starting at time $i$.
*   $y_i$: The number of **contract** nurses assigned to the shift starting at time $i$.

**Domain:** $x_i, y_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ for $i = 1, \dots, 6$.

### 2. Objective Function

The goal is to minimize the total labor cost.
*   Full-time wage: ¥10/hour. Since the shift is 8 hours, the cost per full-time nurse per shift is $10 \times 8 = ¥80$.
*   Contract wage: ¥15/hour. Since the shift is 8 hours, the cost per contract nurse per shift is $15 \times 8 = ¥120$.

**Objective Function:**
$$ \text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{i=1}^{6} (80 x_i + 120 y_i) $$

### 3. Operational Constraints

The hospital must satisfy the minimum nurse requirements for each 4-hour coverage period. A nurse working an 8-hour shift covers exactly two consecutive 4-hour periods.

Let $C_j$ be the constraint for coverage period $j$ ($j=1, \dots, 6$).

**Coverage Periods and Requirements:**
1.  **2:00 – 6:00**: Requires 10 nurses.
    *   Covered by shifts starting at 22:00 ($i=6$) and 2:00 ($i=1$).
2.  **6:00 – 10:00**: Requires 15 nurses.
    *   Covered by shifts starting at 2:00 ($i=1$) and 6:00 ($i=2$).
3.  **10:00 – 14:00**: Requires 25 nurses.
    *   Covered by shifts starting at 6:00 ($i=2$) and 10:00 ($i=3$).
4.  **14:00 – 18:00**: Requires 20 nurses.
    *   Covered by shifts starting at 10:00 ($i=3$) and 14:00 ($i=4$).
5.  **18:00 – 22:00**: Requires 18 nurses.
    *   Covered by shifts starting at 14:00 ($i=4$) and 18:00 ($i=5$).
6.  **22:00 – 2:00**: Requires 12 nurses.
    *   Covered by shifts starting at 18:00 ($i=5$) and 22:00 ($i=6$).

**Mathematical Constraints:**

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_6 + y_6 + x_1 + y_1 &\ge 10 \quad &\text{(2:00–6:00)} \\
x_1 + y_1 + x_2 + y_2 &\ge 15 \quad &\text{(6:00–10:00)} \\
x_2 + y_2 + x_3 + y_3 &\ge 25 \quad &\text{(10:00–14:00)} \\
x_3 + y_3 + x_4 + y_4 &\ge 20 \quad &\text{(14:00–18:00)} \\
x_4 + y_4 + x_5 + y_5 &\ge 18 \quad &\text{(18:00–22:00)} \\
x_5 + y_5 + x_6 + y_6 &\ge 12 \quad &\text{(22:00–2:00)}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Non-negativity and Integrality:**
$$ x_i, y_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}, \quad \forall i \in \{1, \dots, 6\} $$

***

**Note on the Strategic Question:**
The model above allows the solver to determine the optimal mix. Since contract nurses are strictly more expensive (¥120 vs ¥80 per shift), the linear programming relaxation would naturally prefer full-time nurses. However, if there are implicit constraints not listed (such as a maximum number of full-time nurses available or a minimum requirement for contract nurses), those would be added as additional constraints (e.g., $x_i \le U_{FT}$). Based *strictly* on the provided data and costs, the optimal solution will utilize full-time nurses as much as possible, and contract nurses will only be used if there is a capacity limit on full-time hiring or if the problem implies that "hiring" refers to the total headcount pool where full-time nurses are a fixed, limited resource.

*Assuming the question implies that full-time nurses are a fixed, limited pool (e.g., the hospital can only hire up to $N_{max}$ full-time nurses total or per shift) and must use contract nurses to fill the rest, a constraint such as $\sum x_i \le \text{Available Full-Time Nurses}$ would be required. Without such a limit, the mathematical answer is to hire **zero** contract nurses, as they are costlier.*
