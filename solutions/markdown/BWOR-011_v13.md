# Transportation Problem: Power Allocation with Flexible Demand

## Model Type: LP (Linear Programming)

This is a standard transportation problem where total supply (850) exceeds total demand (920). The problem explicitly requires using up all available electricity. Since total supply < total demand, some demand will go unmet, but all supply must be allocated.

## Index Sets & Parameters

**Sets:**
- $I = \{1, 2\}$: Set of power stations (Station I, Station II)
- $J = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of cities

**Parameters:**

| Parameter | Value | Description |
|-----------|-------|-------------|
| $S_1$ | 400 | Max supply capacity of Station I (units) |
| $S_2$ | 450 | Max supply capacity of Station II (units) |
| $d_{1}^{min}$ | 290 | Minimum demand for City 1 (320 - 30 reduction) |
| $d_{1}^{max}$ | 320 | Maximum demand for City 1 |
| $d_{2}^{min}$ | 250 | Fixed demand for City 2 |
| $d_{2}^{max}$ | 250 | Fixed demand for City 2 |
| $d_{3}^{min}$ | 270 | Minimum demand for City 3 |
| $d_{3}^{max}$ | 350 | Maximum demand for City 3 |

**Unit Costs $c_{ij}$ (Yuan per unit):**

| $c_{ij}$ | City 1 | City 2 | City 3 |
|----------|--------|--------|--------|
| Station I ($i=1$) | 15 | 18 | 22 |
| Station II ($i=2$) | 21 | 25 | 16 |

**Total Supply:** $S_{total} = 400 + 450 = 850$ units
**Total Demand Range:** $[290+250+270, 320+250+350] = [810, 920]$

Since $S_{total} = 850$ falls within $[810, 920]$, a feasible solution exists.

## Decision Variables

Let $x_{ij}$ = amount of electricity (units) shipped from Station $i$ to City $j$

**Domain:** $x_{ij} \geq 0$ for all $i \in I, j \in J$ (Continuous variables, as electricity can be divided)

Specifically:
- $x_{11}, x_{12}, x_{13} \geq 0$ (from Station I)
- $x_{21}, x_{22}, x_{23} \geq 0$ (from Station II)

## Objective Function

**MINIMIZE** Total transportation cost:

$$\min Z = 15x_{11} + 18x_{12} + 22x_{13} + 21x_{21} + 25x_{22} + 16x_{23}$$

## Operational Constraints

### 1. Supply Constraints (All supply must be used - EQUALITY per Rule 9/18)

Since the problem states "uses up all available electricity from both stations":

$$x_{11} + x_{12} + x_{13} = 400 \quad \text{(Station I full capacity)}$$

$$x_{21} + x_{22} + x_{23} = 450 \quad \text{(Station II full capacity)}$$

### 2. Demand Constraints (Flexible bounds for City 1 and City 3, fixed for City 2)

**City 1** (can be reduced by up to 30 units from 320):
$$290 \leq x_{11} + x_{21} \leq 320$$

Split into:
$$x_{11} + x_{21} \geq 290$$
$$x_{11} + x_{21} \leq 320$$

**City 2** (fixed at 250):
$$x_{12} + x_{22} = 250$$

**City 3** (at least 270, up to 350):
$$270 \leq x_{12} + x_{23} \leq 350$$

Split into:
$$x_{13} + x_{23} \geq 270$$
$$x_{13} + x_{23} \leq 350$$

### 3. Non-negativity

$$x_{ij} \geq 0 \quad \forall i \in I, j \in J$$

## Feasibility Sanity Check

**Total supply:** 850 units (must all be allocated)

**Minimum total demand that can be met:** $290 + 250 + 270 = 810$ units
**Maximum total demand:** $320 + 250 + 350 = 920$ units

Since $810 \leq 850 \leq 920$, the total supply of 850 can be fully allocated while respecting all demand bounds.

**Verification of aggregate consistency:**
- If all stations supply at max: total = 850
- City 2 takes exactly 250
- Remaining 600 units go to Cities 1 and 3
- City 1 can take $[290, 320]$, City 3 can take $[270, 350]$
- Sum of minimums for Cities 1&3: $290 + 270 = 560 \leq 600$ ✓
- Sum of maximums for Cities 1&3: $320 + 350 = 670 \geq 600$ ✓

The model is feasible.

## Zero-Solution Test (Rule 18 Verification)

- Supply constraints are **EQUALITIES** ($= 400$ and $= 450$), so $x = 0$ is NOT feasible
- Demand constraint for City 2 is an **EQUALITY** ($= 250$), further preventing trivial solution
- The model correctly forces all 850 units to be allocated

The formulation is complete and ready for implementation.
