# Operations Research Model: Chemical Supply Chain Profit Maximization

## 1. Problem Analysis

This is a transportation problem variant with profit maximization. The company aims to maximize total profit by deciding how much product to ship from each of the four plants (A, B, C, D) to each of the six regions (I, II, III, IV, V, VI).

**Key Data:**
*   **Supply (Capacity):** Plant A: 200 tons, Plant B: 300 tons, Plant C: 400 tons, Plant D: 100 tons. Total Supply = 1000 tons.
*   **Demand:** Region I: 200 tons, Region II: 150 tons, Region III: 400 tons, Region IV: 100 tons, Region V: 150 tons, Region VI: 150 tons. Total Demand = 1150 tons.
*   **Imbalance:** Total Supply (1000) < Total Demand (1150). Therefore, not all demands can be met. This is an unbalanced transportation problem.
*   **Costs/Prices:**
    *   Production Cost ($c_i$): A: 1.2, B: 1.4, C: 1.1, D: 1.5 (yuan/kg). *Note: 1 ton = 1000 kg, so costs per ton are 1200, 1400, 1100, 1500 yuan respectively.*
    *   Sales Price ($p_j$): I: 2.0, II: 2.4, III: 1.8, IV: 2.2, V: 1.6, VI: 2.0 (yuan/kg). *Prices per ton are 2000, 2400, 1800, 2200, 1600, 2000 yuan respectively.*
    *   Transportation Cost ($t_{ij}$): Given in the table (yuan/kg). *Transportation costs per ton are 1000 times the table values.*

**Profit Structure:**
Profit = Total Revenue - Total Production Cost - Total Transportation Cost.
Alternatively, Profit for shipping 1 kg from plant $i$ to region $j$ is:
$$ \pi_{ij} = p_j - c_i - t_{ij} $$
Where:
*   $p_j$ is the sales price per kg in region $j$.
*   $c_i$ is the production cost per kg at plant $i$.
*   $t_{ij}$ is the transportation cost per kg from plant $i$ to region $j$.

Let's calculate the unit profit ($\pi_{ij}$) per kg for each route:

*   **Plant A ($c_A = 1.2$):**
    *   I: $2.0 - 1.2 - 0.5 = 0.3$
    *   II: $2.4 - 1.2 - 0.4 = 0.8$
    *   III: $1.8 - 1.2 - 0.3 = 0.3$
    *   IV: $2.2 - 1.2 - 0.4 = 0.6$
    *   V: $1.6 - 1.2 - 0.3 = 0.1$
    *   VI: $2.0 - 1.2 - 0.1 = 0.7$

*   **Plant B ($c_B = 1.4$):**
    *   I: $2.0 - 1.4 - 0.3 = 0.3$
    *   II: $2.4 - 1.4 - 0.8 = 0.2$
    *   III: $1.8 - 1.4 - 0.9 = -0.5$
    *   IV: $2.2 - 1.4 - 0.5 = 0.3$
    *   V: $1.6 - 1.4 - 0.6 = -0.4$
    *   VI: $2.0 - 1.4 - 0.2 = 0.4$

*   **Plant C ($c_C = 1.1$):**
    *   I: $2.0 - 1.1 - 0.7 = 0.2$
    *   II: $2.4 - 1.1 - 0.7 = 0.6$
    *   III: $1.8 - 1.1 - 0.3 = 0.4$
    *   IV: $2.2 - 1.1 - 0.7 = 0.4$
    *   V: $1.6 - 1.1 - 0.4 = 0.1$
    *   VI: $2.0 - 1.1 - 0.4 = 0.5$

*   **Plant D ($c_D = 1.5$):**
    *   I: $2.0 - 1.5 - 0.6 = -0.1$
    *   II: $2.4 - 1.5 - 0.4 = 0.5$
    *   III: $1.8 - 1.5 - 0.2 = 0.1$
    *   IV: $2.2 - 1.5 - 0.6 = 0.1$
    *   V: $1.6 - 1.5 - 0.5 = -0.4$
    *   VI: $2.0 - 1.5 - 0.8 = -0.3$

*Note: Negative profits indicate routes that lose money. The model will likely avoid these unless forced by constraints (though here we can choose not to supply certain regions if it's more profitable to leave demand unmet, but wait: the problem asks to "determine the product transportation plan that maximizes the company’s profit". Usually, in such problems, you only ship if it's profitable or if required. However, standard transportation models assume all supply must be shipped or all demand must be met. Here, supply < demand. We can choose *which* demands to satisfy. Shipping to a region with negative profit reduces total profit. Therefore, the model should only ship to regions/route combinations where $\pi_{ij} > 0$, or more simply, maximize $\sum \pi_{ij} x_{ij}$ subject to supply constraints and demand upper bounds. Since unmet demand has no penalty (other than lost opportunity), and negative profit routes should be avoided, the optimization will naturally handle this.*

## 2. Decision Variables

Let $x_{ij}$ be the amount of product (in tons) shipped from Plant $i$ to Region $j$.

*   $i \in \{A, B, C, D\}$
*   $j \in \{I, II, III, IV, V, VI\}$
*   $x_{ij} \ge 0$

## 3. Objective Function

Maximize Total Profit $Z$:

$$ \text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{i \in \{A,B,C,D\}} \sum_{j \in \{I,...,VI\}} \pi_{ij} x_{ij} $$

Where $\pi_{ij}$ is the profit per ton (yuan/ton). Since the calculated $\pi_{ij}$ above are per kg, we multiply by 1000 to get profit per ton.

**Profit Coefficients (yuan/ton):**

| Plant \ Region | I | II | III | IV | V | VI |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **A** | 300 | 800 | 300 | 600 | 100 | 700 |
| **B** | 300 | 200 | -500 | 300 | -400 | 400 |
| **C** | 200 | 600 | 400 | 400 | 100 | 500 |
| **D** | -100 | 500 | 100 | 100 | -400 | -300 |

$$ Z = 300x_{A,I} + 800x_{A,II} + 300x_{A,III} + 600x_{A,IV} + 100x_{A,V} + 700x_{A,VI} $$
$$ + 300x_{B,I} + 200x_{B,II} - 500x_{B,III} + 300x_{B,IV} - 400x_{B,V} + 400x_{B,VI} $$
$$ + 200x_{C,I} + 600x_{C,II} + 400x_{C,III} + 400x_{C,IV} + 100x_{C,V} + 500x_{C,VI} $$
$$ - 100x_{D,I} + 500x_{D,II} + 100x_{D,III} + 100x_{D,IV} - 400x_{D,V} - 300x_{D,VI} $$

## 4. Operational Constraints

### A. Supply Constraints (Capacity)
The total amount shipped from each plant cannot exceed its production capacity.

1.  **Plant A:** $x_{A,I} + x_{A,II} + x_{A,III} + x_{A,IV} + x_{A,V} + x_{A,VI} \le 200$
2.  **Plant B:** $x_{B,I} + x_{B,II} + x_{B,III} + x_{B,IV} + x_{B,V} + x_{B,VI} \le 300$
3.  **Plant C:** $x_{C,I} + x_{C,II} + x_{C,III} + x_{C,IV} + x_{C,V} + x_{C,VI} \le 400$
4.  **Plant D:** $x_{D,I} + x_{D,II} + x_{D,III} + x_{D,IV} + x_{D,V} + x_{D,VI} \le 100$

### B. Demand Constraints
The amount received by each region cannot exceed its demand. Note that for Region IV, the demand *must* be fully satisfied.

5.  **Region I:** $x_{A,I} + x_{B,I} + x_{C,I} + x_{D,I} \le 200$
6.  **Region II:** $x_{A,II} + x_{B,II} + x_{C,II} + x_{D,II} \le 150$
7.  **Region III:** $x_{A,III} + x_{B,III} + x_{C,III} + x_{D,III} \le 400$
    *   *Additional Constraint for Region III:* At least 100 tons must be supplied.
    *   $x_{A,III} + x_{B,III} + x_{C,III} + x_{D,III} \ge 100$
8.  **Region IV:** $x_{A,IV} + x_{B,IV} + x_{C,IV} + x_{D,IV} = 100$ (Must be fully satisfied)
9.  **Region V:** $x_{A,V} + x_{B,V} + x_{C,V} + x_{D,V} \le 150$
10. **Region VI:** $x_{A,VI} + x_{B,VI} + x_{C,VI} + x_{D,VI} \le 150$

### C. Non-negativity Constraints
$$ x_{ij} \ge 0 \quad \forall i, j $$

### D. Integrality
Since the problem deals with tons, and the inputs are integers, the optimal solution to this linear programming problem will naturally be integer-valued due to the totally unimodular property of transportation problem constraint matrices, provided the supply and demand values are integers. Thus, explicit integer constraints are not strictly necessary for the LP formulation, but the solution will be integer tons.
