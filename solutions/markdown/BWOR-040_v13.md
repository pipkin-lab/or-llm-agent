# Mathematical Optimization Model

## Model Type: LP (Linear Programming)

**Note:** The problem asks to determine whether hiring temporary workers is beneficial and, if so, the optimal number. Since the number of workers is a discrete physical unit, strictly speaking this is a MILP. However, because the optimal solution for this specific linear structure will naturally yield an integer value (as verified by the feasibility check below), we can solve it as an LP and verify integrality. For strictness, we declare the temporary worker variable as INTEGER.

**Model Type: MILP**

### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Sets:**
*   $P = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of products.
    *   $1$: Manuscript paper (bundle)
    *   $2$: Diaries (dozen)
    *   $3$: Exercise books (box)

**Parameters:**

*   **Labor Productivity** ($a_p$): Units produced per worker per month.
    *   $a_1 = 30$ bundles/worker/month
    *   $a_2 = 30$ dozens/worker/month
    *   $a_3 = 30$ boxes/worker/month
*   **Raw Material Consumption** ($b_p$): kg of white base paper per unit.
    *   $b_1 = 3\frac{1}{3} = \frac{10}{3}$ kg/bundle
    *   $b_2 = 13\frac{1}{3} = \frac{40}{3}$ kg/dozen
    *   $b_3 = 26\frac{2}{3} = \frac{80}{3}$ kg/box
*   **Profit per Unit** ($c_p$): Yuan per unit.
    *   $c_1 = 2$ yuan/bundle
    *   $c_2 = 3$ yuan/dozen
    *   $c_3 = 1$ yuan/box
*   **Available Resources:**
    *   $W_{fixed} = 100$: Number of permanent workers.
    *   $M_{avail} = 30,000$: Monthly supply of white base paper (kg).
*   **Costs:**
    *   $w_{temp} = 40$: Wage cost per temporary worker per month (yuan).

### 2. Decision Variables

*   $x_p$: Quantity of product $p$ produced in the month (continuous, $x_p \ge 0$).
*   $N_{temp}$: Number of temporary workers hired (integer, $N_{temp} \ge 0$).

### 3. Objective Function

The goal is to maximize the **net profit**.
Net Profit = (Total Gross Profit from Products) - (Total Wage Cost of Temporary Workers).

$$ \text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{p \in P} c_p x_p - w_{temp} N_{temp} $$

Substituting values:
$$ \text{Maximize } Z = 2x_1 + 3x_2 + 1x_3 - 40 N_{temp} $$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**A. Labor Capacity Constraint**
Total labor required to produce the quantities $x_p$ must not exceed the total labor capacity (permanent + temporary workers).
Labor capacity per worker-month is 30 units of any product (since productivity is 30 units/worker for all products).
Total capacity in "worker-months" is $100 + N_{temp}$.
Total capacity in "units produced" (standardized to 30 units/worker) is $30(100 + N_{temp})$.

$$ \sum_{p \in P} x_p \le 30(100 + N_{temp}) $$
$$ x_1 + x_2 + x_3 \le 3000 + 30 N_{temp} $$

*Note: Since productivity is identical (30 units) for all products in terms of "units per worker-month", we can sum the physical units directly if we treat "unit" as the standard output measure. Let's verify the units carefully.
Worker produces 30 bundles OR 30 dozens OR 30 boxes.
So, 1 worker-month provides 30 "production slots".
Total production slots = $30 \times (\text{Total Workers})$.
Constraint: $\sum x_p \le 30(100 + N_{temp})$. This is correct.*

**B. Raw Material Constraint**
Total raw material consumed must not exceed the monthly supply of 30,000 kg.

$$ \sum_{p \in P} b_p x_p \le 30,000 $$
$$ \frac{10}{3}x_1 + \frac{40}{3}x_2 + \frac{80}{3}x_3 \le 30,000 $$

Multiplying by 3 to clear denominators (optional for formulation, useful for computation):
$$ 10x_1 + 40x_2 + 80x_3 \le 90,000 $$
Or simplified by dividing by 10:
$$ x_1 + 4x_2 + 8x_3 \le 9,000 $$

**C. Non-negativity and Integrality**
$$ x_1, x_2, x_3 \ge 0 \quad (\text{Continuous}) $$
$$ N_{temp} \ge 0, \quad N_{temp} \in \mathbb{Z} $$

### Feasibility & Sanity Check (Mental Simulation)

Let's analyze the profitability per unit of constraint to see if hiring is beneficial.

**Profit per unit of Labor (30 units/worker):**
*   Product 1: Profit 2 yuan / (1/30 worker) = 60 yuan/worker-month.
*   Product 2: Profit 3 yuan / (1/30 worker) = 90 yuan/worker-month.
*   Product 3: Profit 1 yuan / (1/30 worker) = 30 yuan/worker-month.

**Profit per unit of Material (kg):**
*   Product 1: 2 yuan / (10/3 kg) = 0.6 yuan/kg.
*   Product 2: 3 yuan / (40/3 kg) = 0.225 yuan/kg.
*   Product 3: 1 yuan / (80/3 kg) = 0.0375 yuan/kg.

**Analysis:**
*   Product 1 has the highest material efficiency (0.6 yuan/kg) and high labor efficiency (60 yuan/worker).
*   Product 2 has the highest labor efficiency (90 yuan/worker) but lower material efficiency.
*   Product 3 is dominated in both aspects. We likely produce $x_3 = 0$.

**Scenario 1: No temporary workers ($N_{temp}=0$)**
Max Labor = 3000 units. Max Material = 30,000 kg.
Constraint 1: $x_1 + x_2 \le 3000$
Constraint 2: $x_1 + 4x_2 \le 9000$ (using simplified material constraint)

Let's check the intersection of the two constraints for $x_3=0$:
1) $x_1 = 3000 - x_2$
Substitute into (2):
$(3000 - x_2) + 4x_2 \le 9000$
$3000 + 3x_2 \le 9000$
$3x_2 \le 6000 \implies x_2 \le 2000$

If we produce max diaries ($x_2 = 2000$):
$x_1 = 3000 - 2000 = 1000$.
Material used: $10(1000) + 40(2000) = 10,000 + 80,000 = 90,000$ (in units of 10kg? No, in the simplified equation $x_1 + 4x_2 + 8x_3 \le 9000$).
Wait, let's re-verify the simplified material constraint.
Original: $\frac{10}{3}x_1 + \frac{40}{3}x_2 + \frac{80}{3}x_3 \le 30,000$
Multiply by 3: $10x_1 + 40x_2 + 80x_3 \le 90,000$
Divide by 10: $x_1 + 4x_2 + 8x_3 \le 9,000$. Correct.

At $x_2 = 2000, x_1 = 1000$:
LHS Material = $1000 + 4(2000) = 9000$. (Binding)
LHS Labor = $1000 + 2000 = 3000$. (Binding)
Profit = $2(1000) + 3(2000) = 2000 + 6000 = 8000$ yuan.
Cost = 0.
Net Profit = 8000 yuan.

**Scenario 2: Hire 1 temporary worker ($N_{temp}=1$)**
Max Labor = 3030 units. Max Material = 9000 (simplified units).
Constraints:
1) $x_1 + x_2 \le 3030$
2) $x_1 + 4x_2 \le 9000$

Intersection:
$x_1 = 3030 - x_2$
$(3030 - x_2) + 4x_2 = 9000$
$3030 + 3x_2 = 9000$
$3x_2 = 5970 \implies x_2 = 1990$
$x_1 = 3030 - 1990 = 1040$

Profit = $2(1040) + 3(1990) = 2080 + 5970 = 8050$ yuan.
Cost = $1 \times 40 = 40$ yuan.
Net Profit = $8050 - 40 = 8010$ yuan.

Comparison: $8010 > 8000$. Hiring 1 worker increases net profit by 10 yuan.

**Marginal Analysis:**
Is it worth hiring more?
The material constraint is $x_1 + 4x_2 \le 9000$.
The labor constraint is $x_1 + x_2 \le 3000 + 30 N_{temp}$.

As we increase $N_{temp}$, the labor constraint relaxes (shifts right). The material constraint stays fixed.
The optimal solution lies at the intersection of the two binding constraints (assuming we continue to mix P1 and P2).
Slope of Labor line: -1.
Slope of Material line: -4.
Since Material is "steeper" (more resource intensive per unit of x2 relative to x1), and we are limited by both, let's look at the shadow prices or simply step through.

Let $L = 3000 + 30 N_{temp}$.
Intersection:
$x_1 + x_2 = L$
$x_1 + 4x_2 = 9000$
Subtracting: $3x_2 = 9000 - L \implies x_2 = 3000 - \frac{L}{3}$.
$x_1 = L - x_2 = L - (3000 - \frac{L}{3}) = \frac{4L}{3} - 3000$.

For $x_1, x_2 \ge 0$, we need:
$x_2 \ge 0 \implies 3000 - L/3 \ge 0 \implies L \le 9000$.
$x_1 \ge 0 \implies 4L/3 - 3000 \ge 0 \implies L \ge 2250$.
Since $L$ starts at 3000, we are in the valid region where both are positive.

Profit Function in terms of $L$:
$P(L) = 2x_1 + 3x_2$
$P(L) = 2(\frac{4L}{3} - 3000) + 3(3000 - \frac{L}{3})$
$P(L) = \frac{8L}{3} - 6000 + 9000 - L$
$P(L) = \frac{5L}{3} + 3000$

Cost Function in terms of $N_{temp}$:
$L = 3000 + 30 N_{temp}$
Cost $C = 40 N_{temp} = 40 (\frac{L - 3000}{30}) = \frac{4}{3}(L - 3000)$.

Net Profit $Z(L) = P(L) - C(L)$
$Z(L) = (\frac{5L}{3} + 3000) - (\frac{4L}{3} - 4000)$
$Z(L) = \frac{L}{3} + 7000$

We want to maximize $Z(L)$. Since the coefficient of $L$ is positive ($1/3$), we want to maximize $L$.
The maximum $L$ is limited by the material constraint becoming non-binding or variables hitting 0.
The intersection formula holds as long as $x_2 \le 3000$ (derived from $L \le 9000$) and $x_1 \le L$ etc.
Actually, the material constraint $x_1 + 4x_2 \le 9000$ is the upper bound on production volume in "material units".
If we increase $L$ beyond 9000, the intersection moves such that $x_2$ would become negative, which is impossible.
Let's check the vertex where Material is binding and $x_1=0$?
If $x_1=0$, Material: $4x_2 \le 9000 \implies x_2 \le 2250$.
Labor: $x_2 \le L$.
If $L > 2250$, we produce $x_2 = 2250$ (max allowed by material) and $x_1=0$?
Let's check Profit at $x_2=2250, x_1=0$:
Profit = $3 \times 2250 = 6750$.
This is less than 8000. So the optimal mix is always the intersection of Labor and Material lines until one variable hits 0 or the material constraint is no longer the bottleneck for the *mix*.

Wait, let's look at the slope of the objective function (Profit) vs constraints.
Objective: $2x_1 + 3x_2 = Z$. Slope = -2/3.
Labor Constraint: $x_1 + x_2 = L$. Slope = -1.
Material Constraint: $x_1 + 4x_2 = 9000$. Slope = -1/4.

The objective slope (-0.67) is between the two constraint slopes (-1 and -0.25).
Therefore, the optimal solution will always be at the **intersection** of the Labor and Material constraints, provided the intersection has positive coordinates.
We found the intersection coordinates:
$x_2 = 3000 - L/3$
$x_1 = 4L/3 - 3000$

For $x_2 \ge 0$, we need $L \le 9000$.
For $x_1 \ge 0$, we need $L \ge 2250$.
Our base $L=3000$.
As we hire more workers, $L$ increases.
We can keep increasing $L$ (and thus profit) as long as $L \le 9000$.
At $L=9000$:
$x_2 = 3000 - 3000 = 0$.
$x_1 = 12000 - 3000 = 9000$.
Check Material: $9000 + 0 = 9000$. (Binding)
Check Labor: $9000 + 0 = 9000$. (Binding)
Profit = $2(9000) = 18,000$.
Cost: $N_{temp} = (9000 - 3000)/30 = 200$ workers.
Cost = $200 \times 40 = 8,000$.
Net Profit = $18,000 - 8,000 = 10,000$.

If we hire more than 200 workers ($L > 9000$):
The labor constraint is no longer binding (we have excess labor capacity relative to material limits).
The bottleneck becomes Material only.
Max production is limited by $x_1 + 4x_2 + 8x_3 \le 9000$.
We should produce the product with the highest profit per material unit.
Profit per "material unit" (where 1 unit = 1/10 kg effectively, or just relative):
P1: Profit 2, Mat 1 (in simplified units). Ratio 2.
P2: Profit 3, Mat 4. Ratio 0.75.
P3: Profit 1, Mat 8. Ratio 0.125.
So we produce only Product 1.
Max $x_1 = 9000$.
Profit = $2 \times 9000 = 18,000$.
Cost increases by 40 per extra worker.
Net Profit = $18,000 - 40 N_{temp}$.
Since $N_{temp}$ is already 200 at the limit, any additional worker adds cost without increasing profit (profit capped at 18,000).
So, stop hiring at $N_{temp} = 200$.

**Conclusion:**
Yes, the factory should hire temporary workers.
The optimal number is 200.

### Final Formulation Summary

**Model:** MILP

**Sets:**
$P = \{1, 2, 3\}$

**Parameters:**
$a = [30, 30, 30]$
$b = [10/3, 40/3, 80/3]$
$c = [2, 3, 1]$
$W_{fixed} = 100$
$M_{max} = 30000$
$w_{cost} = 40$

**Variables:**
$x_p \ge 0$ (Continuous)
$N_{temp} \ge 0$ (Integer)

**Objective:**
Maximize $Z = 2x_1 + 3x_2 + 1x_3 - 40 N_{temp}$

**Constraints:**
1. Labor: $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 \le 30(100 + N_{temp})$
2. Material: $\frac{10}{3}x_1 + \frac{40}{3}x_2 + \frac{80}{3}x_3 \le 30000$
3. Non-negativity: $x_1, x_2, x_3 \ge 0$
4. Integrality: $N_{temp} \in \mathbb{Z}$
