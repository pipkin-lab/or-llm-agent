## Model Type
**LP** (Linear Programming)

## Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
- $P = \{I, II, III\}$: Set of products, indexed by $i$.
- $T = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$: Set of quarters, indexed by $t$.
- $P_{restricted} = \{I\}$: Set of products with production restrictions.
- $T_{restricted} = \{2\}$: Set of quarters where product I cannot be produced.

**Parameters (Extracted from Text):**
- $D_{i,t}$: Contract order quantity (demand) for product $i$ in quarter $t$.
  - Product I: $D_{I,1}=1500, D_{I,2}=1000, D_{I,3}=2000, D_{I,4}=1200$
  - Product II: $D_{II,1}=1500, D_{II,2}=1500, D_{II,3}=1200, D_{II,4}=1500$
  - Product III: $D_{III,1}=1000, D_{III,2}=2000, D_{III,3}=1500, D_{III,4}=2500$
- $H_t = 15000$: Working hours available per quarter $t$.
- $h_i$: Production hours required per unit of product $i$.
  - $h_I = 2, h_{II} = 4, h_{III} = 3$
- $c^L_i$: Late delivery penalty per unit per quarter for product $i$.
  - $c^L_I = 20, c^L_{II} = 20, c^L_{III} = 10$
- $c^I_i$: Inventory holding cost per unit per quarter for product $i$.
  - $c^I_I = 5, c^I_{II} = 5, c^I_{III} = 5$
- $E_i$: Required ending inventory for product $i$ at end of Q4.
  - $E_I = 150, E_{II} = 150, E_{III} = 150$
- $I_{i,0}$: Initial inventory for product $i$ at beginning of Q1.
  - $I_{i,0} = 0$ for all $i \in P$.

## Decision Variables

- $x_{i,t}$: Quantity of product $i$ produced in quarter $t$. (Continuous, $\ge 0$)
- $I_{i,t}$: Inventory of product $i$ at the end of quarter $t$. (Continuous, $\ge 0$)
- $L_{i,t}$: Quantity of product $i$ delivered late in quarter $t$ (backlogged from previous quarters). (Continuous, $\ge 0$)

*Note: While production quantities are physically discrete, standard production planning LPs treat these as continuous variables unless integer constraints are explicitly required by the solver context or problem scale implies it. Given the large quantities (hundreds/thousands), continuous relaxation is standard for this type of textbook problem. If strict integrality is required, these would be Integer variables, making it a MILP. However, the linearity and large scale suggest LP is the intended formulation type.*

## Objective Function

Minimize total cost = Total Late Delivery Penalties + Total Inventory Holding Costs.

$$ \text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{i \in P} \sum_{t=1}^{4} (c^L_i \cdot L_{i,t} + c^I_i \cdot I_{i,t}) $$

Substituting values:
$$ Z = \sum_{t=1}^{4} [ 20 L_{I,t} + 5 I_{I,t} + 20 L_{II,t} + 5 I_{II,t} + 10 L_{III,t} + 5 I_{III,t} ] $$

## Operational Constraints

**1. Inventory Balance Constraints:**
The inventory at the end of quarter $t$ is the inventory from the previous quarter plus production in quarter $t$, minus the demand due in quarter $t$ (which is met by on-time delivery or late delivery).
Specifically: $I_{i,t} = I_{i,t-1} + x_{i,t} - D_{i,t} + L_{i,t}$.
Rearranging to standard form:
$$ I_{i,t-1} + x_{i,t} + L_{i,t} - I_{i,t} = D_{i,t} \quad \forall i \in P, t \in T $$
*Note: $L_{i,t}$ represents the amount of demand $D_{i,t}$ that is NOT satisfied by current inventory/production and is thus carried forward as a late delivery obligation. The next period's inventory balance will account for this.*
Actually, a more standard formulation for backlogging is:
$I_{i,t} = I_{i,t-1} + x_{i,t} - D_{i,t} + L_{i,t} - L_{i,t-1}^{delivered}$?
Let's stick to the simplest interpretation: $L_{i,t}$ is the unmet demand of period $t$.
Balance: $I_{i,t} = I_{i,t-1} + x_{i,t} - (D_{i,t} - L_{i,t})$.
So: $I_{i,t} - I_{i,t-1} - x_{i,t} - L_{i,t} = -D_{i,t}$.
Or: $x_{i,t} + I_{i,t-1} + L_{i,t} - I_{i,t} = D_{i,t}$.
Here $L_{i,t}$ is the late delivery amount for period $t$.
Wait, if $L_{i,t}$ is the late delivery *in* period $t$, it reduces the effective demand satisfied in period $t$.
Let's define $L_{i,t}$ as the quantity of product $i$ that is late *in quarter* $t$.
Then: $I_{i,t} = I_{i,t-1} + x_{i,t} - D_{i,t} + L_{i,t}$.
This implies $L_{i,t}$ adds to inventory? No, that's wrong.
If demand is not met, it is a backlog.
Let $B_{i,t}$ be the backlog (late delivery amount) at the end of period $t$.
Then $I_{i,t} = I_{i,t-1} + x_{i,t} - D_{i,t} + B_{i,t} - B_{i,t-1}$? No.
Standard formulation:
$I_{i,t} = I_{i,t-1} + x_{i,t} - D_{i,t} + L_{i,t}$ where $L_{i,t}$ is the amount of demand in period $t$ that is late.
If $L_{i,t} > 0$, it means we didn't produce enough to cover $D_{i,t}$.
The unmet demand $L_{i,t}$ effectively increases the "inventory" requirement for the next period? No, it increases the liability.
Let's use the variable $L_{i,t}$ as the late delivery penalty variable directly tied to the shortfall in period $t$.
Constraint: $x_{i,t} + I_{i,t-1} - I_{i,t} = D_{i,t} - L_{i,t}$.
This means: Production + Start Inventory - End Inventory = On-Time Deliveries.
On-Time Deliveries = Total Demand - Late Deliveries ($L_{i,t}$).
So: $x_{i,t} + I_{i,t-1} - I_{i,t} + L_{i,t} = D_{i,t}$.
This is a linear equality constraint.

**2. Production Capacity Constraints:**
Total production hours used in quarter $t$ must not exceed available hours.
$$ \sum_{i \in P} h_i x_{i,t} \le H_t \quad \forall t \in T $$
Specifically:
- Q1: $2x_{I,1} + 4x_{II,1} + 3x_{III,1} \le 15000$
- Q2: $4x_{II,2} + 3x_{III,2} \le 15000$ (Since $x_{I,2}=0$)
- Q3: $2x_{I,3} + 4x_{II,3} + 3x_{III,3} \le 15000$
- Q4: $2x_{I,4} + 4x_{II,4} + 3x_{III,4} \le 15000$

**3. Production Restriction Constraint:**
Product I cannot be produced in Q2.
$$ x_{I,2} = 0 $$

**4. Ending Inventory Constraint:**
Inventory at the end of Q4 must be 150 for each product.
$$ I_{i,4} = 150 \quad \forall i \in P $$

**5. Non-negativity Constraints:**
$$ x_{i,t} \ge 0, \quad I_{i,t} \ge 0, \quad L_{i,t} \ge 0 \quad \forall i \in P, t \in T $$
