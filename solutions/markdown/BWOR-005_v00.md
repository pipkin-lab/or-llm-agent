# Operations Research Model: Cargo Loading Problem

## 1. Decision Variables

Let $x_{ij}$ be the number of pieces of cargo type $i$ loaded in compartment $j$.

*   $i \in \{A, B, C\}$: Cargo types (A, B, C)
*   $j \in \{F, M, R\}$: Compartments (Front, Middle, Rear)

Specifically:
*   $x_{AF}, x_{AM}, x_{AR}$: Pieces of Cargo A in Front, Middle, and Rear compartments.
*   $x_{BF}, x_{BM}, x_{BR}$: Pieces of Cargo B in Front, Middle, and Rear compartments.
*   $x_{CF}, x_{CM}, x_{CR}$: Pieces of Cargo C in Front, Middle, and Rear compartments.

**Domain:**
$x_{ij} \geq 0$ and $x_{ij} \in \mathbb{Z}$ (Integer programming is technically required for "pieces", but often relaxed to continuous $x_{ij} \geq 0$ in standard LP formulations unless specified otherwise. Given the large quantities, LP relaxation is often acceptable, but strictly, these are integers).

## 2. Objective Function

**Maximize Total Revenue ($Z$)**

The revenue is calculated by summing the product of the number of pieces of each cargo type in each compartment and its respective freight rate.

$$
\text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{i \in \{A,B,C\}} \sum_{j \in \{F,M,R\}} r_i \cdot x_{ij}
$$

Where $r_i$ is the freight rate per piece for cargo type $i$:
*   $r_A = 1000$
*   $r_B = 700$
*   $r_C = 600$

Expanded form:
$$
\text{Maximize } Z = 1000(x_{AF} + x_{AM} + x_{AR}) + 700(x_{BF} + x_{BM} + x_{BR}) + 600(x_{CF} + x_{CM} + x_{CR})
$$

## 3. Operational Constraints

### A. Cargo Supply Constraints
The total number of pieces of each cargo type loaded across all compartments cannot exceed the available quantity.

1.  **Cargo A:**
    $$x_{AF} + x_{AM} + x_{AR} \leq 600$$
2.  **Cargo B:**
    $$x_{BF} + x_{BM} + x_{BR} \leq 1000$$
3.  **Cargo C:**
    $$x_{CF} + x_{CM} + x_{CR} \leq 800$$

### B. Volume Constraints
The total volume of cargo in each compartment must not exceed the compartment's volume capacity.
Volume used = $\sum (\text{pieces} \times \text{volume per piece})$.

*   Volume per piece: $v_A=10, v_B=5, v_C=7$.
*   Capacities: $V_F=4000, V_M=5000, V_R=1500$.

1.  **Front Compartment Volume:**
    $$10x_{AF} + 5x_{BF} + 7x_{CF} \leq 4000$$
2.  **Middle Compartment Volume:**
    $$10x_{AM} + 5x_{BM} + 7x_{CM} \leq 5000$$
3.  **Rear Compartment Volume:**
    $$10x_{AR} + 5x_{BR} + 7x_{CR} \leq 1500$$

### C. Weight (Load) Constraints
The total weight of cargo in each compartment must not exceed the compartment's maximum allowable load.
Weight used = $\sum (\text{pieces} \times \text{weight per piece})$.

*   Weight per piece: $w_A=8, w_B=6, w_C=4$.
*   Max Loads: $W_F=2000, W_M=3000, W_R=1500$.

1.  **Front Compartment Weight:**
    $$8x_{AF} + 6x_{BF} + 4x_{CF} \leq 2000$$
2.  **Middle Compartment Weight:**
    $$8x_{AM} + 6x_{BM} + 4x_{CM} \leq 3000$$
3.  **Rear Compartment Weight:**
    $$8x_{AR} + 6x_{BR} + 4x_{CR} \leq 1500$$

### D. Balance (Stability) Constraints
The actual weight distribution must maintain specific ratios relative to the maximum allowable loads.

Let $W_F, W_M, W_R$ denote the actual total weight in Front, Middle, and Rear compartments respectively:
*   $W_F = 8x_{AF} + 6x_{BF} + 4x_{CF}$
*   $W_M = 8x_{AM} + 6x_{BM} + 4x_{CM}$
*   $W_R = 8x_{AR} + 6x_{BR} + 4x_{CR}$

**1. Front vs. Middle Ratio Constraint (15% deviation)**
The ratio of max loads is $2000 / 3000 = 2/3 \approx 0.6667$.
The actual ratio $W_F / W_M$ must be within $\pm 15\%$ of this ratio.
$$
\frac{2000}{3000}(1 - 0.15) \leq \frac{W_F}{W_M} \leq \frac{2000}{3000}(1 + 0.15)
$$
$$
\frac{2000}{3000}(0.85) \leq \frac{W_F}{W_M} \leq \frac{2000}{3000}(1.15)
$$
$$
0.5667 \leq \frac{W_F}{W_M} \leq 0.7667
$$
Assuming $W_M > 0$, this is linearized as:
*   $W_F - 0.7667 W_M \leq 0$
*   $-W_F + 0.5667 W_M \leq 0$

**2. Rear vs. Middle Ratio Constraint (15% deviation)**
The ratio of max loads is $1500 / 3000 = 0.5$.
The actual ratio $W_R / W_M$ must be within $\pm 15\%$ of this ratio.
$$
0.5(1 - 0.15) \leq \frac{W_R}{W_M} \leq 0.5(1 + 0.15)
$$
$$
0.425 \leq \frac{W_R}{W_M} \leq 0.575
$$
Linearized:
*   $W_R - 0.575 W_M \leq 0$
*   $-W_R + 0.425 W_M \leq 0$

**3. Front vs. Rear Ratio Constraint (10% deviation)**
The ratio of max loads is $2000 / 1500 = 4/3 \approx 1.3333$.
The actual ratio $W_F / W_R$ must be within $\pm 10\%$ of this ratio.
$$
\frac{2000}{1500}(1 - 0.10) \leq \frac{W_F}{W_R} \leq \frac{2000}{1500}(1 + 0.10)
$$
$$
1.2 \leq \frac{W_F}{W_R} \leq 1.4667
$$
Linearized (assuming $W_R > 0$):
*   $W_F - 1.4667 W_R \leq 0$
*   $-W_F + 1.2 W_R \leq 0$

*(Note: In a rigorous LP formulation, if there is a possibility that a compartment weight could be zero, these ratio constraints introduce non-linearity or require case analysis. However, given the supply constraints and the requirement to maximize revenue, it is highly probable that all compartments will be utilized. If strict linearity is required without assumption of non-zero weights, these can be handled via indicator variables or by observing that the volume/weight caps prevent any single compartment from being the sole carrier of significant weight relative to others without violating other constraints. For standard textbook formulation, the linearized inequalities above are the standard approach.)*
