# Optimization Model: Assignment Problem (Scenarios a, b, c)

## MANDATORY COST INVENTORY (PRE-MODELING STEP)

The problem provides a 4×5 time matrix (Table 5-3). These are the objective coefficients (completion times). There are no other costs, fees, or rates.

**Time Matrix T[i,j] (Person i, Task j):**

| Person | A | B | C | D | F |
|--------|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| X | 25 | 29 | 31 | 42 | 37 |
| Y | 39 | 38 | 26 | 20 | 33 |
| Z | 34 | 27 | 28 | 40 | 32 |
| R | 24 | 42 | 36 | 23 | 45 |

All 20 values are present. No parameters are [NOT_PROVIDED].

## MODEL TYPE DECLARATION

**MILP** (Mixed Integer Linear Program). Binary assignment variables with linear objective and linear constraints. A standard LP/MILP solver is sufficient.

---

## Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
- $P = \{X, Y, Z, R\}$: Set of persons, $|P| = 4$
- $T = \{A, B, C, D, F\}$: Set of tasks, $|T| = 5$
- $P_{\text{double}}^{(b)} = \{X, Z\}$: Set of persons who may take two tasks in scenario (b)

**Parameters:**
- $T_{i,j}$: Time for person $i$ to complete task $j$, given by Table 5-3 (all 20 values listed above)

---

## Decision Variables

For each scenario, define binary assignment variables:

$$x_{i,j} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall\, i \in P,\; j \in T$$

where $x_{i,j} = 1$ if person $i$ is assigned to task $j$, and $0$ otherwise.

---

## Objective Function

For each scenario (a), (b), and (c), minimize the total completion time:

$$\min \sum_{i \in P} \sum_{j \in T} T_{i,j} \, x_{i,j}$$

The objective structure is identical across all three scenarios; only the constraints differ.

---

## Operational Constraints

### Common Constraints (all scenarios)

**Task assignment constraints:**
- Scenario (a): All 5 tasks must be assigned.
  $$\sum_{i \in P} x_{i,j} = 1 \quad \forall\, j \in T$$
- Scenario (c): Tasks A and B must be assigned; exactly one of {C, D, F} is unassigned.
  $$\sum_{i \in P} x_{i,A} = 1$$
  $$\sum_{i \in P} x_{i,B} = 1$$
  $$\sum_{i \in P} x_{i,C} + \sum_{i \in P} x_{i,D} + \sum_{i \in P} x_{i,F} = 2$$

**Non-negativity / Integrality:**
$$x_{i,j} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall\, i \in P,\; j \in T$$

### Scenario (a): One person takes two tasks, the other three each take one

**Person workload constraints:**
$$\sum_{j \in T} x_{i,j} \leq 2 \quad \forall\, i \in P$$
$$\sum_{j \in T} x_{i,j} \geq 1 \quad \forall\, i \in P$$
$$\sum_{i \in P} \sum_{j \in T} x_{i,j} = 5$$

The last constraint (total assignments = 5) combined with each person having at least 1 and at most 2 assignments forces exactly one person to have 2 tasks and the other three to have 1 each.

### Scenario (b): Either X or Z takes two tasks; Y and R each take one

**Person workload constraints:**
$$\sum_{j \in T} x_{X,j} \in \{1, 2\}$$
$$\sum_{j \in T} x_{Z,j} \in \{1, 2\}$$
$$\sum_{j \in T} x_{Y,j} = 1$$
$$\sum_{j \in T} x_{R,j} = 1$$
$$\sum_{j \in T} x_{X,j} + \sum_{j \in T} x_{Z,j} = 3$$

The last constraint ensures that exactly one of X or Z gets two tasks (since Y and R are fixed at 1 each, and total tasks = 5, we need $\sum x_X + \sum x_Z = 3$, which with each in {1,2} forces one to be 2 and the other to be 1).

### Scenario (c): Each person completes exactly one task; A and B must be done; one of {C, D, F} left unassigned

**Person workload constraints:**
$$\sum_{j \in T} x_{i,j} = 1 \quad \forall\, i \in P$$

**Task constraints** (as stated in Common Constraints above):
$$\sum_{i \in P} x_{i,A} = 1$$
$$\sum_{i \in P} x_{i,B} = 1$$
$$\sum_{i \in P} x_{i,C} + \sum_{i \in P} x_{i,D} + \sum_{i \in P} x_{i,F} = 2$$

---

## Feasibility Sanity Check

**Scenario (a):** 4 persons, 5 tasks. Each person gets 1 or 2 tasks, total = 5. Feasible (e.g., X:2, Y:1, Z:1, R:1).

**Scenario (b):** Y=1, R=1, X+Z=3 with each in {1,2}. So either (X=2, Z=1) or (X=1, Z=2). Feasible.

**Scenario (c):** 4 persons × 1 task = 4 assignments. Tasks A, B assigned (2 tasks), plus 2 of {C,D,F} assigned (2 tasks). Total = 4. Feasible.

All three scenarios are feasible. The solver will produce three separate optimal solutions (one per scenario).

## Verification Notes

- **Objective units:** The problem asks for "minimum total completion time" in the same unspecified units as the table. The objective directly sums $T_{i,j} \cdot x_{i,j}$, so `solver.Objective().Value()` will report the total time in those units. No normalization needed.
- **Magnitude check:** Each task takes ~20–45 units. With 4–5 tasks assigned, the total should be in the range ~100–200. A greedy estimate for scenario (a): best single assignments are R-A(24), Y-D(20), Y-C(26)→but Y can only do one... Let's estimate: R-A(24), Y-D(20), Z-B(27), X-C(31), X-F(37) → total = 24+20+27+31+37 = 139. This is in the expected range.
- **No scale factors:** The table has no multiplier or unit annotation. Values are used as-is.
