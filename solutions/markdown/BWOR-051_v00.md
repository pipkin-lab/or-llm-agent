# Operations Research Model: Task Assignment Problem

## 1. Decision Variables

Let $P = \{X, Y, Z, R\}$ be the set of persons and $T = \{A, B, C, D, F\}$ be the set of tasks.

We define binary decision variables:
$$
x_{ij} = \begin{cases} 
1 & \text{if person } i \in P \text{ is assigned to task } j \in T \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$
where $i \in \{X, Y, Z, R\}$ and $j \in \{A, B, C, D, F\}$.

*Note: The specific constraints on the number of assignments per person and per task vary by scenario (a), (b), and (c) as detailed below.*

## 2. Objective Function

The goal is to minimize the total completion time across all assigned tasks. Let $c_{ij}$ represent the time required for person $i$ to complete task $j$, taken from Table 5-3.

$$
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{i \in P} \sum_{j \in T} c_{ij} x_{ij}
$$

## 3. Operational Constraints

The constraints are defined differently for each of the three scenarios.

### Common Constraint: Task Uniqueness
In all scenarios, a specific task can be assigned to at most one person (standard assignment logic, though "at most" allows for unassigned tasks in scenario c).
$$
\sum_{i \in P} x_{ij} \leq 1 \quad \forall j \in T
$$
*(Note: Depending on interpretation, if a task must be assigned, the RHS becomes 1. In scenario (c), some tasks are not assigned, so $\leq 1$ is appropriate globally, with specific sum constraints below enforcing which ones are active.)*

---

#### Scenario (a): One person takes two tasks, others take one
*   **Person Capacity Constraints:**
    *   Exactly one person is assigned 2 tasks.
    *   The other three persons are each assigned exactly 1 task.
    *   Total assignments = 5 (since there are 5 tasks and presumably all are completed in this scenario description, or at least the load is distributed such that 5 task-slots are filled).
    
    To model this strictly, we can define auxiliary binary variables $y_i$ where $y_i=1$ if person $i$ takes 2 tasks, and $y_i=0$ otherwise.
    *   $\sum_{i \in P} y_i = 1$ (Exactly one person takes 2 tasks)
    *   $x_{iA} + x_{iB} + x_{iC} + x_{iD} + x_{iF} = 1 + y_i \quad \forall i \in P$
    
    *Alternatively, without auxiliary variables, the constraint set is:*
    *   Total assignments: $\sum_{i \in P} \sum_{j \in T} x_{ij} = 5$
    *   Each task assigned exactly once: $\sum_{i \in P} x_{ij} = 1 \quad \forall j \in T$
    *   Person loads: Let $L_i = \sum_{j \in T} x_{ij}$. Then $\{L_X, L_Y, L_Z, L_R\}$ must be a permutation of $\{2, 1, 1, 1\}$.
        *   $1 \leq L_i \leq 2 \quad \forall i \in P$
        *   $\sum_{i \in P} L_i = 5$

#### Scenario (b): X or Z takes two tasks; Y and R take one
*   **Specific Person Capacity Constraints:**
    *   Person Y: Exactly 1 task.
        $$ \sum_{j \in T} x_{Yj} = 1 $$
    *   Person R: Exactly 1 task.
        $$ \sum_{j \in T} x_{Rj} = 1 $$
    *   Person X: Either 1 or 2 tasks.
    *   Person Z: Either 1 or 2 tasks.
    *   Constraint between X and Z: Exactly one of them takes 2 tasks (which implies the other takes 1, since total tasks = 5).
        *   Let $L_X = \sum_{j \in T} x_{Xj}$ and $L_Z = \sum_{j \in T} x_{Zj}$.
        *   $L_X + L_Z = 3$
        *   $L_X \in \{1, 2\}$ and $L_Z \in \{1, 2\}$
        *   This implies either ($L_X=1, L_Z=2$) OR ($L_X=2, L_Z=1$).
    *   Task Assignment: All 5 tasks must be assigned exactly once.
        $$ \sum_{i \in P} x_{ij} = 1 \quad \forall j \in T $$

#### Scenario (c): Each person takes one task; A and B must be done; one of C, D, F is left unassigned
*   **Person Capacity Constraints:**
    *   Every person is assigned exactly 1 task.
        $$ \sum_{j \in T} x_{ij} = 1 \quad \forall i \in P $$
*   **Task Assignment Constraints:**
    *   Task A must be assigned:
        $$ \sum_{i \in P} x_{iA} = 1 $$
    *   Task B must be assigned:
        $$ \sum_{i \in P} x_{iB} = 1 $$
    *   Tasks C, D, F: Exactly two of these three are assigned (since 4 people take 4 tasks total, and A, B take 2 of those slots, leaving 2 slots for C, D, F).
        $$ \sum_{i \in P} x_{iC} + \sum_{i \in P} x_{iD} + \sum_{i \in P} x_{iF} = 2 $$
    *   Each task is assigned to at most one person (standard):
        $$ \sum_{i \in P} x_{ij} \leq 1 \quad \forall j \in T $$
