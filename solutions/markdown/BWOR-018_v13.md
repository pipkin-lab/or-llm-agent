### Model Type Declaration
**Model Type:** MILP (Mixed Integer Linear Programming)
*Note: Since the decision variables are binary (assignment of tasks), this is technically an ILP (Integer Linear Programming). It can also be solved as an LP because the assignment problem constraint matrix is totally unimodular, but explicit integer constraints are required for general solver safety.*

**Index Sets:**

- $J = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$: Set of stores.

**Unit Consistency Check:**

### Decision Variables

Let $x_{ij}$ be a binary decision variable for $i \in I, j \in J$:
$$
x_{ij} = 
\begin{cases} 
1 & \text{if construction company } A_i \text{ is assigned to build store } B_j \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

**Domain:**
$$ x_{ij} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall i \in I, j \in J $$

### Objective Function

Minimize the total construction cost.

$$
\min Z = \sum_{i=1}^{5} \sum_{j=1}^{5} c_{ij} x_{ij}
$$

Expanding the coefficients:
$$
\min Z = 4x_{11} + 8x_{12} + 7x_{13} + 15x_{14} + 12x_{15} + 7x_{21} + 9x_{22} + 17x_{23} + 14x_{24} + 10x_{25} + 6x_{31} + 9x_{32} + 12x_{33} + 8x_{34} + 7x_{35} + 6x_{41} + 7x_{42} + 14x_{43} + 6x_{44} + 10x_{45} + 6x_{51} + 9x_{52} + 12x_{53} + 10x_{54} + 6x_{55}
$$

### Operational Constraints

**1. Each store must be built by exactly one company:**
For each store $j \in J$:
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{5} x_{ij} = 1
$$
Explicitly:
- $\sum_{i=1}^{5} x_{i1} = 1$
- $\sum_{i=1}^{5} x_{i2} = 1$
- $\sum_{i=1}^{5} x_{i3} = 1$
- $\sum_{i=1}^{5} x_{i4} = 1$
- $\sum_{i=1}^{5} x_{i5} = 1$

**2. Each company can be assigned to build at most one store:**
Since there are 5 companies and 5 stores, and every store must be built, each company will be assigned to exactly one store. The constraint is "at most one", but combined with the store constraints, it becomes "exactly one" for all feasible solutions. We formulate it as an equality for clarity or inequality; equality is standard for 1-to-1 assignment.
For each company $i \in I$:
$$
\sum_{j=1}^{5} x_{ij} = 1
$$
Explicitly:
- $\sum_{j=1}^{5} x_{1j} = 1$
- $\sum_{j=1}^{5} x_{2j} = 1$
- $\sum_{j=1}^{5} x_{3j} = 1$
- $\sum_{j=1}^{5} x_{4j} = 1$
- $\sum_{j=1}^{5} x_{5j} = 1$

**3. Non-negativity / Integrality:**
$$
x_{ij} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall i \in I, j \in J
$$

### Feasibility Sanity Check
- Total Supply (Companies) = 5.
- Total Demand (Stores) = 5.
- Supply = Demand.
- The constraints ensure a perfect matching (1-to-1 assignment) between companies and stores.
- The constraint matrix for the assignment problem is totally unimodular, so an LP solver would yield integer solutions without explicitly enforcing integrality, but the MILP formulation is robust.
- All costs are positive and finite.
- A feasible solution always exists (any permutation of assignments).

### Final Answer Formulation Summary

**Model:** MILP
**Objective:** Minimize Total Cost (thousand yuan)
**Variables:** $x_{ij} \in \{0,1\}$
**Constraints:**
1. $\sum_{i=1}^5 x_{ij} = 1, \forall j \in \{1,..,5\}$
2. $\sum_{j=1}^5 x_{ij} = 1, \forall i \in \{1,..,5\}$
