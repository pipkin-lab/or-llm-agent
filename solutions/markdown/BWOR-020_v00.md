# Operations Research Model: Truck Delivery Problem

## 1. Problem Analysis

This is a **Set Covering / Assignment Problem** with side constraints. We need to assign specific quantities of goods to specific trucks such that all customer demands are met, truck capacities are respected, and specific conflict constraints are satisfied, while minimizing total cost.

*   **Customers & Demands**:
    *   Customer A: 1 unit
    *   Customer B: 2 units
    *   Customer C: 3 units
    *   Customer D: 4 units
    *   Customer E: 8 units
*   **Trucks & Capacities**:
    *   Truck 1: 2 units
    *   Truck 2: 6 units
    *   Truck 3: 8 units
    *   Truck 4: 11 units
*   **Costs**: $c_j$ for using Truck $j$ (where $j \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$).
*   **Constraints**:
    1.  Each truck is used at most once (implied by "each used only once" and the assignment nature).
    2.  Truck capacity limits.
    3.  All customer demands must be met exactly.
    4.  **Conflict Constraint 1**: No single truck can serve both Customer A and Customer C.
    5.  **Conflict Constraint 2**: No single truck can serve both Customer B and Customer D.

## 2. Decision Variables

Since the problem involves assigning *quantities* of goods to trucks, and customers have specific unit demands, we must define variables representing the amount of goods shipped from a specific customer to a specific truck. However, typically in these small-scale integer programming formulations for delivery, we define binary variables for *which* truck serves *which* customer, but since a truck can potentially carry goods for multiple customers (up to capacity), we need to track the load.

A more robust formulation for "assigning deliveries" where a truck can serve multiple customers is to use variables $x_{ij}$ representing the **amount of goods** (in units) from Customer $i$ assigned to Truck $j$.

However, looking at the constraints "A truck cannot deliver to *both* Customer A and Customer C", this implies a binary logic about *service*. If $x_{ij} > 0$, Truck $j$ serves Customer $i$.

Let's refine the variables to be integer quantities, as demands are integers and capacities are integers.

**Let:**
*   $i \in \{A, B, C, D, E\}$ be the index for customers.
*   $j \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ be the index for trucks.
*   $d_i$ be the demand for customer $i$ ($d_A=1, d_B=2, d_C=3, d_D=4, d_E=8$).
*   $Cap_j$ be the capacity of truck $j$ ($Cap_1=2, Cap_2=6, Cap_3=8, Cap_4=11$).
*   $c_j$ be the cost of using truck $j$.

**Decision Variables:**
Let $x_{ij}$ be the non-negative integer number of units of goods from Customer $i$ assigned to Truck $j$.

*Note*: In many textbook "Set Covering" or "Assignment" formulations for this specific type of problem (especially given the "Set Covering" label in the prompt, though it behaves more like a Generalized Assignment Problem), it is often assumed that a customer's demand must be satisfied by a *single* truck (splitting not allowed) or splitting is allowed. The constraint "A truck cannot deliver to both A and C" suggests we are tracking which customers are *served* by which truck.

If we assume **splitting of customer demand across multiple trucks is NOT allowed** (which is standard for this specific textbook problem type, often found in operations research contexts like Hillier & Lieberman), we use binary variables $y_{ij}$ indicating if Truck $j$ serves Customer $i$. But wait, if a truck serves Customer E (demand 8), it takes up 8 units of capacity. If a truck serves Customer A (demand 1) and B (demand 2), it takes 3 units.

Let's look at the "Set Covering" hint. Usually, Set Covering implies binary variables. However, simple binary assignment $z_{ij}=1$ if truck $j$ serves customer $i$ is insufficient because we need to check capacity against the *sum* of demands of all customers served by that truck.

Let's define:
$y_{ij} = 1$ if Truck $j$ is assigned to deliver the goods for Customer $i$, and $0$ otherwise.

**Assumption**: A customer's demand must be delivered by exactly one truck (no splitting of a single customer's order across multiple trucks). This is the standard interpretation for "assigning deliveries" in this context. If splitting were allowed, the conflict constraints would be harder to model with simple binaries without big-M or fractional variables, but usually, these problems assume atomic assignment of customers to trucks.

Let's verify if atomic assignment works with the capacities:
- Total Demand = $1+2+3+4+8 = 18$.
- Total Capacity = $2+6+8+11 = 27$.
- Customer E needs 8 units. Only Truck 3 (cap 8) or Truck 4 (cap 11) can handle E alone.
- Customer D needs 4 units. Truck 2 (6), 3 (8), 4 (11) can handle D alone.
- Customer C needs 3 units. Truck 2, 3, 4 can handle C alone.
- Customer B needs 2 units. Truck 1, 2, 3, 4 can handle B alone.
- Customer A needs 1 unit. Any truck can handle A alone.

This structure supports binary variables $y_{ij}$.

## 3. Mathematical Formulation

### Decision Variables
Let $y_{ij}$ be a binary variable defined for each customer $i \in \{A, B, C, D, E\}$ and truck $j \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$:
$$
y_{ij} = \begin{cases} 
1 & \text{if Truck } j \text{ is assigned to deliver to Customer } i \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

Let $z_j$ be a binary variable indicating if Truck $j$ is used:
$$
z_j = \begin{cases} 
1 & \text{if Truck } j \text{ is used (assigned to at least one customer)} \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$
*Note: The objective function uses $z_j$ to charge the cost $c_j$ only if the truck is actually utilized. We must link $z_j$ to $y_{ij}$.*

### Objective Function
Minimize the total transportation cost:
$$
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{j=1}^{4} c_j z_j
$$

### Constraints

**1. Demand Satisfaction (Each customer is served by exactly one truck)**
For each customer $i$, the sum of assignments across all trucks must equal 1.
$$
\sum_{j=1}^{4} y_{ij} = 1 \quad \forall i \in \{A, B, C, D, E\}
$$

**2. Truck Capacity Constraints**
The total demand of all customers assigned to a specific truck $j$ cannot exceed its capacity.
$$
\sum_{i \in \{A, B, C, D, E\}} d_i y_{ij} \leq Cap_j \quad \forall j \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}
$$
Where:
- $d_A=1, d_B=2, d_C=3, d_D=4, d_E=8$
- $Cap_1=2, Cap_2=6, Cap_3=8, Cap_4=11$

**3. Linking Constraints (Truck Usage)**
Truck $j$ is used ($z_j=1$) if and only if it is assigned to at least one customer. If it is assigned to any customer, $z_j$ must be 1.
$$
y_{ij} \leq z_j \quad \forall i \in \{A, B, C, D, E\}, \forall j \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}
$$
*(Since costs are positive, the model will naturally set $z_j=0$ if no $y_{ij}=1$, so this one-way inequality is sufficient for minimization).*

**4. Conflict Constraint 1: No truck serves both A and C**
For any truck $j$, it cannot be that $y_{Aj}=1$ AND $y_{Cj}=1$.
$$
y_{Aj} + y_{Cj} \leq 1 \quad \forall j \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}
$$

**5. Conflict Constraint 2: No truck serves both B and D**
For any truck $j$, it cannot be that $y_{Bj}=1$ AND $y_{Dj}=1$.
$$
y_{Bj} + y_{Dj} \leq 1 \quad \forall j \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}
$$

**6. Integrality Constraints**
$$
y_{ij} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall i, j
$$
$$
z_j \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall j
$$

---

## Final Structured Model

**Decision Variables:**
*   $y_{ij} \in \{0, 1\}$: 1 if Truck $j$ serves Customer $i$, 0 otherwise.
*   $z_j \in \{0, 1\}$: 1 if Truck $j$ is used, 0 otherwise.

**Objective Function:**
$$
\text{Minimize } \sum_{j=1}^{4} c_j z_j
$$

**Subject to:**

1.  **Customer Assignment:**
    $$ \sum_{j=1}^{4} y_{ij} = 1 \quad \forall i \in \{A, B, C, D, E\} $$

2.  **Truck Capacities:**
    $$ \sum_{i \in \{A, B, C, D, E\}} d_i y_{ij} \leq Cap_j \quad \forall j \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\} $$
    *(Specifically: $1y_{Aj} + 2y_{Bj} + 3y_{Cj} + 4y_{Dj} + 8y_{Ej} \leq Cap_j$)*

3.  **Usage Linking:**
    $$ y_{ij} \leq z_j \quad \forall i \in \{A, B, C, D, E\}, \forall j \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\} $$

4.  **Conflict A-C:**
    $$ y_{Aj} + y_{Cj} \leq 1 \quad \forall j \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\} $$

5.  **Conflict B-D:**
    $$ y_{Bj} + y_{Dj} \leq 1 \quad \forall j \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\} $$

6.  **Binary Restrictions:**
    $$ y_{ij}, z_j \in \{0, 1\} $$
