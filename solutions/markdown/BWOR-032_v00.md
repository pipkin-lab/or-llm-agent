Based on the problem description, here is the structured Operations Research model.

### 1. Decision Variables

Let $t$ denote the month index, where $t \in \{1, 2, ..., 12\}$ (January to December).
Let $i$ denote the product index, where $i \in \{1, 2\}$ (Product I and II).

**Primary Decision Variables:**
*   $x_{i,t}$: The number of units of Product $i$ produced in month $t$.
*   $s_{i,t}$: The inventory level (in units) of Product $i$ at the end of month $t$ (held in-house).
*   $r_{i,t}$: The inventory level (in units) of Product $i$ at the end of month $t$ (held in external rented storage).

**Auxiliary/State Variables (for clarity in constraints):**
*   $V_{t}$: Total volume of inventory in the in-house warehouse at the end of month $t$ (m³).
*   $W_{t}$: Total volume of inventory in external rented storage at the end of month $t$ (m³).

### 2. Objective Function

**Goal:** Minimize Total Cost ($Z$)

The total cost consists of:
1.  **Production Cost:** Depends on the product type and the month of production.
2.  **Inventory Holding Cost:** Depends on the volume of inventory held in-house vs. rented space.

Let $c^{prod}_{i,t}$ be the production cost per unit for Product $i$ in month $t$.
Let $v_i$ be the volume per unit for Product $i$ ($v_1 = 0.2$, $v_2 = 0.4$).
Let $h_{in} = 1$ yuan/m³/month (in-house holding cost).
Let $h_{out} = 1.5$ yuan/m³/month (external holding cost).

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{t=1}^{12} \sum_{i=1}^{2} c^{prod}_{i,t} \cdot x_{i,t} \\
+ \sum_{t=1}^{12} \left[ h_{in} \cdot \sum_{i=1}^{2} v_i \cdot s_{i,t} + h_{out} \cdot \sum_{i=1}^{2} v_i \cdot r_{i,t} \right]
\end{aligned}
$$

**Parameter Definitions for Production Costs ($c^{prod}_{i,t}$):**
*   **Product I ($i=1$):**
    *   $c^{prod}_{1,t} = 5.00$ for $t \in \{1, ..., 5\}$
    *   $c^{prod}_{1,t} = 4.50$ for $t \in \{6, ..., 12\}$
*   **Product II ($i=2$):**
    *   $c^{prod}_{2,t} = 8.00$ for $t \in \{1, ..., 5\}$
    *   $c^{prod}_{2,t} = 7.00$ for $t \in \{6, ..., 12\}$

### 3. Operational Constraints

#### A. Inventory Balance Constraints
For each product $i$ and month $t$, the inventory at the end of the period equals the previous period's inventory plus current production minus current demand. We assume no initial inventory ($s_{i,0}=0, r_{i,0}=0$) or that the system starts empty.

$$
s_{i,t} + r_{i,t} = s_{i,t-1} + r_{i,t-1} + x_{i,t} - d_{i,t}, \quad \forall i \in \{1,2\}, \forall t \in \{1,...,12\}
$$

**Demand Parameters ($d_{i,t}$):**
*   **Product I ($i=1$):**
    *   $d_{1,t} = 10,000$ for $t \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$
    *   $d_{1,t} = 30,000$ for $t \in \{5, 6, 7, 8, 9\}$
    *   $d_{1,t} = 100,000$ for $t \in \{10, 11, 12\}$
*   **Product II ($i=2$):**
    *   $d_{2,t} = 15,000$ for $t \in \{3, ..., 9\}$
    *   $d_{2,t} = 50,000$ for $t \in \{1, 2, 10, 11, 12\}$

#### B. Production Capacity Constraints
The total monthly production of both products must not exceed 120,000 units.

$$
x_{1,t} + x_{2,t} \leq 120,000, \quad \forall t \in \{1,...,12\}
$$

#### C. Warehouse Volume Constraints
**1. In-House Warehouse Capacity:**
The total volume of in-house inventory cannot exceed 15,000 m³.

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{2} v_i \cdot s_{i,t} \leq 15,000, \quad \forall t \in \{1,...,12\}
$$
*(Note: $v_1 = 0.2, v_2 = 0.4$)*

**2. External Storage:**
There is no explicit upper limit on external rented storage capacity, but it must be non-negative. The cost is handled in the objective function.

#### D. Non-negativity Constraints
Production and inventory levels cannot be negative.

$$
x_{i,t} \geq 0, \quad s_{i,t} \geq 0, \quad r_{i,t} \geq 0, \quad \forall i \in \{1,2\}, \forall t \in \{1,...,12\}
$$

### Part (a) Analysis: Infeasibility of the Original Problem (Without External Storage)

To show the problem is infeasible without external storage, we must check if demand can be met using only production and the 15,000 m³ in-house warehouse.

1.  **Check Peak Demand vs. Production Capacity:**
    *   In October ($t=10$), Demand for Product I is 100,000 and Product II is 50,000. Total Demand = 150,000 units.
    *   Max Production Capacity = 120,000 units.
    *   Shortfall in Oct = 30,000 units. This must be covered by inventory built up in previous months.

2.  **Check Inventory Volume Required for Shortfall:**
    *   To cover the 30,000 unit shortfall in October, the factory must produce 30,000 extra units in months 1-9.
    *   Let's look at the cumulative balance.
    *   Total Demand for Product I (Jan-Dec): $4(10k) + 5(30k) + 3(100k) = 40k + 150k + 300k = 490,000$.
    *   Total Demand for Product II (Jan-Dec): $5(50k) + 5(15k) + 2(50k) = 250k + 75k + 100k = 425,000$.
    *   Total Annual Demand = 915,000 units.
    *   Max Annual Production = $12 \times 120,000 = 1,440,000$ units.
    *   Aggregate capacity is sufficient. The issue is timing and storage volume.

3.  **Volume Constraint Violation Check (Simplified):**
    *   Consider the period before the high-cost/high-demand season (Oct-Dec) for Product I.
    *   Product I demand is high in Oct-Dec (300k units). Production is cheaper in Jun-Dec (4.5) than Jan-May (5). It is optimal to produce as much as possible in Jun-Sep to store for Oct-Dec.
    *   Even if we produce max capacity (120k) in Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep for Product I (ignoring Product II for a moment to see the volume strain), we can build up inventory.
    *   However, Product II also has demand.
    *   Let's look at the **maximum possible inventory volume** the warehouse can hold.
    *   Max Volume = 15,000 m³.
    *   If filled entirely with Product I (smaller volume 0.2 m³/unit): Max units = $15,000 / 0.2 = 75,000$ units.
    *   If filled entirely with Product II (larger volume 0.4 m³/unit): Max units = $15,000 / 0.4 = 37,500$ units.
    *   Now, look at **October**:
        *   Demand: 150,000 units.
        *   Max Production in Oct: 120,000 units.
        *   Minimum Inventory Required at Start of Oct: $150,000 - 120,000 = 30,000$ units.
    *   Can we store 30,000 units of *mix* by end of September?
        *   Yes, 30,000 units fits in volume. For example, 30,000 units of Product I takes $30,000 \times 0.2 = 6,000$ m³. This fits easily in 15,000 m³.
    *   **Wait, is it infeasible?**
        *   Let's check **November** and **December** as well.
        *   Nov Demand: 150,000. Max Prod: 120,000. Shortfall: 30,000.
        *   Dec Demand: 150,000. Max Prod: 120,000. Shortfall: 30,000.
        *   Total Shortfall for Oct, Nov, Dec = 90,000 units.
        *   These 90,000 units must be produced in Jan-Sep.
        *   Total Production Capacity Jan-Sep = $9 \times 120,000 = 1,080,000$ units.
        *   Total Demand Jan-Sep:
            *   Prod I: $4(10k) + 5(30k) = 190,000$.
            *   Prod II: $2(50k) + 5(15k) + 2(50k) \text{ [Mar-Sep is 15k, Jan-Feb is 50k]}$.
                *   Jan: 50k, Feb: 50k.
                *   Mar-Sep: 7 months * 15k = 105k.
                *   Total Prod II (Jan-Sep) = $100k + 105k = 205,000$.
            *   Total Demand Jan-Sep = $190,000 + 205,000 = 395,000$ units.
        *   Surplus Production Capacity in Jan-Sep = $1,080,000 - 395,000 = 685,000$ units.
        *   We only need to store 90,000 units for the winter. This is well within the *capacity* of production.
        *   **Volume Check for 90,000 units:**
            *   Worst case volume (all Product II): $90,000 \times 0.4 = 36,000$ m³.
            *   Warehouse Limit: 15,000 m³.
            *   $36,000 > 15,000$.
            *   Even if we store all Product I: $90,000 \times 0.2 = 18,000$ m³.
            *   $18,000 > 15,000$.
    *   **Conclusion for (a):** The peak demand in Oct, Nov, Dec creates a cumulative shortfall of 90,000 units that must be held in inventory from previous months. The minimum volume required to store 90,000 units (assuming the most compact product, I) is 18,000 m³. Since the warehouse capacity is only 15,000 m³, it is **physically impossible** to store the required inventory to meet the winter demand using only the in-house warehouse. Thus, the original problem (without external storage) has no feasible solution.

### Summary of Model for Part (b)
The model defined in sections 1-3 above is the correct formulation for Part (b), as it includes the variable $r_{i,t}$ for external storage, which relaxes the strict volume limit on the in-house warehouse while penalizing the excess volume in the objective function.
