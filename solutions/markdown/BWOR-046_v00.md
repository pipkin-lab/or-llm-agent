## 2. Mathematical Formulation

### 1. Decision Variables
Let $x_{ij}$ be the quantity of sugar (in tons) transported from Warehouse $A_i$ to Region $B_j$.
*   $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ corresponding to $A_1, A_2, A_3$.
*   $j \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ corresponding to $B_1, B_2, B_3, B_4, B_5$.

### 2. Objective Function
**Minimize** the total transportation cost $Z$:

$$
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{i=1}^{3} \sum_{j=1}^{5} c_{ij} x_{ij}
$$

Where $c_{ij}$ is the cost per ton from $A_i$ to $B_j$ (from Table 3-19):
*   $c_{11}=10, c_{12}=15, c_{13}=20, c_{14}=20, c_{15}=40$
*   $c_{21}=20, c_{22}=40, c_{23}=15, c_{24}=30, c_{25}=30$
*   $c_{31}=30, c_{32}=35, c_{33}=40, c_{34}=55, c_{35}=25$

Explicitly:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z = & 10x_{11} + 15x_{12} + 20x_{13} + 20x_{14} + 40x_{15} \\
    + & 20x_{21} + 40x_{22} + 15x_{23} + 30x_{24} + 30x_{25} \\
    + & 30x_{31} + 35x_{32} + 40x_{33} + 55x_{34} + 25x_{35}
\end{aligned}
$$

### 3. Operational Constraints

**A. Warehouse Capacity Constraints (Supply Limits)**
The amount shipped out of each warehouse cannot exceed its storage capacity.
*   $\sum_{j=1}^{5} x_{1j} \le 50$
*   $\sum_{j=1}^{5} x_{2j} \le 100$
*   $\sum_{j=1}^{5} x_{3j} \le 150$

**B. Factory Production Constraint (Global Supply Limit)**
The total amount of sugar distributed cannot exceed the factory's monthly production capacity.
*   $\sum_{i=1}^{3} \sum_{j=1}^{5} x_{ij} \le 270$

**C. Regional Demand Constraints**
The amount received by each region must satisfy its demand.
*Note: Since Total Demand (290) > Max Production (270), it is impossible to satisfy all demands with equality. In a real-world "Min Cost" scenario with limited supply, we typically either:*
1. *Prioritize regions (not given in problem).*
2. *Minimize unmet demand (requires penalty costs, not given).*
3. *Assume the problem intended for Demand $\le$ Supply (e.g., typo in factory cap).*

*Assuming standard "Meet Demand" requirement (which implies the problem is infeasible as stated, or requires a penalty function):*
If we strictly follow "Supply to regions", we usually write:
*   $\sum_{i=1}^{3} x_{i1} = 25$
*   $\sum_{i=1}^{3} x_{i2} = 105$
*   $\sum_{i=1}^{3} x_{i3} = 60$
*   $\sum_{i=1}^{3} x_{i4} = 30$
*   $\sum_{i=1}^{3} x_{i5} = 70$

*(If the model must be feasible, we would relax these to $\le$ and add a term to maximize total shipped, or add a dummy supply source with penalty costs. For the purpose of defining the standard OR model structure based on the text, we list them as equality constraints, noting the conflict with Constraint B.)*

**D. Non-negativity Constraints**
*   $x_{ij} \ge 0$ for all $i=1,2,3$ and $j=1,2,3,4,5$.

As defined, the model is **infeasible** because the sum of demand (290) exceeds the global supply constraint (270). To solve this as a standard LP, one of the following adjustments is typically made in practice:
1.  **Relax Demand Constraints:** Change demand equalities to inequalities ($\le$) and **Maximize** $\sum x_{ij}$ (to send as much as possible) or **Minimize Shortage Penalty**.
2.  **Dummy Source:** Add a dummy warehouse $A_4$ with supply 20 and high penalty costs to cover the deficit, then minimize cost.
