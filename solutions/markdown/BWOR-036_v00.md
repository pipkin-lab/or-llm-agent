# Operations Research Model: Timber Storage and Sales Optimization

## 1. Decision Variables

Let $n$ be the number of quarters in the planning horizon, where $n=4$.
The quarters are indexed as $t = 1, 2, 3, 4$ corresponding to Winter, Spring, Summer, and Autumn, respectively.

Define the following decision variables for each quarter $t \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$:

*   $x_t$: The quantity of timber purchased at the beginning of quarter $t$ (thousand $\text{m}^3$).
*   $s_t$: The quantity of timber sold during quarter $t$ (thousand $\text{m}^3$).
*   $I_t$: The inventory level of timber at the **end** of quarter $t$ (thousand $\text{m}^3$).
    *   *Note:* $I_0 = 0$ (Initial inventory is zero).
    *   *Note:* $I_4 = 0$ (Constraint that all timber must be sold by the end of Autumn).

## 2. Objective Function

The goal is to **Maximize** the annual profit.

Profit is calculated as Total Revenue minus Total Costs (Purchase Costs + Storage Costs).

**Parameters:**
*   $c_t$: Purchase price in quarter $t$.
    *   $c_1 = 410, c_2 = 430, c_3 = 460, c_4 = 450$ (thousand yuan/thousand $\text{m}^3$).
*   $p_t$: Selling price in quarter $t$.
    *   $p_1 = 425, p_2 = 440, p_3 = 465, p_4 = 455$ (thousand yuan/thousand $\text{m}^3$).
*   $h_t$: Storage cost per unit of inventory held from the end of quarter $t-1$ to the end of quarter $t$.
    *   The problem states the storage cost is $(a + b u)$ yuan per $\text{m}^3$, where $u$ is the storage time in quarters.
    *   $a = 70$, $b = 100$.
    *   **Unit Conversion:** Prices are in *thousand* yuan/thousand $\text{m}^3$. The storage cost formula gives *yuan* per $\text{m}^3$.
        *   $1$ yuan/$\text{m}^3 = 1$ thousand yuan/thousand $\text{m}^3$. (Since $1000 \text{ yuan} / 1000 \text{ m}^3 = 1$).
        *   Therefore, the cost coefficients can be used directly in the same units (thousand yuan/thousand $\text{m}^3$).
    *   If timber is stored for $u=1$ quarter (e.g., bought in Winter, sold in Spring), the cost is $70 + 100(1) = 170$.
    *   If timber is stored for $u=2$ quarters (e.g., bought in Winter, sold in Summer), the cost is $70 + 100(2) = 270$.
    *   Generally, the storage cost for inventory $I_{t-1}$ carried over to quarter $t$ is determined by how long it has been sitting. However, in a standard dynamic inventory LP, it is easier to define the storage cost based on the inventory level $I_{t-1}$ at the start of period $t$ (or end of $t-1$).
    *   Let's refine the storage cost interpretation. The cost is incurred on the timber that is *in storage*.
        *   Inventory $I_1$ (end of Winter) is stored for 1 quarter before being used/sold in Spring. Cost per unit = $70 + 100(1) = 170$.
        *   Inventory $I_2$ (end of Spring) is stored for 1 quarter before being used/sold in Summer. Cost per unit = $70 + 100(1) = 170$.
        *   Inventory $I_3$ (end of Summer) is stored for 1 quarter before being used/sold in Autumn. Cost per unit = $70 + 100(1) = 170$.

        *   If we buy in Winter ($t=1$) and sell in Spring ($t=2$), $u=1$. Cost = 170.
        *   If we buy in Winter ($t=1$) and sell in Summer ($t=3$), $u=2$. Cost = 270.
        *   If we buy in Winter ($t=1$) and sell in Autumn ($t=4$), $u=3$. Cost = 370.
    *   To model this linearly with state variable $I_t$, we must decompose the storage cost. The standard approach is to charge the marginal storage cost for each period the unit is held.
        *   Holding a unit for the first quarter costs $C_1 = 70 + 100(1) = 170$.
        *   Holding a unit for a second consecutive quarter adds an additional cost. The total for 2 quarters is 270. So the *incremental* cost for the 2nd quarter is $270 - 170 = 100$.

        *   In a sequential LP model, we usually define the cost of holding inventory $I_{t-1}$ during quarter $t$.
        *   Let $h_t$ be the storage cost per unit for inventory held at the end of quarter $t-1$ (i.e., inventory $I_{t-1}$).
        *   If a unit is in $I_0$ (start), it has been stored 0 quarters.
        *   If a unit is in $I_1$ (end of Winter), it will be stored for at least 1 quarter if sold in Spring.

        *   The cost to hold 1 unit for 1 quarter is effectively the difference between holding it for $u$ quarters vs $u-1$ quarters? No, the problem gives the *total* storage cost as a function of total time $u$.
        *   Let's assume the cost is incurred when the timber is stored.
        *   Cost for inventory $I_1$ (held from Winter to Spring): The timber was purchased in Winter. It is stored for 1 quarter. Cost = $170 \times I_1$? Or is it $170$ per unit that *remains*?
        *   Let's look at the structure: $Cost = (70 + 100u)$.
        *   This can be rewritten as $70 + 100 \times (\text{number of quarters held})$.
        *   In a linear model, we can assign a storage cost $h_t$ to the inventory level $I_{t-1}$ entering period $t$.

            The cost to hold a unit for $k$ periods is the sum of the holding costs for each period.

            The cost function is $C(u) = 70 + 100u$.
            This can be decomposed into a fixed cost of 70 per unit *if stored at all* and a variable cost of 100 per quarter.

            The marginal cost of holding a unit for the $k$-th quarter is 100.

            Total Cost = (Sum of marginal costs for each quarter held).

            The cost of the *second* quarter of storage is 100.

            Let $I_{t-1}$ be the inventory at the start of quarter $t$ (end of $t-1$).
            These units have already been stored for $t-1$ quarters.
            
            - For $I_1$ (start of Spring): These units were stored in Winter. They have completed 1 quarter of storage.

              - If they are kept for Summer, they will have been stored for 2 quarters.

            However, notice the pattern:
            Cost($u=1$) = 170
            Cost($u=2$) = 270
            Cost($u=3$) = 370
            
            We can assign the storage cost to the **inventory levels** as follows:
            The cost of holding inventory $I_{t-1}$ during quarter $t$ should reflect the *additional* cost incurred by holding it for that specific quarter.

            Total Storage Cost = $\sum_{\text{units}} C(u_{\text{unit}})$.
            
            Let's use the marginal cost logic on the inventory variables:
            - Inventory $I_1$ consists of units that have been stored for 1 quarter.
            - Inventory $I_2$ consists of units that have been stored for 2 quarters.
            - Inventory $I_3$ consists of units that have been stored for 3 quarters.

            - It is in $I_1$.
            - It is NOT in $I_2$.

            Let's test with 1 unit stored for 3 quarters (Winter to Autumn).
            - It is in $I_1, I_2, I_3$.

            - Actual Cost for $u=3$: 370. **Matches.**
            
            **Conclusion on Storage Costs:**
            The total annual storage cost is:
            $$ \text{Storage Cost} = 170 I_1 + 100 I_2 + 100 I_3 $$
            *(Note: $I_0=0$, so no term for it. $I_4=0$, so no term for it.)*
            
            Here:
            - $170$ is the cost for the first quarter of storage (applied to $I_1$).
            - $100$ is the incremental cost for the second quarter of storage (applied to $I_2$).
            - $100$ is the incremental cost for the third quarter of storage (applied to $I_3$).

**Objective Function:**

$$ \text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{t=1}^{4} (p_t s_t) - \sum_{t=1}^{4} (c_t x_t) - (170 I_1 + 100 I_2 + 100 I_3) $$

Substituting the values:
$$ Z = (425 s_1 + 440 s_2 + 465 s_3 + 455 s_4) - (410 x_1 + 430 x_2 + 460 x_3 + 450 x_4) - (170 I_1 + 100 I_2 + 100 I_3) $$

## 3. Operational Constraints

### A. Inventory Balance Constraints
For each quarter $t$, the inventory at the end of the quarter is equal to the inventory at the beginning, plus purchases, minus sales.
$$ I_t = I_{t-1} + x_t - s_t \quad \text{for } t = 1, 2, 3, 4 $$
With initial condition $I_0 = 0$.

Specifically:
1.  **Winter:** $I_1 = x_1 - s_1$
2.  **Spring:** $I_2 = I_1 + x_2 - s_2$
3.  **Summer:** $I_3 = I_2 + x_3 - s_3$
4.  **Autumn:** $I_4 = I_3 + x_4 - s_4$

### B. Terminal Constraint
All stored timber must be sold by the end of autumn.
$$ I_4 = 0 $$
*(This implies $s_4 = I_3 + x_4$)*

### C. Sales Capacity Constraints
Sales volume cannot exceed the estimated maximum sales volume for each quarter.
$$ s_t \le S_{\text{max}, t} \quad \text{for } t = 1, 2, 3, 4 $$
Where:
- $S_{\text{max}, 1} = 100$
- $S_{\text{max}, 2} = 140$
- $S_{\text{max}, 3} = 200$
- $S_{\text{max}, 4} = 160$

### D. Warehouse Capacity Constraints
The inventory at the end of each quarter cannot exceed the maximum warehouse capacity.
$$ I_t \le 20 \quad \text{for } t = 1, 2, 3 $$
*(Note: $I_4=0$ is already constrained to 0, which is $\le 20$).*

### E. Non-negativity Constraints
All decision variables must be non-negative.
$$ x_t \ge 0, \quad s_t \ge 0, \quad I_t \ge 0 \quad \text{for } t = 1, 2, 3, 4 $$

### Final Linear Programming Model Summary

**Maximize:**
$$ Z = 425 s_1 + 440 s_2 + 465 s_3 + 455 s_4 - 410 x_1 - 430 x_2 - 460 x_3 - 450 x_4 - 170 I_1 - 100 I_2 - 100 I_3 $$

**Subject to:**

1.  **Inventory Balances:**
    $$ x_1 - s_1 = I_1 $$
    $$ x_2 - s_2 + I_1 = I_2 $$
    $$ x_3 - s_3 + I_2 = I_3 $$
    $$ x_4 - s_4 + I_3 = 0 $$  *(Since $I_4=0$)*

2.  **Sales Limits:**
    $$ s_1 \le 100 $$
    $$ s_2 \le 140 $$
    $$ s_3 \le 200 $$
    $$ s_4 \le 160 $$

3.  **Storage Capacity:**
    $$ I_1 \le 20 $$
    $$ I_2 \le 20 $$
    $$ I_3 \le 20 $$

4.  **Non-negativity:**
    $$ x_t, s_t, I_t \ge 0 \quad \text{for } t=1,2,3,4 $$
