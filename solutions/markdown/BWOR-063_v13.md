Model Type: MILP (Mixed-Integer Linear Programming)

*   $R_2 = 3$: Total units of Resource 2.
*   $P_{i,x,y}$: Profit for department $i$ given $x$ units of Resource 1 and $y$ units of Resource 2.

### Decision Variables

Since the profit functions $r_i(x,y)$ are non-linear (discrete tables) with respect to the continuous/integer allocation variables, we linearize the model using binary variables.

Let $z_{i,x,y}$ be a binary decision variable for each department $i$, resource allocation pair $(x, y)$:
$$
z_{i,x,y} = 
\begin{cases} 
1 & \text{if Department } i \text{ receives exactly } x \text{ units of Resource 1 and } y \text{ units of Resource 2} \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$
Domain: $z_{i,x,y} \in \{0, 1\}$ for all $i \in I, x \in X, y \in Y$.

### Objective Function

Maximize the total profit across all departments:
$$
\text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{i=1}^{3} \sum_{x=0}^{3} \sum_{y=0}^{3} P_{i,x,y} \cdot z_{i,x,y}
$$

### Operational Constraints

**1. Exclusive Allocation Constraint (One combination per department):**
Each department must receive exactly one specific combination of resources $(x, y)$.
$$
\sum_{x=0}^{3} \sum_{y=0}^{3} z_{i,x,y} = 1 \quad \forall i \in I
$$

**2. Resource 1 Capacity Constraint:**
The total amount of Resource 1 allocated to all departments cannot exceed the available supply (3 units).
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{3} \sum_{x=0}^{3} \sum_{y=0}^{3} x \cdot z_{i,x,y} \le 3
$$

**3. Resource 2 Capacity Constraint:**
The total amount of Resource 2 allocated to all departments cannot exceed the available supply (3 units).
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{3} \sum_{x=0}^{3} \sum_{y=0}^{3} y \cdot z_{i,x,y} \le 3
$$

*(Note: Although the problem states "x units... to be allocated", implying all might be used, standard resource allocation problems usually allow unused resources unless specified otherwise. However, since profits are increasing with resources (monotonicity can be checked from the table: $P(x+1, y) \ge P(x,y)$ and $P(x, y+1) \ge P(x,y)$), the solver will naturally push to use all resources if beneficial. Looking at the tables, profit always increases or stays same as x or y increases. Thus, the optimal solution will likely satisfy the capacity constraints as equalities. We keep them as $\le$ to be formally correct, but they will likely bind.)*
