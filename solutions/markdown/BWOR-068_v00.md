# Production Planning Model: Snow Boot Seasonal Inventory

## 1. Decision Variables

Let $t$ represent the month index, where $t \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$ corresponding to months October, November, December, January, February, and March, respectively.

**Binary Decision Variables:**
Let $y_{t,k} \in \{0, 1\}$ for each month $t$ and order quantity option $k$.
The index $k$ corresponds to the discrete order quantity options:
- $k=1 \rightarrow Q_1 = 10$
- $k=2 \rightarrow Q_2 = 20$
- $k=3 \rightarrow Q_3 = 30$
- $k=4 \rightarrow Q_4 = 40$
- $k=5 \rightarrow Q_5 = 50$

$y_{t,k} = 1$ if an order of size $Q_k$ is placed in month $t$, and $0$ otherwise.

**Continuous/Integer Decision Variable (Implicit via constraint):**
Let $I_t$ be the inventory level (in pairs) at the end of month $t$.

*(Note: The actual order quantity in month $t$ is defined as $Q_t = \sum_{k=1}^{5} Q_k y_{t,k}$)*

## 2. Objective Function

**Minimize Total Cost ($Z$)**

The total cost consists of three components:
1.  **Ordering Costs**: Includes the fixed cost per order and the variable cost associated with the specific quantity ordered.
2.  **Storage (Holding) Costs**: Based on end-of-month inventory.
3.  **Backorder Costs**: None (assumed demand must be met from inventory/orders).

$$
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{t=1}^{6} \left[ \sum_{k=1}^{5} (C_{fixed} + C_{var,k}) y_{t,k} \right] + \sum_{t=1}^{6} (h \cdot I_t)
$$

Where:
-   $C_{fixed} = 10$ yuan (Fixed ordering cost per order).
-   $C_{var,k}$ is the variable order cost for quantity $Q_k$:
    -   $C_{var,1} = 48$ (for 10 pairs)
    -   $C_{var,2} = 86$ (for 20 pairs)
    -   $C_{var,3} = 118$ (for 30 pairs)
    -   $C_{var,4} = 138$ (for 40 pairs)
    -   $C_{var,5} = 160$ (for 50 pairs)
-   $h = 0.2$ yuan/pair/month (Storage cost rate).
-   $I_t$ is the end-of-month inventory for month $t$.

## 3. Operational Constraints

### A. Inventory Balance Constraints
For each month $t \in \{1, \dots, 6\}$, the inventory balance must hold. Since demand is satisfied and inventory starts at 0:
$$ I_{t-1} + \sum_{k=1}^{5} Q_k y_{t,k} - D_t = I_t $$
With boundary condition $I_0 = 0$.

*   $I_{t-1}$: Inventory at the end of the previous month (beginning of current month).
*   $\sum_{k=1}^{5} Q_k y_{t,k}$: Total quantity ordered in month $t$.
*   $D_t$: Demand in month $t$ (from Table 7-7).
    -   $D_1=40, D_2=20, D_3=30, D_4=40, D_5=30, D_6=20$.
*   $I_t$: Inventory at the end of month $t$.

**Non-negativity of Inventory:**
$$ I_t \ge 0, \quad \forall t \in \{1, \dots, 6\} $$
*(This ensures no backorders; all demand is met in the month it occurs or from prior inventory.)*

### B. Seasonal Boundary Constraints
The problem states inventory at the beginning and end of the peak season is zero.
$$ I_0 = 0 $$
$$ I_6 = 0 $$

### C. Inventory Capacity Constraints
The end-of-month inventory must not exceed 40 pairs.
$$ I_t \le 40, \quad \forall t \in \{1, \dots, 6\} $$

### D. Order Quantity Constraints
In any given month $t$, the store can place **at most one** order. This means the sum of binary variables for all quantity options in a single month cannot exceed 1.
$$ \sum_{k=1}^{5} y_{t,k} \le 1, \quad \forall t \in \{1, \dots, 6\} $$

*(Note: It is permissible to place no order in a month if existing inventory covers the demand, hence the inequality $\le 1$ rather than $=1$.)*

### E. Variable Domains
-   $y_{t,k} \in \{0, 1\}$ for all $t \in \{1,\dots,6\}, k \in \{1,\dots,5\}$.
-   $I_t \ge 0$ (and integer, as demand and orders are integers, though LP relaxation may be used initially, the structure implies integer inventory).
