**Model Type:** LP (Linear Programming)

### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Sets:**
*   $G = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of base liquor grades (I, II, III).
*   $B = \{R, Y, Bl\}$: Set of branded liquor products (Red, Yellow, Blue).

**Parameters:**

*   **Supply and Cost of Base Liquors:**
    *   $S_g$: Daily supply (kg)
    *   $C_g$: Cost (Yuan/kg)
    *   $S_1 = 1500, C_1 = 6.0$
    *   $S_2 = 2000, C_2 = 4.5$
    *   $S_3 = 1000, C_3 = 3.0$

*   **Product Specifications and Prices:**
    *   $P_b$: Selling price (Yuan/kg)
        *   $P_R = 5.5$
        *   $P_Y = 5.0$
        *   $P_{Bl} = 4.8$
    *   **Blending Constraints (Proportions):**
        *   *Red Brand:*
            *   Grade III fraction $< 10\% \implies x_{3,R} \le 0.10 X_R$
            *   Grade I fraction $> 50\% \implies x_{1,R} \ge 0.50 X_R$
            *   (Implicitly, Grade II fraction is the remainder)
        *   *Yellow Brand:*
            *   Grade III fraction $< 70\% \implies x_{3,Y} \le 0.70 X_Y$
            *   Grade I fraction $> 20\% \implies x_{1,Y} \ge 0.20 X_Y$
        *   *Blue Brand:*
            *   Grade III fraction $< 50\% \implies x_{3,Bl} \le 0.50 X_{Bl}$
            *   Grade I fraction $> 10\% \implies x_{1,Bl} \ge 0.10 X_{Bl}$

*   **Production Requirement:**
    *   $D_R = 2000$: Minimum production of Red brand (kg).

### 2. Decision Variables

*   $x_{g,b}$: Amount (kg) of Grade $g$ base liquor used in Brand $b$.
    *   Domain: $x_{g,b} \ge 0$ for all $g \in G, b \in B$.
*   $X_b$: Total amount (kg) of Brand $b$ produced.
    *   Domain: $X_b \ge 0$ for all $b \in B$.
    *   *Note: $X_b$ is defined by the sum of components, so it is a continuous variable determined by $x_{g,b}$.*

### 3. Objective Function

The problem asks to **Maximize Profit**.
Profit = Total Revenue - Total Cost.

$$ \text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{b \in B} P_b X_b - \sum_{g \in G} \sum_{b \in B} C_g x_{g,b} $$

Substituting the explicit values:
$$ \text{Maximize } Z = 5.5 X_R + 5.0 X_Y + 4.8 X_{Bl} - (6.0 \sum_{b} x_{1,b} + 4.5 \sum_{b} x_{2,b} + 3.0 \sum_{b} x_{3,b}) $$

*Alternative linear form using only $x_{g,b}$ (since $X_b = \sum_g x_{g,b}$):*
$$ \text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{b \in B} \left( P_b \sum_{g \in G} x_{g,b} - \sum_{g \in G} C_g x_{g,b} \right) $$
$$ \text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{b \in B} \sum_{g \in G} (P_b - C_g) x_{g,b} $$

Let's calculate the unit profit coefficients $(P_b - C_g)$ for the objective function to simplify:
*   **Red ($P_R=5.5$):**
    *   Grade I: $5.5 - 6.0 = -0.5$
    *   Grade II: $5.5 - 4.5 = 1.0$
    *   Grade III: $5.5 - 3.0 = 2.5$
*   **Yellow ($P_Y=5.0$):**
    *   Grade I: $5.0 - 6.0 = -1.0$
    *   Grade II: $5.0 - 4.5 = 0.5$
    *   Grade III: $5.0 - 3.0 = 2.0$
*   **Blue ($P_{Bl}=4.8$):**
    *   Grade I: $4.8 - 6.0 = -1.2$
    *   Grade II: $4.8 - 4.5 = 0.3$
    *   Grade III: $4.8 - 3.0 = 1.8$

**Objective Function:**
$$ \text{Maximize } Z = -0.5 x_{1,R} + 1.0 x_{2,R} + 2.5 x_{3,R} - 1.0 x_{1,Y} + 0.5 x_{2,Y} + 2.0 x_{3,Y} - 1.2 x_{1,Bl} + 0.3 x_{2,Bl} + 1.8 x_{3,Bl} $$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**A. Production Balance (Definition of Total Brand Output)**
For each brand $b$, the total output is the sum of the base liquors used.
$$ X_R = x_{1,R} + x_{2,R} + x_{3,R} $$
$$ X_Y = x_{1,Y} + x_{2,Y} + x_{3,Y} $$
$$ X_{Bl} = x_{1,Bl} + x_{2,Bl} + x_{3,Bl} $$

**B. Supply Constraints (Hard Constraints)**
The total usage of each grade cannot exceed its daily supply. Since all costs are positive and we are maximizing profit, and there is no penalty for unused supply (it's a daily supply limit, not a mandatory purchase), these are inequalities.
$$ \sum_{b \in B} x_{1,b} \le 1500 $$
$$ \sum_{b \in B} x_{2,b} \le 2000 $$
$$ \sum_{b \in B} x_{3,b} \le 1000 $$

**C. Blending Proportion Constraints (Hard Constraints - Priority 1)**
These constraints must hold strictly.

*   **Red Brand:**
    1.  Grade III < 10%:
        $$ x_{3,R} \le 0.10 X_R \implies x_{3,R} - 0.10(x_{1,R} + x_{2,R} + x_{3,R}) \le 0 \implies -0.10 x_{1,R} - 0.10 x_{2,R} + 0.90 x_{3,R} \le 0 $$
        *Simplified:* $x_{3,R} \le \frac{1}{9} (x_{1,R} + x_{2,R})$ or $9 x_{3,R} \le x_{1,R} + x_{2,R}$
    2.  Grade I > 50%:
        $$ x_{1,R} \ge 0.50 X_R \implies x_{1,R} - 0.50(x_{1,R} + x_{2,R} + x_{3,R}) \ge 0 \implies 0.50 x_{1,R} - 0.50 x_{2,R} - 0.50 x_{3,R} \ge 0 $$
        *Simplified:* $x_{1,R} \ge x_{2,R} + x_{3,R}$

*   **Yellow Brand:**
    1.  Grade III < 70%:
        $$ x_{3,Y} \le 0.70 X_Y \implies x_{3,Y} - 0.70(x_{1,Y} + x_{2,Y} + x_{3,Y}) \le 0 \implies -0.70 x_{1,Y} - 0.70 x_{2,Y} + 0.30 x_{3,Y} \le 0 $$
        *Simplified:* $3 x_{3,Y} \le 7 (x_{1,Y} + x_{2,Y})$
    2.  Grade I > 20%:
        $$ x_{1,Y} \ge 0.20 X_Y \implies x_{1,Y} - 0.20(x_{1,Y} + x_{2,Y} + x_{3,Y}) \ge 0 \implies 0.80 x_{1,Y} - 0.20 x_{2,Y} - 0.20 x_{3,Y} \ge 0 $$
        *Simplified:* $4 x_{1,Y} \ge x_{2,Y} + x_{3,Y}$

*   **Blue Brand:**
    1.  Grade III < 50%:
        $$ x_{3,Bl} \le 0.50 X_{Bl} \implies x_{3,Bl} - 0.50(x_{1,Bl} + x_{2,Bl} + x_{3,Bl}) \le 0 \implies -0.50 x_{1,Bl} - 0.50 x_{2,Bl} + 0.50 x_{3,Bl} \le 0 $$
        *Simplified:* $x_{3,Bl} \le x_{1,Bl} + x_{2,Bl}$
    2.  Grade I > 10%:
        $$ x_{1,Bl} \ge 0.10 X_{Bl} \implies x_{1,Bl} - 0.10(x_{1,Bl} + x_{2,Bl} + x_{3,Bl}) \ge 0 \implies 0.90 x_{1,Bl} - 0.10 x_{2,Bl} - 0.10 x_{3,Bl} \ge 0 $$
        *Simplified:* $9 x_{1,Bl} \ge x_{2,Bl} + x_{3,Bl}$

**D. Minimum Production Requirement (Hard Constraint - Priority 3)**
At least 2,000 kg of Red brand must be produced.
$$ X_R \ge 2000 $$
Substituting $X_R$:
$$ x_{1,R} + x_{2,R} + x_{3,R} \ge 2000 $$

**E. Non-negativity**
$$ x_{g,b} \ge 0, \quad X_b \ge 0 \quad \forall g \in G, b \in B $$

---

### Feasibility & Sanity Check

1.  **Red Brand Requirement:** $X_R \ge 2000$.
    *   Constraint: $x_{1,R} \ge x_{2,R} + x_{3,R}$ and $9 x_{3,R} \le x_{1,R} + x_{2,R}$.
    *   From $x_{1,R} \ge x_{2,R} + x_{3,R}$, we know $x_{1,R} \ge 0.5 X_R$. So for $X_R=2000$, we need at least 1000 kg of Grade I for Red.
    *   Total Grade I supply is 1500 kg.
    *   If we use 1000 kg of Grade I for Red, we have 500 kg left for Yellow and Blue.
2.  **Supply Check:**
    *   Red needs $x_{1,R} \ge 1000$. Let's assume minimum usage to save Grade I for others if profitable, or maximize profit.
    *   Grade I is the most expensive ($6.0) and has the lowest margin (or negative margin) in all brands.
        *   Red Grade I margin: -0.5
        *   Yellow Grade I margin: -1.0
        *   Blue Grade I margin: -1.2
    *   Grade III is the cheapest ($3.0) and has the highest margin.
        *   Red Grade III margin: 2.5
        *   Yellow Grade III margin: 2.0
        *   Blue Grade III margin: 1.8
    *   The solver will try to use as much Grade III as possible, constrained by the blending rules.
    *   Red Brand: Max Grade III is 10%. For 2000 kg Red, max $x_{3,R} = 200$. Min $x_{1,R} = 1000$.
    *   Yellow/Blue Brand: Can use more Grade III.
    *   Is it feasible?
        *   Red: $x_{1,R}=1000, x_{2,R}=800, x_{3,R}=200$. (Sum=2000).
            *   Check Red constraints: $x_{1,R} (1000) \ge x_{2,R}+x_{3,R} (1000)$ (OK). $9(200)=1800 \le 1000+800=1800$ (OK).
        *   Remaining Supply:
            *   Grade I: $1500 - 1000 = 500$.
            *   Grade II: $2000 - 800 = 1200$.
            *   Grade III: $1000 - 200 = 800$.
        *   Can we make more Yellow/Blue?
            *   We have 500 Grade I, 1200 Grade II, 800 Grade III.
            *   Let's try to maximize profit with remaining resources.
            *   Yellow constraints: $4 x_{1,Y} \ge x_{2,Y} + x_{3,Y}$ and $3 x_{3,Y} \le 7(x_{1,Y} + x_{2,Y})$.
            *   Blue constraints: $9 x_{1,Bl} \ge x_{2,Bl} + x_{3,Bl}$ and $x_{3,Bl} \le x_{1,Bl} + x_{2,Bl}$.
            *   Since Grade I is expensive/negative margin, we want to minimize $x_{1,Y}$ and $x_{1,Bl}$ while satisfying the "Grade I > threshold" constraints.
            *   For Yellow: Min $x_{1,Y} = (x_{2,Y} + x_{3,Y})/3$. Also $x_{1,Y} > 0.2 X_Y$.
            *   For Blue: Min $x_{1,Bl} = (x_{2,Bl} + x_{3,Bl})/8$. Also $x_{1,Bl} > 0.1 X_{Bl}$.
            *   It is feasible to produce more Yellow and Blue with the remaining resources. For example, if we set $x_{1,Y}=100$, then $x_{2,Y}+x_{3,Y} \le 400$. If we set $x_{1,Bl}=100$, $x_{2,Bl}+x_{3,Bl} \le 800$.
            *   Total Grade I used would be $1000+100+100=1200 \le 1500$.
            *   The model is feasible.

### Final Mathematical Formulation

**Model Type:** LP

**Parameters:**
*   $S = [1500, 2000, 1000]$
*   $C = [6.0, 4.5, 3.0]$
*   $P = [5.5, 5.0, 4.8]$ (for R, Y, Bl)
*   $D_R = 2000$

**Variables:**
*   $x_{g,b} \ge 0$ for $g \in \{1,2,3\}, b \in \{R,Y,Bl\}$
*   $X_b \ge 0$ for $b \in \{R,Y,Bl\}$

**Objective:**
$$ \text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{b \in B} \sum_{g \in G} (P_b - C_g) x_{g,b} $$

**Constraints:**
1.  **Balance:**
    $$ X_R = x_{1,R} + x_{2,R} + x_{3,R} $$
    $$ X_Y = x_{1,Y} + x_{2,Y} + x_{3,Y} $$
    $$ X_{Bl} = x_{1,Bl} + x_{2,Bl} + x_{3,Bl} $$

2.  **Supply:**
    $$ x_{1,R} + x_{1,Y} + x_{1,Bl} \le 1500 $$
    $$ x_{2,R} + x_{2,Y} + x_{2,Bl} \le 2000 $$
    $$ x_{3,R} + x_{3,Y} + x_{3,Bl} \le 1000 $$

3.  **Blending (Red):**
    $$ x_{1,R} \ge x_{2,R} + x_{3,R} $$
    $$ 9 x_{3,R} \le x_{1,R} + x_{2,R} $$

4.  **Blending (Yellow):**
    $$ 4 x_{1,Y} \ge x_{2,Y} + x_{3,Y} $$
    $$ 3 x_{3,Y} \le 7 (x_{1,Y} + x_{2,Y}) $$

5.  **Blending (Blue):**
    $$ 9 x_{1,Bl} \ge x_{2,Bl} + x_{3,Bl} $$
    $$ x_{3,Bl} \le x_{1,Bl} + x_{2,Bl} $$

6.  **Minimum Production:**
    $$ X_R \ge 2000 $$
