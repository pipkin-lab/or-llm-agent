# Operations Research Problem Analysis: Transportation with Storage and Minimum Shipment Constraints

## 1. Problem Context
This is a balanced transportation problem with **90 total supply** (20 + 40 + 30) and **70 total demand** (30 + 20 + 20). Since supply exceeds demand, not all goods will be shipped.
- **Storage Cost**: Incurred for unsent goods at each origin.
- **Minimum Shipment Constraints**: Specific minimum amounts must be shipped from Origins 2 and 3.

## 2. Decision Variables
Let $x_{ij}$ be the quantity shipped from origin $i$ to destination $j$.
- $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ (Origins)
- $j \in \{A, B, C\}$ (Destinations)

Variables:
- $x_{1A}, x_{1B}, x_{1C}$
- $x_{2A}, x_{2B}, x_{2C}$
- $x_{3A}, x_{3B}, x_{3C}$

## 3. Objective Function
**Minimize** Total Cost = Transportation Cost + Storage Cost

**Transportation Costs** (from Table 3-17):
$$ Z_{trans} = 1x_{1A} + 2x_{1B} + 2x_{1C} + 1x_{2A} + 4x_{2B} + 5x_{2C} + 2x_{3A} + 3x_{3B} + 3x_{3C} $$

**Storage Costs**:
Storage cost is incurred on the difference between Supply and Total Shipped from each origin.
- Storage at Origin 1: $5 \times (20 - (x_{1A} + x_{1B} + x_{1C}))$
- Storage at Origin 2: $4 \times (40 - (x_{2A} + x_{2B} + x_{2C}))$
- Storage at Origin 3: $3 \times (30 - (x_{3A} + x_{3B} + x_{3C}))$

**Total Objective Function**:
$$ \text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{i,j} c_{ij}x_{ij} + 5(20 - \sum_j x_{1j}) + 4(40 - \sum_j x_{2j}) + 3(30 - \sum_j x_{3j}) $$

*Note: This can be simplified by incorporating storage costs into effective shipping costs or treating it as a standard transportation problem with a dummy destination (Demand) for unsent goods. For clarity in modeling, we keep the explicit form above, but for solution logic, we can view the "cost" of leaving a unit at origin $i$ as the storage cost.*

## 4. Operational Constraints

### A. Demand Constraints (Equality)
All demand must be met exactly.
1. **Destination A**: $x_{1A} + x_{2A} + x_{3A} = 30$
2. **Destination B**: $x_{1B} + x_{2B} + x_{3B} = 20$
3. **Destination C**: $x_{1C} + x_{2C} + x_{3C} = 20$

### B. Supply Constraints (Inequality)
Shipment from an origin cannot exceed its supply.
4. **Origin 1**: $x_{1A} + x_{1B} + x_{1C} \leq 20$
5. **Origin 2**: $x_{2A} + x_{2B} + x_{2C} \leq 40$
6. **Origin 3**: $x_{3A} + x_{3B} + x_{3C} \leq 30$

### C. Minimum Shipment Constraints (Explicit)
The problem specifies minimum shipment quantities for Origins 2 and 3.
7. **Min Shipment from Origin 2**: $x_{2A} + x_{2B} + x_{2C} \geq 38$
8. **Min Shipment from Origin 3**: $x_{3A} + x_{3B} + x_{3C} \geq 27$

### D. Non-negativity Constraints
9. $x_{ij} \geq 0$ for all $i, j$

## 5. Solution Derivation

To solve this, we can transform the problem into a standard balanced transportation problem by adding a **Dummy Destination D** which represents "Storage at Origin".
- **Supply**: Remains 20, 40, 30.
- **Demand**: A=30, B=20, C=20, D=20 (Total Demand = 90, matching Supply).
- **Costs to Dummy Destination D**: These are the storage costs.
    - $c_{1D} = 5$
    - $c_{2D} = 4$
    - $c_{3D} = 3$
- **Other Costs**: Same as transportation costs in Table 3-17.

However, we have additional constraints:
- Shipment from Origin 2 to {A,B,C} $\geq 38$. Since Supply 2 is 40, this means Shipment from Origin 2 to D $\leq 2$.
- Shipment from Origin 3 to {A,B,C} $\geq 27$. Since Supply 3 is 30, this means Shipment from Origin 3 to D $\leq 3$.

Let $x_{2D}$ and $x_{3D}$ be the amounts stored at Origin 2 and 3.
Constraints become:
- $x_{2D} \leq 2$
- $x_{3D} \leq 3$

**Effective Cost Matrix (including Dummy D):**

| Origin \ Dest | A | B | C | D (Storage) | Supply |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |

| **2** | 1 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 40 |
| **3** | 2 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 30 |
| **Demand** | 30 | 20 | 20 | 20 | 90 |

*Constraints on Dummy Columns:*
- $x_{2D} \leq 2$
- $x_{3D} \leq 3$
- $x_{1D}$ is free (up to 20).
