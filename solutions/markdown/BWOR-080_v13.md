**Model Type:** Linear Programming (LP)

### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
- $T = \{1, \dots, 6\}$: Set of months (1=Jan, ..., 6=Jun).
- $P = \{1, \dots, 7\}$: Set of products (I to VII).
- $M = \{1, \dots, 5\}$: Set of machine types (1=Grinding, 2=Upright Drill, 3=Horizontal Drill, 4=Boring Machine, 5=Planer).

**Parameters:**
- $a_{m,p}$: Machine-hours required per unit of product $p$ on machine type $m$ (from Table C-3).
  - Note: "--" implies 0 hours required.
- $c_p$: Profit per unit of product $p$ (yuan).
  - $c = [100, 60, 80, 40, 110, 90, 30]$
- $D_{t,p}$: Maximum market demand in month $t$ for product $p$ (from Table C-4).
- $N_m$: Number of machines of type $m$ in the factory.
  - $N_1=4$ (Grinding), $N_2=2$ (Upright), $N_3=3$ (Horizontal), $N_4=1$ (Boring), $N_5=1$ (Planer).
- $B_{t,m}$: Number of machines of type $m$ undergoing maintenance in month $t$ (unavailable).
  - Jan ($t=1$): $B_{1,1}=1$, others 0.
  - Feb ($t=2$): $B_{2,3}=2$, others 0.
  - Mar ($t=3$): $B_{3,4}=1$, others 0.
  - Apr ($t=4$): $B_{4,2}=1$, others 0.
  - May ($t=5$): $B_{5,1}=1, B_{5,2}=1$, others 0.
  - Jun ($t=6$): $B_{6,5}=1, B_{6,3}=1$, others 0.
- $H_{t,m}$: Available hours for machine type $m$ in month $t$.
  - Calculation: 24 days $\times$ 2 shifts $\times$ 8 hours/shift = 384 hours/machine/month.
  - $H_{t,m} = (N_m - B_{t,m}) \times 384$.
- $h_p$: Storage cost per unit per month = 5 yuan.
- $S_{max}$: Maximum inventory limit = 100 units per product.
- $I_{1,p}$: Initial inventory = 0 for all $p$.
- $I_{final,p}$: Required final inventory at end of June = 50 for all $p$.

### 2. Decision Variables

- $x_{t,p}$: Quantity of product $p$ produced in month $t$. (Continuous, $x_{t,p} \ge 0$)
- $I_{t,p}$: Inventory level of product $p$ at the **end** of month $t$. (Continuous, $I_{t,p} \ge 0$)

### 3. Objective Function

Maximize total profit minus storage costs.
$$
\text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{t=1}^{6} \sum_{p=1}^{7} (c_p \cdot x_{t,p}) - \sum_{t=1}^{6} \sum_{p=1}^{7} (h_p \cdot I_{t,p})
$$
Note: The problem asks to maximize "total profit". Usually, production profit is realized upon production or sale. Given "unsold units can be stored", it is standard to assume profit is recognized on production (or sale, but with inventory balance, production volume drives the flow). However, strictly speaking, profit is earned when goods are sold. But in linear production planning with inventory, the standard formulation is Max $\sum \text{Profit}_p x_{t,p} - \sum \text{Storage}_p I_{t,p}$. This assumes the profit margin $c_p$ is the contribution margin per unit produced. If profit were only on sales, we would need sales variables $s_{t,p}$ and $x_{t,p} = s_{t,p} + \Delta I_{t,p}$. The demand constraint is "Maximum market demand", implying we can sell up to $D_{t,p}$. Let's refine:
Let $s_{t,p}$ be units sold in month $t$.
Profit = $\sum c_p s_{t,p} - \sum h_p I_{t,p}$.
Constraints: $s_{t,p} \le D_{t,p}$.
Inventory Balance: $I_{t,p} = I_{t-1,p} + x_{t,p} - s_{t,p}$.
This is equivalent to maximizing $\sum c_p x_{t,p} - \sum c_p (\text{units not sold}) ...$ No, simpler:
Since $s_{t,p} = I_{t-1,p} + x_{t,p} - I_{t,p}$, substituting into objective:
$Z = \sum_{t,p} c_p (I_{t-1,p} + x_{t,p} - I_{t,p}) - \sum_{t,p} h_p I_{t,p}$.
Rearranging, this is equivalent to maximizing $\sum c_p x_{t,p} - \sum c_p I_{t,p} + \sum c_p I_{t-1,p} - \sum h_p I_{t,p}$.
Actually, the standard form for "Max Profit" where profit is per unit sold is:
Max $\sum_{t,p} (c_p s_{t,p} - h_p I_{t,p})$
Subject to $s_{t,p} \le D_{t,p}$, $s_{t,p} \le I_{t-1,p} + x_{t,p}$.
To keep the model simple and linear without extra variables if we assume all produced goods are either sold or stored (and stored goods retain their value but incur holding cost), we can stick to production $x$ and inventory $I$.
However, "Profit per unit" usually refers to the unit margin. If we produce but don't sell, do we make the profit? No.
So, we introduce sales variable $s_{t,p}$.

**Revised Decision Variables:**
- $x_{t,p}$: Production quantity in month $t$ for product $p$. ($x_{t,p} \ge 0$)
- $s_{t,p}$: Sales quantity in month $t$ for product $p$. ($s_{t,p} \ge 0$)
- $I_{t,p}$: End-of-month inventory for product $p$. ($I_{t,p} \ge 0$)

**Objective:**
$$
\text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{t=1}^{6} \sum_{p=1}^{7} (c_p \cdot s_{t,p}) - \sum_{t=1}^{6} \sum_{p=1}^{7} (5 \cdot I_{t,p})
$$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**1. Demand Constraints:**
Sales cannot exceed maximum market demand.
$$ s_{t,p} \le D_{t,p} \quad \forall t \in T, p \in P $$

**2. Inventory Balance Constraints:**
Inventory at end of month $t$ = Inventory at end of month $t-1$ + Production in month $t$ - Sales in month $t$.
$$ I_{t,p} = I_{t-1,p} + x_{t,p} - s_{t,p} \quad \forall t \in T, p \in P $$
With boundary conditions:
- $I_{0,p} = 0$ for all $p$.
- $I_{6,p} = 50$ for all $p$ (Final inventory requirement).
  *(Note: This specific constraint for $t=6$ overrides the general balance equation for the final state, or is added as an equality constraint on $I_{6,p}$.)*

**3. Machine Capacity Constraints:**
Total machine hours used in month $t$ for machine type $m$ cannot exceed available hours $H_{t,m}$.
$$ \sum_{p=1}^{7} a_{m,p} \cdot x_{t,p} \le H_{t,m} \quad \forall t \in T, m \in M $$

**4. Storage Capacity Constraints:**
Inventory at end of any month cannot exceed the limit.
$$ I_{t,p} \le 100 \quad \forall t \in T, p \in P $$
*(Note: This includes $t=6$, so $I_{6,p} \le 100$, which is consistent with $I_{6,p}=50$.)*

**5. Non-negativity:**
$$ x_{t,p} \ge 0, \quad s_{t,p} \ge 0, \quad I_{t,p} \ge 0 \quad \forall t, p $$
