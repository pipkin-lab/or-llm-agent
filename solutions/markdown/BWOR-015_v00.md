Here is the Operations Research model for the Jinyuan Company production planning problem, formulated as a **Preemptive Multi-Objective Linear Programming (Goal Programming)** problem.

### 1. Decision Variables

Let $x_{i,t}$ be the quantity of Product $i$ produced in Stage $t$.
Let $s_{i,t}$ be the inventory of Product $i$ at the end of Stage $t$.

**Indices:**
*   $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ : Product index
*   $t \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ : Stage (period) index

**Variables:**
*   $x_{1,t}, x_{2,t}, x_{3,t} \ge 0$ : Production quantity of products 1, 2, 3 in stage $t$.
*   $s_{1,t}, s_{2,t}, s_{3,t} \ge 0$ : Ending inventory of products 1, 2, 3 at the end of stage $t$.

**Deviation Variables (for Goal Programming):**
Since this is a goal programming problem, we introduce positive ($d^+$) and negative ($d^-$) deviation variables for each prioritized goal.

**Priority 1: Minimize Stockouts (Unmet Demand)**
*   $u_{i,t}^-$ : Underage of demand for Product $i$ in Stage $t$ (Stockout).
*   $u_{i,t}^+$ : Overage of demand for Product $i$ in Stage $t$ (Excess inventory carried over, though typically in pure stockout minimization, we focus on $u^-$. However, inventory balance usually handles the flow. In standard goal programming for demand satisfaction, we define the goal as satisfying demand $D_{i,t}$).
    *   Let $d_{i,t}^-$ be the deviation below the demand target (Stockout).
    *   Let $d_{i,t}^+$ be the deviation above the demand target (Oversatisfaction/Excess produced specifically for immediate demand, though inventory $s$ handles this naturally. In goal programming context, we usually target $D_{i,t}$).
    *   *Clarification*: In production planning with inventory, "stockout" occurs when production + beginning inventory < demand. The unmet demand is the "shortage".
    *   Let $y_{i,t}^-$ be the unmet demand (shortage) for product $i$ in period $t$.
    *   Let $y_{i,t}^+$ be the excess satisfaction (rarely penalized directly if inventory is allowed, but let's stick to the standard goal: Meet Demand $D_{i,t}$).
    *   Actually, a cleaner way for "Minimizing Stockouts" in a multi-period context is to ensure that the available supply (Production + Beginning Inventory) meets the Demand. The shortage is the negative deviation from the demand constraint.

    Let's define the deviation variables for **Priority 1 (Demand Satisfaction)**:
    *   $d_{i,t}^-$ : Shortage of Product $i$ in Stage $t$ (Stockout).
    *   $d_{i,t}^+$ : Excess of Product $i$ in Stage $t$ relative to demand (This usually flows into inventory, so we often don't penalize $d^+$ here, or we treat inventory separately. Given "initial and final inventories are zero", and demand is specific per stage, we assume demand must be met in that stage or backlogged? The problem says "minimizing stockouts". Usually, this implies meeting demand on time. If we cannot produce enough, we have a stockout. Let's assume backlogging is not allowed or is the "stockout". We will model the constraint as: Production + Beginning Inventory + Backlog_{prev} - Backlog_{curr} = Demand.
    *   *Simpler Interpretation for OR Models*: Often, "stockout" means the demand is not met in the current period. Let's define $S_{i,t}$ as the inventory at end of $t$.
    *   Constraint: $x_{i,t} + S_{i,t-1} - S_{i,t} = D_{i,t} - d_{i,t}^- + d_{i,t}^+$
        Where $d_{i,t}^-$ is the unmet demand (stockout) and $d_{i,t}^+$ is the excess (which just stays in inventory, so $d_{i,t}^+$ is not necessarily penalized in P1 if inventory is allowed, BUT the problem says "minimizing stockouts". It does not say minimize inventory. However, producing extra creates inventory. Usually, we only penalize $d^-$.
        *Note*: The problem states "initial and final inventories are zero". This is a hard constraint on $S_{i,0}=0$ and $S_{i,3}=0$.

    Let's refine Priority 1 Deviations:
    *   $d_{i,t}^-$ : Unmet demand (Stockout) for product $i$ in stage $t$.
    *   We do not penalize $d_{i,t}^+$ in the objective because excess production just becomes inventory $S_{i,t}$. However, to strictly follow "timely delivery", we want $d_{i,t}^- = 0$.

**Priority 2: Minimize Equipment Overloading**
*   $e_t^-$ : Underutilization of equipment in stage $t$.
*   $e_t^+$ : Overutilization of equipment in stage $t$.

**Priority 3: Minimize Working Capital Overrun**
*   $c_t^-$ : Underutilization of capital in stage $t$ (usually not penalized).
*   $c_t^+$ : Overutilization of capital in stage $t$.

### 2. Objective Function

We use a **Preemptive (Lexicographic) Goal Programming** approach. The objective is to minimize the weighted sum of deviations in order of priority.

**Priority 1: Minimize Weighted Stockouts**
*   Weight for Product 1: 1
*   Weight for Product 2: 1
*   Weight for Product 3: 2 (Twice that of P1/P2)

$$ \text{Minimize } Z_1 = 1 \cdot \sum_{t=1}^3 d_{1,t}^- + 1 \cdot \sum_{t=1}^3 d_{2,t}^- + 2 \cdot \sum_{t=1}^3 d_{3,t}^- $$

**Priority 2: Minimize Equipment Overloading**
*   We only penalize the positive deviation (overloading).
$$ \text{Minimize } Z_2 = \sum_{t=1}^3 e_t^+ $$

**Priority 3: Minimize Working Capital Overrun**
*   We only penalize the positive deviation (exceeding the limit).
$$ \text{Minimize } Z_3 = \sum_{t=1}^3 c_t^+ $$

**Composite Objective (Lexicographic Order):**
$$ \text{Minimize } \mathbf{Z} = [Z_1, Z_2, Z_3] $$
*(Note: In computational solving, this is often handled by sequential optimization or by assigning large, distinct weights $M_1 \gg M_2 \gg M_3$ to the deviations in a single linear objective function).*

### 3. Operational Constraints

#### A. Inventory Balance Constraints (Linking Production, Demand, and Inventory)
For each product $i$ and stage $t$:
$$ x_{i,t} + S_{i,t-1} - S_{i,t} = D_{i,t} - d_{i,t}^- + d_{i,t}^+ $$
*   Where $D_{i,t}$ is the demand for product $i$ in stage $t$ (from Table 4-16).
*   $S_{i,0} = 0$ (Initial inventory is zero).
*   $S_{i,3} = 0$ (Final inventory is zero).
*   $d_{i,t}^- \ge 0$ represents the stockout.
*   $d_{i,t}^+ \ge 0$ represents the excess production that goes into inventory (since $S_{i,t}$ accumulates this). *Note: In strict goal programming for "meeting demand", one might argue $d^+$ is not needed if $S$ is an explicit variable, but including it ensures the equation balances if production exceeds demand + inventory change. Actually, simpler form: $x_{i,t} + S_{i,t-1} + d_{i,t}^- - d_{i,t}^+ - S_{i,t} = D_{i,t}$ is incorrect. The standard is: Supply = Demand + Change in Inventory + Shortage - Excess.
    Let's stick to:
    $$ x_{i,t} + S_{i,t-1} - S_{i,t} + d_{i,t}^- - d_{i,t}^+ = D_{i,t} $$
    Here, $d_{i,t}^-$ is unmet demand (shortage), and $d_{i,t}^+$ is excess demand satisfied (which implies we produced more than needed for current demand and inventory buildup, effectively just increasing $S_{i,t}$). Actually, if we produce more, $S_{i,t}$ increases. We don't need $d^+$ in the objective, but it must be in the constraint to allow $x > D + \Delta S$.
    *Correction*: If $S_{i,t}$ is a free variable (bounded below by 0), we can simply write:
    $$ x_{i,t} + S_{i,t-1} - S_{i,t} + d_{i,t}^- = D_{i,t} $$
    Here $d_{i,t}^-$ is the shortage. If we produce more than $D_{i,t} - S_{i,t-1} + S_{i,t}$, the LHS would exceed RHS unless we allow "excess" slack. However, since $S_{i,t}$ can grow arbitrarily (within capital limits), any excess production just increases $S_{i,t}$. So, strictly:
    $$ x_{i,t} + S_{i,t-1} - S_{i,t} + d_{i,t}^- = D_{i,t} $$
    With $S_{i,t} \ge 0$, this works. If $x + S_{prev} > D + S_{curr}$, then $d^- = 0$ and the equality holds by adjusting $S_{curr}$ (if we can increase it). Wait, if we fix $S_{curr}$, then we need a slack.
    Let's use the standard goal programming formulation for demand:
    $$ x_{i,t} + S_{i,t-1} - S_{i,t} + d_{i,t}^- - d_{i,t}^+ = D_{i,t} $$
    We penalize $d_{i,t}^-$ in $Z_1$. $d_{i,t}^+$ is not penalized.

#### B. Equipment Working Hours Constraints (Priority 2)
For each stage $t \in \{1, 2, 3\}$:
$$ 2.0 x_{1,t} + 1.0 x_{2,t} + 3.1 x_{3,t} + e_t^- - e_t^+ = 5000 $$
*   $e_t^+$ is the overloading (penalized in $Z_2$).
*   $e_t^-$ is underutilization (not penalized).

#### C. Working Capital Constraints (Priority 3)
For each stage $t \in \{1, 2, 3\}$:
$$ 40 x_{1,t} + 20 x_{2,t} + 55 x_{3,t} + c_t^- - c_t^+ = 93000 $$
*   $c_t^+$ is the capital overrun (penalized in $Z_3$).
*   $c_t^-$ is unused capital (not penalized).

#### D. Non-negativity and Boundary Constraints
*   $x_{i,t} \ge 0 \quad \forall i, t$
*   $S_{i,t} \ge 0 \quad \forall i, t$
*   $S_{i,0} = 0 \quad \forall i$
*   $S_{i,3} = 0 \quad \forall i$
*   $d_{i,t}^-, d_{i,t}^+, e_t^-, e_t^+, c_t^-, c_t^+ \ge 0 \quad \forall i, t$

### Summary of the Mathematical Model

**Decision Variables:**
$x_{i,t}$ (Production), $S_{i,t}$ (Inventory), and deviation variables $(d^-, d^+, e^-, e^+, c^-, c^+)$.

**Goal Hierarchy:**
1.  **Minimize** $\sum_{t=1}^3 (d_{1,t}^- + d_{2,t}^- + 2d_{3,t}^-)$
2.  **Minimize** $\sum_{t=1}^3 e_t^+$
3.  **Minimize** $\sum_{t=1}^3 c_t^+$

**Constraints:**
1.  **Inventory Balance:** $x_{i,t} + S_{i,t-1} - S_{i,t} + d_{i,t}^- - d_{i,t}^+ = D_{i,t}$ for all $i,t$.
2.  **Equipment:** $2.0x_{1,t} + 1.0x_{2,t} + 3.1x_{3,t} + e_t^- - e_t^+ = 5000$ for all $t$.
3.  **Capital:** $40x_{1,t} + 20x_{2,t} + 55x_{3,t} + c_t^- - c_t^+ = 93000$ for all $t$.
4.  **Boundaries:** $S_{i,0}=0, S_{i,3}=0$, all variables $\ge 0$.
