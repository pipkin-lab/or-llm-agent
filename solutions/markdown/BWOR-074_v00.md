# OR Model: Optimal Sorting Order for Exam Answer Sheets

## 1. Decision Variables

Let the set of grades be $G = \{A, B, C, D, E\}$.
Let $\pi$ be a permutation of the grades representing the sorting order, where $\pi(k)$ denotes the grade sorted at the $k$-th step ($k=1, \dots, 5$).

We define binary decision variables $x_{g,k}$:
$$
x_{g,k} = 
\begin{cases} 
1 & \text{if grade } g \in G \text{ is assigned to position } k \text{ in the sorting sequence} \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$
where $g \in \{A, B, C, D, E\}$ and $k \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$.

## 2. Objective Function

**Goal:** Minimize the total sorting workload (total number of scan operations).

**Workload Analysis:**
- When sorting the first grade (position $k=1$), the computer must scan **all** $N$ sheets to identify those matching that grade.
- When sorting the second grade (position $k=2$), the computer only needs to scan the **remaining** sheets that were *not* identified in the first step.
- In general, at step $k$, the computer scans only the sheets that have not been sorted in steps $1$ through $k-1$.

Let $N = 50,000$ be the total number of sheets.  
Let $n_g$ be the number of sheets with grade $g$, given by $n_g = N \cdot p_g$, where $p_g$ is the historical percentage for grade $g$:
- $n_A = 0.10N = 5,000$
- $n_B = 0.25N = 12,500$
- $n_C = 0.30N = 15,000$
- $n_D = 0.20N = 10,000$
- $n_E = 0.15N = 7,500$

The number of sheets scanned at step $k$ is:
$$
S_k = N - \sum_{j=1}^{k-1} n_{\pi(j)}
$$
The total workload $W$ is:
$$
W = \sum_{k=1}^{5} S_k = \sum_{k=1}^{5} \left( N - \sum_{j=1}^{k-1} n_{\pi(j)} \right)
$$
This can be rewritten to show that the total workload depends on the position of each grade:
$$
W = 5N - \sum_{k=1}^{5} (5-k) \cdot n_{\pi(k)}
$$
Alternatively, and more intuitively for minimization: **each sheet of grade $g$ is scanned exactly as many times as the number of sorting steps that occur *before or at* its own sorting step.** If grade $g$ is sorted at position $k$, it is scanned $k$ times (once in each of steps $1, 2, \dots, k$). Thus:
$$
W = \sum_{g \in G} n_g \cdot k_g
$$
where $k_g$ is the position of grade $g$ in the sorting order.

To minimize $W = \sum_{g \in G} n_g \cdot k_g$, we should assign **larger** $n_g$ (more frequent grades) to **smaller** $k_g$ (earlier positions), because the coefficient $k_g$ multiplies $n_g$. This is an application of the **Rearrangement Inequality**.

Thus, the objective is:
$$
\text{Minimize } W = \sum_{g \in G} \sum_{k=1}^{5} n_g \cdot k \cdot x_{g,k}
$$

## 3. Operational Constraints

1. **Each grade is assigned to exactly one position:**
   $$
   \sum_{k=1}^{5} x_{g,k} = 1 \quad \forall g \in G
   $$

2. **Each position is assigned exactly one grade:**
   $$
   \sum_{g \in G} x_{g,k} = 1 \quad \forall k \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}
   $$

3. **Binary constraints:**
   $$
   x_{g,k} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall g \in G, \, k \in \{1, \dots, 5\}
   $$

---

### Optimal Solution Derivation (Analytical)

By the Rearrangement Inequality, to minimize $\sum n_g \cdot k_g$, we pair the **largest** $n_g$ with the **smallest** $k_g$.

Sort grades by descending frequency:
| Grade | Frequency $n_g$ | Rank |
|-------|----------------|------|
| C     | 15,000         | 1st (largest) → assign to $k=1$ |
| B     | 12,500         | 2nd → assign to $k=2$ |
| D     | 10,000         | 3rd → assign to $k=3$ |
| E     | 7,500          | 4th → assign to $k=4$ |
| A     | 5,000          | 5th (smallest) → assign to $k=5$ |

**Optimal Sorting Order:**  
$$
\boxed{C \rightarrow B \rightarrow D \rightarrow E \rightarrow A}
$$

**Total Minimum Workload:**
$$
W_{\min} = 15,000(1) + 12,500(2) + 10,000(3) + 7,500(4) + 5,000(5)
$$
$$
W_{\min} = 15,000 + 25,000 + 30,000 + 30,000 + 25,000 = 125,000 \text{ scan operations}
$$
