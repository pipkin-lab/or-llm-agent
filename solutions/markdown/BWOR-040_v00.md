# Operations Research Model: Production Planning with Labor Constraints

## 1. Decision Variables

Let the decision variables represent the production quantities of the three products:

*   $x_1$: Number of **bundles** of manuscript paper produced per month.
*   $x_2$: Number of **dozens** of diaries produced per month.
*   $x_3$: Number of **boxes** of exercise books produced per month.

*(Note: Since the problem asks whether to hire temporary workers and how many, we can model this by relaxing the labor constraint and incorporating the cost of temporary labor into the objective function, or by adding a variable for temporary workers. A more direct modeling approach for "should we hire" is to include the cost of temporary labor in the objective function if the standard labor constraint is binding. However, a cleaner OR formulation treats the labor constraint as a limit on total labor hours, and if the optimal solution requires more labor than available, the "cost" of that extra labor is the wage. Let's refine this to a standard LP where we maximize net profit.)*

**Refined Decision Variables:**
*   $x_1, x_2, x_3 \geq 0$: Production quantities (as defined above).
*   $y \geq 0$: Number of **temporary workers** hired.

## 2. Objective Function

**Goal:** Maximize Total Profit (Revenue - Cost of Temporary Workers).

*   **Profit per unit:**
    *   Manuscript paper ($x_1$): 2 yuan/bundle
    *   Diaries ($x_2$): 3 yuan/dozen
    *   Exercise books ($x_3$): 1 yuan/box
*   **Cost:**
    *   Temporary worker wage: 40 yuan/person/month.

$$
\text{Maximize } Z = 2x_1 + 3x_2 + 1x_3 - 40y
$$

## 3. Operational Constraints

### A. Raw Material Constraint (White Base Paper)
Total consumption of white base paper must not exceed the monthly supply of 30,000 kg.

*   Consumption rates:
    *   $x_1$: $3\frac{1}{3} = \frac{10}{3}$ kg/bundle
    *   $x_2$: $13\frac{1}{3} = \frac{40}{3}$ kg/dozen
    *   $x_3$: $26\frac{2}{3} = \frac{80}{3}$ kg/box

$$
\frac{10}{3}x_1 + \frac{40}{3}x_2 + \frac{80}{3}x_3 \leq 30,000
$$

*(Simplified: $10x_1 + 40x_2 + 80x_3 \leq 90,000$)*

### B. Labor Constraint
Total labor required for production must not exceed the total available labor (permanent + temporary).

*   **Productivity Rates (per worker per month):**
    *   Manuscript paper: 30 bundles $\rightarrow$ Labor per bundle = $1/30$ worker-months
    *   Diaries: 30 dozens $\rightarrow$ Labor per dozen = $1/30$ worker-months
    *   Exercise books: 30 boxes $\rightarrow$ Labor per box = $1/30$ worker-months

*   **Available Labor:**
    *   Permanent workers: 100
    *   Temporary workers: $y$

$$
\frac{1}{30}x_1 + \frac{1}{30}x_2 + \frac{1}{30}x_3 \leq 100 + y
$$

*(Simplified: $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 \leq 3,000 + 30y$)*

### C. Non-negativity Constraints
Production quantities and number of temporary workers cannot be negative.

$$
x_1, x_2, x_3, y \geq 0
$$

---

### Summary of the Linear Programming Model

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Maximize } & Z = 2x_1 + 3x_2 + x_3 - 40y \\
\text{Subject to: } & 10x_1 + 40x_2 + 80x_3 \leq 90,000 \quad \text{(Material)} \\
& x_1 + x_2 + x_3 - 30y \leq 3,000 \quad \text{(Labor)} \\
& x_1, x_2, x_3, y \geq 0
\end{aligned}
$$

**Interpretation for the Question:**
To answer "Should the factory hire temporary workers?", solve this model.
*   If the optimal solution has $y^* > 0$, then **yes**, the factory should hire temporary workers.
*   The optimal number to hire is $y^*$.
*   If $y^* = 0$, then **no**, the current workforce is sufficient for the optimal production plan.
