To determine the optimal replacement strategy, we can model this problem as a **Shortest Path Problem** (or a specific type of Linear Programming/Integer Programming).

### 1. Problem Analysis & Parameter Extraction

**Planning Horizon**: 4 years.
**Goal**: Minimize Total Cost (Purchase - Salvage + Operating/Maintenance).
**Note on Units**: All values are in "thousand yuan".

**Parameters from Table 8-7:**
*   **Purchase Price ($P_t$)**: Cost to buy a new machine at the *beginning* of year $t$.
    *   $P_1 = 2.5$
    *   $P_2 = 2.6$
    *   $P_3 = 2.8$
    *   $P_4 = 3.1$
*   **Salvage Value ($S_k$)**: Value received when selling a machine that has been used for $k$ years.
    *   $S_1 = 2.0$ (Sold after 1 year of use)
    *   $S_2 = 1.6$ (Sold after 2 years of use)
    *   $S_3 = 1.3$ (Sold after 3 years of use)
    *   $S_4 = 1.1$ (Sold after 4 years of use)
    *   *Note*: The problem states the machine "can operate continuously for 4 years". This implies the maximum useful life is 4 years. If a machine is bought in year $t$, it can be kept for at most 4 years, but the planning horizon ends at the end of year 4.

**Operating & Maintenance Costs ($C_{age}$)**:
The cost depends on how long the *current* machine has been in service.
*   Age 1 (First year of life): $0.3$
*   Age 2 (Second year of life): $0.8$
*   Age 3 (Third year of life): $1.5$
*   Age 4 (Fourth year of life): $2.0$

**Cost Structure for a "Service Interval"**:
If a machine is purchased at the beginning of year $i$ and sold at the end of year $j$ (where $j \ge i$), the total cost for this interval $[i, j]$ consists of:
1.  Purchase cost at start of year $i$: $P_i$
2.  Operating costs for years $i$ through $j$.
    *   In year $i$, the machine is 1 year old (Age 1). Cost: $0.3$.
    *   In year $i+1$, the machine is 2 years old (Age 2). Cost: $0.8$.
    *   ...
    *   In year $j$, the machine is $(j - i + 1)$ years old. Cost: $C_{j-i+1}$.
3.  Salvage value at end of year $j$: $- S_{j-i+1}$ (Subtracted because it's revenue).

Let $c_{i,j}$ be the total net cost of buying a machine at the start of year $i$ and selling it at the end of year $j$.
$$ c_{i,j} = P_i + \sum_{t=0}^{j-i} C_{t+1} - S_{j-i+1} $$
Where $C_k$ is the O&M cost for a machine of age $k$, and $S_k$ is the salvage value after $k$ years.

**Calculate $c_{i,j}$ for all valid intervals within the 4-year horizon:**

*   **Interval Length 1 (1 year of use)**:
    *   Cost = $P_i + C_1 - S_1 = P_i + 0.3 - 2.0 = P_i - 1.7$
    *   $c_{1,1} = 2.5 - 1.7 = 0.8$
    *   $c_{2,2} = 2.6 - 1.7 = 0.9$
    *   $c_{3,3} = 2.8 - 1.7 = 1.1$
    *   $c_{4,4} = 3.1 - 1.7 = 1.4$

*   **Interval Length 2 (2 years of use)**:
    *   Cost = $P_i + C_1 + C_2 - S_2 = P_i + 0.3 + 0.8 - 1.6 = P_i - 0.5$
    *   $c_{1,2} = 2.5 - 0.5 = 2.0$
    *   $c_{2,3} = 2.6 - 0.5 = 2.1$
    *   $c_{3,4} = 2.8 - 0.5 = 2.3$

*   **Interval Length 3 (3 years of use)**:
    *   Cost = $P_i + C_1 + C_2 + C_3 - S_3 = P_i + 0.3 + 0.8 + 1.5 - 1.3 = P_i + 1.3$
    *   $c_{1,3} = 2.5 + 1.3 = 3.8$
    *   $c_{2,4} = 2.6 + 1.3 = 3.9$

*   **Interval Length 4 (4 years of use)**:
    *   Cost = $P_i + C_1 + C_2 + C_3 + C_4 - S_4 = P_i + 0.3 + 0.8 + 1.5 + 2.0 - 1.1 = P_i + 3.5$
    *   $c_{1,4} = 2.5 + 3.5 = 6.0$

*(Note: Intervals starting at $i$ and ending at $j$ are only valid if $j-i+1 \le 4$. Since the horizon is 4 years, max length is 4. Also, a machine bought in year 4 can only be used for 1 year within the horizon, so $j$ must be $\le 4$.)*

### 2. Mathematical Model Formulation

We define a decision variable for each possible service interval.

**Index Sets:**
*   $I = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ : Set of years (start of interval).
*   $J = \{j \in I \mid j \ge i \}$ : Set of potential end years for an interval starting at $i$.

**Parameters:**
*   $c_{i,j}$ : Total net cost of purchasing a machine at the beginning of year $i$ and selling it at the end of year $j$. Values calculated above.

**Decision Variables:**
*   $x_{i,j}$ : Binary variable.
    *   $x_{i,j} = 1$ if a machine is purchased at the beginning of year $i$ and sold at the end of year $j$.
    *   $x_{i,j} = 0$ otherwise.
    *   Domain: $i \in \{1,2,3,4\}$, $j \in \{i, i+1, \dots, 4\}$.

**Objective Function:**
Minimize the total cost over the 4-year period.
$$ \text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{i=1}^{4} \sum_{j=i}^{4} c_{i,j} x_{i,j} $$

**Constraints:**
The strategy must ensure that every year is covered by exactly one machine service interval. This is a partition of the time horizon $\{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ into disjoint consecutive segments.

1.  **Year 1 Coverage**: A machine must be active during year 1. This means the interval must start at $i=1$. It can end at $j=1, 2, 3,$ or $4$.
    $$ x_{1,1} + x_{1,2} + x_{1,3} + x_{1,4} = 1 $$

2.  **Year 2 Coverage**:
    *   If the machine from Year 1 was sold at the end of Year 1 ($x_{1,1}=1$), a new machine must start at the beginning of Year 2.
    *   If the machine from Year 1 is still active (ended at $j > 1$), no new purchase is made at the start of Year 2.
    *   Formally, the sum of intervals *starting* at year 2 must equal 1 if the previous interval ended at 1, and 0 otherwise.
    *   Constraint: The number of intervals ending at year $t-1$ must equal the number of intervals starting at year $t$, EXCEPT for the very first start (Year 1) and the very last end (Year 4).
    *   Let's use flow conservation logic on the "replacement points".
    *   Start of Year 1: Must start exactly one interval. (Constraint 1 above handles this).
    *   Transition to Year 2: If $x_{1,1}=1$, we need a new start. If $x_{1,2}=1$ or later, we don't.
        $$ x_{2,2} + x_{2,3} + x_{2,4} = x_{1,1} $$
    *   Transition to Year 3:
        $$ x_{3,3} + x_{3,4} = x_{1,2} + x_{2,2} $$
        *(Explanation: A new interval must start at Year 3 if the previous interval ended at the end of Year 2. The previous interval could be the one starting at 1 and ending at 2, or the one starting at 2 and ending at 2.)*
    *   Transition to Year 4:
        $$ x_{4,4} = x_{1,3} + x_{2,3} + x_{3,3} $$
        *(Explanation: A new interval must start at Year 4 if the previous interval ended at the end of Year 3.)*
    *   End of Horizon (Year 4): All active intervals must end by the end of Year 4.
        $$ x_{1,4} + x_{2,4} + x_{3,4} + x_{4,4} = 1 $$
        *(Note: This is actually redundant if the previous transition constraints and the Year 1 start constraint are satisfied, but it ensures the horizon is closed properly. Let's verify:
        Sum of all RHS in transition eqs:
        $x_{1,1} + (x_{1,2}+x_{2,2}) + (x_{1,3}+x_{2,3}+x_{3,3})$.
        This equals the total number of intervals started.
        The final constraint ensures the last interval ends at 4. Since we can't go beyond year 4, any interval starting before 4 that doesn't end earlier must end at 4.)*

    Actually, a simpler set of constraints for a shortest path / partition problem is:
    1.  $x_{1,1} + x_{1,2} + x_{1,3} + x_{1,4} = 1$ (Must start in Year 1)
    2.  $x_{2,2} + x_{2,3} + x_{2,4} = x_{1,1}$ (Start Year 2 iff Year 1 interval ended at 1)
    3.  $x_{3,3} + x_{3,4} = x_{1,2} + x_{2,2}$ (Start Year 3 iff Year 2 interval ended at 2)
    4.  $x_{4,4} = x_{1,3} + x_{2,3} + x_{3,3}$ (Start Year 4 iff Year 3 interval ended at 3)
    5.  $x_{1,4} + x_{2,4} + x_{3,4} + x_{4,4} = 1$ (Must end in Year 4)
    
    *Check consistency*:
    From (1), sum of starts at 1 is 1.
    From (2), sum of starts at 2 equals ends at 1.
    From (3), sum of starts at 3 equals ends at 2.
    From (4), sum of starts at 4 equals ends at 3.
    From (5), sum of ends at 4 is 1.
    Total intervals started = Total intervals ended.
    Total started = $1 + x_{1,1} + (x_{1,2}+x_{2,2}) + (x_{1,3}+x_{2,3}+x_{3,3})$.
    Total ended = $x_{1,1} + (x_{1,2}+x_{2,2}) + (x_{1,3}+x_{2,3}+x_{3,3}) + 1$.
    They match. The system is consistent.

    **Binary Constraint**:
    $$ x_{i,j} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall i,j $$

### 3. Solution via Dynamic Programming (for verification)

Let $f(t)$ be the minimum cost to cover the period from the beginning of year $t$ to the end of year 4.
$f(5) = 0$ (Base case, past the horizon).
$f(t) = \min_{j \in \{t, \dots, 4\}} \{ c_{t,j} + f(j+1) \}$

**Backward Induction:**

**Year 4 ($t=4$):**
Possible intervals: $(4,4)$.
$c_{4,4} = 1.4$.
$f(4) = c_{4,4} + f(5) = 1.4 + 0 = 1.4$.
*Decision: Buy in Y4, sell in Y4.*

**Year 3 ($t=3$):**
Possible intervals: $(3,3), (3,4)$.
1.  Buy in Y3, sell in Y3: Cost $c_{3,3} + f(4) = 1.1 + 1.4 = 2.5$.
2.  Buy in Y3, sell in Y4: Cost $c_{3,4} + f(5) = 2.3 + 0 = 2.3$.
Min is 2.3.
$f(3) = 2.3$.
*Decision: Buy in Y3, sell in Y4.*

**Year 2 ($t=2$):**
Possible intervals: $(2,2), (2,3), (2,4)$.
1.  Buy in Y2, sell in Y2: Cost $c_{2,2} + f(3) = 0.9 + 2.3 = 3.2$.
2.  Buy in Y2, sell in Y3: Cost $c_{2,3} + f(4) = 2.1 + 1.4 = 3.5$.
3.  Buy in Y2, sell in Y4: Cost $c_{2,4} + f(5) = 3.9 + 0 = 3.9$.
Min is 3.2.
$f(2) = 3.2$.
*Decision: Buy in Y2, sell in Y2.*

**Year 1 ($t=1$):**
Possible intervals: $(1,1), (1,2), (1,3), (1,4)$.
1.  Buy in Y1, sell in Y1: Cost $c_{1,1} + f(2) = 0.8 + 3.2 = 4.0$.
2.  Buy in Y1, sell in Y2: Cost $c_{1,2} + f(3) = 2.0 + 2.3 = 4.3$.
3.  Buy in Y1, sell in Y3: Cost $c_{1,3} + f(4) = 3.8 + 1.4 = 5.2$.
4.  Buy in Y1, sell in Y4: Cost $c_{1,4} + f(5) = 6.0 + 0 = 6.0$.
Min is 4.0.
$f(1) = 4.0$.
*Decision: Buy in Y1, sell in Y1.*

**Optimal Strategy Reconstruction:**
*   Start at $t=1$: Choose $(1,1)$. Next state $t=2$.
*   At $t=2$: Choose $(2,2)$. Next state $t=3$.
*   At $t=3$: Choose $(3,4)$. Next state $t=5$ (End).

**Strategy**:
1.  Buy new machine at start of Year 1. Operate for 1 year. Sell at end of Year 1.
2.  Buy new machine at start of Year 2. Operate for 1 year. Sell at end of Year 2.
3.  Buy new machine at start of Year 3. Operate for 2 years. Sell at end of Year 4.

**Total Cost**: 4.0 thousand yuan.

### Final Output: Mathematical Model

**Model Type**: MILP (Mixed Integer Linear Programming)

**Index Sets**
*   $I = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$: Set of years.
*   $J_{i} = \{j \in I \mid j \ge i\}$: Set of potential end years for an interval starting at year $i$.

**Parameters**
*   $c_{i,j}$: Net cost (thousand yuan) of purchasing a machine at the beginning of year $i$ and selling it at the end of year $j$.
    *   $c_{1,1} = 0.8, c_{1,2} = 2.0, c_{1,3} = 3.8, c_{1,4} = 6.0$
    *   $c_{2,2} = 0.9, c_{2,3} = 2.1, c_{2,4} = 3.9$
    *   $c_{3,3} = 1.1, c_{3,4} = 2.3$
    *   $c_{4,4} = 1.4$

**Decision Variables**
*   $x_{i,j}$: Binary variable, 1 if the machine purchased at the beginning of year $i$ is sold at the end of year $j$, 0 otherwise.
    *   Domain: $i \in \{1,2,3,4\}, j \in \{i, \dots, 4\}$.

**Objective Function**
$$ \text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{i=1}^{4} \sum_{j=i}^{4} c_{i,j} x_{i,j} $$

**Constraints**
1.  **Start Constraint (Year 1)**:
    $$ x_{1,1} + x_{1,2} + x_{1,3} + x_{1,4} = 1 $$
2.  **Transition Constraint (Year 2)**:
    $$ x_{2,2} + x_{2,3} + x_{2,4} = x_{1,1} $$
3.  **Transition Constraint (Year 3)**:
    $$ x_{3,3} + x_{3,4} = x_{1,2} + x_{2,2} $$
4.  **Transition Constraint (Year 4)**:
    $$ x_{4,4} = x_{1,3} + x_{2,3} + x_{3,3} $$
5.  **End Constraint (Year 4)**:
    $$ x_{1,4} + x_{2,4} + x_{3,4} + x_{4,4} = 1 $$
6.  **Binary Restriction**:
    $$ x_{i,j} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall i,j $$

**Optimal Solution**:
*   $x_{1,1} = 1$
*   $x_{2,2} = 1$
*   $x_{3,4} = 1$
*   All other $x_{i,j} = 0$
*   **Minimum Total Cost**: 4.0 thousand yuan.
