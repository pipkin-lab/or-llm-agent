# Operations Research Model: Hongsheng Factory Production Planning

## 1. Problem Analysis

The goal is to maximize the total profit for Hongsheng Factory by determining the production quantities of Products I, II, and III. The production process involves two stages (A and B) with specific machine constraints, processing times, costs, and revenue data.

**Key Logic:**
- **Profit per unit** is calculated as: $\text{Selling Price} - \text{Raw Material Cost} - \text{Processing Cost}$.
- **Processing Cost** is variable: $\text{Processing Time on Machine } M \times \text{Cost per Hour of Machine } M$.
- **Flow Balance**: The amount of a product entering Stage B must equal the amount produced in Stage A (assuming no inventory buildup between stages within the planning period, which is standard for this type of short-term production planning problem). Thus, we can model the production quantity of each product as a single decision variable, subject to the constraint that the total processing hours required in Stage A and Stage B do not exceed available hours.

However, because different machines have different costs and capacities, and products have restricted machine options, we must track which machine processes which product to accurately calculate costs. This requires **decision variables representing the quantity of each product processed on each specific machine**.

## 2. Decision Variables

Let $x_{ij}$ be the number of units of Product $i$ processed on Machine $j$ in Stage A.
Let $y_{ij}$ be the number of units of Product $i$ processed on Machine $j$ in Stage B.

**Products ($i$):** I, II, III
**Machines in Stage A ($j$):** $A_1, A_2$
**Machines in Stage B ($j$):** $B_1, B_2, B_3$

**Valid Decision Variables (based on processing rules):**

*   **Product I:** Can use any machine in both stages.
    *   $x_{I,A_1}, x_{I,A_2}$
    *   $y_{I,B_1}, y_{I,B_2}, y_{I,B_3}$
*   **Product II:** Any A machine, only $B_2$ in Stage B.
    *   $x_{II,A_1}, x_{II,A_2}$
    *   $y_{II,B_2}$
*   **Product III:** Only $A_2$ in Stage A, only $B_2$ in Stage B.
    *   $x_{III,A_2}$
    *   $y_{III,B_2}$

All decision variables must be non-negative: $x_{ij}, y_{ij} \ge 0$.

**Objective Function Formulation:**

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Maximize } Z = & \sum_{i,j} (P_i - R_i)x_{ij} \quad \text{(Revenue - Raw Material, based on total units)} \\
& - \sum_{j \in A} C_j^A \sum_{i} T_{i,j}^{A} x_{ij} \quad \text{(Stage A Processing Cost)} \\
& - \sum_{j \in B} C_j^B \sum_{i} T_{i,j}^{B} y_{ij} \quad \text{(Stage B Processing Cost)}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since Revenue and Raw Material depend on the total quantity of each product, and Flow Balance implies $x_{i, \text{total}} = y_{i, \text{total}}$, we can simplify by grouping terms by product and machine.

Let $Q_i$ be the total production of product $i$.
$Q_I = x_{I,A_1} + x_{I,A_2} = y_{I,B_1} + y_{I,B_2} + y_{I,B_3}$
$Q_{II} = x_{II,A_1} + x_{II,A_2} = y_{II,B_1}$
$Q_{III} = x_{III,A_2} = y_{III,B_2}$

We can substitute the marginal profit per unit processed on specific machines.

**Contribution Margin per unit of Product I processed on $A_j$ then $B_k$:**
This is complex because the A and B assignments are coupled by the flow balance. It is easier to write the objective as:
$Z = \sum_i Q_i (P_i - R_i) - \sum_{j \in A} \sum_i (T_{i,j}^A \cdot C_j^A) x_{ij} - \sum_{k \in B} \sum_i (T_{i,k}^B \cdot C_k^B) y_{ik}$

Expanding this with the specific valid variables:

**Revenue - Raw Material Terms:**
*   Product I: $(1.25 - 0.25)(x_{I,A_1} + x_{I,A_2}) = 1.00(x_{I,A_1} + x_{I,A_2})$

## 4. Operational Constraints

### A. Machine Capacity Constraints
The total processing hours required on each machine must not exceed the available hours.

**Stage A:**
1.  **Machine $A_1$ Capacity (6,000 hours):**
    $$5x_{I,A_1} + 10x_{II,A_1} \le 6,000$$
2.  **Machine $A_2$ Capacity (10,000 hours):**
    $$7x_{I,A_2} + 9x_{II,A_2} + 12x_{III,A_2} \le 10,000$$

**Stage B:**
3.  **Machine $B_1$ Capacity (4,000 hours):**
    $$6y_{I,B_1} + 8y_{II,B_1} \le 4,000$$
4.  **Machine $B_2$ Capacity (7,000 hours):**
    $$4y_{I,B_2} + 11y_{III,B_2} \le 7,000$$
5.  **Machine $B_3$ Capacity (4,000 hours):**
    $$7y_{I,B_3} \le 4,000$$

### B. Flow Balance Constraints
The quantity of each product processed in Stage A must equal the quantity processed in Stage B.

6.  **Product I Balance:**
    $$x_{I,A_1} + x_{I,A_2} = y_{I,B_1} + y_{I,B_2} + y_{I,B_3}$$
7.  **Product II Balance:**
    $$x_{II,A_1} + x_{II,A_2} = y_{II,B_1}$$
8.  **Product III Balance:**
    $$x_{III,A_2} = y_{III,B_2}$$

### C. Non-negativity Constraints
$$x_{ij} \ge 0, \quad y_{ij} \ge 0 \quad \text{for all valid } i, j$$
