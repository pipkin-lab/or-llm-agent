# Flight Engine Maintenance Optimization Model

## 1. Decision Variables

Let $t$ denote the time period index, where $t \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$ represents the six half-year periods over the next three years.

We define the following decision variables:

*   $x_t$: The number of **new engines** purchased and installed in period $t$.
*   $f_t$: The number of **used engines sent for fast repair** in period $t$.
    *   *Constraint Logic:* These engines are delivered and available for installation in period $t+1$.
*   $s_t$: The number of **used engines sent for slow repair** in period $t$.
    *   *Constraint Logic:* These engines are delivered and available for installation in period $t+2$.

**Note on Inventory of Used Engines:**
To ensure we do not repair more engines than are actually available from previous replacements, we implicitly track the inventory of used engines. However, since the problem states engines are compulsorily replaced and overhauled, and we are deciding *how* to meet the demand (new vs. repaired), the "supply" of used engines for repair is exactly the number of engines that were taken off the planes in previous periods.

Let $D_t$ be the demand for engine replacements in period $t$:
$D = [100, 70, 80, 120, 150, 140]$

The total number of engines removed from the fleet in period $t$ is $D_t$. These removed engines become the pool of "used engines" available for repair.
*   Engines removed in period $t$ can be sent for fast repair (to be used in $t+1$) or slow repair (to be used in $t+2$).
*   Therefore, the total engines sent for repair in period $t$ ($f_t + s_t$) cannot exceed the number of engines removed in period $t$ ($D_t$).

## 3. Operational Constraints

### A. Demand Satisfaction Constraints
In each period $t$, the total number of engines installed (new + repaired from previous periods) must exactly meet the demand $D_t$.

*   **Period 1:** Only new engines are available (no prior used engines to repair).
    $$ x_1 = D_1 = 100 $$
*   **Period 2:** New engines + Fast repairs from Period 1.
    $$ x_2 + f_1 = D_2 = 70 $$
*   **Period 3:** New engines + Fast repairs from Period 2 + Slow repairs from Period 1.
    $$ x_3 + f_2 + s_1 = D_3 = 80 $$
*   **Period 4:** New engines + Fast repairs from Period 3 + Slow repairs from Period 2.
    $$ x_4 + f_3 + s_2 = D_4 = 120 $$
*   **Period 5:** New engines + Fast repairs from Period 4 + Slow repairs from Period 3.
    $$ x_5 + f_4 + s_3 = D_5 = 150 $$
*   **Period 6:** New engines + Fast repairs from Period 5 + Slow repairs from Period 4.
    $$ x_6 + f_5 + s_4 = D_6 = 140 $$

### B. Availability of Used Engines (Repair Source Constraints)
The number of engines sent for repair in any period $t$ cannot exceed the number of engines removed from the aircraft in that same period $t$ (since those are the only ones available to send out).

*   **Period 1:**
    $$ f_1 + s_1 \le D_1 = 100 $$
*   **Period 2:**
    $$ f_2 + s_2 \le D_2 = 70 $$
*   **Period 3:**
    $$ f_3 + s_3 \le D_3 = 80 $$
*   **Period 4:**
    $$ f_4 + s_4 \le D_4 = 120 $$
*   **Period 5:**
    $$ f_5 + s_5 \le D_5 = 150 $$
    *(Note: Engines sent for repair in period 6, $f_6, s_6$, would be delivered after the 3-year horizon. Since the aircrafts are decommissioned and engines scrapped after 3 years, there is no need to send engines for repair in period 6 for future use. Thus, $f_6 = 0$ and $s_6 = 0$ is implied, or we simply do not define them as active variables for cost minimization within the horizon. We only define $f_t, s_t$ for $t=1..5$ for fast, and $t=1..4$ for slow, as they must arrive by period 6.)*

    *Correction on indices for repair decisions:*
    *   $f_t$ arrives in $t+1$. For it to be useful, $t+1 \le 6 \Rightarrow t \le 5$.
    *   $s_t$ arrives in $t+2$. For it to be useful, $t+2 \le 6 \Rightarrow t \le 4$.
    *   Therefore, we do not decide $f_6$ or $s_5, s_6$ because they won't be ready in time.
    *   The availability constraint applies to the engines *removed* in period $t$. Even if we don't send them for repair (because they won't be ready in time), they are just scrapped. The constraint is simply that we cannot send *more* for repair than we have available.

    So, the constraints are:
    $$ f_1 + s_1 \le 100 $$
    $$ f_2 + s_2 \le 70 $$
    $$ f_3 + s_3 \le 80 $$
    $$ f_4 + s_4 \le 120 $$
    $$ f_5 \le 150 \quad (\text{since } s_5 \text{ is not defined/used}) $$

### C. Non-negativity and Integrality
All decision variables must be non-negative integers.
$$ x_t, f_t, s_t \ge 0, \quad \text{and integer} \quad \text{for all valid } t. $$
