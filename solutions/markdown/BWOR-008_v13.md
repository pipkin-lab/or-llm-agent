**Model Type:** MILP (Mixed Integer Linear Programming)

        *   $T_{C,A} = 2$ (Route 2 end to Route 3 start)

### 2. Decision Variables

Let $x_{r,s}$ be a binary variable indicating the sequence of routes for a single ship's cycle.
$$ x_{r,s} = \begin{cases} 
1 & \text{if the ship performs route } s \text{ immediately after completing route } r \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases} $$
Domain: $x_{r,s} \in \{0, 1\}$ for all $r, s \in R$, $r \neq s$.

Note: Since all routes must be served, a single "fleet cycle" in this context refers to the sequence of routes a ship executes before returning to its initial state or simply the steady-state flow of ships. However, for fleet sizing with distinct routes and fixed frequencies, the standard formulation minimizes the total fleet size by determining how many ships are needed to cover the total workload. A more direct approach for "minimum fleet size" with distinct daily frequencies is to calculate the total ship-days required and divide by the available ship-days (which is 1 per ship per day), effectively summing the occupancy times.

Actually, the most robust formulation for "Minimum Fleet Size" with different frequencies on different routes is to treat this as a **Vehicle Routing Problem with Time Windows / Scheduling** or simply calculate the **Total Workload**.

Let's refine the variable definition based on the "Workload" interpretation which is standard for this specific type of textbook problem (often found in OR texts like "Operations Research" by Hillier/Lieberman or similar).

The problem asks for the minimum number of ships.
Let $N_{ships}$ be the integer decision variable representing the total number of ships.

However, a pure workload summation assumes ships can be perfectly synchronized or that we are just summing required ship-days.
Total Ship-Days Required = $\sum_{r \in R} (\text{Frequency}_r \times \text{Duration}_r)$.
Is this sufficient?
If Route 1 takes 19 days and happens 3 times a day, we need 57 ship-days of capacity per day? No, that's not right.

Let's look at the **Cycle Time** approach.
If a ship operates in a fixed sequence of all routes, the fleet size is determined by the maximum number of concurrent sailings.
But the frequencies are different.

Let's use the **Workload Balance** formulation which is equivalent for this specific structure (single ship type, fixed routes):
The minimum number of ships is the ceiling of the total daily workload (in ship-days) required to meet the frequency constraints.
Wait, if Route 1 requires 3 sailings/day, and each sailing takes 19 days (1 load + 17 sail + 1 unload), does it need 57 ships dedicated to that route?
Yes, if the ships are dedicated. But ships can switch routes.
If ships can switch routes, they must travel from the destination of one route to the origin of the next.

Let's formulate it as a **Circulation Problem** on a route-graph.
Nodes: Routes $1, 2, 3, 4$.
Arc $(r, s)$: Ship completes route $r$ and then performs route $s$.
Flow on arc $(r, s)$: Let $y_{r,s}$ be the number of ships per day that transition from route $r$ to route $s$.

**Decision Variables:**
*   $y_{r,s} \ge 0$: Continuous or Integer? Since frequencies are integers, flows can be fractional if we are averaging over days, but physically ships are discrete. However, for "minimum fleet size" in steady state, we often solve for the minimum total flow $Y = \sum y_{r,s}$ such that the flow conservation matches the required frequencies.
*   Actually, the standard result for this problem type is:
    **Minimize** $N = \sum_{r \in R} f_r \cdot \tau_r$ ? No.
    
    Let's look at the conservation of ships.
    Let $F_r$ be the flow of ships entering route $r$.
    We need $F_r = f_r$ (sailings per day).
    A ship performing route $r$ then goes to route $s$.
    Let $x_{r,s}$ be the fraction (or number) of ships going from $r$ to $s$.
    Flow conservation at route $r$:
    $\sum_{s \in R, s \neq r} x_{r,s} = f_r$ (Total ships leaving route $r$ equals ships completing it)
    $\sum_{s \in R, s \neq r} x_{s,r} = f_r$ (Total ships entering route $r$ equals ships starting it)
    
    The total number of ships in the system is the sum of ships currently on any route plus ships in transit between routes?
    No, the "Fleet Size" is the total number of physical ships owned.
    In a steady-state loop, the total number of ships $N$ is equal to the sum of the "ship-days" occupied by each route's service, plus the "ship-days" occupied by repositioning?
    
    Actually, there is a simpler logic often applied to these specific "shipping route" problems:
    The minimum number of ships is the **maximum** over all possible cuts in the route graph? No.
    
    Let's stick to the **Total Workload** method, which is valid if we assume ships can be scheduled to minimize deadhead or if deadhead is included in the "route" definition for capacity planning.
    
    **Correct Interpretation for Fleet Sizing with Repositioning:**
    The total number of ships required is the minimum $N$ such that a schedule exists.
    This is equivalent to finding the minimum total flow in the route-transition graph such that node demands (frequencies) are met.
    
    Let $z_{r,s}$ be the number of ships per day transitioning from route $r$ to route $s$.
    Constraints:
    1.  **Flow Conservation (Supply/Demand of Routes):**
        For each route $r$:
        $\sum_{s \neq r} z_{s,r} = f_r$ (Inflow to route $r$)
        $\sum_{s \neq r} z_{r,s} = f_r$ (Outflow from route $r$)
        
    2.  **Objective:**
        We want to minimize the total number of ships.
        The total number of ships $N$ is the total number of ships in the system at any given time.
        $N = \sum_{r} (\text{Ships on Route } r) + \sum_{r \neq s} (\text{Ships in Transit } r \to s)$.
        
        Ships on Route $r$: Since $f_r$ sailings occur per day, and each takes $\tau_r$ days, the average number of ships occupied on route $r$ is $f_r \cdot \tau_r$.
        Ships in Transit $r \to s$: The flow is $z_{r,s}$ ships/day. Each transit takes $T_{D_r, O_s}$ days (plus potentially loading/unloading? No, loading/unloading is part of the route service time $\tau_r$).
        So, ships in transit $r \to s$ = $z_{r,s} \cdot T_{D_r, O_s}$.
        
        Therefore, Total Ships $N = \sum_{r \in R} f_r \tau_r + \sum_{r \in R} \sum_{s \in R, s \neq r} z_{r,s} T_{D_r, O_s}$.
        
        Note: The term $\sum f_r \tau_r$ is constant.
        So, **Minimize** $N = C_{const} + \sum_{r \in R} \sum_{s \in R, s \neq r} z_{r,s} T_{D_r, O_s}$.
        
        Subject to:
        $\sum_{s \neq r} z_{s,r} = f_r \quad \forall r \in R$
        $z_{r,s} \ge 0 \quad \forall r, s \neq r$
        
        Since $f_r$ are integers, the optimal solution for this transportation-like problem will have integer flows $z_{r,s}$ if the transportation costs allow, but strictly speaking, $z_{r,s}$ can be continuous in the LP relaxation, but since the demand $f_r$ is integer and the network is bipartite-like (actually a directed graph), the extreme points are integer. We can declare $z_{r,s}$ as Continuous for the LP, or Integer for MILP. Given small numbers, Integer is safer for physical interpretation, but LP is often sufficient for the "minimum fleet" calculation if we round up. However, the question asks for the minimum number, implying an integer answer. The LP objective will yield the exact minimum continuous fleet size; if it's not an integer, we might need to round, but usually, these problems are constructed to have integer optimal flows. Let's use **Continuous** for $z_{r,s}$ as it's a standard Transportation Problem structure, and note that the optimal value is the lower bound. *Correction*: The problem asks for the minimum *number* of ships. If the LP yields 15.5, we need 16. But typically, the flow variables $z_{r,s}$ will be integers in the optimal solution of this specific sub-problem because the demand vector $f$ is integer. Let's declare $z_{r,s}$ as **Continuous** for the core formulation, as the "Transportation Problem" LP guarantees integer solutions for integer supplies/demands.

### 3. Objective Function

$$ \text{Minimize } Z = \underbrace{\sum_{r \in R} f_r \tau_r}_{\text{Service Time}} + \underbrace{\sum_{r \in R} \sum_{s \in R, s \neq r} z_{r,s} T_{D_r, O_s}}_{\text{Repositioning Time}} $$

Where:
*   $\tau_r = t_{load} + T_{O_r, D_r} + t_{unload} = 1 + T_{O_r, D_r} + 1$.
*   $T_{D_r, O_s}$ is the sailing time from the destination of route $r$ to the origin of route $s$.

### 4. Operational Constraints

**Flow Conservation (Demand Satisfaction):**
For each route $r \in R$:
$$ \sum_{s \in R, s \neq r} z_{s,r} = f_r $$
*(Total ships arriving at route $r$ must equal the daily frequency of route $r$)*

**Variable Bounds:**
$$ z_{r,s} \ge 0, \quad \forall r, s \in R, r \neq s $$
*(Ships cannot travel from a route to itself in this transition model, as that would be a separate cycle or handled within the frequency. Note: If a ship does the same route twice in a row, it's equivalent to $r \to r$. Should we allow $r=r$? If a ship does E->D, then immediately does E->D again, it must travel D->E. So yes, $r \to r$ transitions are possible and represent doing the same route consecutively.)*

**Revised Index Sets for Transitions:**
Allow $s = r$.
$$ \sum_{s \in R} z_{s,r} = f_r \quad \forall r \in R $$
$$ \sum_{s \in R} z_{r,s} = f_r \quad \forall r \in R $$
$$ z_{r,s} \ge 0 \quad \forall r, s \in R $$

**Objective Update:**
$$ \text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{r \in R} f_r \tau_r + \sum_{r \in R} \sum_{s \in R} z_{r,s} T_{D_r, O_s} $$
*(Note: If $s=r$, the cost is $T_{D_r, O_r}$, the return trip to the same origin).*

### Parameter Calculation for Implementation

**1. Service Durations ($\tau_r$):**
*   Route 1 (E $\to$ D): $\tau_1 = 1 + T_{E,D} + 1 = 1 + 17 + 1 = 19$ days.
*   Route 2 (B $\to$ C): $\tau_2 = 1 + T_{B,C} + 1 = 1 + 3 + 1 = 5$ days.
*   Route 3 (A $\to$ F): $\tau_3 = 1 + T_{A,F} + 1 = 1 + 7 + 1 = 9$ days.
*   Route 4 (D $\to$ B): $\tau_4 = 1 + T_{D,B} + 1 = 1 + 13 + 1 = 15$ days.

**Constant Term (Service Workload):**
$$ C_{const} = (3 \times 19) + (2 \times 5) + (1 \times 9) + (1 \times 15) $$
$$ C_{const} = 57 + 10 + 9 + 15 = 91 $$

**2. Repositioning Times ($T_{D_r, O_s}$):**
We need the sailing time from Destination of $r$ to Origin of $s$.
Origins: $O_1=E, O_2=B, O_3=A, O_4=D$.
Destinations: $D_1=D, D_2=C, D_3=F, D_4=B$.

Matrix of $T_{D_r, O_s}$:

| From \ To | $O_1=E$ | $O_2=B$ | $O_3=A$ | $O_4=D$ |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **$D_1=D$** | $T_{D,E}=17$ | $T_{D,B}=13$ | $T_{D,A}=14$ | $T_{D,D}=0$ |
| **$D_2=C$** | $T_{C,E}=5$ | $T_{C,B}=3$ | $T_{C,A}=2$ | $T_{C,D}=15$ |
| **$D_3=F$** | $T_{F,E}=3$ | $T_{F,B}=8$ | $T_{F,A}=7$ | $T_{F,D}=20$ |
| **$D_4=B$** | $T_{B,E}=8$ | $T_{B,B}=0$ | $T_{B,A}=1$ | $T_{B,D}=13$ |

**Objective to Solve:**
Minimize $Z = 91 + \sum_{r=1}^4 \sum_{s=1}^4 z_{r,s} C_{rs}$
Subject to:
$\sum_{s=1}^4 z_{s,1} = 3$ (Inflow to R1)
$\sum_{s=1}^4 z_{s,2} = 2$ (Inflow to R2)
$\sum_{s=1}^4 z_{s,3} = 1$ (Inflow to R3)
$\sum_{s=1}^4 z_{s,4} = 1$ (Inflow to R4)
$\sum_{s=1}^4 z_{1,s} = 3$ (Outflow from R1)
$\sum_{s=1}^4 z_{2,s} = 2$ (Outflow from R2)
$\sum_{s=1}^4 z_{3,s} = 1$ (Outflow from R3)
$\sum_{s=1}^4 z_{4,s} = 1$ (Outflow from R4)
$z_{r,s} \ge 0$

*(Note: Since supply = demand = 7 total sailings/day, this is a balanced transportation problem. The optimal $z_{r,s}$ will be integers.)*

### Final Answer Formulation
