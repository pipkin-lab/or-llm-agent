# Operations Research Model: University Computer Lab Staffing

## 1. Decision Variables

Let $x_{i,d}$ be a continuous variable representing the number of hours student $i$ works on day $d$.
*   $i \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$ is the index of the student.
*   $d \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ is the index of the day (1 = Monday, ..., 5 = Friday).

*Note: Since the requirement is "exactly one student on duty at all times" and shifts are continuous blocks of hours, we assume labor can be scheduled in fractional hours (e.g., 0.5 hours) to satisfy the exact coverage constraint. If shifts must be in integer hours, $x_{i,d}$ would be restricted to non-negative integers. Given the context of minimizing cost with specific hour caps, continuous relaxation is standard for such staffing formulations unless "shifts" are discrete blocks. However, looking at the "Max daily work hours" table, they are integers. Let's assume $x_{i,d} \ge 0$.*

## 2. Objective Function

Minimize the total weekly wage payment.

$$
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{i=1}^{6} \sum_{d=1}^{5} w_i \cdot x_{i,d}
$$

Where $w_i$ is the hourly wage for student $i$:
*   $w_1 = 10.0$
*   $w_2 = 10.0$
*   $w_3 = 9.9$
*   $w_4 = 9.8$
*   $w_5 = 10.8$
*   $w_6 = 11.3$

## 3. Operational Constraints

### A. Daily Coverage Constraints
The lab is open from 8:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m., which is a duration of 14 hours. Exactly one student must be on duty at all times, meaning the total hours worked by all students on any given day must sum to 14.

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{6} x_{i,d} = 14, \quad \forall d \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}
$$

### B. Maximum Daily Work Hours
The hours assigned to student $i$ on day $d$ cannot exceed the maximum allowed hours $M_{i,d}$ specified in Table 5-9.

$$
x_{i,d} \le M_{i,d}, \quad \forall i \in \{1..6\}, \forall d \in \{1..5\}
$$

Where $M_{i,d}$ values are:
*   $M_{1,1}=6, M_{1,2}=0, M_{1,3}=9, M_{1,4}=0, M_{1,5}=7$
*   $M_{2,1}=0, M_{2,2}=9, M_{2,3}=0, M_{2,4}=6, M_{2,5}=0$
*   $M_{3,1}=4, M_{3,2}=8, M_{3,3}=3, M_{3,4}=0, M_{3,5}=5$
*   $M_{4,1}=5, M_{4,2}=5, M_{4,3}=6, M_{4,4}=0, M_{4,5}=4$
*   $M_{5,1}=3, M_{5,2}=0, M_{5,3}=4, M_{5,4}=8, M_{5,5}=0$
*   $M_{6,1}=0, M_{6,2}=9, M_{6,3}=0, M_{6,4}=6, M_{6,5}=3$

*Note: If $M_{i,d} = 0$, this implies $x_{i,d} = 0$, meaning student $i$ is not scheduled on day $d$.*

### C. Weekly Minimum Work Hours
*   **Undergraduate Students ($i \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$):** Must work at least 8 hours per week.
    $$
    \sum_{d=1}^{5} x_{i,d} \ge 8, \quad \forall i \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}
    $$

*   **Graduate Students ($i \in \{5, 6\}$):** Must work at least 7 hours per week.
    $$
    \sum_{d=1}^{5} x_{i,d} \ge 7, \quad \forall i \in \{5, 6\}
    $$

### D. Maximum Days Scheduled per Week
Each student can be assigned for duty no more than twice a week. This means the sum of days where $x_{i,d} > 0$ must be $\le 2$. To linearize this, we can introduce binary variables or use a direct constraint if we assume that if a student works, they work a significant chunk, but strictly speaking, "assigned for duty" implies the day is active.

Let $y_{i,d}$ be a binary variable where $y_{i,d} = 1$ if student $i$ works on day $d$, and $0$ otherwise.
$$
x_{i,d} \le M_{i,d} y_{i,d}, \quad \forall i, d
$$
$$
\sum_{d=1}^{5} y_{i,d} \le 2, \quad \forall i \in \{1..6\}
$$

*(Alternative Interpretation without binaries: If "assigned for duty" strictly means "has non-zero hours", the constraint is $\sum_{d=1}^5 \mathbb{I}(x_{i,d}>0) \le 2$. In standard LP formulation, we must use the binary variables $y_{i,d}$ as shown above to enforce this disjunctive logic linearly.)*

### E. Maximum Students Per Day
No more than 3 students can be scheduled for duty per day. Using the binary variables $y_{i,d}$:
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{6} y_{i,d} \le 3, \quad \forall d \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}
$$

### F. Non-negativity
$$
x_{i,d} \ge 0, \quad \forall i, d
$$
$$
y_{i,d} \in \{0, 1\}, \quad \forall i, d
$$
