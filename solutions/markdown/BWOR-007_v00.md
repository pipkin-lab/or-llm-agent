# Problem Analysis: Tongxin Toy Factory Cash Flow Optimization

## 1. Decision Variables

Let $t$ denote the month index, where $t \in \{1, 2, ..., 12\}$ corresponds to January through December.

**Loan Variables:**
*   $A$: The principal amount (in thousand yuan) of the **Annual Loan** borrowed at the end of the previous year (available at the start of Month 1). This is a scalar variable.
*   $S_t$: The principal amount (in thousand yuan) of the **Short-term Monthly Loan** taken at the beginning of month $t$.
    *   $S_t \ge 0$ for all $t \in \{1, ..., 12\}$.
    *   Specifically, $S_{12}$ is the loan taken at the beginning of December, repaid at the beginning of January next year.

**Deposit Variables:**
*   $D_t$: The principal amount (in thousand yuan) invested in a **Short-term Deposit** at the beginning of month $t$.
    *   $D_t \ge 0$ for all $t \in \{1, ..., 12\}$.
    *   The deposit is made at the beginning of month $t$ and withdrawn at the end of month $t$ (equivalent to the beginning of month $t+1$).

**State Variable (Implicit):**
*   $C_t$: The net cash balance available at the **beginning** of month $t$, *after* receiving any loans and making any deposits, but *before* covering the month's operational cash flow $c_t$.
    *   Alternatively, we can define the balance at the **end** of month $t$ (which is the same as the beginning of month $t+1$) to formulate the balance constraints directly. Let $B_t$ be the cash balance at the **end** of month $t$.

## 2. Objective Function

**Maximize** the total cash available at the beginning of next year (end of Month 12).

The final cash balance at the end of Month 12, denoted as $B_{12}$, is the objective.

$$ \text{Maximize } Z = B_{12} $$

*Note: Since the goal is to maximize the final cash, and there are no explicit costs for holding cash other than the opportunity cost (which is handled by the deposit/loan interest rates in the balance equation), maximizing the final balance naturally accounts for minimizing unnecessary borrowing and maximizing effective deposits.*

## 3. Operational Constraints

### A. Cash Balance Constraints (Month-by-Month)

The cash balance evolves month by month. Let $c_t$ be the net cash flow for month $t$ (given in the table). A negative $c_t$ represents a cash outflow (need for funds), and a positive $c_t$ represents a cash inflow (excess funds).

The balance at the **beginning** of month $t$ (before operations) is equal to the balance at the **end** of month $t-1$.
*   For $t=1$, the beginning balance is determined by the Annual Loan $A$ and any initial cash (assumed 0, or incorporated into $A$). Let's assume the factory starts Month 1 with the Annual Loan $A$ and no other cash.

**General Balance Equation for Month $t$ ($1 \le t \le 11$):**

The cash available at the **beginning** of month $t$ comes from:
1.  The ending balance of the previous month $B_{t-1}$ (for $t>1$).
2.  The Short-term Loan $S_t$ received at the beginning of month $t$.
3.  The Annual Loan $A$ (only available at the start, so it affects $B_0$ or the start of $B_1$).

From this beginning balance, the factory:
1.  Pays off the previous month's Short-term Loan $S_{t-1}$ plus interest ($1.5\%$).
2.  Makes a Deposit $D_t$ (cash outflow for the deposit principal).
3.  Covers the month's cash flow requirement $c_t$.

The remaining cash becomes the **ending** balance $B_t$.

Let's refine the timing to be precise:
*   **Start of Month $t$**:
    *   Cash on hand from previous month end: $B_{t-1}$ (where $B_0$ is the initial cash, which includes the Annual Loan $A$).
    *   Receive Short-term Loan: $+ S_t$.
    *   **Total Cash Available**: $B_{t-1} + S_t + \text{Interest from Deposits?}$ No, deposits are made *at* the beginning.
    *   **Actions at Beginning**:
        *   Repay previous short-term loan: $- S_{t-1}(1 + 0.015)$. (For $t=1$, $S_0=0$).
        *   Make deposit: $- D_t$.
    *   **Cash Remaining for Operations**: $B_{t-1} + S_t - S_{t-1}(1.015) - D_t$.
    *   **During Month $t$**:
        *   Cash flow $c_t$ occurs. If $c_t < 0$, cash is used. If $c_t > 0$, cash is added.
    *   **End of Month $t$**:
        *   The deposit $D_t$ matures and is withdrawn with interest: $+ D_t(1 + 0.004)$.
        *   The remaining cash becomes $B_t$.

Therefore, the balance equation for month $t$ is:
$$ B_t = B_{t-1} + S_t - S_{t-1}(1.015) - D_t + c_t + D_t(1.004) $$
Simplifying:
$$ B_t = B_{t-1} + S_t - 1.015 S_{t-1} + c_t + 0.004 D_t $$

**Initial Condition (Start of Month 1):**
*   $B_0$: Initial cash. The problem states the Annual Loan is borrowed at the end of the previous year. Let's assume $B_0 = A$.
*   $S_0 = 0$ (no previous short-term loan).
*   Constraint for Month 1:
    $$ B_1 = A + S_1 - 1.015(0) + c_1 + 0.004 D_1 $$
    $$ B_1 = A + S_1 + c_1 + 0.004 D_1 $$

**Constraints for Months $t = 2$ to $11$:**
$$ B_t = B_{t-1} + S_t - 1.015 S_{t-1} + c_t + 0.004 D_t $$

**Constraint for Month 12:**
At the beginning of Month 12, the factory takes loan $S_{12}$ and makes deposit $D_{12}$. It must also repay the loan $S_{11}$ from the previous month.
$$ B_{12} = B_{11} + S_{12} - 1.015 S_{11} + c_{12} + 0.004 D_{12} $$

**Annual Loan Repayment Constraint:**
The Annual Loan $A$ is repaid at the **end of January next year** (which is effectively the end of the planning horizon, after Month 12). The problem states: "repay the principal plus the final interest payment at the end of January next year".
*   Interest on Annual Loan: "Starting from the end of February, the factory must pay 1% interest each month".
    *   Let's trace the interest payments on $A$:
        *   End of Feb (End of Month 2): Pay $0.01 A$.
        *   End of Mar (End of Month 3): Pay $0.01 A$.
        *   ...
        *   End of Dec (End of Month 12): Pay $0.01 A$.
        *   End of Jan Next Year (After Month 12): Pay Principal $A$ + Final Interest $0.01 A$.
    *   Wait, the problem says "Starting from the end of February... pay 1% interest each month... and then repay the principal plus the final interest payment at the end of January next year".
    *   This implies interest is paid monthly at the end of each month from Month 2 to Month 12, and the final interest is paid at the end of Month 1 (next year).
    *   Let's adjust the balance equations to include these interest payments for the Annual Loan.

**Revised Balance Equations including Annual Loan Interest:**

Let $I_A$ be the monthly interest on the Annual Loan. $I_A = 0.01 A$.

*   **Month 1 (Jan):**
    *   No interest payment on Annual Loan is due *during* or *at the end of* Jan? "Starting from the end of February". So, no Annual Loan interest payment in Month 1.
    *   $B_1 = A + S_1 + c_1 + 0.004 D_1$
    *   (Note: $S_0=0$)

*   **Month 2 (Feb):**
    *   Pay Annual Loan Interest: $- 0.01 A$.
    *   $B_2 = B_1 + S_2 - 1.015 S_1 + c_2 + 0.004 D_2 - 0.01 A$

*   **Month $t$ (for $3 \le t \le 12$):**
    *   Pay Annual Loan Interest: $- 0.01 A$.
    *   $B_t = B_{t-1} + S_t - 1.015 S_{t-1} + c_t + 0.004 D_t - 0.01 A$

*   **End of Year / Beginning of Next Year:**
    *   The objective is the cash available at the **beginning** of next year.
    *   The Annual Loan principal $A$ and the **final** interest payment (for Jan next year) are due at the end of January next year.
    *   Does "cash available at the beginning of next year" mean *before* or *after* the Annual Loan repayment?
    *   Usually, "available" means net of immediate obligations. The obligation "at the end of January next year" happens *after* the beginning of January next year.
    *   However, the problem asks to "maximize the total amount of cash... available at the beginning of next year".
    *   If the repayment is at the *end* of January, it is not an obligation at the *beginning* of January.
    *   BUT, the factory must have enough cash to pay it. If we maximize cash at the *beginning* of Jan next year, we haven't paid the principal yet.
    *   Let's re-read carefully: "repay the principal plus the final interest payment at the end of January next year".
    *   The planning horizon is "throughout the year" (Month 1 to 12).
    *   The cash balance $B_{12}$ is the cash at the **end** of Month 12 (Dec).
    *   The **beginning** of next year (Jan) is immediately after the end of Month 12.
    *   So, Cash at Beginning of Next Year = $B_{12}$.
    *   The Annual Loan repayment happens later (End of Jan Next Year). So $B_{12}$ does *not* subtract the principal $A$ or the final interest.
    *   However, the factory must ensure it *can* pay this later. Since we are maximizing $B_{12}$, and the repayment is a fixed future liability, the constraint is simply that the factory survives the year. The "maximize cash at beginning of next year" effectively means maximizing the net wealth at that point, ignoring the future liability because the liability is fixed and the objective is the *available* cash *at that specific time*.
    *   *Correction*: If the goal is to maximize cash *available*, and a large debt is coming due, one might argue the "available" cash is net of that debt. However, standard interpretation of "cash available at time T" is the balance on hand at time T. The obligation at T+1 month is a separate constraint (non-negativity of cash after repayment).
    *   Let's assume the objective is simply $B_{12}$. The model must ensure that after $B_{12}$, the factory can pay $A + 0.01A$.
    *   Constraint: $B_{12} \ge A + 0.01 A$? No, the cash is "available" at the beginning. The repayment is at the end of the month. So the cash is available, but will be used. The problem asks to maximize the amount *available*. This is slightly ambiguous. Usually, in such OR problems, "maximize cash available" means maximize the ending balance of the planning period. The planning period is the 12 months. The end of the period is End of Month 12.
    *   Let's stick to $Z = B_{12}$.

### B. Non-Negativity Constraints

1.  **Loan Principal:**
    $$ A \ge 0 $$
    $$ S_t \ge 0 \quad \forall t \in \{1, ..., 12\} $$

2.  **Deposit Principal:**
    $$ D_t \ge 0 \quad \forall t \in \{1, ..., 12\} $$

3.  **Cash Balance Non-Negativity (Solvency):**
    The factory cannot have negative cash balance at the **end** of any month.
    $$ B_t \ge 0 \quad \forall t \in \{1, ..., 12\} $$
    *(Note: It is assumed that cash flows $c_t$ and loan/deposit transactions are such that intramonth shortfalls are covered by the loans taken at the beginning of the month. The constraint $B_t \ge 0$ ensures that after all transactions, the factory remains solvent.)*

4.  **Final Solvency (Implicit):**
    Although the objective is $B_{12}$, the factory must be able to pay the Annual Loan at the end of next January.
    $$ B_{12} + \text{Cash Flow from Jan Next Year?} \ge 1.01 A $$
    Since the problem ends at the beginning of next year, we typically only constrain the planning horizon. However, to be rigorous, the "available cash" might be interpreted as net of the immediate upcoming liability if it were due immediately. Given the "end of January" timing, it is not immediate. We will assume the standard end-of-horizon balance is the objective.

### Summary of Constraints for Formulation

1.  **Balance Month 1:**
    $$ B_1 = A + S_1 + c_1 + 0.004 D_1 $$
2.  **Balance Month 2:**
    $$ B_2 = B_1 + S_2 - 1.015 S_1 + c_2 + 0.004 D_2 - 0.01 A $$
3.  **Balance Months 3-12:**
    $$ B_t = B_{t-1} + S_t - 1.015 S_{t-1} + c_t + 0.004 D_t - 0.01 A, \quad t=3,...,12 $$
4.  **Solvency:**
    $$ B_t \ge 0, \quad t=1,...,12 $$
5.  **Variable Bounds:**
    $$ A \ge 0, \quad S_t \ge 0, \quad D_t \ge 0 $$
6.  **Objective:**
    $$ \text{Maximize } B_{12} $$
