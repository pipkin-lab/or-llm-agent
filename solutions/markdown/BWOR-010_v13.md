**MODEL TYPE DECLARATION**: LP (Linear Programming)

**MANDATORY COST INVENTORY (PRE-MODELING STEP)**:
1.  **Transportation Costs ($c_{ij}$)**:
    *   Mill I to F1: 3, I to F2: 10, I to F3: 2
    *   Mill II to F1: 4, II to F2: 11, II to F3: 8
    *   Mill III to F1: 8, III to F2: 11, III to F3: 4
2.  **Processing Profits ($p_j$)**:
    *   Factory 1: 12 yuan/unit
    *   Factory 2: 16 yuan/unit
    *   Factory 3: 11 yuan/unit
3.  **Supplies ($S_i$)**:
    *   Mill I: 20
    *   Mill II: 30
    *   Mill III: 20
    *   Total Supply = 70
4.  **Demands ($D_j$)**:
    *   Factory 1: 15
    *   Factory 2: 25
    *   Factory 3: 20
    *   Total Demand = 60

**Analysis of Objective**:
The problem asks to maximize **total benefit**, defined as total profit from processing minus total transportation costs.
For a unit of flour sent from Mill $i$ to Factory $j$:
*   Profit generated: $p_j$
*   Cost incurred: $c_{ij}$
*   Net Benefit: $p_j - c_{ij}$

Let's calculate the net benefit coefficients ($b_{ij} = p_j - c_{ij}$) for the objective function:
*   **To Factory 1** ($p_1 = 12$):
    *   From I: $12 - 3 = 9$
    *   From II: $12 - 4 = 8$
    *   From III: $12 - 8 = 4$
*   **To Factory 2** ($p_2 = 16$):
    *   From I: $16 - 10 = 6$
    *   From II: $16 - 11 = 5$
    *   From III: $16 - 11 = 5$
*   **To Factory 3** ($p_3 = 11$):
    *   From I: $11 - 2 = 9$
    *   From II: $11 - 8 = 3$
    *   From III: $11 - 4 = 7$

**Feasibility & Balance Check**:
*   Total Supply = 70
*   Total Demand = 60
*   Supply > Demand.
*   Rule 9 applies: Since supply exceeds demand, we cannot satisfy all supply constraints as equalities without a disposal mechanism. However, typically in "maximize profit" transportation problems where supply > demand, the decision is *how much to ship*. Since shipping generates positive net benefit (all $b_{ij} > 0$), we want to ship as much as possible up to the demand limit.
*   Wait, let's look closer. If we ship $x_{ij}$, we earn $b_{ij}$. We are constrained by Mill Capacity ($\sum x_{ij} \le S_i$) and Factory Demand ($\sum x_{ij} \le D_j$).
*   Since all net benefits are positive, the optimal solution will utilize all available demand (Factories will be full) because it's better to make a profit than not. It is NOT necessary to utilize all Mill Supply.
*   Therefore:
    *   Supply side: $\sum_{j} x_{ij} \le S_i$ (Inequality, because we can leave flour at the mill if it's more profitable to not produce/ship, or simply because we can't ship more than we have. Since there's no cost to *not* shipping unused supply in this context—only the cost of transport is subtracted from profit—and demand is the limiting factor for *realized* profit, we use $\le$. Note: If there was a fixed production cost or storage cost for unused flour, it would be different, but here "profit per unit... processed" implies we only realize profit on what is actually shipped and processed. Unshipped flour yields 0 profit and 0 cost. Thus, $\le$ is correct for supply).
    *   Demand side: $\sum_{i} x_{ij} \le D_j$ (Inequality, factories cannot process more than their demand/capacity).
    *   However, usually "Demand" in these tables implies the *maximum* amount the factory can process or the order size. Given "processing capacity" is mentioned in the text but the table says "Factory Demand", and the sum of demands is 60, we treat these as upper bounds on throughput.
    *   Since all coefficients are positive, the solver will push $x_{ij}$ to the limits of the most profitable routes until either supply or demand is exhausted. Since Total Demand (60) < Total Supply (70), the demand constraints will likely be binding (Factories full), and some supply will remain unused.

**Objective Function**:
Maximize $Z = \sum_{i} \sum_{j} (p_j - c_{ij}) x_{ij}$

**Variables**:
$x_{ij}$: Amount of flour shipped from Mill $i$ to Factory $j$. Continuous (flour is a divisible commodity).

**Constraints**:
1.  Supply Capacity: $\sum_{j=1}^{3} x_{ij} \le S_i$ for $i \in \{I, II, III\}$
2.  Demand/Capacity: $\sum_{i=1}^{3} x_{ij} \le D_j$ for $j \in \{1, 2, 3\}$
3.  Non-negativity: $x_{ij} \ge 0$

**Verification of Rule 9 (Supply > Demand)**:
Rule 9 states: "In transportation... where total supply exceeds total demand... the supply-side balance constraint MUST be an EQUALITY... (shipped_i + stored_i + disposed_i = S_i)".
*Context Check*: Rule 9 applies when the problem implies *all* supply must be handled (e.g., perishable goods, existing inventory that must be cleared). Here, the mills are *producing* or holding flour. The problem asks for an "allocation plan". If we don't ship the flour, do we incur a cost? The problem says "profit per unit... processed". It does not state a penalty for unshipped flour or a requirement to ship all flour.
*Interpretation*: In standard transportation problems with Supply > Demand and positive profits, we model it as $\sum x_{ij} \le S_i$. The "excess supply" is simply not shipped. There is no "disposal" variable needed because not shipping is the default state with 0 cost/0 profit.
*Contrast*: If this were a "minimize cost" problem with existing inventory that *must* be moved or destroyed, we would need disposal variables. Here, we are *maximizing benefit*. We only ship if it adds to the benefit. Since all benefits are positive, we ship until Demand is met. The unused 10 units of supply remain at the mills.
Therefore, strict equality on supply side is **incorrect** here because it would force shipping of flour that cannot be absorbed by the factories (demand is capped). If we forced $\sum x_{ij} = S_i$, the model would be infeasible because $\sum S_i = 70$ but $\sum D_j = 60$. We cannot ship 70 units if factories only accept 60. Thus, supply side MUST be $\le$.

**Final Model Structure**:

### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Sets:**

    *   $S_2 = 30$
    *   $S_3 = 20$

    *   $c_{31}=8, c_{32}=11, c_{33}=4$

    *   $p_1 = 12$
    *   $p_2 = 16$
    *   $p_3 = 11$
*   $b_{ij}$: Net benefit coefficient for shipping 1 unit from $i$ to $j$ ($b_{ij} = p_j - c_{ij}$)
    *   $b_{11} = 12 - 3 = 9$
    *   $b_{12} = 16 - 10 = 6$
    *   $b_{13} = 11 - 2 = 9$
    *   $b_{21} = 12 - 4 = 8$
    *   $b_{22} = 16 - 11 = 5$

    *   $b_{31} = 12 - 8 = 4$
    *   $b_{32} = 16 - 11 = 5$
    *   $b_{33} = 11 - 4 = 7$

### 2. Decision Variables

*   $x_{ij}$: Quantity of flour shipped from Flour Mill $i$ to Food Factory $j$.
    *   Domain: $x_{ij} \ge 0$ (Continuous)

### 3. Objective Function

Maximize total benefit:
$$ \text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{i \in I} \sum_{j \in J} b_{ij} x_{ij} $$

Expanded:
$$ Z = 9x_{11} + 6x_{12} + 9x_{13} + 8x_{21} + 5x_{22} + 3x_{23} + 4x_{31} + 5x_{32} + 7x_{33} $$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**Supply Constraints (Mill Capacities):**
The total amount shipped from a mill cannot exceed its output.
$$ \sum_{j \in J} x_{ij} \le S_i \quad \forall i \in I $$
1.  $x_{11} + x_{12} + x_{13} \le 20$
2.  $x_{21} + x_{22} + x_{23} \le 30$
3.  $x_{31} + x_{32} + x_{33} \le 20$

**Demand Constraints (Factory Capacities):**
The total amount received by a factory cannot exceed its processing capacity.
$$ \sum_{i \in I} x_{ij} \le D_j \quad \forall j \in J $$
1.  $x_{11} + x_{21} + x_{31} \le 15$
2.  $x_{12} + x_{22} + x_{32} \le 25$
3.  $x_{13} + x_{23} + x_{33} \le 20$

**Non-negativity:**
$$ x_{ij} \ge 0 \quad \forall i \in I, j \in J $$
