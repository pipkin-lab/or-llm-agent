**MODEL TYPE:** LP (Linear Programming)

### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
*   $I = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of supply origins (I, II, III).
*   $J = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$: Set of demand destinations (A, B, C, D, E).

**Parameters:**

**Supply Capacities ($S_i$):**
*   $S_1 = 50$
*   $S_2 = 100$
*   $S_3 = 150$
*   *Total Supply* $= 50 + 100 + 150 = 300$

**Demand Requirements ($D_j$):**
*   $D_1 = 25$ (Destination A)
*   $D_2 = 115$ (Destination B)
*   $D_3 = 60$ (Destination C)
*   $D_4 = 30$ (Destination D)
*   $D_5 = 70$ (Destination E)
*   *Total Demand* $= 25 + 115 + 60 + 30 + 70 = 300$

**Unit Transportation Costs ($c_{ij}$):**
The cost matrix $C$ is defined as:
$$
C = \begin{bmatrix}
10 & 15 & 20 & 20 & 40 \\
20 & 10 & 15 & 30 & 30 \\
30 & 35 & 40 & 55 & 25
\end{bmatrix}
$$
Where row $i$ corresponds to origin $i \in I$ and column $j$ corresponds to destination $j \in J$.

### 2. Decision Variables

Let $x_{ij}$ be the quantity of goods shipped from origin $i$ to destination $j$.
*   **Domain:** $x_{ij} \ge 0$ (Continuous) for all $i \in I, j \in J$.
    *   *Note:* Since supply and demand are integers and the cost structure is linear, the transportation problem is guaranteed to have integer optimal solutions at the vertices. Therefore, continuous variables are sufficient and standard for this LP formulation.

### 3. Objective Function

Minimize the total transportation cost:
$$
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{i \in I} \sum_{j \in J} c_{ij} x_{ij}
$$

Explicitly:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Minimize } Z = \;& 10x_{11} + 15x_{12} + 20x_{13} + 20x_{14} + 40x_{15} \\
+ & 20x_{21} + 10x_{22} + 15x_{23} + 30x_{24} + 30x_{25} \\
+ & 30x_{31} + 35x_{32} + 40x_{33} + 55x_{34} + 25x_{35}
\end{aligned}
$$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**Supply Constraints:**
The total quantity shipped from each origin must equal its supply capacity (since Total Supply = Total Demand, these are equalities).
$$
\sum_{j \in J} x_{ij} = S_i \quad \forall i \in I
$$
*   $x_{11} + x_{12} + x_{13} + x_{14} + x_{15} = 50$
*   $x_{21} + x_{22} + x_{23} + x_{24} + x_{25} = 100$
*   $x_{31} + x_{32} + x_{33} + x_{34} + x_{35} = 150$

**Demand Constraints:**
The total quantity received at each destination must equal its demand requirement.
$$
\sum_{i \in I} x_{ij} = D_j \quad \forall j \in J
$$
*   $x_{11} + x_{21} + x_{31} = 25$
*   $x_{12} + x_{22} + x_{32} = 115$
*   $x_{13} + x_{23} + x_{33} = 60$
*   $x_{14} + x_{24} + x_{34} = 30$
*   $x_{15} + x_{25} + x_{35} = 70$

**Non-negativity Constraints:**
$$
x_{ij} \ge 0 \quad \forall i \in I, j \in J
$$
