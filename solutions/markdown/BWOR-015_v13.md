To formulate this problem, we must first identify the nature of the objectives. The problem specifies three **prioritized objectives**, which indicates a **Goal Programming (GP)** model. Since the decision variables (production quantities) are continuous (quantities of goods) and all relationships are linear, this is a **Linear Goal Programming (L-GP)** model.

### 1. MANDATORY COST INVENTORY & PARAMETER EXTRACTION

**Decision Entities:**
- Products: $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$
- Stages (Periods): $t \in \{1, 2, 3\}$

**Parameters from Table 4-16 (Demand $D_{i,t}$):**
- $D_{1,1}=500, D_{1,2}=680, D_{1,3}=800$
- $D_{2,1}=750, D_{2,2}=800, D_{2,3}=950$
- $D_{3,1}=900, D_{3,2}=800, D_{3,3}=1000$

**Parameters from Table 4-17 (Resource Consumption $a_{i,t}$ and Limits $R_t$):**
- Equipment Hours ($a^{eq}_{i}$): $a^{eq}_1=2.0, a^{eq}_2=1.0, a^{eq}_3=3.1$. Limit $R^{eq}_t = 5000$ for all $t$.
- Working Capital ($a^{cap}_{i}$): $a^{cap}_1=40, a^{cap}_2=20, a^{cap}_3=55$. Limit $R^{cap}_t = 93000$ for all $t$.
*Note: The table implies these consumption rates are constant across stages for each product type, which is standard unless specified otherwise.*

**Goal Priorities & Weights:**
1.  **Priority 1 ($P_1$):** Minimize stockouts (underproduction).
    -   Weights: Product 3 is twice as important as Products 1 and 2.
    -   Let $w_{i}$ be the weight for product $i$ in stockout minimization.
    -   $w_1 = 1, w_2 = 1, w_3 = 2$.
2.  **Priority 2 ($P_2$):** Minimize overloading of processing equipment.
    -   This refers to exceeding the 5000 hours limit.
3.  **Priority 3 ($P_3$):** Ensure working capital usage does not exceed the limit.
    -   This refers to exceeding the 93,000 yuan limit.

**Inventory Constraints:**
-   Initial inventory $I_{i,0} = 0$.
-   Final inventory $I_{i,3} = 0$.
-   Inventory balance: $I_{i,t} = I_{i,t-1} + x_{i,t} - D_{i,t}$, where $x_{i,t}$ is production.
-   Since final inventory must be 0, total production over 3 stages must equal total demand, *unless* stockouts allow demand to go unmet. In Goal Programming for "timely delivery," "stockout" usually means $x_{i,t} < D_{i,t}$ in a specific period, leading to a shortage $n_{i,t}$. However, the problem states "initial and final inventories are zero." This implies a cumulative balance or strict period-by-period matching?
    -   *Interpretation:* "Timely delivery" usually implies meeting demand in the specific period. If we produce less than demand in period $t$, that is a stockout for period $t$. The condition "final inventory is zero" combined with "initial inventory is zero" implies $\sum_{t=1}^3 x_{i,t} = \sum_{t=1}^3 D_{i,t}$ if there are no permanent losses. However, Goal Programming allows for under-achievement.
    -   Standard formulation for "minimizing stockouts" with inventory constraints:
        Let $x_{i,t}$ be production.
        Let $I_{i,t}$ be ending inventory.
        $I_{i,t} = I_{i,t-1} + x_{i,t} - D_{i,t} - s_{i,t} + e_{i,t}$? No, usually stockout is modeled as the portion of demand not met.
        Let's define the stockout variable $n_{i,t}$ (negative deviation) for each product $i$ at each stage $t$.
        The constraint for timely delivery is: $x_{i,t} + I_{i,t-1} - D_{i,t} + n_{i,t} - p_{i,t} = 0$?
        Actually, simpler: The goal is to meet demand. The "stockout" is the amount by which production (plus available inventory) falls short of demand.
        Given "initial and final inventories are zero," and assuming no backlogging is desired (just minimizing it), we can model the flow.
        Let's use the standard GP formulation for production planning:
        Balance Equation: $x_{i,t} + I_{i,t-1} + n_{i,t} - p_{i,t} = D_{i,t} + I_{i,t}$
        Where:
        - $n_{i,t}$: Under-achievement (Stockout) at time $t$ for product $i$. We want to minimize this.
        - $p_{i,t}$: Over-achievement (Excess) at time $t$ for product $i$. We likely don't penalize this heavily in P1, but it contributes to inventory.
        - $I_{i,t}$: Inventory at end of period $t$.
        - $I_{i,0} = 0, I_{i,3} = 0$.
        
        However, often in these textbook problems, "stockout" is simply $D_{i,t} - x_{i,t}$ if we assume immediate production meets demand and inventory handles the buffer. But since inventory is a state variable, we must include it.
        
        Wait, look at the priorities again.
        1. Minimize stockouts.
        2. Minimize equipment overloading.
        3. Minimize capital overloading.
        
        The constraints for 2 and 3 are "limits". Overloading means the actual usage exceeds the limit.
        Equipment Usage $E_t = \sum_i a^{eq}_i x_{i,t}$. Goal: $E_t \le 5000$. Deviation $d^+_t$ (overload) is minimized in P2.
        Capital Usage $C_t = \sum_i a^{cap}_i x_{i,t}$. Goal: $C_t \le 93000$. Deviation $c^+_t$ (overload) is minimized in P3.

### 2. MODEL FORMULATION

**Model Type:** Linear Goal Programming (MILP not strictly required as quantities can be continuous, but if "units" are discrete, they are integers. Usually, production quantities like 500+ are treated as continuous in LP/GP unless specified "integer units". Given the magnitudes, Continuous is standard).

**Index Sets:**
- $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ : Products
- $t \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ : Stages (Periods)

**Parameters:**
- $D_{i,t}$ : Demand for product $i$ in stage $t$.
- $a^{eq}_i$ : Equipment hours per unit of product $i$.
- $a^{cap}_i$ : Working capital per unit of product $i$.
- $R^{eq} = 5000$ : Equipment limit per stage.
- $R^{cap} = 93000$ : Capital limit per stage.
- $w_i$ : Weight for stockout of product $i$ in P1. $w_1=1, w_2=1, w_3=2$.

**Decision Variables:**
- $x_{i,t} \ge 0$ : Quantity of product $i$ produced in stage $t$.
- $I_{i,t} \ge 0$ : Inventory of product $i$ at the end of stage $t$.
- $n_{i,t} \ge 0$ : Stockout (under-fulfillment) of demand for product $i$ in stage $t$.
- $p_{i,t} \ge 0$ : Excess production (over-fulfillment) of demand for product $i$ in stage $t$.
- $d^{eq}_t \ge 0$ : Amount by which equipment usage exceeds the limit in stage $t$ (Overload).
- $d^{cap}_t \ge 0$ : Amount by which capital usage exceeds the limit in stage $t$ (Overload).
- $d^{eq-}_t, d^{cap-}_t \ge 0$ : Under-utilization (usually not penalized in "minimize overloading", so we only care about positive deviations).

**Objective Function:**
Minimize $Z = P_1 \left( \sum_{t=1}^3 \sum_{i=1}^3 w_i n_{i,t} \right) + P_2 \left( \sum_{t=1}^3 d^{eq}_t \right) + P_3 \left( \sum_{t=1}^3 d^{cap}_t \right)$

*Note: $P_1, P_2, P_3$ are pre-emptive priority factors, with $P_1 \gg P_2 \gg P_3$. In a single-pass weighted sum approximation often used in solvers, we might assign large weights, but strictly speaking, this is a lexicographic problem. For the purpose of this formulation, we define the structure.*

**Constraints:**

1.  **Inventory Balance (Material Balance):**
    For each $i \in \{1,2,3\}$ and $t \in \{1,2,3\}$:
    $$x_{i,t} + I_{i,t-1} - n_{i,t} + p_{i,t} = D_{i,t} + I_{i,t}$$
    *Explanation:* Production + Beginning Inventory - Shortage + Surplus = Demand + Ending Inventory.
    *Boundary Conditions:* $I_{i,0} = 0$ and $I_{i,3} = 0$.

2.  **Equipment Usage Goal (Priority 2):**
    For each $t \in \{1,2,3\}$:
    $$\sum_{i=1}^3 a^{eq}_i x_{i,t} - d^{eq-}_t + d^{eq}_t = R^{eq}$$
    $$\sum_{i=1}^3 a^{eq}_i x_{i,t} - d^{eq-}_t + d^{eq}_t = 5000$$
    *We minimize $d^{eq}_t$ in the objective. $d^{eq-}_t$ is free/unused and not in the objective.*

3.  **Working Capital Usage Goal (Priority 3):**
    For each $t \in \{1,2,3\}$:
    $$\sum_{i=1}^3 a^{cap}_i x_{i,t} - d^{cap-}_t + d^{cap}_t = R^{cap}$$
    $$\sum_{i=1}^3 a^{cap}_i x_{i,t} - d^{cap-}_t + d^{cap}_t = 93000$$
    *We minimize $d^{cap}_t$ in the objective.*

4.  **Non-negativity:**
    $$x_{i,t} \ge 0, \quad I_{i,t} \ge 0, \quad n_{i,t} \ge 0, \quad p_{i,t} \ge 0, \quad d^{eq}_t \ge 0, \quad d^{eq-}_t \ge 0, \quad d^{cap}_t \ge 0, \quad d^{cap-}_t \ge 0$$

### FINAL MATHEMATICAL MODEL

**Model Type:** Linear Goal Programming (Lexicographic)

**Index Sets:**
- $I = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of products.
- $T = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of stages.

**Parameters:**
- $D_{1,1}=500, D_{1,2}=680, D_{1,3}=800$
- $D_{2,1}=750, D_{2,2}=800, D_{2,3}=950$
- $D_{3,1}=900, D_{3,2}=800, D_{3,3}=1000$
- $a^{eq}_1=2.0, a^{eq}_2=1.0, a^{eq}_3=3.1$ (hours/unit)
- $a^{cap}_1=40, a^{cap}_2=20, a^{cap}_3=55$ (yuan/unit)
- $R^{eq} = 5000$ (hours/stage)
- $R^{cap} = 93000$ (yuan/stage)
- Weights for P1: $w_1=1, w_2=1, w_3=2$

**Decision Variables:**
- $x_{i,t} \in \mathbb{R}^+$: Production quantity of product $i$ in stage $t$.
- $I_{i,t} \in \mathbb{R}^+$: Ending inventory of product $i$ at stage $t$.
- $n_{i,t} \in \mathbb{R}^+$: Stockout (unmet demand) for product $i$ in stage $t$.
- $p_{i,t} \in \mathbb{R}^+$: Excess production (surplus) for product $i$ in stage $t$.
- $d^{eq}_t \in \mathbb{R}^+$: Equipment overload in stage $t$.
- $d^{eq-}_t \in \mathbb{R}^+$: Equipment underutilization in stage $t$.
- $d^{cap}_t \in \mathbb{R}^+$: Capital overload in stage $t$.
- $d^{cap-}_t \in \mathbb{R}^+$: Capital underutilization in stage $t$.

**Objective Function:**
Minimize the pre-emptive priority vector:
$$
\min Z = \begin{cases}
Z_1 = \sum_{t=1}^3 \sum_{i=1}^3 w_i n_{i,t} \\
Z_2 = \sum_{t=1}^3 d^{eq}_t \\
Z_3 = \sum_{t=1}^3 d^{cap}_t
\end{cases}
$$
Where $w_1=1, w_2=1, w_3=2$.

**Constraints:**

1.  **Inventory Balance:**
    For all $i \in I, t \in T$:
    $$x_{i,t} + I_{i,t-1} - n_{i,t} + p_{i,t} = D_{i,t} + I_{i,t}$$
    Boundary conditions:
    $$I_{i,0} = 0 \quad \forall i \in I$$
    $$I_{i,3} = 0 \quad \forall i \in I$$

2.  **Equipment Resource Goal:**
    For all $t \in T$:
    $$\sum_{i=1}^3 a^{eq}_i x_{i,t} - d^{eq-}_t + d^{eq}_t = 5000$$

3.  **Working Capital Resource Goal:**
    For all $t \in T$:
    $$\sum_{i=1}^3 a^{cap}_i x_{i,t} - d^{cap-}_t + d^{cap}_t = 93000$$

4.  **Non-negativity:**
    $$x_{i,t}, I_{i,t}, n_{i,t}, p_{i,t}, d^{eq}_t, d^{eq-}_t, d^{cap}_t, d^{cap-}_t \ge 0 \quad \forall i \in I, t \in T$$
