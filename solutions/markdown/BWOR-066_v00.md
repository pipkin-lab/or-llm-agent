# Operations Research Model: Warehouse Distribution Planning

## 1. Decision Variables

Let $k \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ represent the months (January, February, March, April).

*   $x_k$: The number of units purchased in month $k$.
*   $y_k$: The number of units sold in month $k$.
*   $I_k$: The inventory level at the **end** of month $k$.

**Variable Constraints:**
*   $x_k, y_k, I_k \geq 0$ and are integers for all $k \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$.

## 2. Objective Function

**Maximize Total Profit ($Z$)**

Profit is calculated as total revenue from sales minus total cost of purchases. Storage costs are ignored.

$$ Z = \sum_{k=1}^{4} (\text{Selling Price}_k \cdot y_k) - \sum_{k=1}^{4} (\text{Buying Price}_k \cdot x_k) $$

Substituting the values from Table 7-6:
*   $P_{buy} = [10, 9, 11, 15]$
*   $P_{sell} = [12, 9, 13, 17]$

$$ \text{Maximize } Z = (12y_1 - 10x_1) + (9y_2 - 9x_2) + (13y_3 - 11x_3) + (17y_4 - 15x_4) $$

## 3. Operational Constraints

### A. Inventory Balance Constraints
The inventory at the end of a month is determined by the inventory at the start of the month, plus purchases made in the *previous* month (since current purchases are available next month), minus sales made in the current month.

*Note: Purchases made in month $k$ ($x_k$) are added to inventory for month $k+1$.*

1.  **Month 1:**
    *   Start Inventory: 500 units (given).
    *   Purchases available: None from previous month (or 0).
    *   $I_1 = 500 - y_1$
    *(Note: $x_1$ is purchased in Month 1 but arrives in Month 2, so it does not affect $I_1$).*

2.  **Month 2:**
    *   Start Inventory: $I_1$.
    *   Purchases available: $x_1$ (purchased in Month 1).
    *   $I_2 = I_1 + x_1 - y_2$

3.  **Month 3:**
    *   Start Inventory: $I_2$.
    *   Purchases available: $x_2$ (purchased in Month 2).
    *   $I_3 = I_2 + x_2 - y_3$

4.  **Month 4:**
    *   Start Inventory: $I_3$.
    *   Purchases available: $x_3$ (purchased in Month 3).
    *   $I_4 = I_3 + x_3 - y_4$

    *(Note: $x_4$ is purchased in Month 4 but arrives in Month 5, which is outside the planning horizon, so it does not affect sales in Month 4).*

### B. Warehouse Capacity Constraints
The warehouse capacity is 1,000 units. The inventory at the end of each month cannot exceed this limit. Also, inventory cannot be negative.

*   $0 \leq I_1 \leq 1000$
*   $0 \leq I_2 \leq 1000$
*   $0 \leq I_3 \leq 1000$
*   $0 \leq I_4 \leq 1000$

### C. Sales Feasibility Constraints
The store can only sell what is available in the warehouse during that month. This is inherently handled by the non-negativity constraint on inventory ($I_k \geq 0$) combined with the balance equations, ensuring $y_k$ does not exceed available stock.

Specifically:
*   $y_1 \leq 500$
*   $y_2 \leq I_1 + x_1$
*   $y_3 \leq I_2 + x_2$
*   $y_4 \leq I_3 + x_3$

### D. Integer Constraints
*   $x_k, y_k, I_k \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ (Non-negative Integers)
