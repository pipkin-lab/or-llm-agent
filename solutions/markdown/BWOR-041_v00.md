# Operations Research Model: Toy Distribution Problem

## 1. Decision Variables

Let $x_{ij}$ be the number of units of Toy $i$ shipped to Store $j$, where:
*   $i \in \{A, B, C\}$ represents the type of toy.
*   $j \in \{X, Y, Z\}$ represents the department store.

Specifically:
*   $x_{AX}, x_{AY}, x_{AZ}$: Units of Toy A to Stores X, Y, Z.
*   $x_{BX}, x_{BY}, x_{BZ}$: Units of Toy B to Stores X, Y, Z.
*   $x_{CX}, x_{CY}, x_{CZ}$: Units of Toy C to Stores X, Y, Z.

All decision variables must be non-negative integers:
$$x_{ij} \ge 0, \quad x_{ij} \in \mathbb{Z}$$

## 2. Objective Function

**Maximize** the total profit $Z$.

Based on Table 3-6, the profit coefficients are:
*   Toy A: 5 (Store X), 4 (Store Y), 0 (Store Z - see constraints).
*   Toy B: 16 (Store X), 8 (Store Y), 6 (Store Z).
*   Toy C: 12 (Store X), 10 (Store Y), 11 (Store Z).

$$Z = 5x_{AX} + 4x_{AY} + 0x_{AZ} + 16x_{BX} + 8x_{BY} + 6x_{BZ} + 12x_{CX} + 10x_{CY} + 11x_{CZ}$$

*(Note: Since Store Z refuses Toy A, the coefficient for $x_{AZ}$ is effectively 0, and the variable is constrained to 0.)*

## 3. Operational Constraints

### A. Supply Constraints (Source Limits)
The total units of each toy shipped cannot exceed the monthly supply.

1.  **Toy A Supply:**
    $$x_{AX} + x_{AY} + x_{AZ} \le 1,000$$
2.  **Toy B Supply:**
    $$x_{BX} + x_{BY} + x_{BZ} \le 2,000$$
3.  **Toy C Supply:**
    $$x_{CX} + x_{CY} + x_{CZ} \le 2,000$$

### B. Demand Constraints (Store Sales Volumes)
Each store has an expected sales volume of 1,500 units *for each type of toy*. In standard transportation problems, this is typically interpreted as the store's capacity to sell up to 1,500 units of any specific item, or the total demand is fixed. However, the phrasing "expected monthly sales volume of 1,500 units for each type" usually implies these are the **upper bounds** for what each store can sell per toy type (Demand Limits).

*Interpretation Note:* In many OR contexts, if the total supply ($1000+2000+2000=5000$) is less than the total theoretical demand ($3 \text{ toys} \times 3 \text{ stores} \times 1500 = 13,500$), the constraints are usually "less than or equal to" demand limits. We will model these as maximum sales capacities per store per toy type.

4.  **Store X Demand Limits:**
    *   Toy A: $x_{AX} \le 1,500$
    *   Toy B: $x_{BX} \le 1,500$
    *   Toy C: $x_{CX} \le 1,500$
5.  **Store Y Demand Limits:**
    *   Toy A: $x_{AY} \le 1,500$
    *   Toy B: $x_{BY} \le 1,500$
    *   Toy C: $x_{CY} \le 1,500$
6.  **Store Z Demand Limits:**
    *   Toy A: $x_{AZ} \le 1,500$
    *   Toy B: $x_{BZ} \le 1,500$
    *   Toy C: $x_{CZ} \le 1,500$

**Supply:**
