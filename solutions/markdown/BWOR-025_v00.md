# Operations Research Model: Two-Machine Flow Shop Scheduling

## 1. Decision Variables

Let $n = 12$ be the number of jobs.
Let $J = \{1, 2, \dots, 12\}$ be the set of jobs.
Let $M = \{A, B\}$ be the set of machines.

**Binary Decision Variables:**
Define $x_{ij}$ as a binary variable indicating the relative position of jobs $i$ and $j$ in the sequence:
$$
x_{ij} = \begin{cases} 
1 & \text{if job } i \text{ is scheduled before job } j \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$
*Note: In a permutation flow shop, the sequence is the same on both machines. Therefore, $x_{ij} + x_{ji} = 1$ for all $i \neq j$.*

**Continuous Decision Variables:**
Define $C_{i, M}$ as the completion time of job $i$ on machine $M$.
Specifically:
- $C_{i, A}$: Completion time of job $i$ on Machine A.
- $C_{i, B}$: Completion time of job $i$ on Machine B.

Define $C_{max}$ as the makespan (total processing time).

## 2. Objective Function

**Minimize** the total processing time (Makespan):

$$
\min Z = C_{max}
$$

Where $C_{max} = \max_{i \in J} (C_{i, B})$.

## 3. Operational Constraints

### A. Machine A Constraints (Sequential Processing)
Since all jobs go through Machine A first, the start time of a job on A depends on the completion time of the previous job on A.

1.  **Precedence on Machine A:**
    For all $i, j \in J, i \neq j$:
    $$
    C_{i, A} \le C_{j, A} + M(1 - x_{ij})
    $$
    $$
    C_{j, A} \le C_{i, A} + M x_{ij}
    $$
    *(Where $M$ is a sufficiently large constant, e.g., sum of all processing times.)*

2.  **Processing Time on A:**
    The completion time on A for job $i$ is determined by the start time plus processing time $p_{iA}$. Since there is no prior machine, the start time is 0 for the first job. More formally, using start times $S_{i,A}$:
    $$
    C_{i, A} = S_{i, A} + p_{iA}
    $$
    $$
    S_{i, A} \ge C_{j, A} \cdot x_{ji}
    $$
    *(Simplification for standard formulation: The sequence on A is determined by $x_{ij}$. The actual value of $C_{i,A}$ is calculated based on the sorted order.)*

    *Alternative Standard Formulation using Start Times:*
    Let $S_{i, M}$ be the start time of job $i$ on machine $M$.
    $$
    C_{i, A} = S_{i, A} + p_{iA}
    $$
    $$
    S_{i, A} \ge C_{j, A} \quad \text{if } x_{ji}=1
    $$

### B. Machine B Constraints (Flow Shop Logic)
A job can start on Machine B only after it has completed on Machine A **AND** the previous job in the sequence has completed on Machine B.

1.  **Flow Constraint (A to B):**
    For all $i \in J$:
    $$
    S_{i, B} \ge C_{i, A}
    $$
    $$
    C_{i, B} = S_{i, B} + p_{iB}
    $$

2.  **Precedence on Machine B:**
    For all $i, j \in J, i \neq j$:
    $$
    S_{i, B} \le S_{j, B} + M(1 - x_{ij})
    $$
    $$
    S_{j, B} \le S_{i, B} + M x_{ij}
    $$
    *(This ensures that if job $i$ is before job $j$ in the sequence, job $j$ cannot start on B until job $i$ finishes on B.)*

### C. Makespan Definition
$$
C_{max} \ge C_{i, B} \quad \forall i \in J
$$

### D. Data Parameters
The processing times $p_{iA}$ and $p_{iB}$ are given by the table:

| Job ($i$) | $p_{iA}$ | $p_{iB}$ |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| 1 | 5 | 5 |
| 2 | 8 | 9 |
| 3 | 11 | 4 |
| 4 | 2 | 3 |
| 5 | 7 | 7 |
| 6 | 6 | 9 |
| 7 | 3 | 5 |
| 8 | 9 | 4 |
| 9 | 8 | 9 |
| 10 | 3 | 5 |
| 11 | 6 | 3 |
| 12 | 10 | 4 |

*(Note: While the mathematical formulation above defines the general OR model, this specific problem instance is a classic **Permutation Flow Shop** with 2 machines. In practice, this is often solved using **Johnson's Rule** rather than a full MIP solver due to its specific structure.)*

### **Solution Execution (Johnson's Rule)**

Since the problem asks to *determine* the sequence and calculate the time, we apply Johnson's Algorithm for $F2||C_{max}$.

**Step 1: Classify Jobs**
- **Set $J_1$** (Jobs where $p_{iA} < p_{iB}$):
    - Job 1: $5 < 5$ (Equal, usually grouped with $J_1$ or $J_2$, let's check: $5=5$. Standard rule: if $p_{iA} \le p_{iB}$, put in first group.)
    - Job 4: $2 < 3$
    - Job 7: $3 < 5$
    - Job 10: $3 < 5$
    - Job 11: $6 > 3$? No. Wait. $p_{11A}=6, p_{11B}=3$. $6 > 3$.
    - Job 3: $11 > 4$.
    - Job 8: $9 > 4$.
    - Job 12: $10 > 4$.
    - Job 6: $6 < 9$.
    - Job 2: $8 < 9$.
    - Job 5: $7 = 7$.
    - Job 9: $8 < 9$.

    Let's re-evaluate strictly:
    - **Group 1 ($p_{iA} \le p_{iB}$)**:
        - Job 1 ($5, 5$)
        - Job 2 ($8, 9$)
        - Job 4 ($2, 3$)
        - Job 5 ($7, 7$)
        - Job 6 ($6, 9$)
        - Job 7 ($3, 5$)
        - Job 9 ($8, 9$)
        - Job 10 ($3, 5$)

    - **Group 2 ($p_{iA} > p_{iB}$)**:
        - Job 3 ($11, 4$)
        - Job 8 ($9, 4$)
        - Job 11 ($6, 3$)
        - Job 12 ($10, 4$)

**Step 2: Sort Groups**
- **Group 1**: Sort by $p_{iA}$ in **ascending** order.
    - Job 4 ($2$)
    - Job 7 ($3$)
    - Job 10 ($3$)
    - Job 1 ($5$)
    - Job 6 ($6$)
    - Job 5 ($7$)
    - Job 2 ($8$)
    - Job 9 ($8$)
    *(Tie-breaker for 7/10 and 2/9 is arbitrary, but let's keep indices order or secondary sort. Let's stick to index for ties: 7, 10 and 2, 9.)*
    Sequence 1: **4, 7, 10, 1, 6, 5, 2, 9**

- **Group 2**: Sort by $p_{iB}$ in **descending** order.
    - Job 11 ($3$)
    - Job 3 ($4$)
    - Job 8 ($4$)
    - Job 12 ($4$)
    *(Tie-breaker for 3, 8, 12 by index: 3, 8, 12.)*
    Sequence 2: **11, 3, 8, 12**

**Step 3: Combine Sequences**
Total Sequence: **4, 7, 10, 1, 6, 5, 2, 9, 11, 3, 8, 12**

**Step 4: Calculate Makespan**

| Job | $p_A$ | $p_B$ | Start A | End A | Start B (max EndA, Prev EndB) | End B |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **4** | 2 | 3 | 0 | 2 | max(2, 0) = 2 | 5 |
| **7** | 3 | 5 | 2 | 5 | max(5, 5) = 5 | 10 |
| **10** | 3 | 5 | 5 | 8 | max(8, 10) = 10 | 15 |
| **1** | 5 | 5 | 8 | 13 | max(13, 15) = 15 | 20 |
| **6** | 6 | 9 | 13 | 19 | max(19, 20) = 20 | 29 |
| **5** | 7 | 7 | 19 | 26 | max(26, 29) = 29 | 36 |
| **2** | 8 | 9 | 26 | 34 | max(34, 36) = 36 | 45 |
| **9** | 8 | 9 | 34 | 42 | max(42, 45) = 45 | 54 |
| **11** | 6 | 3 | 42 | 48 | max(48, 54) = 54 | 57 |
| **3** | 11 | 4 | 48 | 59 | max(59, 57) = 59 | 63 |
| **8** | 9 | 4 | 59 | 68 | max(68, 63) = 68 | 72 |
| **12** | 10 | 4 | 68 | 78 | max(78, 72) = 78 | **82** |

**Final Result:**
- **Optimal Sequence:** 4, 7, 10, 1, 6, 5, 2, 9, 11, 3, 8, 12
- **Total Processing Time (Makespan):** 82
