# Operations Research Model: Hongyin Company Loan Funding Optimization

## 1. Decision Variables

Let the time index $t \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}$ correspond to the beginning of years 2003, 2004, 2005, 2006, and the end of 2006 (or beginning of 2007).
*Note: The problem states funds are raised before the end of 2002. We model the cash flow at the start of each year.*

Let $x_{i,t}$ be the amount of funds (in thousand dollars) invested in specific instruments at the beginning of year $t$.
Let $y_t$ be the amount of funds (in thousand dollars) deposited in the bank account at the beginning of year $t$.

Specific Decision Variables:
1.  **$x_{A,2003}$**: Amount invested in Bond A at the beginning of 2003.
2.  **$x_{B,2003}$**: Amount invested in Bond B at the beginning of 2003.
3.  **$x_{C,2004}$**: Amount invested in Bond C at the beginning of 2004.
4.  **$y_{2003}$**: Amount deposited in the bank at the beginning of 2003.
5.  **$y_{2004}$**: Amount deposited in the bank at the beginning of 2004.
6.  **$y_{2005}$**: Amount deposited in the bank at the beginning of 2005.
7.  **$y_{2006}$**: Amount deposited in the bank at the beginning of 2006.
8.  **$F$**: Total amount of funds raised before the end of 2002 (in thousand dollars). *This is the variable to be minimized.*

*(Note: Since bonds have fixed terms and specific start dates, we do not need variables for investing in these bonds in other years. Bank deposits can happen every year, so we define $y_t$ for each year $t=2003, 2004, 2005, 2006$.)*

## 2. Objective Function

Minimize the total initial capital raised, $F$.

$$ \text{Minimize } Z = F $$

## 3. Operational Constraints

### A. Initial Funding Constraint (Year 2003 Start)
The total funds raised $F$ must cover the loan for 2003, the investment in Bond A, Bond B, and the bank deposit for 2003.

$$ F = 100 + x_{A,2003} + x_{B,2003} + y_{2003} $$

### B. Cash Flow Balance for Year 2004
Funds available at the beginning of 2004 come from:
1.  Return from Bank deposit in 2003: $1.04 \times y_{2003}$
2.  *(Bond A and B are still running; no return yet)*

These funds must cover the loan for 2004, investment in Bond C, and the bank deposit for 2004.

$$ 1.04 y_{2003} = 150 + x_{C,2004} + y_{2004} $$

### C. Cash Flow Balance for Year 2005
Funds available at the beginning of 2005 come from:
1.  Return from Bank deposit in 2004: $1.04 \times y_{2004}$
2.  Maturity of Bond B (invested in 2003, 2-year term): $1.25 \times x_{B,2003}$
3.  *(Bond A is still running; Bond C is still running)*

These funds must cover the loan for 2005 and the bank deposit for 2005.

$$ 1.04 y_{2004} + 1.25 x_{B,2003} = 120 + y_{2005} $$

### D. Cash Flow Balance for Year 2006
Funds available at the beginning of 2006 come from:
1.  Return from Bank deposit in 2005: $1.04 \times y_{2005}$
2.  Maturity of Bond C (invested in 2004, 2-year term): $1.30 \times x_{C,2004}$
3.  *(Bond A is still running)*

These funds must cover the loan for 2006 and the bank deposit for 2006.

$$ 1.04 y_{2005} + 1.30 x_{C,2004} = 110 + y_{2006} $$

### E. Final Settlement / No Surplus Constraint (End of 2006)
The problem implies we want to minimize the initial raise. Usually, in such models, we assume any remaining funds at the very end are not required to be zero, but rather that the system is self-sustaining. However, since the objective is to minimize the *initial* raise, and all future cash flows are determined by the initial $F$ and the investment choices, we must ensure that the funds available at the end of the project period (after the last loan in 2006) are non-negative.
Actually, looking at the cash flow for 2006, we defined $y_{2006}$ as the deposit. At the end of 2006, we receive $1.04 y_{2006}$ and the maturity of Bond A ($1.40 x_{A,2003}$).
Does the company need to have zero cash at the end? The problem asks to minimize the amount raised. It does not state that there is a terminal value requirement. However, to ensure feasibility, the cash flows must balance.
Wait, the constraint in D says funds *available* at start of 2006 equal Loan 2006 + Deposit 2006.
At the end of 2006, the company receives $1.04 y_{2006}$ and $1.40 x_{A,2003}$. There are no more obligations. To minimize the initial $F$, we generally do not constrain the final terminal wealth to zero unless specified, but rather we just need the constraints above to hold. The "minimization" will naturally drive the system to use the most efficient investments.
*Correction*: If we don't constrain the end, the model is still valid because the link between years is through the balance equations. The variable $F$ is linked to the first year. The chain of equations propagates the value of $F$ forward.
However, usually, in "minimize initial capital" problems with fixed outflows, we assume that any leftover at the end is just leftover. But is there a constraint that the funds must be *exactly* sufficient?
Let's look at the structure again.
$F$ is the only variable not on the RHS of the cash flow equations (except in the first one).
Actually, we can substitute $F$ out.
From (A): $y_{2003} = F - 100 - x_A - x_B$.
Substitute into (B): $1.04(F - 100 - x_A - x_B) = 150 + x_C + y_{2004} \implies y_{2004} = 1.04F - 104 - 1.04x_A - 1.04x_B - 150 - x_C$.
This becomes an algebraic system.
Alternatively, we can view the problem as finding the minimum $F$ such that there exist non-negative investments satisfying the cash flow balances.
The "constraint" that closes the loop is simply that all decision variables must be non-negative.
There is no explicit "End of Project Balance = 0" constraint in the problem statement ("ensure that the total amount needed to be raised... is minimized"). It doesn't say "and have zero cash left". It just says the loans must be paid. So, having cash left over at the end of 2006 is allowed (it just means we could have raised less? No, because the cash flows are fixed by the investments. If we have cash left, it comes from the maturity of Bond A and the last bank deposit. If we raised less initially, we couldn't have made those investments).
Actually, the minimization of $F$ is achieved by maximizing the efficiency of the funds. The constraints are the cash flow balances and the non-negativity of variables.

**Refined Constraint Set:**

1.  **Year 2003 Balance**:
    $$ F - 100 - x_{A,2003} - x_{B,2003} - y_{2003} = 0 $$
2.  **Year 2004 Balance**:
    $$ 1.04 y_{2003} - 150 - x_{C,2004} - y_{2004} = 0 $$
3.  **Year 2005 Balance**:
    $$ 1.04 y_{2004} + 1.25 x_{B,2003} - 120 - y_{2005} = 0 $$
4.  **Year 2006 Balance**:
    $$ 1.04 y_{2005} + 1.30 x_{C,2004} - 110 - y_{2006} = 0 $$
5.  **Bond A Limit**:
    $$ 0 \le x_{A,2003} \le 60 $$
6.  **Bond B Limit**:
    $$ 0 \le x_{B,2003} \le 90 $$
7.  **Bond C Limit**:
    $$ 0 \le x_{C,2004} \le 50 $$
8.  **Non-negativity of Bank Deposits**:
    $$ y_{2003} \ge 0, \quad y_{2004} \ge 0, \quad y_{2005} \ge 0, \quad y_{2006} \ge 0 $$

*(Note: There is no constraint forcing the final cash balance at the end of 2006 to be zero. The objective is to minimize $F$. The system will naturally find the smallest $F$ that allows the positive cash flows to cover the obligations given the investment caps. If there is excess cash at the end, it is simply the result of the optimal investment mix.)*
