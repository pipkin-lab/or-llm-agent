## Model Type Declaration
**Model Type:** LP (Linear Program)

## Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
- $t \in T = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Time periods (Months 1, 2, 3)

**Parameters:**
- $P_{buy,t}$: Purchase price (yuan/dan)
- $P_{sell,t}$: Selling price (yuan/dan)
- $C_{cap}$: Warehouse capacity (5,000 dan)
- $I_0$: Initial inventory (1,000 dan)
- $K_0$: Initial capital (20,000 yuan)
- $I_{target}$: Target ending inventory for Month 3 (2,000 dan)

**Data Table:**
| $t$ | $P_{buy,t}$ | $P_{sell,t}$ |
|:---:|:-----------:|:------------:|
| 1   | 2.85        | 3.10         |
| 2   | 3.05        | 3.25         |
| 3   | 2.90        | 2.95         |

## Decision Variables
- $x_t \ge 0$: Quantity of grain purchased in month $t$ (dan) [CONTINUOUS]
- $s_t \ge 0$: Quantity of grain sold in month $t$ (dan) [CONTINUOUS]

## Objective Function
The goal is to maximize the total profit (Total Revenue - Total Cost).
$$ \text{MAXIMIZE } Z = \sum_{t=1}^{3} (P_{sell,t} \cdot s_t) - \sum_{t=1}^{3} (P_{buy,t} \cdot x_t) $$
Substituting values:
$$ Z = 3.10 s_1 + 3.25 s_2 + 2.95 s_3 - (2.85 x_1 + 3.05 x_2 + 2.90 x_3) $$

## Operational Constraints

1. **Inventory Balance (Flow Conservation):**
   The inventory at the end of month $t$ equals the previous month's inventory plus purchases minus sales.
   - $I_1 = 1000 + x_1 - s_1$
   - $I_2 = I_1 + x_2 - s_2$
   - $I_3 = I_2 + x_3 - s_3$
   
   *Explicit Expansion (to eliminate intermediate inventory variables for solver clarity):*
   - $I_1 = 1000 + x_1 - s_1$
   - $I_2 = 1000 + x_1 + x_2 - s_1 - s_2$
   - $I_3 = 1000 + x_1 + x_2 + x_3 - s_1 - s_2 - s_3$

2. **Target Ending Inventory:**
   The problem specifies a target inventory at the end of March. We model this as an equality constraint to fix the final state.
   - $I_3 = 2000$
   - $\Rightarrow 1000 + x_1 + x_2 + x_3 - s_1 - s_2 - s_3 = 2000$
   - $\Rightarrow \sum_{t=1}^{3} x_t - \sum_{t=1}^{3} s_t = 1000$

3. **Warehouse Capacity Constraints:**
   Inventory must not exceed the warehouse capacity of 5,000 dan in any month.
   - $I_1 \le 5000 \Rightarrow 1000 + x_1 - s_1 \le 5000$
   - $I_2 \le 5000 \Rightarrow 1000 + x_1 + x_2 - s_1 - s_2 \le 5000$
   - $I_3 = 2000 \le 5000$ (This is automatically satisfied by the target constraint, but strictly $I_3 \le 5000$ holds).

4. **Cash Flow / Capital Constraints:**
   The company pays cash upon delivery. The available capital is 20,000 yuan. We assume the company cannot spend more cash than it has available (capital constraint), and cash is released by sales.
   
   *Note on Cash Flow Timing:*
   - **Month 1:**
     - Outflow: Purchase cost $2.85 x_1$
     - Inflow: None (Sales $s_1$ occur in Month 1? Wait, "can only be sold starting in the next month". This means grain purchased in Month 1 cannot be sold in Month 1. It can be sold in Month 2 or 3. What about initial stock? "As of Jan 1... 1000 dan in stock". This stock was purchased in the past. Can it be sold in Month 1? Yes, it is already in the warehouse. The restriction "grain arrives in the month it is purchased, but can only be sold starting in the next month" typically applies to *new* purchases. Initial stock is available for sale immediately in Month 1.
     - Let's refine the sale restriction.
       - $s_1$: Can only come from Initial Stock ($I_0$). $x_1$ cannot be sold in month 1.
       - $s_2$: Can come from $I_0$ (unsold) or $x_1$.
       - $s_3$: Can come from $I_0$ (unsold), $x_1$ (unsold), or $x_2$.
     
     *Cash Balance Equations:*
     Let $K_t$ be the cash balance at the end of month $t$ (or available for next month).
     - $K_0 = 20000$
     - **Month 1 Cash:**
       - Start: $K_0 = 20000$
       - Spend: $2.85 x_1$
       - Earn: $3.10 s_1$ (Sale of initial stock)
       - $K_1 = K_0 + 3.10 s_1 - 2.85 x_1$
       - Constraint: Cash cannot go negative during the month. Since payment is "upon delivery" (purchase) and sales are likely paid "upon delivery" (sale), we must ensure $K_0 + 3.10 s_1 - 2.85 x_1 \ge 0$.
       
     - **Month 2 Cash:**
       - Start: $K_1$
       - Spend: $3.05 x_2$
       - Earn: $3.25 s_2$
       - $K_2 = K_1 + 3.25 s_2 - 3.05 x_2$
       - Constraint: $K_1 + 3.25 s_2 - 3.05 x_2 \ge 0$
       
     - **Month 3 Cash:**
       - Start: $K_2$
       - Spend: $2.90 x_3$
       - Earn: $2.95 s_3$
       - $K_3 = K_2 + 2.95 s_3 - 2.90 x_3$
       - Constraint: $K_2 + 2.95 s_3 - 2.90 x_3 \ge 0$

     *Substituting recursively to get explicit constraints:*
     1. $20000 + 3.10 s_1 - 2.85 x_1 \ge 0$
     2. $20000 + 3.10 s_1 - 2.85 x_1 + 3.25 s_2 - 3.05 x_2 \ge 0$
     3. $20000 + 3.10 s_1 - 2.85 x_1 + 3.25 s_2 - 3.05 x_2 + 2.95 s_3 - 2.90 x_3 \ge 0$

5. **Sales Availability Constraints (Logical Flow):**
   - $s_1 \le 1000$ (Can only sell initial stock in Month 1)
   - $s_2 \le 1000 + x_1$ (Can sell remaining initial stock + Month 1 purchases)
   - $s_3 \le 1000 + x_1 + x_2$ (Can sell remaining initial stock + Month 1 & 2 purchases)
   
   *Note:* These are implicitly enforced by the non-negativity of inventory $I_t \ge 0$ and the definition of $I_t$.
   - $I_1 = 1000 + x_1 - s_1 \ge 0 \Rightarrow s_1 \le 1000 + x_1$. But we know $x_1$ cannot be sold in month 1. Does the model enforce this?
     If $s_1$ is defined as total sales in month 1, and $I_1 \ge 0$, the model allows $s_1$ to consume $x_1$ if not careful.
     However, standard inventory models assume "First-In-First-Out" or fungibility. If grain is fungible, $I_1$ is just a number. But the constraint "can only be sold starting in the next month" is a **time-lag constraint** on the specific units.
     
     To strictly enforce that $x_1$ cannot be sold in month 1, we must rely on the physical interpretation:
     Sales in month 1 ($s_1$) must be covered by Inventory available *before* month 1 purchases arrive? Or does the purchase happen, and then we just can't sell *that specific batch*?
     Usually, in these problems, it implies:
     $s_1 \le I_0 = 1000$.
     
     Let's add explicit constraints for sales availability based on the "next month" rule:
     - $s_1 \le 1000$
     - $s_2 \le 1000 + x_1$
     - $s_3 \le 1000 + x_1 + x_2$
     
     Are these redundant with $I_t \ge 0$?
     $I_1 = 1000 + x_1 - s_1 \ge 0 \Rightarrow s_1 \le 1000 + x_1$. This allows selling $x_1$ in month 1 if $s_1 > 1000$.
     So, **YES**, we MUST add the explicit sales availability constraints to enforce the time lag.
     
     Revised Sales Constraints:
     - $s_1 \le 1000$
     - $s_2 \le 1000 + x_1$
     - $s_3 \le 1000 + x_1 + x_2$

6. **Non-negativity:**
   - $x_t \ge 0, s_t \ge 0$ for $t=1,2,3$.
   - $I_t \ge 0$ for $t=1,2,3$ (Implicitly handled by sales constraints and target, but good to state).
     - $I_1 \ge 0 \Leftrightarrow s_1 \le 1000 + x_1$ (We have tighter $s_1 \le 1000$)
     - $I_2 \ge 0 \Leftrightarrow s_1 + s_2 \le 1000 + x_1 + x_2$
     - $I_3 = 2000 \ge 0$ (Satisfied)
