**Model Type:** QP (Quadratic Programming)
*Note: The production cost function $x^2$ introduces quadratic terms into the objective function. A non-linear solver (e.g., scipy.optimize.minimize with method='SLSQP' or a dedicated QP solver) is required.*

### Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
- $t \in \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of months.

**Parameters:**
- $d_t$: Demand in month $t$.
  - $d_1 = 100$
  - $d_2 = 110$
  - $d_3 = 120$
- $h$: Inventory holding cost per unit per month.
  - $h = 1$
- $s_0$: Initial inventory at the beginning of month 1.
  - $s_0 = 0$
- $s_3$: Final inventory at the end of month 3.
  - $s_3 = 0$

### Decision Variables

- $x_t$: Quantity of Product A produced in month $t$ for $t \in \{1, 2, 3\}$.
  - **Domain:** $x_t \ge 0$ (Continuous)
- $s_t$: Inventory level at the **end** of month $t$ for $t \in \{1, 2, 3\}$.
  - **Domain:** $s_t \ge 0$ (Continuous)

*(Note: While $s_t$ can be eliminated by substitution to reduce variable count, keeping them explicit improves model clarity and adheres to standard inventory modeling practices.)*

### Objective Function

Minimize the total cost, which is the sum of production costs and inventory holding costs over the 3 months.

$$
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{t=1}^{3} x_t^2 + \sum_{t=1}^{3} h \cdot s_t
$$

Substituting the parameter values:
$$
\text{Minimize } Z = x_1^2 + x_2^2 + x_3^2 + 1 \cdot s_1 + 1 \cdot s_2 + 1 \cdot s_3
$$

### Operational Constraints

**1. Inventory Balance Constraints:**
For each month $t$, the inventory at the end of the month is equal to the inventory at the beginning of the month plus production minus demand.
Beginning inventory for month $t$ is $s_{t-1}$ (where $s_0=0$).

- **Month 1:**
  $$s_1 = s_0 + x_1 - d_1$$
  $$s_1 = 0 + x_1 - 100$$
  $$s_1 - x_1 = -100$$

- **Month 2:**
  $$s_2 = s_1 + x_2 - d_2$$
  $$s_2 = s_1 + x_2 - 110$$
  $$s_2 - s_1 - x_2 = -110$$

- **Month 3:**
  $$s_3 = s_2 + x_3 - d_3$$
  $$s_3 = s_2 + x_3 - 120$$
  $$s_3 - s_2 - x_3 = -120$$

**2. Boundary Conditions:**
- Initial Inventory:
  $$s_0 = 0$$ (Used in Month 1 constraint above)
- Final Inventory:
  $$s_3 = 0$$

**3. Non-negativity Constraints:**
- Production quantities must be non-negative:
  $$x_1 \ge 0, \quad x_2 \ge 0, \quad x_3 \ge 0$$
- Inventory levels must be non-negative (no backlogging allowed):
  $$s_1 \ge 0, \quad s_2 \ge 0, \quad s_3 \ge 0$$
  *(Note: $s_3$ is fixed at 0, so $s_3 \ge 0$ is redundant but consistent.)*

**Summary of Constraints in Standard Form:**
1. $s_1 - x_1 = -100$
2. $s_2 - s_1 - x_2 = -110$
3. $s_3 - s_2 - x_3 = -120$
4. $s_3 = 0$
5. $x_1, x_2, x_3, s_1, s_2 \ge 0$

**Feasibility Sanity Check:**
Total Demand = $100 + 110 + 120 = 330$.
Total Production = $x_1 + x_2 + x_3$.
From the balance equations summed over $t=1$ to $3$:
$(s_3 - s_0) + (x_1+x_2+x_3) - (d_1+d_2+d_3) = 0$
$0 - 0 + \sum x_t - 330 = 0 \implies \sum x_t = 330$.
Since $x_t \ge 0$ and there is no upper bound on production, a feasible solution exists (e.g., produce exactly demand each month: $x_1=100, x_2=110, x_3=120$). The convex quadratic objective ensures a unique global minimum.
