**Model Type:** MILP (Mixed Integer Linear Programming)
*(Note: While the variables are technically continuous, in the context of physical "units" of equipment, they are often treated as integers in practice. However, since the data (capacities, demands) are integers and costs are linear, the constraint matrix is typically totally unimodular for this type of single-product flow problem, yielding integer solutions even if variables are defined as continuous. For strictness with "units", we define them as non-negative integers.)*

### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
*   $T = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$: Set of quarters in the year.

**Parameters (from Table 3-30):**
*   $C_t$: Production capacity in quarter $t$ (units).
    *   $C_1 = 25$
    *   $C_2 = 35$
    *   $C_3 = 30$
    *   $C_4 = 20$
*   $D_t$: Delivery quantity (demand) required at the end of quarter $t$ (units).
    *   $D_1 = 15$
    *   $D_2 = 20$
    *   $D_3 = 25$
    *   $D_4 = 20$
*   $p_t$: Production cost per unit in quarter $t$ (thousand yuan).
    *   $p_1 = 12.0$
    *   $p_2 = 11.0$
    *   $p_3 = 11.5$
    *   $p_4 = 12.5$
*   $h$: Storage and maintenance fee per unit per quarter (thousand yuan).
    *   $h = 0.1$

### 2. Decision Variables

Let $x_t$ be the quantity of equipment produced in quarter $t$.
Domain: $x_t \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ (Non-negative Integers) for all $t \in T$.

Let $I_t$ be the inventory of equipment held at the **end** of quarter $t$ (after delivery).
Domain: $I_t \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ (Non-negative Integers) for all $t \in T$.
*Note: We assume $I_0 = 0$ (no initial inventory) and typically $I_4 = 0$ is not strictly required unless specified, but since all demand must be met by production within the year and there is no salvage value or future demand, excess production is wasteful. The constraints will naturally push $I_4$ to 0 if costs are positive, but we can explicitly set $I_4=0$ or leave it $\ge 0$. Given "minimize cost", any unit in $I_4$ adds production cost and storage cost without satisfying a demand, so $I_4$ will be 0 at the optimum.*

### 3. Objective Function

Minimize the total annual cost, which includes production costs and storage costs.

$$ \text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{t=1}^{4} (p_t x_t + h I_t) $$

Expanding the terms:
$$ Z = 12.0 x_1 + 11.0 x_2 + 11.5 x_3 + 12.5 x_4 + 0.1 I_1 + 0.1 I_2 + 0.1 I_3 + 0.1 I_4 $$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**1. Production Capacity Constraints:**
Production in any quarter cannot exceed the available capacity.
$$ x_t \le C_t, \quad \forall t \in T $$
Specifically:
*   $x_1 \le 25$
*   $x_2 \le 35$
*   $x_3 \le 30$
*   $x_4 \le 20$

**2. Inventory Balance Constraints:**
The inventory at the end of quarter $t$ equals the inventory from the previous quarter plus production in quarter $t$ minus the delivery (demand) in quarter $t$.
$$ I_t = I_{t-1} + x_t - D_t, \quad \forall t \in T $$
With initial condition $I_0 = 0$.

Specifically:
*   $I_1 = 0 + x_1 - 15 \implies I_1 = x_1 - 15$
*   $I_2 = I_1 + x_2 - 20$
*   $I_3 = I_2 + x_3 - 25$
*   $I_4 = I_3 + x_4 - 20$

**3. Non-negativity and Integrality Constraints:**
*   $x_t \ge 0, \quad \forall t \in T$
*   $I_t \ge 0, \quad \forall t \in T$
*   $x_t, I_t \in \mathbb{Z}$

**Feasibility Check:**
Total Demand = $15 + 20 + 25 + 20 = 80$ units.
Total Capacity = $25 + 35 + 30 + 20 = 110$ units.
Since Total Capacity > Total Demand, a feasible solution exists. The model will determine the optimal production mix to minimize the trade-off between cheaper production in some quarters (requiring storage) vs. expensive production in later quarters (avoiding storage).
