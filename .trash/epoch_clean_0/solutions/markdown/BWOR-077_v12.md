# Assembly Line Worker Assignment Optimization

## Model Type: MILP (Mixed-Integer Linear Program)

This is a classic **Assignment Problem** (a special case of transportation/assignment LP), formulated as a 0-1 integer linear program.

## Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
- $W = \{A, B, C, D, E\}$: Set of workers, $|W| = 5$
- $S = \{I, II, III, IV, V\}$: Set of stations, $|S| = 5$

**Parameters:**
- $c_{w,s}$: Production efficiency (pieces/min) of worker $w$ at station $s$.

| Worker \ Station | I | II | III | IV | V |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| A | 2 | 3 | 4 | 1 | 7 |
| B | 3 | 4 | 2 | 5 | 6 |
| C | 2 | 5 | 3 | 4 | 1 |
| D | 5 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 5 |
| E | 3 | 7 | 6 | 2 | 4 |

## Decision Variables

- $x_{w,s} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall w \in W, s \in S$
  - $x_{w,s} = 1$ if worker $w$ is assigned to station $s$
  - $x_{w,s} = 0$ otherwise

## Objective Function

**MAXIMIZE** the bottleneck (minimum) production rate of the assembly line.

*Note: The overall production capacity of a serial assembly line is determined by the slowest station (the bottleneck). The problem asks to "maximize the overall production capacity." In a serial line, the throughput is $\min_{s} (\text{rate at station } s)$. To linearize this max-min problem, we introduce an auxiliary continuous variable $R$ representing the minimum station rate.*

Let $R$ be the production rate (pieces/min) of the entire line.

**Objective:**
$$ \text{MAXIMIZE } R $$

## Operational Constraints

**1. Worker Assignment Constraint (Each worker assigned to exactly one station):**
$$ \sum_{s \in S} x_{w,s} = 1 \quad \forall w \in W $$

**2. Station Coverage Constraint (Each station assigned exactly one worker):**
$$ \sum_{w \in W} x_{w,s} = 1 \quad \forall s \in S $$

**3. Bottleneck Rate Constraint (The line rate $R$ cannot exceed the rate of any station):**
$$ R \le c_{w,s} \cdot x_{w,s} + M(1 - x_{w,s}) \quad \forall w \in W, s \in S $$
*Alternatively, and more elegantly for an assignment problem where exactly one worker is assigned per station:*
$$ R \le \sum_{w \in W} c_{w,s} x_{w,s} \quad \forall s \in S $$
*(Since $\sum_{w} x_{w,s} = 1$, the term $\sum_{w} c_{w,s} x_{w,s}$ equals the efficiency of the specific worker assigned to station $s$. This constraint ensures $R$ is no larger than the actual rate of any station.)*

**4. Variable Bounds & Domains:**
$$ x_{w,s} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall w \in W, s \in S $$
$$ R \ge 0 $$

---

### Feasibility & Sanity Check

- **Total Supply/Demand:** 5 workers, 5 stations. Balanced.
- **Objective Verification:** The problem asks for "overall production capacity." In a serial assembly line, this is indeed the minimum throughput of the stations. Maximizing the minimum is the correct interpretation for "maximizing capacity" in a balanced line context.
- **Parameter Completeness:** All efficiency values $c_{w,s}$ are provided in the table. No [NOT_PROVIDED] values.
- **Model Type:** The core assignment variables are binary. The auxiliary variable $R$ is continuous. The constraints are linear. This is a **MILP** (specifically, a 0-1 ILP with one continuous variable, often solvable as a pure LP due to the totally unimodular structure of the assignment constraints, but formally it is a MILP due to the binary variables).

*(Self-Correction/Refinement on Objective Interpretation):*
Sometimes "maximize overall production capacity" in simple assignment problems might be interpreted as maximizing the *sum* of efficiencies (total output if stations ran in parallel, which is physically incorrect for a serial line). However, the standard OR interpretation of an "assembly line" implies a **serial process** where the throughput is limited by the bottleneck. Therefore, the **max-min** objective is the operationally correct model. If the problem intended a parallel system, it would usually specify "independent units." Given "assembly line," serial is the default.

However, let's double-check standard textbook problems of this type. Often, if the question is "maximize the minimum rate," it is explicit. If it just says "maximize overall production capacity," it *could* be a trick where they want the sum? No, "capacity of the line" is universally the bottleneck. I will stick with the Max-Min formulation as it is the physically correct model for an assembly line.

**Final Model Structure:**

**Model Type:** MILP

**Decision Variables:**
- $x_{w,s} \in \{0, 1\}$ for all $w \in \{A,B,C,D,E\}, s \in \{I,II,III,IV,V\}$
- $R \in \mathbb{R}^+$

**Objective:**
$$ \text{Maximize } R $$

**Subject to:**
1. $\sum_{s=1}^{5} x_{w,s} = 1, \quad \forall w \in \{A,B,C,D,E\}$
2. $\sum_{w \in \{A,B,C,D,E\}} x_{w,s} = 1, \quad \forall s \in \{1,2,3,4,5\}$
3. $R \le \sum_{w \in \{A,B,C,D,E\}} c_{w,s} x_{w,s}, \quad \forall s \in \{1,2,3,4,5\}$
4. $x_{w,s} \in \{0, 1\}, \quad R \ge 0$

**Parameters ($c_{w,s}$):**
- $c_{A,1}=2, c_{A,2}=3, c_{A,3}=4, c_{A,4}=1, c_{A,5}=7$
- $c_{B,1}=3, c_{B,2}=4, c_{B,3}=2, c_{B,4}=5, c_{B,5}=6$
- $c_{C,1}=2, c_{C,2}=5, c_{C,3}=3, c_{C,4}=4, c_{C,5}=1$
- $c_{D,1}=5, c_{D,2}=2, c_{D,3}=3, c_{D,4}=2, c_{D,5}=5$
- $c_{E,1}=3, c_{E,2}=7, c_{E,3}=6, c_{E,4}=2, c_{E,5}=4$
