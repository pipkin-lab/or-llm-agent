Based on the problem description, this is a combinatorial optimization problem involving sequencing to minimize total operations. Since the number of participants is fixed and the grades are discrete categories, we formulate this as a **MILP** (Mixed Integer Linear Programming) model, although the structure is purely combinatorial.

### Model Type Declaration
**Model Type:** MILP (Mixed Integer Linear Programming)
*Note: While this can be solved by enumeration or logic, it is formally an integer sequencing problem. The decision variables are binary/permutation variables representing the order of grades.*

### Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
*   $G = \{A, B, C, D, E\}$: Set of grade categories.
*   $P = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$: Set of sorting positions (steps) in the sequence.

**Parameters:**
*   $N = 50,000$: Total number of exam participants.
*   $p_g$: Historical percentage distribution for grade $g \in G$.
    *   $p_A = 0.10$
    *   $p_B = 0.25$
    *   $p_C = 0.30$
    *   $p_D = 0.20$
    *   $p_E = 0.15$
*   $n_g$: Absolute count of participants for grade $g$.
    *   $n_A = 50,000 \times 0.10 = 5,000$
    *   $n_B = 50,000 \times 0.25 = 12,500$
    *   $n_C = 50,000 \times 0.30 = 15,000$
    *   $n_D = 50,000 \times 0.20 = 10,000$
    *   $n_E = 50,000 \times 0.15 = 7,500$

**Derived Logic Explanation:**
When sorting "one grade at a time," the computer scans the current pool of unsorted sheets to identify and remove all sheets of the target grade.
*   If grade $g$ is selected for the $k$-th step, the computer must scan all sheets that remain in the pool at that time.
*   The number of sheets in the pool at step $k$ is the sum of counts of all grades that have *not* been sorted in steps $1$ to $k-1$.
*   Therefore, the cost (workload) of selecting grade $g$ at position $k$ is equal to the number of remaining sheets.
*   To minimize total workload, we generally want to remove the *largest* groups first or last? Let's analyze:
    *   If we remove Grade A (5,000) first, we scan 50,000 sheets.
    *   If we remove Grade C (15,000) first, we scan 50,000 sheets.
    *   The cost of the first step is always $N$.
    *   The cost of the second step is $N - n_{g_1}$.
    *   Total Workload $W = \sum_{k=1}^{5} (\text{Pool Size at start of step } k)$.
    *   Pool Size at step $k = N - \sum_{j=1}^{k-1} n_{g_j}$.
    *   $W = N + (N - n_{g_1}) + (N - n_{g_1} - n_{g_2}) + \dots$
    *   $W = 5N - 4n_{g_1} - 3n_{g_2} - 2n_{g_3} - 1n_{g_4} - 0n_{g_5}$.
    *   To **minimize** $W$, we need to **maximize** the subtracted terms: $\sum_{k=1}^{4} (5-k)n_{g_k}$.
    *   This implies we should assign larger $n_g$ to earlier positions (higher coefficients $4, 3, 2, 1$).
    *   Therefore, the optimal strategy is to sort the grades in **descending order of their counts** (largest group first).

### Decision Variables

Let $x_{g, k}$ be a binary variable:
*   $x_{g, k} = 1$ if grade $g$ is sorted at position $k$.
*   $x_{g, k} = 0$ otherwise.

**Domain:**
*   $x_{g, k} \in \{0, 1\}$ for all $g \in G, k \in P$.

### Objective Function

Minimize Total Workload $Z$:

$$
\min Z = \sum_{k=1}^{5} \left( N - \sum_{j=1}^{k-1} \sum_{g \in G} n_g x_{g, j} \right)
$$

Expanding this to linear form for clarity in the solver:
The total workload is the sum of the remaining sheets at each step.
Step 1 Cost: $N$
Step 2 Cost: $N - \sum_{g} n_g x_{g,1}$
Step 3 Cost: $N - \sum_{g} n_g x_{g,1} - \sum_{g} n_g x_{g,2}$
Step 4 Cost: $N - \sum_{g} n_g x_{g,1} - \sum_{g} n_g x_{g,2} - \sum_{g} n_g x_{g,3}$
Step 5 Cost: $N - \sum_{g} n_g x_{g,1} - \sum_{g} n_g x_{g,2} - \sum_{g} n_g x_{g,3} - \sum_{g} n_g x_{g,4}$

Total $Z = 5N - 4\sum_{g} n_g x_{g,1} - 3\sum_{g} n_g x_{g,2} - 2\sum_{g} n_g x_{g,3} - 1\sum_{g} n_g x_{g,4}$

Since $5N$ is a constant, minimizing $Z$ is equivalent to maximizing the weighted sum of removed counts. However, standard formulation is:

$$
\min Z = \sum_{k=1}^{5} \left( 5 - k + 1 \right) \left( N - \sum_{j=1}^{k-1} \sum_{g \in G} n_g x_{g, j} \right)
$$

*Wait, simpler linear formulation:*
The workload contributed by a specific sheet of grade $g$ is equal to the number of steps it survives. A sheet of grade $g$ survives step $k$ if $g$ is not selected in position $k$ or earlier.
Actually, it is easier to state the objective based on the "remaining pool" logic derived above.

Linear Objective Function:
$$
\min Z = 5N - \sum_{k=1}^{4} (5-k) \sum_{g \in G} n_g x_{g, k}
$$
*Note: The coefficient for position $k$ in the subtraction term is the number of subsequent steps where that removed amount is no longer present. If $g$ is removed at step $k$, it is absent from steps $k+1$ to $5$. That is $5-k$ steps.*

### Operational Constraints

1.  **Each position must have exactly one grade assigned:**
    $$
    \sum_{g \in G} x_{g, k} = 1 \quad \forall k \in P
    $$

2.  **Each grade must be assigned to exactly one position:**
    $$
    \sum_{k \in P} x_{g, k} = 1 \quad \forall g \in G
    $$

3.  **Binary Bounds:**
    $$
    x_{g, k} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall g \in G, k \in P
    $$

### Solution Verification (Sanity Check)

To find the optimal order, we look at the term being maximized (to minimize the negative part of the objective):
Maximize $\sum_{k=1}^{4} (5-k) \sum_{g \in G} n_g x_{g, k}$
Coefficients for positions $k=1, 2, 3, 4$ are $4, 3, 2, 1$.
We should assign the largest $n_g$ to the largest coefficients.

Counts:
*   $n_C = 15,000$ (Largest) $\rightarrow$ Assign to $k=1$ (Coeff 4)
*   $n_B = 12,500$ (2nd Largest) $\rightarrow$ Assign to $k=2$ (Coeff 3)
*   $n_D = 10,000$ (3rd Largest) $\rightarrow$ Assign to $k=3$ (Coeff 2)
*   $n_E = 7,500$ (4th Largest) $\rightarrow$ Assign to $k=4$ (Coeff 1)
*   $n_A = 5,000$ (Smallest) $\rightarrow$ Assign to $k=5$ (Coeff 0)

**Optimal Order:** C, B, D, E, A

**Calculation of Minimum Workload:**
*   Step 1 (Scan C): Pool = 50,000. Remove 15,000.
*   Step 2 (Scan B): Pool = 35,000. Remove 12,500.
*   Step 3 (Scan D): Pool = 22,500. Remove 10,000.
*   Step 4 (Scan E): Pool = 12,500. Remove 7,500.
*   Step 5 (Scan A): Pool = 5,000. Remove 5,000.
*   Total Workload = $50,000 + 35,000 + 22,500 + 12,500 + 5,000 = 125,000$ scans.

If we did the reverse (A, E, D, B, C):
*   Step 1: 50,000
*   Step 2: 45,000
*   Step 3: 37,500
*   Step 4: 27,500
*   Step 5: 17,500
*   Total = 177,500.

The model correctly identifies that sorting largest groups first minimizes the cumulative scan workload.
