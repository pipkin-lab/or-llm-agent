**Model Type:** LP (Linear Programming)

### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
*   $I = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of flour mills (Mill I, II, III).
*   $J = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of food processing factories (Factory 1, 2, 3).

**Parameters:**

*   **Supply ($S_i$):** Output of each flour mill (units).
    *   $S_1 = 20$
    *   $S_2 = 30$
    *   $S_3 = 20$
    *   Total Supply = $20 + 30 + 20 = 70$.

*   **Demand ($D_j$):** Processing capacity/demand of each food factory (units).
    *   $D_1 = 15$
    *   $D_2 = 25$
    *   $D_3 = 20$
    *   Total Demand = $15 + 25 + 20 = 60$.
    *   *Note: Total Supply (70) > Total Demand (60). There is excess supply of 10 units.*

*   **Transportation Cost ($C_{ij}$):** Unit transportation cost from Mill $i$ to Factory $j$ (yuan/unit).
    *   Row 1 (Mill I): $C_{11}=3, C_{12}=10, C_{13}=2$
    *   Row 2 (Mill II): $C_{21}=4, C_{22}=11, C_{23}=8$
    *   Row 3 (Mill III): $C_{31}=8, C_{32}=11, C_{33}=4$

*   **Profit ($P_j$):** Profit per unit of flour processed into food at Factory $j$ (yuan/unit).
    *   $P_1 = 12$
    *   $P_2 = 16$
    *   $P_3 = 11$

### 2. Decision Variables

Let $x_{ij}$ be the amount of flour shipped from Flour Mill $i$ to Food Factory $j$.
Since flour is a bulk continuous resource (not discrete countable items like ships or workers in this context, and standard transportation problems treat flow as continuous), we define:

*   $x_{ij} \ge 0, \quad \forall i \in I, j \in J$ (Continuous variables)

### 3. Objective Function

The objective is to **maximize total benefit**.
Total Benefit = Total Processing Profit - Total Transportation Cost.

For each unit shipped from $i$ to $j$:
*   Gain: $P_j$ (profit from processing at factory $j$)
*   Cost: $C_{ij}$ (transportation cost)
*   Net Benefit per unit: $P_j - C_{ij}$

$$
\text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{i=1}^{3} \sum_{j=1}^{3} (P_j - C_{ij}) x_{ij}
$$

Expanding the coefficients ($P_j - C_{ij}$):

*   **To Factory 1 ($P_1=12$):**
    *   $i=1: 12 - 3 = 9$
    *   $i=2: 12 - 4 = 8$
    *   $i=3: 12 - 8 = 4$
*   **To Factory 2 ($P_2=16$):**
    *   $i=1: 16 - 10 = 6$
    *   $i=2: 16 - 11 = 5$
    *   $i=3: 16 - 11 = 5$
*   **To Factory 3 ($P_3=11$):**
    *   $i=1: 11 - 2 = 9$
    *   $i=2: 11 - 8 = 3$
    *   $i=3: 11 - 4 = 7$

$$
\text{Maximize } Z = 9x_{11} + 8x_{21} + 4x_{31} + 6x_{12} + 5x_{22} + 5x_{32} + 9x_{13} + 3x_{23} + 7x_{33}
$$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**1. Supply Constraints (Mill Output Limits):**
The total flour shipped from each mill cannot exceed its output. Since Total Supply (70) > Total Demand (60), mills may have leftover flour. The problem asks for an allocation plan to factories. Usually, in such max-profit scenarios with excess supply, we assume unused flour has zero additional cost and zero profit (it stays at the mill). Thus, we use inequalities ($\le$) for supply.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{11} + x_{12} + x_{13} &\le 20 & (\text{Mill I}) \\
x_{21} + x_{22} + x_{23} &\le 30 & (\text{Mill II}) \\
x_{31} + x_{32} + x_{33} &\le 20 & (\text{Mill III})
\end{aligned}
$$

**2. Demand Constraints (Factory Capacity Limits):**
The total flour received by each factory cannot exceed its processing capacity/demand. Since we want to maximize profit and all net benefits ($P_j - C_{ij}$) are positive for all routes (minimum net benefit is 3 for $x_{23}$, maximum is 9), the optimal solution will utilize the full capacity of all factories. Therefore, these can be modeled as equalities or inequalities. Using equalities is standard when demand represents a "must-fill" capacity that yields positive net return, but inequalities are safer if we interpret "capacity" strictly. Given the positive returns, the solver will push these to the upper bound. We write them as $\le$ to be strictly consistent with "capacity", noting that the objective will drive them to equality.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{11} + x_{21} + x_{31} &\le 15 & (\text{Factory 1}) \\
x_{12} + x_{22} + x_{32} &\le 25 & (\text{Factory 2}) \\
x_{13} + x_{23} + x_{33} &\le 20 & (\text{Factory 3})
\end{aligned}
$$

**3. Non-negativity Constraints:**
$$
x_{ij} \ge 0, \quad \forall i \in I, j \in J
$$

### Feasibility Sanity Check
*   Total Demand = 60.
*   Total Supply = 70.
*   Since Supply > Demand, and all net profits are positive, it is feasible to satisfy all factory demands ($x_{..} = D_j$) using a subset of the available flour. The constraints are not mutually exclusive.
*   Example feasible solution: Send 15 to F1, 25 to F2, 20 to F3. Total sent = 60. Available from mills: 70. 10 units remain unused. This is a valid allocation.

### Final Model Summary

**Model Type:** LP

**Sets:**
$I = \{1, 2, 3\}$ (Mills)
$J = \{1, 2, 3\}$ (Factories)

**Parameters:**
$S = [20, 30, 20]$
$D = [15, 25, 20]$
$C = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 10 & 2 \\ 4 & 11 & 8 \\ 8 & 11 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$
$P = [12, 16, 11]$

**Variables:**
$x_{ij} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ for $i \in I, j \in J$

**Objective:**
Maximize $\sum_{i \in I} \sum_{j \in J} (P_j - C_{ij}) x_{ij}$

**Constraints:**
1.  $\sum_{j \in J} x_{ij} \le S_i, \quad \forall i \in I$
2.  $\sum_{i \in I} x_{ij} \le D_j, \quad \forall j \in J$
3.  $x_{ij} \ge 0, \quad \forall i \in I, j \in J$
