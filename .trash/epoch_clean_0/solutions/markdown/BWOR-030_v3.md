# Operations Research Model: Grain Trading Optimization

## 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
*   $T = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of months (January, February, March).
*   $t \in T$: Current month index.

**Parameters:**
*   $C_{wh} = 5000$: Warehouse capacity (dan).
*   $I_0 = 1000$: Initial inventory in stock at the beginning of Month 1 (dan).
*   $K_0 = 20000$: Available capital at the beginning of Month 1 (yuan).
*   $I_{target} = 2000$: Required ending inventory at the end of Month 3 (dan).
*   $p_t$: Purchase price in month $t$ (yuan/dan).
    *   $p_1 = 2.85$
    *   $p_2 = 3.05$
    *   $p_3 = 2.90$
*   $s_t$: Selling price in month $t$ (yuan/dan).
    *   $s_1 = 3.10$
    *   $s_2 = 3.25$
    *   $s_3 = 2.95$
*   $M = 10000$: A large constant (Big-M) used for logical constraints (upper bound on inventory).

## 2. Decision Variables

Let the decision variables be continuous (LP relaxation is standard for aggregate trading volumes):

*   $x_t \ge 0$: Quantity of grain purchased in month $t$ (dan).
*   $y_t \ge 0$: Quantity of grain sold in month $t$ (dan).
    *   *Note*: $y_t$ represents sales occurring in month $t$. Due to the "sold starting in the next month" rule, sales in month $t$ must come from inventory available at the start of month $t$ (which is generated by purchases in $t-1$ and prior).
*   $I_t \ge 0$: Inventory level at the **end** of month $t$ (dan).

## 3. Objective Function

The objective is to **Maximize** the net profit (or equivalently, the final wealth/cash position, assuming no other costs).

Profit is calculated as Total Revenue from Sales minus Total Cost of Purchases.

$$ \text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{t=1}^{3} (s_t y_t - p_t x_t) $$

*Note: Since initial capital and initial inventory are fixed constants, maximizing profit is equivalent to maximizing final cash balance.*

## 4. Operational Constraints

### A. Inventory Balance Constraints
The inventory at the end of month $t$ is determined by the inventory at the start of month $t$, plus purchases made in month $t$, minus sales made in month $t$.

*   **Month 1:**
    $$ I_1 = I_0 + x_1 - y_1 $$
    $$ I_1 = 1000 + x_1 - y_1 $$

*   **Month 2:**
    $$ I_2 = I_1 + x_2 - y_2 $$

*   **Month 3:**
    $$ I_3 = I_2 + x_3 - y_3 $$

### B. Warehouse Capacity Constraints
Inventory at the end of each month cannot exceed warehouse capacity.
$$ I_t \le C_{wh} \quad \forall t \in T $$
$$ I_1 \le 5000 $$
$$ I_2 \le 5000 $$
$$ I_3 \le 5000 $$

### C. Target Ending Inventory Constraint
The problem specifies a target inventory at the end of March. In optimization contexts, "target" usually implies an equality constraint unless a penalty is specified. Given the phrasing "The target inventory... is 2,000 dan", we model this as an equality to ensure the final state meets the requirement.
$$ I_3 = 2000 $$

### D. Sales Feasibility Constraints (Lag Constraint)
Grain purchased in month $t$ cannot be sold until month $t+1$. This implies that sales in month $t$ ($y_t$) must be covered by the inventory available *before* the month starts (i.e., end of month $t-1$) or purchases from previous months that are still in stock.

Mathematically, this is naturally handled if we define $I_{t-1}$ as the available stock. However, we must explicitly ensure that we do not sell more than what is physically present *at the time of sale*.
Since $I_{t-1}$ represents the stock available at the start of month $t$, the constraint is:
$$ y_t \le I_{t-1} \quad \forall t \in T $$

Where:
*   $I_0 = 1000$ (Given initial stock)
*   $I_1, I_2$ are variables defined above.

So:
1.  $y_1 \le 1000$
2.  $y_2 \le I_1$
3.  $y_3 \le I_2$

*Verification*: Does this correctly model "purchased in month $t$, sold in $t+1$"?
Yes. $x_t$ adds to $I_t$. $I_t$ is available for sale as $y_{t+1}$ because $y_{t+1} \le I_t$. Thus, grain bought in month 1 contributes to $I_1$ and can be sold as part of $y_2$. Grain bought in month 2 contributes to $I_2$ and can be sold as part of $y_3$. Grain bought in month 3 contributes to $I_3$ and cannot be sold in the quarter (since $y_4$ is out of scope).

### E. Cash Flow / Capital Constraints
The company starts with 20,000 yuan. Cash is paid upon delivery (purchase). Cash is received upon sale.
We must ensure that at no point does the cash balance drop below zero.

Let $Cash_t$ be the cash available at the **end** of month $t$ (or available for use in month $t+1$).
Actually, it is safer to constrain the cumulative cash flow at the *beginning* of the month before the transaction, or ensure cumulative cash flow remains non-negative at all times.

Let's define the Cash Balance at the end of month $t$, denoted $C_t$.
$C_0 = 20000$.

Cash flow in month $t$:
*   Outflow: Purchase cost $p_t x_t$.
*   Inflow: Sale revenue $s_t y_t$.

Constraint: The cash available at the start of month $t$ must cover the purchases.
Start Cash of Month $t$ = End Cash of Month $t-1$.
$C_{t-1} - p_t x_t + s_t y_t = C_t$
And $C_t \ge 0$.

Alternatively, to avoid auxiliary variables $C_t$, we can write cumulative constraints:
The cumulative cash outflow for purchases minus cumulative cash inflow from sales cannot exceed the initial capital at any point in time.

1.  **End of Month 1 (or during Month 1):**
    $$ -p_1 x_1 + s_1 y_1 + K_0 \ge 0 $$
    $$ 20000 - 2.85 x_1 + 3.10 y_1 \ge 0 $$

2.  **End of Month 2 (or during Month 2):**
    $$ K_0 - (p_1 x_1 + p_2 x_2) + (s_1 y_1 + s_2 y_2) \ge 0 $$
    $$ 20000 - 2.85 x_1 - 3.05 x_2 + 3.10 y_1 + 3.25 y_2 \ge 0 $$

3.  **End of Month 3 (or during Month 3):**
    $$ K_0 - (p_1 x_1 + p_2 x_2 + p_3 x_3) + (s_1 y_1 + s_2 y_2 + s_3 y_3) \ge 0 $$
    $$ 20000 - 2.85 x_1 - 3.05 x_2 - 2.90 x_3 + 3.10 y_1 + 3.25 y_2 + 2.95 y_3 \ge 0 $$

*Note on Timing*: "Cash payment upon delivery". This means cash leaves the account when $x_t$ is incurred. Cash enters when $y_t$ is incurred. The constraints above ensure that the net cash position never goes negative at the end of each period. In practice, one might argue cash is needed *at the start* of the month to pay for purchases. However, since sales and purchases happen within the month, and we assume perfect liquidity management within the month (or that sales proceeds can be used for purchases within the same month if timing allows), the end-of-period constraint is standard for LP models unless intra-month sequencing is specified. Given "Cash payment upon delivery", it is safest to assume the net cash flow constraint applies to the cumulative position.

*Refinement on Cash Constraint Logic*:
If purchases happen at the start of the month and sales at the end, the cash constraint for month $t$ would be:
$Cash_{start, t} \ge p_t x_t$.
$Cash_{start, 1} = 20000$.
$Cash_{start, 2} = 20000 - 2.85 x_1 + 3.10 y_1$.
$Cash_{start, 3} = Cash_{start, 2} - 3.05 x_2 + 3.25 y_2$.

This leads to:
1.  $20000 \ge 2.85 x_1$
2.  $20000 - 2.85 x_1 + 3.10 y_1 \ge 3.05 x_2$
3.  $20000 - 2.85 x_1 + 3.10 y_1 - 3.05 x_2 + 3.25 y_2 \ge 2.90 x_3$

This "Start of Month" cash availability constraint is stricter and more physically accurate for "payment upon delivery" if delivery occurs at the start of the month. If delivery/sale timing is flexible within the month, the cumulative net cash constraint (Section E above) is appropriate. Given standard LP formulations for such problems, the **cumulative net cash constraint** (ensuring final cash $\ge 0$ and intermediate cash $\ge 0$) is often used. However, the "Start of Month" constraint prevents borrowing. Let's use the **Start of Month** constraints as they are more robust against intra-month timing ambiguities.

**Revised Cash Constraints:**
1.  $2.85 x_1 \le 20000$
2.  $3.05 x_2 \le 20000 - 2.85 x_1 + 3.10 y_1$
3.  $2.90 x_3 \le 20000 - 2.85 x_1 + 3.10 y_1 - 3.05 x_2 + 3.25 y_2$

### F. Non-negativity
$$ x_t \ge 0, \quad y_t \ge 0, \quad I_t \ge 0 \quad \forall t \in T $$

---

### Final Model Summary

**Maximize:**
$$ Z = 3.10 y_1 + 3.25 y_2 + 2.95 y_3 - 2.85 x_1 - 3.05 x_2 - 2.90 x_3 $$

**Subject to:**

1.  **Inventory Dynamics:**
    $$ I_1 - x_1 + y_1 = 1000 $$
    $$ I_2 - I_1 - x_2 + y_2 = 0 $$
    $$ I_3 - I_2 - x_3 + y_3 = 0 $$

2.  **Target Inventory:**
    $$ I_3 = 2000 $$

3.  **Warehouse Capacity:**
    $$ I_1 \le 5000 $$
    $$ I_2 \le 5000 $$
    $$ I_3 \le 5000 $$

4.  **Sales Feasibility (Lag):**
    $$ y_1 \le 1000 $$
    $$ y_2 \le I_1 $$
    $$ y_3 \le I_2 $$

5.  **Cash Flow (No Borrowing):**
    $$ 2.85 x_1 \le 20000 $$
    $$ 3.05 x_2 - 3.10 y_1 \le 20000 - 2.85 x_1 $$
    $$ 2.90 x_3 - 3.25 y_2 \le 20000 - 2.85 x_1 + 3.10 y_1 - 3.05 x_2 $$

6.  **Non-negativity:**
    $$ x_1, x_2, x_3, y_1, y_2, y_3, I_1, I_2, I_3 \ge 0 $$
