**Model Type:** MILP (Mixed Integer Linear Programming)

### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
- $J = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$ : Set of jobs.
- $K = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$ : Set of sequence positions (1 is first, 6 is last).

**Parameters:**
- $A_j$ : Processing time of job $j$ on Machine A (minutes).
  - $A_1 = 30, A_2 = 60, A_3 = 60, A_4 = 20, A_5 = 80, A_6 = 90$
- $B_j$ : Processing time of job $j$ on Machine B (minutes).
  - $B_1 = 70, B_2 = 70, B_3 = 50, B_4 = 60, B_5 = 30, B_6 = 40$

### 2. Decision Variables

- $x_{j,k} \in \{0, 1\}$ : Binary variable where $x_{j,k} = 1$ if job $j$ is assigned to position $k$ in the sequence, and 0 otherwise.
- $T_{A,k} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ : Cumulative completion time of the job in position $k$ on Machine A.
- $T_{B,k} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ : Completion time of the job in position $k$ on Machine B.
- $C_{max} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ : Total makespan (maximum completion time of all jobs on Machine B).

### 3. Objective Function

Minimize the total processing time (makespan):
$$ \min Z = C_{max} $$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**1. Assignment Constraints:**
Each job must be assigned to exactly one position, and each position must contain exactly one job.
$$ \sum_{k \in K} x_{j,k} = 1 \quad \forall j \in J $$
$$ \sum_{j \in J} x_{j,k} = 1 \quad \forall k \in K $$

**2. Machine A Timing Constraints:**
The completion time on Machine A for the job at position $k$ is the sum of its processing time on A and the completion time of the previous job on A.
$$ T_{A,k} = \sum_{j \in J} A_j x_{j,k} + T_{A,k-1} \quad \forall k \in K, k > 1 $$
$$ T_{A,1} = \sum_{j \in J} A_j x_{j,1} $$

**3. Machine B Timing Constraints:**
The start time of the job at position $k$ on Machine B is the maximum of:
1. The completion time of the previous job on Machine B ($T_{B,k-1}$).
2. The completion time of the current job on Machine A ($T_{A,k}$).

The completion time on B is the start time plus the processing time on B. To linearize the `max` function, we use the following formulation:
$$ T_{B,k} \ge T_{B,k-1} + \sum_{j \in J} B_j x_{j,k} \quad \forall k \in K, k > 1 $$
$$ T_{B,k} \ge T_{A,k} + \sum_{j \in J} B_j x_{j,k} \quad \forall k \in K $$
$$ T_{B,1} = T_{A,1} + \sum_{j \in J} B_j x_{j,1} $$

*Note: The standard linearization for the recurrence relation of a 2-machine flow shop is often expressed as:*
$$ T_{B,k} = \max(T_{B,k-1}, T_{A,k}) + B_{job\_at\_k} $$
Using the inequality constraints above with a minimization objective ensures that $T_{B,k}$ takes the minimal feasible value, effectively computing the maximum.

**4. Makespan Definition:**
The makespan is the completion time of the last job on Machine B.
$$ C_{max} \ge T_{B,k} \quad \forall k \in K $$
*(Since we minimize $C_{max}$, it will equal $T_{B,6}$)*.

### Feasibility Sanity Check
- Total processing on A: $30+60+60+20+80+90 = 340$ minutes.
- Total processing on B: $70+70+50+60+30+40 = 320$ minutes.
- The makespan will be at least $\max(340, 320) = 340$ minutes.
- The model structure is a standard Flow Shop Scheduling Problem (FSP), which is NP-hard but solvable as an MILP for small $N$ (here $N=6$).

2. Find the minimum time among all remaining times.
3. If the minimum is on Machine A, schedule that job as early as possible.
4. If the minimum is on Machine B, schedule that job as late as possible.
5. Repeat until all jobs are scheduled.
