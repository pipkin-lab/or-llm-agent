To solve the problem of minimizing the total number of nurses required under **Plan 1**, we must first understand the structure of the scheduling plan and how it maps to the daily demand.

### 1. Problem Analysis & Model Type

**Model Type:** Linear Programming (LP) / Integer Programming (IP)
*   Since we are dealing with discrete nurses, strictly speaking, this is an Integer Program. However, due to the structure of the constraints (a standard shift scheduling problem with a cyclic pattern), the constraint matrix is often totally unimodular or close to it, allowing for integer solutions via LP relaxation. We will formulate it as an IP for correctness.

**Understanding Plan 1:**
*   **Cycle:** Each nurse works 5 consecutive days and rests 2 days.
*   **Shift Rotation:** A nurse cycles through Shifts 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 in sequence on their working days.
    *   Day 1 of work: Shift 1
    *   Day 2 of work: Shift 2
    *   Day 3 of work: Shift 3
    *   Day 4 of work: Shift 4
    *   Day 5 of work: Shift 5
    *   Day 6-7: Off
*   **Weekly Cycle:** The schedule repeats every week (7 days).
*   **Objective:** Minimize the total number of nurses in the hospital's workforce.

**Key Insight:**
Let $N_j$ be the number of nurses who start their 5-day work cycle on day $j$ (where $j \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7\}$ corresponding to Monday through Sunday).
The total number of nurses is $N = \sum_{j=1}^{7} N_j$.
We need to determine how many nurses are on duty for each **Time Period** on each **Day**.

Let $x_{j,k}$ be the number of nurses from the group starting on day $j$ who are working on day $k$ of the week. However, it is more efficient to define variables based on the **starting day** of the 5-day block.

A nurse starting on day $j$ works on days $j, j+1, j+2, j+3, j+4$ (mod 7).
On their $k$-th working day (where $k=1$ is the first day of the block), they work Shift $k$.

*   Nurses working Shift $s$ on day $d$ are those who started their block $s-1$ days ago.

## Mathematical Formulation

### Model Type
Integer Linear Programming (ILP)

### Index Sets & Parameters

**Sets:**
*   $D = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7\}$: Set of days in the weekly cycle (1=Monday, ..., 7=Sunday).
*   $P = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$: Set of daily time periods.

**Parameters:**
*   $Req_{d,p}$: Minimum number of nurses required on day $d$ in period $p$.
    *   $Req_{d,1} = 18$ for all $d$
    *   $Req_{d,2} = 20$ for all $d$
    *   $Req_{d,3} = 19$ for all $d$
    *   $Req_{d,4} = 17$ for all $d$
    *   $Req_{d,5} = 12$ for all $d$

### Decision Variables

*   $N_j$: Number of nurses who begin their 5-day work cycle on day $j$ (where $j \in D$).
    *   Domain: $N_j \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ (Non-negative integers).

### Objective Function

Minimize the total number of nurses:
$$ \text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{j=1}^{7} N_j $$

### Operational Constraints

For each day $d \in D$, the following constraints must hold. Indices are taken modulo 7 (i.e., $N_0 = N_7$, $N_{-1} = N_6$, etc., or explicitly mapped as $N_{(d-k) \pmod 7 + 1}$).

1.  **Period 1 (6:00–10:00) Coverage:**
    Nurses on Shift 1 (started on day $d$) and Shift 2 (started on day $d-1$) are on duty.
    $$ N_d + N_{d-1} \ge 18, \quad \forall d \in D $$

2.  **Period 2 (10:00–14:00) Coverage:**
    Nurses on Shift 2 (started on day $d-1$) and Shift 3 (started on day $d-2$) are on duty.
    $$ N_{d-1} + N_{d-2} \ge 20, \quad \forall d \in D $$

3.  **Period 3 (14:00–18:00) Coverage:**
    Nurses on Shift 3 (started on day $d-2$) and Shift 4 (started on day $d-3$) are on duty.
    $$ N_{d-2} + N_{d-3} \ge 19, \quad \forall d \in D $$

4.  **Period 4 (18:00–22:00) Coverage:**
    Nurses on Shift 4 (started on day $d-3$) and Shift 5 (started on day $d-4$) are on duty.
    $$ N_{d-3} + N_{d-4} \ge 17, \quad \forall d \in D $$

5.  **Period 5 (22:00–06:00) Coverage:**
    This period is split into two segments with different shift assignments:
    *   **Segment 5a (22:00–02:00):** Covered only by Shift 5 (started on day $d-4$).
        $$ N_{d-4} \ge 12, \quad \forall d \in D $$
    *   **Segment 5b (02:00–06:00):** Covered only by Shift 1 of the *next* day (started on day $d+1$).
        $$ N_{d+1} \ge 12, \quad \forall d \in D $$
