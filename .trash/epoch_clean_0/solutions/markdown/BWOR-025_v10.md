### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
- $J = \{1, 2, \dots, 12\}$: Set of jobs.
- $P = \{1, 2, \dots, 12\}$: Set of positions in the sequence (1 is first, 12 is last).

**Parameters:**
- $a_i$: Processing time of job $i$ on Machine A.
  - $a = [5, 8, 11, 2, 7, 6, 3, 9, 8, 3, 6, 10]$ for jobs $1$ to $12$.
- $b_i$: Processing time of job $i$ on Machine B.
  - $b = [5, 9, 4, 3, 7, 9, 5, 4, 9, 5, 3, 4]$ for jobs $1$ to $12$.
- $M$: A sufficiently large constant for Big-M constraints.
  - Let $M = \sum_{j \in J} (a_j + b_j) = \sum a + \sum b = 78 + 68 = 146$.
  - Alternatively, a tighter Big-M can be used, but $M=146$ is safe and simple.

### 2. Decision Variables

- $x_{j,p} \in \{0, 1\}$: Binary variable where $x_{j,p} = 1$ if job $j$ is scheduled at position $p$, and $0$ otherwise.
- $S^A_p \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$: Continuous variable representing the start time of the job at position $p$ on Machine A.
- $C^A_p \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$: Continuous variable representing the completion time of the job at position $p$ on Machine A.
- $S^B_p \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$: Continuous variable representing the start time of the job at position $p$ on Machine B.
- $C^B_p \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$: Continuous variable representing the completion time of the job at position $p$ on Machine B.
- $Z \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$: Continuous variable representing the total makespan (completion time of the last job on Machine B).

*Note: Since the problem involves sequencing discrete jobs (12 jobs), the assignment variables $x_{j,p}$ must be binary. The time variables are continuous as times can be fractional in the model formulation, though with integer processing times, optimal integer solutions usually exist. However, standard flow-shop formulations typically allow continuous time variables.*

### 3. Objective Function

Minimize the makespan $Z$:

$$
\min Z
$$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**Assignment Constraints:**
Each job must be assigned to exactly one position:
$$
\sum_{p \in P} x_{j,p} = 1 \quad \forall j \in J
$$

Each position must be assigned exactly one job:
$$
\sum_{j \in J} x_{j,p} = 1 \quad \forall p \in P
$$

**Machine A Sequencing & Timing:**
Since Machine A processes jobs sequentially from position 1 to 12 with no idle time required between jobs (in an optimal makespan schedule for 2-machine flow shop, we can assume no unnecessary idle time on A, or rather, the start time of the next job on A is constrained by the completion of the previous one).
Actually, it is more robust to define:
1. The job at position $p$ starts on A immediately after the job at position $p-1$ completes on A (for $p>1$).
2. The job at position 1 starts at time 0.

$$
S^A_1 = 0
$$
$$
S^A_p = C^A_{p-1} \quad \forall p \in P, p > 1
$$
$$
C^A_p = S^A_p + \sum_{j \in J} a_j x_{j,p} \quad \forall p \in P
$$

**Machine B Sequencing & Timing:**
1. A job at position $p$ cannot start on Machine B until it has completed on Machine A.
   $$
   S^B_p \ge C^A_p \quad \forall p \in P
   $$
2. Machine B processes jobs sequentially. A job at position $p$ cannot start on Machine B before the job at position $p-1$ has completed on Machine B.
   $$
   S^B_p \ge C^B_{p-1} \quad \forall p \in P, p > 1
   $$
   *(Note: For $p=1$, $C^B_0 = 0$, so $S^B_1 \ge 0$ is implied by non-negativity, but we can keep the constraint structure consistent or just rely on $S^B_1 \ge C^A_1$).*
   Let's refine the B constraints to be strictly linear without logical ORs by using the sequential nature of the fixed positions. Since the order is fixed by position $p$, the precedence on Machine B is simply $p-1 \to p$.
   
   So, for $p > 1$:
   $$
   S^B_p = \max(C^A_p, C^B_{p-1})
   $$
   This max operation is non-linear. We linearize it using an auxiliary variable or big-M, but since we are minimizing makespan and $S^B_p$ only appears in the constraint $C^B_p = S^B_p + \dots$ and $S^B_{p+1} \ge C^B_p$, we can use the following linear constraints:
   
   $$
   S^B_p \ge C^A_p \quad \forall p \in P
   $$
   $$
   S^B_p \ge C^B_{p-1} \quad \forall p \in P, p > 1
   $$
   $$
   C^B_p = S^B_p + \sum_{j \in J} b_j x_{j,p} \quad \forall p \in P
   $$
   
   *Wait*, is $S^B_p$ determined uniquely by these inequalities?
   In a minimization problem for makespan, the start time $S^B_p$ will be pushed as early as possible. However, the solver needs to "know" that $S^B_p$ is the *minimum* of the upper bounds? No, $S^B_p$ is a variable. The constraints say $S^B_p$ must be *at least* $C^A_p$ and *at least* $C^B_{p-1}$.
   Does the objective push $S^B_p$ down?
   $Z \ge C^B_{12}$.
   $C^B_{12} = S^B_{12} + b_{job@12}$.
   $S^B_{12} \ge C^B_{11}$ and $S^B_{12} \ge C^A_{12}$.
   To minimize $Z$, we want $C^B_{12}$ small, which means $S^B_{12}$ small.
   The constraints $S^B_{12} \ge \dots$ set a lower bound. The solver will set $S^B_{12}$ to the maximum of its lower bounds?
   Actually, standard LP solvers minimize the objective. If $S^B_p$ only appears in "greater than or equal to" constraints and in the objective chain (via $C^B$), the solver will set $S^B_p$ to the *smallest* feasible value, which is $\max(C^A_p, C^B_{p-1})$.
   So the linear formulation is:
   
   1. $S^B_p \ge C^A_p$ for all $p$.
   2. $S^B_p \ge C^B_{p-1}$ for all $p > 1$.
   3. $C^B_p = S^B_p + \sum_{j} b_j x_{j,p}$.
   
   This correctly models the start time as the earliest possible time.

**Makespan Constraint:**
The makespan is the completion time of the last job on Machine B.
$$
Z \ge C^B_{12}
$$
Since we are minimizing $Z$, and $Z$ has no other cost, $Z$ will equal $C^B_{12}$ at optimality.

**Summary of Time Constraints:**
1. $S^A_1 = 0$
2. $C^A_p = S^A_p + \sum_{j \in J} a_j x_{j,p}$ for all $p$
3. $S^A_p = C^A_{p-1}$ for $p=2,\dots,12$
4. $S^B_p \ge C^A_p$ for all $p$
5. $S^B_p \ge C^B_{p-1}$ for $p=2,\dots,12$
6. $C^B_p = S^B_p + \sum_{j \in J} b_j x_{j,p}$ for all $p$
7. $Z = C^B_{12}$ (or $Z \ge C^B_{12}$)

**Variable Bounds:**
- $x_{j,p} \in \{0, 1\}$
- $S^A_p, C^A_p, S^B_p, C^B_p, Z \ge 0$
