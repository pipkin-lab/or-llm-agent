*   For Jobs $j \in \{8, \dots, 12\}$: Process on B, then A.

### 2. Decision Variables

*   $x_{jk} \in \{0, 1\}$: Binary variable where $x_{jk} = 1$ if job $j$ is scheduled at position $k$ in the sequence, and 0 otherwise. Here $k \in \{1, \dots, 12\}$ represents the position in the processing order.
*   $C_{j,A}$: Continuous variable representing the completion time of job $j$ on Machine A ($C_{j,A} \ge 0$).
*   $C_{j,B}$: Continuous variable representing the completion time of job $j$ on Machine B ($C_{j,B} \ge 0$).
*   $T_{A,k}$: Continuous variable representing the total accumulated processing time on Machine A up to position $k$.
*   $T_{B,k}$: Continuous variable representing the total accumulated processing time on Machine B up to position $k$.
*   $Z$: Continuous variable representing the Makespan (total processing time).

### 3. Objective Function

Minimize the makespan $Z$:
$$ \text{Minimize } Z $$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**1. Assignment Constraints (Each job has exactly one position, each position has exactly one job):**
$$ \sum_{k=1}^{12} x_{jk} = 1 \quad \forall j \in J $$
$$ \sum_{j \in J} x_{jk} = 1 \quad \forall k \in \{1, \dots, 12\} $$

**2. Machine Load Accumulation (Defining $T_{A,k}$ and $T_{B,k}$):**
These constraints ensure that $T_{A,k}$ and $T_{B,k}$ represent the sum of processing times of the jobs assigned to positions $1$ through $k$.
$$ T_{A,k} = \sum_{h=1}^{k} \sum_{j \in J} p_{j,A} x_{jh} \quad \forall k \in \{1, \dots, 12\} $$
$$ T_{B,k} = \sum_{h=1}^{k} \sum_{j \in J} p_{j,B} x_{jh} \quad \forall k \in \{1, \dots, 12\} $$

*Note: These are linear constraints if expanded, or can be left as definitions linked to the binary variables.*

**3. Precedence and Sequencing Constraints:**
For any job $j$ assigned to position $k$ ($x_{jk}=1$), its start and completion times on each machine are determined by the cumulative times of previous jobs and the routing logic.

*Case 1: Jobs $j \in \{1, \dots, 7\}$ (Route A $\to$ B)*
If job $j$ is at position $k$:
*   Start on A: $S_{j,A} = T_{A,k-1}$ (where $T_{A,0}=0$)
*   Completion on A: $C_{j,A} = T_{A,k}$
*   Start on B: Must be after completion on A AND after previous job finished on B.
    *   $S_{j,B} = \max(C_{j,A}, T_{B,k-1})$
    *   $C_{j,B} = S_{j,B} + p_{j,B}$

To linearize the $\max$ function, we can use the following constraints for all $j \in \{1,\dots,7\}$ and $k \in \{1,\dots,12\}$:
$$ C_{j,A} \le T_{A,k} + M(1 - x_{jk}) $$
$$ C_{j,A} \ge T_{A,k} - M(1 - x_{jk}) $$
*(This enforces $C_{j,A} = T_{A,k}$ if $x_{jk}=1$)*

$$ C_{j,B} \ge C_{j,A} + p_{j,B} - M(1 - x_{jk}) $$
$$ C_{j,B} \ge T_{B,k-1} + p_{j,B} - M(1 - x_{jk}) $$
$$ C_{j,B} \le T_{B,k-1} + p_{j,B} + C_{j,A} - T_{A,k} + M(1 - x_{jk}) $$ 
*(Note: The third constraint above is a linearization of $C_{j,B} = \max(C_{j,A}, T_{B,k-1}) + p_{j,B}$. A simpler standard formulation for two-machine flow/permutation shop is often preferred to avoid large M values if possible, but for a general MILP formulation, the following "Big-M" logic holds:)*

Let's use a cleaner standard formulation for the completion times based on the sequence position $k$:

For $j \in \{1, \dots, 7\}$ (A then B):
1.  **Completion on A**:
    $$ C_{j,A} = \sum_{h=1}^{k} p_{j,A} x_{jh} $$
    *(Wait, this is not linear if $j$ is fixed. Better to stick to the definition: If job $j$ is at position $k$, $C_{j,A}$ is the sum of processing times of all jobs $i$ at positions $h \le k$ on machine A.)*
    
    Let's refine the variables to be more direct for the solver.
    Let $y_{j,k}$ be binary, 1 if job $j$ is at position $k$.
    
    **Revised Linear Constraints:**

    **A. Position Assignment:**
    $$ \sum_{k=1}^{12} y_{j,k} = 1 \quad \forall j \in J $$
    $$ \sum_{j \in J} y_{j,k} = 1 \quad \forall k \in \{1, \dots, 12\} $$

    **B. Machine A Completion Times ($C_{j,A}$):**
    For any job $j$, if it is at position $k$, its completion time on A is the sum of processing times of all jobs $i$ assigned to positions $1 \dots k$ on A.
    $$ C_{j,A} \ge \sum_{i \in J} \sum_{h=1}^{k} p_{i,A} y_{i,h} - M(1 - y_{j,k}) \quad \forall j, k $$
    $$ C_{j,A} \le \sum_{i \in J} \sum_{h=1}^{k} p_{i,A} y_{i,h} + M(1 - y_{j,k}) \quad \forall j, k $$
    *(This ensures if $y_{j,k}=1$, $C_{j,A}$ equals the cumulative load of A up to $k$. Note: Since only one job is at position $k$, this effectively sets $C_{j,A}$ for the job at that slot.)*

    **C. Machine B Completion Times ($C_{j,B}$):**
    The start time of job $j$ on B depends on its routing.
    
    *For Jobs $j \in \{1, \dots, 7\}$ (A $\to$ B):*
    Job $j$ can start on B only after it finishes on A ($C_{j,A}$) AND after the previous job in sequence (position $k-1$) finishes on B.
    Let $k$ be the position of job $j$.
    $$ C_{j,B} \ge C_{j,A} + p_{j,B} - M(1 - y_{j,k}) $$
    $$ C_{j,B} \ge C_{prev,B} + p_{j,B} - M(1 - y_{j,k}) $$
    Where $C_{prev,B}$ is the completion time of the job at position $k-1$ on B.
    To linearize without recursive dependency on specific job identities, we can use the cumulative time variables $T_{B,k}$.
    
    Let $T_{B,k}$ be the time machine B becomes free after processing the job at position $k$.
    $T_{B,k} = \max(T_{B,k-1}, C_{j,A}) + p_{j,B}$ where $j$ is the job at position $k$.
    
    This max function is tricky. However, for a 2-machine problem, a common trick is to use the fact that the makespan is the max of the completion times.
    
    Let's use a more robust formulation using start times $S_{j,M}$.
    
    **Simplified Robust Formulation:**
    
    Variables:
    *   $y_{j,k} \in \{0,1\}$: Job $j$ at position $k$.
    *   $S_{j,A}, S_{j,B}$: Start times of job $j$ on machines A and B.
    *   $Z$: Makespan.
    
    Constraints:
    1.  **Assignment**: As above.
    2.  **Precedence within Job**:
        *   For $j \in \{1,\dots,7\}$: $S_{j,B} \ge S_{j,A} + p_{j,A}$
        *   For $j \in \{8,\dots,12\}$: $S_{j,A} \ge S_{j,B} + p_{j,B}$
    3.  **Machine Capacity (Non-overlap)**:
        For any pair of distinct jobs $i, j$ and machine $M \in \{A,B\}$:
        Either $i$ is before $j$ OR $j$ is before $i$.
        Let $u_{ij}^M \in \{0,1\}$ be 1 if $i$ is before $j$ on machine $M$.
        $S_{j,M} \ge S_{i,M} + p_{i,M} - M(1 - u_{ij}^M)$
        $S_{i,M} \ge S_{j,M} + p_{j,M} - M u_{ij}^M$
        $u_{ij}^M + u_{ji}^M = 1$
        
        *Crucial Link*: The sequence on A and B is the **same** (permutation schedule). Therefore, the relative order of $i$ and $j$ on A must be the same as on B.
        Let $z_{ij} \in \{0,1\}$ be 1 if job $i$ precedes job $j$ in the global sequence.
        $S_{j,A} \ge S_{i,A} + p_{i,A} - M(1 - z_{ij})$
        $S_{i,A} \ge S_{j,A} + p_{j,A} - M z_{ij}$
        $S_{j,B} \ge S_{i,B} + p_{i,B} - M(1 - z_{ij})$
        $S_{i,B} \ge S_{j,B} + p_{j,B} - M z_{ij}$
        
    4.  **Objective**:
        $$ Z \ge S_{j,A} + p_{j,A} \quad \forall j $$
        $$ Z \ge S_{j,B} + p_{j,B} \quad \forall j $$
        Minimize $Z$.

    **Parameter M**: $M$ can be set to the sum of all processing times on the respective machine.
    $M_A = \sum p_{j,A} = 5+8+11+2+7+6+3+9+8+3+6+10 = 78$
    $M_B = \sum p_{j,B} = 5+9+4+3+7+9+5+4+9+5+3+4 = 67$
    Use $M = 78$ for all Big-M constraints involving time.

    **Final Check on "Total Processing Time"**:
    The problem asks to minimize "total processing time" and compute "makespan". In scheduling, "minimizing total processing time" usually refers to Makespan ($C_{max}$) for the whole system, as the sum of processing times is constant regardless of sequence. The prompt explicitly says "determine the optimal job sequence... and compute the makespan", confirming the objective is Makespan.

### Final Model Formulation

**Model Type:** MILP

**Sets:**
*   $J = \{1, \dots, 12\}$
*   $P = \{1, \dots, 12\}$ (Positions)

**Parameters:**
*   $p_{j,A}$: Processing time of job $j$ on Machine A.
*   $p_{j,B}$: Processing time of job $j$ on Machine B.
*   $M = 100$ (A sufficiently large constant, e.g., sum of all times + 1).

**Variables:**
*   $x_{j,k} \in \{0, 1\}$: 1 if job $j$ is scheduled at position $k$.
*   $s_{j,A} \ge 0$: Start time of job $j$ on Machine A.
*   $s_{j,B} \ge 0$: Start time of job $j$ on Machine B.
*   $z_{i,k} \in \{0, 1\}$: 1 if job at position $i$ precedes job at position $k$ in the global sequence (for $i < k$). *Actually, it is simpler to define precedence between jobs directly.*
    Let $u_{ij} \in \{0, 1\}$: 1 if job $i$ precedes job $j$ in the sequence.
*   $C_{max}$: Makespan.

**Objective:**
$$ \text{Minimize } C_{max} $$

**Constraints:**

1.  **Assignment:**
    $$ \sum_{k=1}^{12} x_{j,k} = 1 \quad \forall j \in J $$
    $$ \sum_{j \in J} x_{j,k} = 1 \quad \forall k \in P $$

2.  **Precedence Consistency (Permutation Schedule):**
    The relative order of any two jobs $i, j$ must be the same on both machines.
    $$ u_{ij} + u_{ji} = 1 \quad \forall i, j \in J, i < j $$
    
    *   **Machine A Sequencing:**
        $$ s_{j,A} \ge s_{i,A} + p_{i,A} - M(1 - u_{ij}) \quad \forall i, j \in J, i < j $$
        $$ s_{i,A} \ge s_{j,A} + p_{j,A} - M u_{ij} \quad \forall i, j \in J, i < j $$
        
    *   **Machine B Sequencing:**
        $$ s_{j,B} \ge s_{i,B} + p_{i,B} - M(1 - u_{ij}) \quad \forall i, j \in J, i < j $$
        $$ s_{i,B} \ge s_{j,B} + p_{j,B} - M u_{ij} \quad \forall i, j \in J, i < j $$

3.  **Job Routing (Internal Precedence):**
    *   For $j \in \{1, \dots, 7\}$ (A then B):
        $$ s_{j,B} \ge s_{j,A} + p_{j,A} $$
    *   For $j \in \{8, \dots, 12\}$ (B then A):
        $$ s_{j,A} \ge s_{j,B} + p_{j,B} $$

4.  **Makespan Definition:**
    $$ C_{max} \ge s_{j,A} + p_{j,A} \quad \forall j \in J $$
    $$ C_{max} \ge s_{j,B} + p_{j,B} \quad \forall j \in J $$

5.  **Bounds:**
    $$ s_{j,A}, s_{j,B}, C_{max} \ge 0 $$
    $$ x_{j,k}, u_{ij} \in \{0, 1\} $$

**Note on Solution:**
This is a classic $n \times 2$ flow shop with mixed routing. Johnson's Rule can be adapted or used as a heuristic, but the MILP provides the exact optimal solution. The "optimal job sequence" is derived from the values of $x_{j,k}$.
