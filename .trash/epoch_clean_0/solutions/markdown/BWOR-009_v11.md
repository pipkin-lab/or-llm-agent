## Model Type Declaration
**Model Type:** LP (Linear Programming)

All decision variables are continuous quantities of equipment units. Although the physical units are discrete, in production planning problems of this scale, it is standard practice to treat production and inventory as continuous variables unless integer constraints are explicitly required. The objective and all constraints are linear.

===============================================================================
## Formal Mathematical Formulation

### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
- $T = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$: Set of quarters in the year.

**Parameters (extracted from Table 3-30):**
- $C_t$: Production capacity in quarter $t$ (units).
  - $C_1 = 25$
  - $C_2 = 35$
  - $C_3 = 30$
  - $C_4 = 20$
- $D_t$: Required delivery quantity at the end of quarter $t$ (units).
  - $D_1 = 15$
  - $D_2 = 20$
  - $D_3 = 25$
  - $D_4 = 20$
- $p_t$: Production cost per unit in quarter $t$ (thousand yuan).
  - $p_1 = 12.0$
  - $p_2 = 11.0$
  - $p_3 = 11.5$
  - $p_4 = 12.5$
- $h$: Storage and maintenance fee per unit per quarter (thousand yuan).
  - $h = 0.1$

**Derived Parameter:**
- Total Demand $D_{total} = \sum_{t \in T} D_t = 15 + 20 + 25 + 20 = 80$ units.
- Total Capacity $C_{total} = \sum_{t \in T} C_t = 25 + 35 + 30 + 20 = 110$ units.
- Since $C_{total} > D_{total}$, the problem is feasible.

### 2. Decision Variables

Let:
- $x_t$: Quantity of equipment produced in quarter $t$.
  - Domain: $x_t \ge 0, \forall t \in T$ (Continuous, non-negative)
- $I_t$: Inventory level at the **end** of quarter $t$ (units held for future delivery).
  - Domain: $I_t \ge 0, \forall t \in T$ (Continuous, non-negative)
  - Note: $I_0 = 0$ (Assume no initial inventory).
  - Note: $I_4 = 0$ (All demand must be met by the end of Q4, and excess production is not beneficial due to positive production costs).

### 3. Objective Function

Minimize the total annual cost, which includes production costs and inventory holding costs.

$$
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{t=1}^{4} p_t x_t + \sum_{t=1}^{4} h I_t
$$

Substituting the specific values:
$$
Z = 12.0 x_1 + 11.0 x_2 + 11.5 x_3 + 12.5 x_4 + 0.1(I_1 + I_2 + I_3 + I_4)
$$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**A. Capacity Constraints**
Production in each quarter cannot exceed the available capacity.
$$
x_t \le C_t, \quad \forall t \in T
$$
Specifically:
- $x_1 \le 25$
- $x_2 \le 35$
- $x_3 \le 30$
- $x_4 \le 20$

**B. Inventory Balance Constraints**
The inventory at the end of quarter $t$ equals the previous inventory plus current production minus current delivery.
$$
I_t = I_{t-1} + x_t - D_t, \quad \forall t \in T
$$
With $I_0 = 0$.

Specifically:
- **Quarter 1:** $I_1 = 0 + x_1 - 15 \implies x_1 - I_1 = 15$
- **Quarter 2:** $I_2 = I_1 + x_2 - 20 \implies I_1 - I_2 + x_2 = 20$
- **Quarter 3:** $I_3 = I_2 + x_3 - 25 \implies I_2 - I_3 + x_3 = 25$
- **Quarter 4:** $I_4 = I_3 + x_4 - 20 \implies I_3 - I_4 + x_4 = 20$

**C. Final Inventory Constraint**
All contractual deliveries must be met by the end of the year. We assume no leftover inventory is desired at the end of the planning horizon (any extra production would incur cost without benefit).
$$
I_4 = 0
$$
*(Note: Even if $I_4$ were allowed to be positive, the minimization objective would naturally drive it to 0 because $p_t > 0$ and $h > 0$. However, explicitly setting $I_4=0$ ensures strict satisfaction of the "meeting the delivery contract" condition without carrying over unused stock.)*

**D. Non-negativity Constraints**
$$
x_t \ge 0, \quad I_t \ge 0, \quad \forall t \in T
$$

## Feasibility Sanity Check

1. **Total Supply vs. Demand:**
   - Total Demand = 80 units.
   - Total Capacity = 110 units.
   - Feasible.

2. **Cumulative Demand vs. Cumulative Capacity:**
   - End of Q1: Demand = 15, Capacity = 25. (OK)
   - End of Q2: Demand = 35, Capacity = 60. (OK)
   - End of Q3: Demand = 60, Capacity = 90. (OK)
   - End of Q4: Demand = 80, Capacity = 110. (OK)
   - Since cumulative capacity always exceeds cumulative demand, there is no forced backlogging or infeasibility due to timing. The company can always produce enough to meet current and past demands.

3. **Logic Check:**
   - The model allows production in Q1 to satisfy demand in Q2, Q3, or Q4, incurring storage costs for each quarter held.
   - The model allows production in Q4 to satisfy only Q4 demand (since it's the last period).
   - The objective function correctly penalizes both production (variable cost) and holding (inventory cost).

This formulation is a standard Linear Programming problem solvable by the Simplex method or interior-point algorithms.
