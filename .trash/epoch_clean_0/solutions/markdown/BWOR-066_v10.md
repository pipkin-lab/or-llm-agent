Model Type: MILP

## Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Set:**
- $t \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$: Set of months (January to April).

**Parameters:**
- $S_t$: Buying price in month $t$ (units: currency/unit).
- $P_t$: Selling price in month $t$ (units: currency/unit).
- $W_{max}$: Maximum warehouse capacity (units).
- $I_0$: Initial inventory at the start of Month 1 (units).

**Parameter Values (from Table 7-6 and problem statement):**
- $S_1 = 10, S_2 = 9, S_3 = 11, S_4 = 15$
- $P_1 = 12, P_2 = 9, P_3 = 13, P_4 = 17$
- $W_{max} = 1000$
- $I_0 = 500$

## Decision Variables

Let:
- $x_t$: Quantity of product purchased in month $t$ (Integer, $\ge 0$).
- $y_t$: Quantity of product sold in month $t$ (Integer, $\ge 0$).
- $I_t$: Inventory remaining in the warehouse at the **end** of month $t$ (Integer, $\ge 0$).

*Note on Timing:*
- Purchases made in month $t$ ($x_t$) are added to the inventory available for sale in month $t+1$. Therefore, they contribute to $I_t$ (end of month $t$) but are not available for $y_t$ (sales in month $t$).
- Sales $y_t$ are fulfilled from the inventory available at the beginning of month $t$ (which is $I_{t-1}$, with $I_0$ being the initial stock).

## Objective Function

Maximize total profit:
$$ \text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{t=1}^{4} (P_t y_t - S_t x_t) $$

Substituting values:
$$ \text{Maximize } Z = (12y_1 - 10x_1) + (9y_2 - 9x_2) + (13y_3 - 11x_3) + (17y_4 - 15x_4) $$

## Operational Constraints

**1. Inventory Balance Constraints:**
For each month $t$, the ending inventory $I_t$ equals the beginning inventory ($I_{t-1}$) plus purchases made in the previous month ($x_{t-1}$) minus sales made in the current month ($y_t$).
*Note: Purchases made in month $t$ ($x_t$) arrive at the end of month $t$, so they are included in $I_t$ but were not available for sale during month $t$.*

- **Month 1 ($t=1$):**
  $$ I_1 = I_0 + x_0 - y_1 $$
  Since there is no month 0 purchase, $x_0 = 0$.
  $$ I_1 = 500 - y_1 $$
  *(Note: $x_1$ is purchased in Month 1, arrives end of Month 1, so it is not part of the balance for $I_1$ in the sense of being sold, but physically it is in the warehouse at end of Month 1. Let's refine the balance equation to be precise about physical flow.)*

  **Refined Inventory Logic:**
  - Inventory available for sale in Month $t$ is $I_{t-1}$ (for $t>1$) or $I_0$ (for $t=1$).
  - Sales $y_t$ must satisfy $y_t \le I_{t-1}$ (for $t>1$) and $y_1 \le I_0$.
  - Inventory at the end of Month $t$ ($I_t$) consists of unsold stock from the beginning ($I_{t-1} - y_t$) plus purchases made during Month $t$ ($x_t$) which arrive at the end of the month.
  
  Therefore, the balance equations are:
  - **Month 1:**
    $$ I_1 = I_0 - y_1 + x_1 $$
    $$ I_1 = 500 - y_1 + x_1 $$
  - **Month 2:**
    $$ I_2 = I_1 - y_2 + x_2 $$
  - **Month 3:**
    $$ I_3 = I_2 - y_3 + x_3 $$
  - **Month 4:**
    $$ I_4 = I_3 - y_4 + x_4 $$

**2. Capacity Constraints:**
The warehouse capacity is 1,000 units. This applies to the inventory held at the end of each month.
- $I_1 \le 1000$
- $I_2 \le 1000$
- $I_3 \le 1000$
- $I_4 \le 1000$

**3. Sales Availability Constraints:**
The store can only sell what is in the warehouse at the start of the month.
- **Month 1:** $y_1 \le I_0 \implies y_1 \le 500$
- **Month 2:** $y_2 \le I_1$
- **Month 3:** $y_3 \le I_2$
- **Month 4:** $y_4 \le I_3$

**4. Non-negativity and Integrality Constraints:**
All variables must be non-negative integers.
- $x_t \in \mathbb{Z}^+ \cup \{0\}$ for $t = 1, 2, 3, 4$
- $y_t \in \mathbb{Z}^+ \cup \{0\}$ for $t = 1, 2, 3, 4$
- $I_t \in \mathbb{Z}^+ \cup \{0\}$ for $t = 1, 2, 3, 4$

*(Self-Correction/Verification on Month 4)*:
The problem states the store distributes over the next 4 months. Usually, unless specified that leftover inventory has salvage value or is lost, we assume we want to maximize profit. Purchasing in Month 4 ($x_4$) adds to $I_4$. Since there is no Month 5 to sell this inventory, and storage cost is ignored, buying in Month 4 only incurs cost ($15x_4$) with no revenue. Thus, the optimal solution will naturally set $x_4 = 0$. Similarly, if $I_4$ remains positive, it implies unsold stock. The model allows $I_4 > 0$, but since $x_4$ is costly and $y_4$ is limited by $I_3$, the solver will decide if it's worth holding stock. However, typically in such "4-month horizon" problems, one might argue that inventory left at the end is worthless. The model as formulated allows ending inventory. If the problem implies all goods must be sold or that ending inventory has zero value, the objective function already handles this because holding cost is 0 but purchase cost is incurred. If we bought $x_4$, we pay for it but don't sell it. So $x_4$ will be 0. What about $I_4$ from previous months? If $I_4 > 0$, it represents stock bought in months 1, 2, or 3 that was not sold in months 1, 2, 3, or 4. This is a valid state.

**Final Check on Constraint Logic:**
- $y_1$ is limited by initial stock 500.
- $I_1$ includes $x_1$. So $x_1$ is available for sale in Month 2. Correct.
- $y_2$ is limited by $I_1$. Correct.
- $I_2$ includes $x_2$. So $x_2$ is available for sale in Month 3. Correct.
- And so on.

This formulation accurately captures the "lag" in purchase availability.
