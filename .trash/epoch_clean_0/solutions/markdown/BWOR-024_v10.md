## Model Type: MILP (Mixed Integer Linear Programming)

This is a classic **two-machine flow shop scheduling problem** ($F2||C_{\max}$) with $n=6$ jobs. Each job must be processed first on Machine A, then on Machine B. The objective is to minimize the total processing time (makespan), i.e., the time by which all jobs are completed on Machine B.

We use a **position-based (sequence index) formulation** with binary variables indicating which job is assigned to each position in the sequence.

---

## Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
- $J = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$: set of jobs, $|J| = n = 6$
- $P = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$: set of sequence positions (1st through 6th), $|P| = n = 6$

**Parameters (processing times in minutes):**

| Job $j$ | $a_j$ (Machine A) | $b_j$ (Machine B) |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 1       | 30                | 70                |
| 2       | 60                | 70                |
| 3       | 60                | 50                |
| 4       | 20                | 60                |
| 5       | 80                | 30                |
| 6       | 90                | 40                |

Let $a_j$ and $b_j$ denote the processing time of job $j$ on machines A and B, respectively, as listed above.

---

## Decision Variables

1. **$x_{j,p} \in \{0, 1\}$** for all $j \in J, p \in P$:
   $x_{j,p} = 1$ if job $j$ is assigned to position $p$ in the sequence; $0$ otherwise.

2. **$C_A(p) \geq 0$** (continuous) for all $p \in P$:
   Completion time of the job at position $p$ on Machine A.

3. **$C_B(p) \geq 0$** (continuous) for all $p \in P$:
   Completion time of the job at position $p$ on Machine B.

---

## Objective Function

Minimize the makespan (total processing time until all jobs are finished):

$$\min \; C_B(n) = C_B(6)$$

where $C_B(6)$ is the completion time of the last job (position 6) on Machine B.

---

## Operational Constraints

### 1. Assignment Constraints (each job assigned to exactly one position)

$$\sum_{p=1}^{n} x_{j,p} = 1 \quad \forall \; j \in J$$

### 2. Position Constraints (each position holds exactly one job)

$$\sum_{j \in J} x_{j,p} = 1 \quad \forall \; p \in P$$

### 3. Machine A Completion Times (sequential processing on A)

The job at position $p$ cannot start on A until the job at position $p-1$ finishes on A:

$$C_A(p) \geq C_A(p-1) + \sum_{j \in J} a_j \, x_{j,p} \quad \forall \; p = 2, 3, \ldots, n$$

For the first position:

$$C_A(1) = \sum_{j \in J} a_j \, x_{j,1}$$

### 4. Machine B Completion Times (sequential processing on B with flow-shop precedence)

The job at position $p$ cannot start on B until:
- (a) It finishes on A: $C_A(p)$
- (b) The previous job finishes on B: $C_B(p-1)$

$$C_B(p) \geq C_A(p) + \sum_{j \in J} b_j \, x_{j,p} \quad \forall \; p = 1, \ldots, n$$

$$C_B(p) \geq C_B(p-1) + \sum_{j \in J} b_j \, x_{j,p} \quad \forall \; p = 2, 3, \ldots, n$$

### 5. Non-negativity & Domain Bounds

$$x_{j,p} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall \; j \in J, \; p \in P$$

$$C_A(p) \geq 0 \quad \forall \; p \in P$$

$$C_B(p) \geq 0 \quad \forall \; p \in P$$

---

## Feasibility Sanity Check

- Total processing on A: $30+60+60+20+80+90 = 340$ minutes.
- Total processing on B: $70+70+50+60+30+40 = 320$ minutes.
- Lower bound on makespan: $\max(\sum a_j, \sum b_j) = \max(340, 320) = 340$ minutes.
- The model is feasible; the assignment constraints guarantee a valid permutation of all 6 jobs.

> **Note:** This problem is well-known to be solvable optimally via **Johnson's Rule** for the two-machine case. The MILP formulation above is provided for completeness and generalization. The optimal sequence can be found by:
> - Jobs with $a_j < b_j$ (Jobs 1, 4, 5, 6) sorted ascending by $a_j$: Job 4 (20), Job 1 (30), Job 6 (90), Job 5 (80) → 4, 1, 5, 6
> - Jobs with $a_j \geq b_j$ (Jobs 2, 3) sorted descending by $b_j$: Job 2 (70), Job 3 (50) → 2, 3
> - Optimal sequence: **4, 1, 5, 6, 2, 3**
> - Makespan: compute $C_B(6)$ along this sequence = **340 + max(0, 70+70+50+60+30+40 - (30+60+60+20+80+90) + \text{overlap})$. Direct computation:
>   - A completions: 20, 50, 130, 220, 280, 340
>   - B completions: 60, 130, 160, 220, 290, 330
>   - Wait — let me recompute carefully:
>     - Pos 1 (J4): $C_A=20$, $C_B=20+60=80$
>     - Pos 2 (J1): $C_A=50$, $C_B=\max(50,80)+70=150$
>     - Pos 3 (J5): $C_A=130$, $C_B=\max(130,150)+30=180$
>     - Pos 4 (J6): $C_A=220$, $C_B=\max(220,180)+40=260$
>     - Pos 5 (J2): $C_A=280$, $C_B=\max(280,260)+70=350$
>     - Pos 6 (J3): $C_A=340$, $C_B=\max(340,350)+50=400$
>   - **Optimal makespan = 400 minutes.**
