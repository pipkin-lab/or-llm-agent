# Transportation Problem Formulation

## Model Type
**LP** (Linear Programming). All variables are continuous (tonnage), and the objective and constraints are linear.

## Index Sets & Parameters

**Sets:**
- Warehouses (transit/storage nodes): $i \in I = \{A_1, A_2, A_3\}$
- Regions (demand nodes): $j \in J = \{B_1, B_2, B_3, B_4, B_5\}$

**Parameters (all in tons and cost-units per ton):**

- Factory production capacity: $P = 270$ tons.
- Warehouse storage capacities:
  - $C_{A_1} = 50$
  - $C_{A_2} = 100$
  - $C_{A_3} = 150$
- Regional demands:
  - $D_{B_1} = 25$
  - $D_{B_2} = 105$
  - $D_{B_3} = 60$
  - $D_{B_4} = 30$
  - $D_{B_5} = 70$
  - Total demand $= 25+105+60+30+70 = 290$ tons.
- Total warehouse capacity $= 50+100+150 = 300$ tons.

- Unit transportation + storage costs $c_{ij}$ (from Table 3-19):

| $c_{ij}$ | $B_1$ | $B_2$ | $B_3$ | $B_4$ | $B_5$ |
|----------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|
| $A_1$    | 10    | 15    | 20    | 20    | 40    |
| $A_2$    | 20    | 40    | 15    | 30    | 30    |
| $A_3$    | 30    | 35    | 40    | 55    | 25    |

**Feasibility Note:** Total demand (290) ≤ factory capacity (270)? No — 290 > 270. The factory can produce only 270 tons, so not all demand can be satisfied. We therefore model this as a **supply-limited transportation problem** with a dummy "unmet demand" option at zero penalty is NOT appropriate here because the problem says "determine a transportation plan that minimizes the total cost" — but since supply < demand, the standard approach is to recognize that only 270 tons can be shipped. We introduce unmet demand variables to absorb the shortfall (20 tons) at zero cost, since the problem does not specify a penalty for unmet demand. The model below treats the factory as a source with supply 270, warehouses as transshipment nodes with capacity limits, and regions as demand nodes where demand is a *maximum* (≤) rather than equality, allowing unmet demand.

## Decision Variables

Let $x_{ij} \geq 0$ be the amount of sugar (in tons) shipped **from warehouse $i$ to region $j$**, for all $i \in I, j \in J$.

Additionally, let $y_i \geq 0$ be the amount of sugar (in tons) **received by warehouse $i$ from the factory**, for all $i \in I$.

## Objective Function

Minimize total transportation and storage cost:

$$\min \sum_{i \in I} \sum_{j \in J} c_{ij} \, x_{ij}$$

## Operational Constraints

**1. Factory supply limit:**
$$\sum_{i \in I} y_i \leq 270$$

**2. Warehouse flow conservation & capacity (inflow to warehouse = outflow from warehouse, and total throughput ≤ storage capacity):**
$$y_i = \sum_{j \in J} x_{ij}, \quad \forall i \in I$$
$$\sum_{j \in J} x_{ij} \leq C_i, \quad \forall i \in I$$

Since $y_i = \sum_j x_{ij}$, the capacity constraint becomes:
$$\sum_{j \in J} x_{ij} \leq C_i, \quad \forall i \in I$$
i.e.,
$$\sum_{j=1}^{5} x_{A_1 j} \leq 50$$
$$\sum_{j=1}^{5} x_{A_2 j} \leq 100$$
$$\sum_{j=1}^{5} x_{A_3 j} \leq 150$$

**3. Regional demand constraints (demand is upper-bounded; unmet demand is allowed since supply < total demand):**
$$\sum_{i \in I} x_{ij} \leq D_j, \quad \forall j \in J$$
i.e.,
$$\sum_{i=1}^{3} x_{i,B_1} \leq 25$$
$$\sum_{i=1}^{3} x_{i,B_2} \leq 105$$
$$\sum_{i=1}^{3} x_{i,B_3} \leq 60$$
$$\sum_{i=1}^{3} x_{i,B_4} \leq 30$$
$$\sum_{i=1}^{3} x_{i,B_5} \leq 70$$

**4. Non-negativity:**
$$x_{ij} \geq 0, \quad \forall i \in I, j \in J$$
$$y_i \geq 0, \quad \forall i \in I$$

## Feasibility Sanity Check

- Total supply available: $\min(270, 50+100+150) = 270$ tons.
- Total demand: 290 tons.
- Shortfall: 20 tons. Since the model uses $\leq$ on demand constraints, the model is feasible (the solver will simply not ship 20 tons of demand). The binding constraint will be the factory capacity (270) and/or warehouse capacities.
- Warehouse capacities sum to 300 > 270, so warehouse capacities are not the binding aggregate constraint; the factory capacity (270) is.

The model is a standard unbalanced transportation problem solved as an LP.
