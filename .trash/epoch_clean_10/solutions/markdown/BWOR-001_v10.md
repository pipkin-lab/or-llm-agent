To formulate the linear programming model for the candy factory problem, we must carefully interpret the composition constraints and cost structures provided in the table.

### 1. Analysis of the Problem Data

**Products:**
- Brand X
- Brand Y
- Brand Z

**Raw Materials:**
- Material A: Cost \$2.00/kg, Limit 2000 kg
- Material B: Cost \$1.50/kg, Limit 2500 kg
- Material C: Cost \$1.00/kg, Limit 1200 kg

**Product Specifications (from Table 1-17):**
*Note: The table structure implies composition ratios for each brand. Empty cells usually imply "no specific constraint" or that the material is not used, but in mixture problems, we must look at the constraints given.*

- **Brand X:**
  - A: $\ge 60\%$
  - C: $\le 20\%$
  - B: Not explicitly constrained in the row for B, but since $A+B+C=100\%$, if $A \ge 60\%$ and $C \le 20\%$, then $B = 1 - A - C$. The constraint on B is implicit via the sum to 100%. However, looking at the table, there is no entry for B in column X. This usually means no specific lower/upper bound *other* than non-negativity and the sum constraint.
  
- **Brand Y:**
  - A: $\ge 30\%$
  - C: $\le 50\%$
  - B: Implicit via sum.

- **Brand Z:**
  - C: $\le 60\%$
  - A: No entry (implies no specific constraint other than $\ge 0$)
  - B: No entry (implies no specific constraint other than $\ge 0$)
  - *Correction/Refinement*: In standard mixture problems, if a cell is empty, it often means that material is **not present** or has **no specific percentage requirement**. However, looking at the "Raw Material Cost" column, all three materials are available. Let's assume all three materials can be used in all brands unless restricted.
  - For Brand Z, only $C \le 60\%$ is specified. This means $A_Z + B_Z + C_Z = 100\%$, $C_Z \le 60\%$.

**Costs and Prices (per kg of finished candy):**
- **Brand X:**
  - Processing Cost: \$0.50
  - Selling Price: \$3.40
  - *Variable Cost of Materials*: Depends on the mix.
- **Brand Y:**
  - Processing Cost: \$0.40
  - Selling Price: \$2.85
- **Brand Z:**
  - Processing Cost: \$0.30
  - Selling Price: \$2.25

**Objective:** Maximize Total Profit.
Profit = Total Revenue - Total Material Costs - Total Processing Costs.

### 2. Decision Variables

Let $x_X, x_Y, x_Z$ be the total kilograms of Brand X, Y, and Z produced.

However, because the *proportion* of materials in each brand is variable (within limits), we need variables for the amount of each raw material used in each brand.

Let $x_{i,j}$ be the amount (kg) of raw material $i$ used in brand $j$.
- Indices $i \in \{A, B, C\}$
- Indices $j \in \{X, Y, Z\}$

The total production of each brand is the sum of its ingredients:
- $P_X = x_{A,X} + x_{B,X} + x_{C,X}$
- $P_Y = x_{A,Y} + x_{B,Y} + x_{C,Y}$
- $P_Z = x_{A,Z} + x_{B,Z} + x_{C,Z}$

### 3. Objective Function

**Revenue:**
$R = 3.40 P_X + 2.85 P_Y + 2.25 P_Z$

**Material Costs:**
$C_{mat} = 2.00 (x_{A,X} + x_{A,Y} + x_{A,Z}) + 1.50 (x_{B,X} + x_{B,Y} + x_{B,Z}) + 1.00 (x_{C,X} + x_{C,Y} + x_{C,Z})$

**Processing Costs:**
$C_{proc} = 0.50 P_X + 0.40 P_Y + 0.30 P_Z$

**Total Profit (Z) to Maximize:**
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Max } Z = & \sum_{j} (\text{Price}_j \cdot P_j) - \sum_{i,j} (\text{Cost}_i \cdot x_{i,j}) - \sum_{j} (\text{ProcCost}_j \cdot P_j) \\
= & \sum_{j} (\text{Price}_j - \text{ProcCost}_j) P_j - \sum_{i,j} \text{Cost}_i x_{i,j}
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting the specific values:
- Net Margin X (before materials) = $3.40 - 0.50 = 2.90$
- Net Margin Y (before materials) = $2.85 - 0.40 = 2.45$
- Net Margin Z (before materials) = $2.25 - 0.30 = 1.95$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Max } Z = & 2.90(x_{A,X} + x_{B,X} + x_{C,X}) + 2.45(x_{A,Y} + x_{B,Y} + x_{C,Y}) + 1.95(x_{A,Z} + x_{B,Z} + x_{C,Z}) \\
& - [2.00(x_{A,X} + x_{A,Y} + x_{A,Z}) + 1.50(x_{B,X} + x_{B,Y} + x_{B,Z}) + 1.00(x_{C,X} + x_{C,Y} + x_{C,Z})]
\end{aligned}
$$

Grouping by decision variables $x_{i,j}$:

**For Brand X:**
- $x_{A,X}$: $2.90 - 2.00 = 0.90$
- $x_{B,X}$: $2.90 - 1.50 = 1.40$
- $x_{C,X}$: $2.90 - 1.00 = 1.90$

**For Brand Y:**
- $x_{A,Y}$: $2.45 - 2.00 = 0.45$
- $x_{B,Y}$: $2.45 - 1.50 = 0.95$
- $x_{C,Y}$: $2.45 - 1.00 = 1.45$

**For Brand Z:**
- $x_{A,Z}$: $1.95 - 2.00 = -0.05$
- $x_{B,Z}$: $1.95 - 1.50 = 0.45$
- $x_{C,Z}$: $1.95 - 1.00 = 0.95$

$$
\text{Max } Z = 0.90 x_{A,X} + 1.40 x_{B,X} + 1.90 x_{C,X} + 0.45 x_{A,Y} + 0.95 x_{B,Y} + 1.45 x_{C,Y} - 0.05 x_{A,Z} + 0.45 x_{B,Z} + 0.95 x_{C,Z}
$$

### 4. Constraints

**1. Raw Material Availability Constraints:**
The total usage of each material across all brands cannot exceed the monthly limit.
- Material A: $x_{A,X} + x_{A,Y} + x_{A,Z} \le 2000$
- Material B: $x_{B,X} + x_{B,Y} + x_{B,Z} \le 2500$
- Material C: $x_{C,X} + x_{C,Y} + x_{C,Z} \le 1200$

**2. Composition Constraints (Proportions):**
These constraints ensure the ratio of materials in each brand meets the specifications. We express these as linear inequalities using the total production of that brand.

*Brand X Specifications:*
- $A \ge 60\%$: $x_{A,X} \ge 0.60 (x_{A,X} + x_{B,X} + x_{C,X})$
  $\Rightarrow 0.40 x_{A,X} - 0.60 x_{B,X} - 0.60 x_{C,X} \ge 0$
  $\Rightarrow 2 x_{A,X} - 3 x_{B,X} - 3 x_{C,X} \ge 0$ (Divided by 0.2 for simplicity)
- $C \le 20\%$: $x_{C,X} \le 0.20 (x_{A,X} + x_{B,X} + x_{C,X})$
  $\Rightarrow -0.20 x_{A,X} - 0.20 x_{B,X} + 0.80 x_{C,X} \le 0$
  $\Rightarrow -x_{A,X} - x_{B,X} + 4 x_{C,X} \le 0$ (Divided by 0.2)
- *Implicit*: $x_{B,X} \ge 0$ is covered by non-negativity.

*Brand Y Specifications:*
- $A \ge 30\%$: $x_{A,Y} \ge 0.30 (x_{A,Y} + x_{B,Y} + x_{C,Y})$
  $\Rightarrow 0.70 x_{A,Y} - 0.30 x_{B,Y} - 0.30 x_{C,Y} \ge 0$
  $\Rightarrow 7 x_{A,Y} - 3 x_{B,Y} - 3 x_{C,Y} \ge 0$ (Divided by 0.1)
- $C \le 50\%$: $x_{C,Y} \le 0.50 (x_{A,Y} + x_{B,Y} + x_{C,Y})$
  $\Rightarrow -0.50 x_{A,Y} - 0.50 x_{B,Y} + 0.50 x_{C,Y} \le 0$
  $\Rightarrow -x_{A,Y} - x_{B,Y} + x_{C,Y} \le 0$ (Divided by 0.5)

*Brand Z Specifications:*
- $C \le 60\%$: $x_{C,Z} \le 0.60 (x_{A,Z} + x_{B,Z} + x_{C,Z})$
  $\Rightarrow -0.60 x_{A,Z} - 0.60 x_{B,Z} + 0.40 x_{C,Z} \le 0$
  $\Rightarrow -3 x_{A,Z} - 3 x_{B,Z} + 2 x_{C,Z} \le 0$ (Divided by 0.2)
- *Note*: No specific constraint on A or B for Z, other than non-negativity and sum.

**3. Non-negativity Constraints:**
All decision variables must be non-negative.
$x_{i,j} \ge 0$ for all $i \in \{A,B,C\}, j \in \{X,Y,Z\}$.

### Final Mathematical Model

**Decision Variables:**
Let $x_{i,j}$ denote the kilograms of raw material $i$ used in brand $j$, where $i \in \{A, B, C\}$ and $j \in \{X, Y, Z\}$.

**Objective Function:**
Maximize
$$
Z = 0.90 x_{A,X} + 1.40 x_{B,X} + 1.90 x_{C,X} + 0.45 x_{A,Y} + 0.95 x_{B,Y} + 1.45 x_{C,Y} - 0.05 x_{A,Z} + 0.45 x_{B,Z} + 0.95 x_{C,Z}
$$

**Subject to Constraints:**

1.  **Raw Material Limits:**
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x_{A,X} + x_{A,Y} + x_{A,Z} &\le 2000 \\
    x_{B,X} + x_{B,Y} + x_{B,Z} &\le 2500 \\
    x_{C,X} + x_{C,Y} + x_{C,Z} &\le 1200
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  **Composition Constraints:**
    
    *Brand X:*
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2 x_{A,X} - 3 x_{B,X} - 3 x_{C,X} &\ge 0 \quad (\text{A} \ge 60\%) \\
    -x_{A,X} - x_{B,X} + 4 x_{C,X} &\le 0 \quad (\text{C} \le 20\%)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    *Brand Y:*
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    7 x_{A,Y} - 3 x_{B,Y} - 3 x_{C,Y} &\ge 0 \quad (\text{A} \ge 30\%) \\
    -x_{A,Y} - x_{B,Y} + x_{C,Y} &\le 0 \quad (\text{C} \le 50\%)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    *Brand Z:*
    $$
    -3 x_{A,Z} - 3 x_{B,Z} + 2 x_{C,Z} \le 0 \quad (\text{C} \le 60\%)
    $$

3.  **Non-negativity:**
    $$
    x_{i,j} \ge 0 \quad \forall i \in \{A, B, C\}, j \in \{X, Y, Z\}
    $$
