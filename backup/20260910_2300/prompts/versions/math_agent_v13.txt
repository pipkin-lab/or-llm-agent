BALANCE CONSTRAINT EQUALITY (SUPPLY > DEMAND): In transportation, supply, or allocation problems where total supply exceeds total demand, the supply-side balance constraint MUST be an EQUALITY: (shipped_i + stored_i + disposed_i = S_i) for each origin. Every unit of supply must be explicitly allocated to a downstream activity (shipment, storage, disposal). Using an inequality (<=) on the supply side allows the solver to silently discard excess supply without incurring the associated holding/storage/disposal cost, which underestimates the true objective. Only use <= on the supply side if the problem explicitly states that unused supply has zero cost and no physical consequence.

INVENTORY & ACCUMULATION BOUND VERIFICATION: When expressing end-of-period-t inventory or cumulative quantities as a sum over source periods, lots, or cohorts, the summation MUST include the current period t in its upper bound (i.e., sum over i = 1 to t, NOT i = 1 to t-1). End-of-period-t inventory includes all units acquired in period t that remain unconsumed at the end of that period. MANDATORY VERIFICATION: After writing any summation-based inventory or accumulation constraint, substitute t = 1 and confirm the expression captures all first-period acquisitions not consumed in period 1. If the formula yields an empty sum or identically zero for t = 1 while first-period storage is physically possible, the summation bounds are incorrect and must be revised. Additionally, when using lot-tracking or cohort variables (e.g., w[t,k] for "acquired in t, consumed in t+k"), ensure that the capacity or upper-bound constraint on end-of-period-t inventory sums over ALL cohorts still present at that time, including the cohort acquired in period t itself.

OBJECTIVE UNIT & NORMALIZATION VERIFICATION: Before finalizing the objective function, verify that the units and scale of the objective value match the exact quantity the problem asks to compute or report. If the problem defines a natural "unit" of measurement (e.g., "one full pass through all N items," "per-unit cost," "in thousands of dollars," "as a fraction of total volume"), the objective MUST be expressed in those units rather than raw absolute counts. QUICK SANITY CHECK: If the problem's context and data suggest the answer should be a small number (single or double digits) but your objective yields a very large number (hundreds of thousands or more), suspect a missing normalization factor. Common cases: (a) the problem measures total workload in "full-batch-equivalent passes" (divide raw item-scans by total items N), (b) the problem asks for average cost per unit (divide total cost by total units), (c) the problem states costs "in thousands" but lists raw dollar values. Always re-read the final question sentence to confirm the exact quantity and its units before locking the objective.

BOUNDARY-TIME EVENT INCLUSION: When a financial obligation (loan principal repayment, bond maturity, lease end, penalty) is stated to occur "at the end of [final period] of next [time unit]" and the objective is evaluated at "the beginning of next [time unit]," these describe the SAME time point. The obligation MUST be included as a cash outflow in the final period's balance. Do NOT exclude a boundary event on the grounds that it occurs "after" the objective evaluation unless the problem provides two explicitly distinct timestamps (e.g., "end of December" vs. "end of January"). DEFAULT RULE: If the repayment/maturity language references the same calendar period as the last period of the planning horizon, include it in the final period equation. Excluding it renders the model unbounded (the solver can inflate the loan principal without penalty) or structurally infeasible.
13. FLEET / VEHICLE WORKLOAD SCOPE VERIFICATION: In problems asking for minimum fleet size, total vehicle-days, or total ship-days, explicitly separate (a) mandatory service time (sailing/transport duration + loading + unloading) from (b) repositioning/deadhead/waiting time. Before adding repositioning time to the objective, perform this verification: (1) Does the problem text explicitly state that repositioning requires additional vehicles or adds to the fleet count? (2) Compute the total mandatory service workload as sum of (frequency_r × service_duration_r) for all routes/services. If this value alone is a plausible answer given the problem's data scale, and the problem does not explicitly mandate counting deadhead time, then the objective is the service workload ONLY. Do NOT default to including repositioning in the fleet count unless the problem structure (e.g., a time-window constraint or explicit "each ship must be assigned to a continuous route") requires it. When in doubt, check whether the answer equals the pure service workload (no deadhead) before adding repositioning terms.
