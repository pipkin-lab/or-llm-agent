Model Type: LP

### Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
- $I = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$: Set of supply origins (A1, A2, A3, A4).
- $J = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$: Set of demand destinations (B1, B2, B3, B4, B5, B6).

**Parameters:**

1.  **Supply ($S_i$) for each origin $i \in I$:**
    -   $S_1$ (A1) = 50
    -   $S_2$ (A2) = 40
    -   $S_3$ (A3) = 60
    -   $S_4$ (A4) = 31
    -   *Total Supply = 181*

2.  **Demand ($D_j$) for each destination $j \in J$:**
    -   $D_1$ (B1) = 30
    -   $D_2$ (B2) = 50
    -   $D_3$ (B3) = 20
    -   $D_4$ (B4) = 40
    -   $D_5$ (B5) = 30
    -   $D_6$ (B6) = 11
    -   *Total Demand = 181*

    *Note: Total Supply (181) equals Total Demand (181). This is a balanced transportation problem.*

3.  **Unit Transportation Costs ($C_{ij}$) for $i \in I, j \in J$:**
    -   **Row A1 ($i=1$):** $C_{11}=2, C_{12}=1, C_{13}=3, C_{14}=3, C_{15}=3, C_{16}=5$
    -   **Row A2 ($i=2$):** $C_{21}=4, C_{22}=2, C_{23}=2, C_{24}=4, C_{25}=4, C_{26}=4$
    -   **Row A3 ($i=3$):** $C_{31}=3, C_{32}=5, C_{33}=4, C_{34}=2, C_{35}=4, C_{36}=1$
    -   **Row A4 ($i=4$):** $C_{41}=4, C_{42}=2, C_{43}=2, C_{44}=1, C_{45}=2, C_{46}=2$

### Decision Variables

Let $x_{ij}$ be the quantity of goods shipped from origin $i$ to destination $j$.
-   **Domain:** $x_{ij} \ge 0, \forall i \in I, j \in J$
-   **Type:** Continuous (standard transportation problem assumption unless integer units are explicitly required; given the integer supplies/demands, the optimal solution to a balanced transportation LP will naturally have integer values due to the total unimodularity of the constraint matrix).

### Objective Function

Minimize the total transportation cost:

$$ \text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{i=1}^{4} \sum_{j=1}^{6} C_{ij} x_{ij} $$

Explicitly:
$$ Z = 2x_{11} + 1x_{12} + 3x_{13} + 3x_{14} + 3x_{15} + 5x_{16} $$
$$ + 4x_{21} + 2x_{22} + 2x_{23} + 4x_{24} + 4x_{25} + 4x_{26} $$
$$ + 3x_{31} + 5x_{32} + 4x_{33} + 2x_{34} + 4x_{35} + 1x_{36} $$
$$ + 4x_{41} + 2x_{42} + 2x_{43} + 1x_{44} + 2x_{45} + 2x_{46} $$

### Operational Constraints

**1. Supply Constraints (Equality):**
The total amount shipped from each origin must equal its available supply. Since the problem is balanced, we use equality constraints.

-   $\sum_{j=1}^{6} x_{1j} = 50$  (From A1)
-   $\sum_{j=1}^{6} x_{2j} = 40$  (From A2)
-   $\sum_{j=1}^{6} x_{3j} = 60$  (From A3)
-   $\sum_{j=1}^{6} x_{4j} = 31$  (From A4)

**2. Demand Constraints (Equality):**
The total amount received at each destination must equal its required demand.

-   $\sum_{i=1}^{4} x_{i1} = 30$  (To B1)
-   $\sum_{i=1}^{4} x_{i2} = 50$  (To B2)
-   $\sum_{i=1}^{4} x_{i3} = 20$  (To B3)
-   $\sum_{i=1}^{4} x_{i4} = 40$  (To B4)
-   $\sum_{i=1}^{4} x_{i5} = 30$  (To B5)
-   $\sum_{i=1}^{4} x_{i6} = 11$  (To B6)

**3. Non-negativity Constraints:**
-   $x_{ij} \ge 0$ for all $i \in \{1,2,3,4\}, j \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$
