To determine the power allocation plan that minimizes total cost while utilizing all available supply, we must first analyze the supply, demand, and cost parameters.

### **MANDATORY COST INVENTORY**
*   **Costs (Yuan/Unit):**
    *   Station I to City 1: 15
    *   Station I to City 2: 18
    *   Station I to City 3: 22
    *   Station II to City 1: 21
    *   Station II to City 2: 25
    *   Station II to City 3: 16
*   **Capacities (Units):**
    *   Station I Max: 400
    *   Station II Max: 450
    *   Total Supply: 850
*   **Demands (Units):**
    *   City 1: Min 290, Max 320
    *   City 2: Fixed 250
    *   City 3: Min 270, Max 350
    *   Total Max Demand: 320 + 250 + 350 = 920
    *   Total Min Demand: 290 + 250 + 270 = 810
*   **Feasibility Check:** Total Supply (850) is between Total Min Demand (810) and Total Max Demand (920). Therefore, a feasible solution exists where all supply is used and all demand bounds are respected.

### **Model Type Declaration**
**LP (Linear Programming)**
The objective function and all constraints are linear. All variables are continuous (electricity units can be fractional).

---

### **1. Index Sets & Parameters**

**Sets:**
*   $I = \{1, 2\}$: Set of power stations (I, II). Let $i=1$ be Station I, $i=2$ be Station II.
*   $J = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of cities (1, 2, 3).

**Parameters:**
*   $c_{ij}$: Unit cost (Yuan) of supplying electricity from Station $i$ to City $j$.
    *   $c_{11}=15, c_{12}=18, c_{13}=22$
    *   $c_{21}=21, c_{22}=25, c_{23}=16$
*   $S_i$: Maximum supply capacity of Station $i$.
    *   $S_1 = 400$
    *   $S_2 = 450$
*   $L_j$: Lower bound on demand for City $j$.
    *   $L_1 = 290$
    *   $L_2 = 250$
    *   $L_3 = 270$
*   $U_j$: Upper bound on demand for City $j$.
    *   $U_1 = 320$
    *   $U_2 = 250$
    *   $U_3 = 350$

### **2. Decision Variables**

Let $x_{ij}$ be the amount of electricity (in units) supplied from Station $i$ to City $j$.

*   **Domain:** $x_{ij} \ge 0$ for all $i \in I, j \in J$.
*   Since electricity is a divisible commodity, $x_{ij}$ are **CONTINUOUS** variables.

### **3. Objective Function**

Minimize the total cost of electricity supply.

$$
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{i \in I} \sum_{j \in J} c_{ij} x_{ij}
$$

Expanding with the specific values:
$$
\text{Minimize } Z = 15x_{11} + 18x_{12} + 22x_{13} + 21x_{21} + 25x_{22} + 16x_{23}
$$

### **4. Operational Constraints**

**A. Supply Constraints (Use up all available electricity)**
The problem states: "uses up all available electricity from both stations". This implies the total shipment from each station must equal its maximum capacity. Even though it says "maximum supply capacities", the instruction to "use up all available" combined with the fact that total supply (850) < total max demand (920) but > total min demand (810) forces the supply side to be binding at the maximum. If we used $\le$, the solver might choose to supply less if it reduced cost, but the problem explicitly requires utilizing the available capacity. Given the costs, supplying is required to meet demand anyway, but the "use up all" constraint fixes the supply side.

$$
\sum_{j \in J} x_{1j} = 400 \quad (\text{Station I})
$$
$$
\sum_{j \in J} x_{2j} = 450 \quad (\text{Station II})
$$

*Note: While rule 9 suggests equality for supply > demand, here supply (850) is less than max demand (920) but greater than min demand (810). The constraint "use up all available electricity" explicitly mandates that the total outflow from the stations equals their full capacity. Thus, equalities are correct for the supply side.*

**B. Demand Constraints (City Bounds)**
Each city has a minimum and maximum acceptable supply level.

*   **City 1:**
    $$
    290 \le \sum_{i \in I} x_{i1} \le 320
    $$
    $$
    290 \le x_{11} + x_{21} \le 320
    $$

*   **City 2:**
    $$
    \sum_{i \in I} x_{i2} = 250
    $$
    $$
    x_{12} + x_{22} = 250
    $$

*   **City 3:**
    $$
    270 \le \sum_{i \in I} x_{i3} \le 350
    $$
    $$
    270 \le x_{13} + x_{23} \le 350
    $$

**C. Non-negativity Constraints**
$$
x_{ij} \ge 0 \quad \forall i \in I, j \in J
$$

### **Feasibility & Consistency Check**

1.  **Total Supply:** $400 + 450 = 850$.
2.  **Total Demand Range:**
    *   Min: $290 + 250 + 270 = 810$.
    *   Max: $320 + 250 + 350 = 920$.
3.  Since $810 \le 850 \le 920$, it is possible to satisfy the lower bounds of all cities and the exact supply constraints simultaneously. The system is feasible.
4.  **Objective Magnitude:**
    *   Costs are in Yuan.
    *   Quantities are in Units.
    *   Total Cost will be in Yuan.
    *   Estimated magnitude: $850 \text{ units} \times \sim 20 \text{ Yuan/unit} \approx 17,000 \text{ Yuan}$. This is a reasonable scale for the objective function. No normalization is required as the question asks for the "allocation plan" (implied minimization of cost in the given units).
