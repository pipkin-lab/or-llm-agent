# Operations Research Model: Grain Trading Optimization

## 1. Problem Analysis & Logic Deduction

**Objective:**
The company aims to maximize profit (or minimize cost, but here we frame it as maximizing net gain). Profit is generated by buying low and selling high, subject to storage and cash flow constraints.

**Key Constraints & Rules:**
1.  **Time Horizon:** 3 months (January, February, March). Let $t \in \{1, 2, 3\}$.
2.  **Inventory Dynamics:**
    *   Initial Stock ($I_0$): 1,000 dan.
    *   Target Ending Stock ($I_3$): 2,000 dan.
    *   Warehouse Capacity: 5,000 dan (applies to end-of-month inventory).
3.  **Cash Flow:**
    *   Initial Capital: 20,000 yuan.
    *   "Cash payment upon delivery": This implies that purchasing in month $t$ requires cash available at the start of month $t$ (or accumulated by then).
    *   Sales in month $t$ generate cash at the end of month $t$ (or immediately upon sale, which effectively adds to the cash available for the next period or covers costs within the period). Standard formulation: Cash available at start of month $t$ = Cash from start of month $t-1$ - Purchase Cost in month $t-1$ + Sales Revenue in month $t-1$.
    *   *Correction on Timing*: The problem states "Grain arrives in the month it is purchased... Cash payment upon delivery." and "can only be sold starting in the next month."
    *   Let $P_t$ be purchase quantity in month $t$. Cost $C_{buy,t} \cdot P_t$ is incurred in month $t$.
    *   Let $S_t$ be sales quantity in month $t$. Revenue $R_{sell,t} \cdot S_t$ is received in month $t$.
    *   Constraint: You cannot sell grain purchased in month $t$ in month $t$. You can only sell grain held in inventory from previous months.
    *   Cash Balance:
        *   Start of Month 1 Cash = 20,000.
        *   End of Month 1 Cash = Start Cash - Buy Cost$_1$ + Sell Revenue$_1$.
        *   Start of Month 2 Cash = End of Month 1 Cash.
        *   ...and so on.
        *   Constraint: Cash balance at the start of any month $t$ must be non-negative (or strictly, the cash outflow for purchases in month $t$ must not exceed the cash available at the beginning of month $t$).

4.  **Inventory Balance Equation:**
    *   $I_t = I_{t-1} + P_t - S_t$
    *   Where $I_t$ is inventory at end of month $t$.
    *   $I_0 = 1,000$.
    *   $I_3 = 2,000$.
    *   $0 \le I_t \le 5,000$ for $t=1,2,3$.

5.  **Sales Constraint:**
    *   Sales $S_t$ can only come from inventory available *before* the month starts? Or just total inventory?
    *   "Can only be sold starting in the next month." This means $S_t$ is constrained by $I_{t-1} + P_{t-1}$? No, standard inventory logic:
        *   Inventory available for sale in Month $t$ is $I_{t-1} + P_t$? No, purchases arrive in month $t$. Can we sell what we just bought?
        *   "Grain arrives in the month it is purchased, but can only be sold starting in the next month."
        *   This implies $S_t$ must be $\le I_{t-1}$. You cannot sell grain bought in month $t$ in month $t$.
        *   So, $S_t \le I_{t-1}$.
        *   And $I_t = I_{t-1} + P_t - S_t$.

6.  **Objective Function:**
    *   Maximize Total Revenue - Total Purchase Cost.
    *   $Z = \sum_{t=1}^3 (R_{sell,t} S_t - C_{buy,t} P_t)$.
    *   Note: Initial and final inventory have no explicit buy/sell price in the objective unless we consider opportunity cost, but typically in these problems, we just maximize cash flow from trades. The initial stock is a given asset, and the final stock is a constraint. We don't "sell" the final stock, so it doesn't generate revenue. We don't "buy" the initial stock, so it doesn't incur cost in the objective.

## 2. Formal Mathematical Model

### Index Sets & Parameters

**Sets:**
*   $T = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of months (Jan, Feb, Mar).

**Parameters:**
*   $Cap = 5,000$: Warehouse capacity (dan).
*   $I_{init} = 1,000$: Initial inventory (dan).
*   $I_{end} = 2,000$: Target ending inventory (dan).
*   $C_{init} = 20,000$: Initial capital (yuan).
*   $c_t$: Purchase price in month $t$ (yuan/dan).
    *   $c_1 = 2.85, c_2 = 3.05, c_3 = 2.90$.
*   $r_t$: Selling price in month $t$ (yuan/dan).
    *   $r_1 = 3.10, r_2 = 3.25, r_3 = 2.95$.

### Decision Variables

Let the following variables be defined for each month $t \in T$:

*   $x_t$: Quantity of grain purchased in month $t$ (dan).
    *   Domain: $x_t \ge 0$ (Continuous, as grain is a bulk commodity measured in dan, which is a continuous unit in this context. If "dan" were discrete sacks, it would be integer, but usually bulk grain is continuous. Given the precision of prices, continuous is appropriate).
*   $y_t$: Quantity of grain sold in month $t$ (dan).
    *   Domain: $y_t \ge 0$ (Continuous).
*   $I_t$: Inventory level at the end of month $t$ (dan).
    *   Domain: $I_t \ge 0$.
*   $K_t$: Cash balance at the end of month $t$ (yuan).
    *   Domain: $K_t \ge 0$.

*(Note: We can eliminate $I_t$ and $K_t$ as variables by substituting them into constraints, but keeping them makes the model clearer. We will define the model with these auxiliary variables.)*

### Objective Function

Maximize total profit (Revenue from sales - Cost of purchases):

$$
\text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{t=1}^{3} (r_t y_t - c_t x_t)
$$

### Operational Constraints

**1. Inventory Balance Constraints:**
The inventory at the end of month $t$ equals the inventory from the previous month plus purchases in month $t$ minus sales in month $t$.
$$
I_t = I_{t-1} + x_t - y_t, \quad \forall t \in \{1, 2, 3\}
$$
*   Where $I_0 = I_{init} = 1,000$.
*   Target ending inventory:
    $$
    I_3 = I_{end} = 2,000
    $$
*   Warehouse Capacity Constraints:
    $$
    0 \le I_t \le Cap \quad \forall t \in \{1, 2, 3\}
    $$
    $$
    I_0 = 1,000 \le Cap \quad (\text{Given, valid})
    $$

**2. Sales Feasibility Constraints:**
Grain purchased in month $t$ cannot be sold in month $t$. Therefore, sales in month $t$ are limited by the inventory available at the *start* of month $t$ (which is $I_{t-1}$).
$$
y_t \le I_{t-1}, \quad \forall t \in \{1, 2, 3\}
$$
*   Note: For $t=1$, $y_1 \le I_0 = 1,000$.
*   This constraint ensures that we only sell existing stock, not just-purchased stock.

**3. Cash Flow Constraints:**
The cash balance at the end of month $t$ is the previous balance minus purchase costs plus sales revenue. Purchases are paid upon delivery (in month $t$), and sales generate revenue in month $t$.
$$
K_t = K_{t-1} - c_t x_t + r_t y_t, \quad \forall t \in \{1, 2, 3\}
$$
*   Where $K_0 = C_{init} = 20,000$.
*   Non-negativity of Cash (Cannot spend more than available):
    $$
    K_t \ge 0, \quad \forall t \in \{1, 2, 3\}
    $$
    *Alternative formulation*: The cost of purchases in month $t$ must not exceed the cash available at the start of month $t$ plus any revenue generated *before* the purchase? The problem says "Cash payment upon delivery". Usually, in monthly aggregation, we assume net cash flow at end of month or that cash is available throughout. However, the strictest interpretation of "cash payment upon delivery" combined with "sales... next month" suggests we need to ensure we have the cash to buy.
    Let's refine the cash constraint to be safe:
    The cash available at the *start* of month $t$ is $K_{t-1}$.
    We buy $x_t$ costing $c_t x_t$.
    We sell $y_t$ gaining $r_t y_t$.
    Usually, if sales happen in the same month, we might use that cash to pay for purchases if timing allows. However, "Cash payment upon delivery" and "Sales... next month" usually implies a lag.
    Let's assume the standard linear programming cash flow constraint:
    $$
    K_t = K_{t-1} - c_t x_t + r_t y_t \ge 0
    $$
    This ensures that at the end of the month, we are not in debt. If the company cannot borrow (implied by "available capital"), this is the correct constraint.

**4. Variable Bounds:**
$$
x_t \ge 0, \quad \forall t \in \{1, 2, 3\}
$$
$$
y_t \ge 0, \quad \forall t \in \{1, 2, 3\}
$$

### Consolidated Model Summary

**Variables:**
*   $x_1, x_2, x_3$: Purchases in Jan, Feb, Mar (dan)
*   $y_1, y_2, y_3$: Sales in Jan, Feb, Mar (dan)
*   $I_1, I_2, I_3$: End-of-month inventory (dan)
*   $K_1, K_2, K_3$: End-of-month cash balance (yuan)

**Objective:**
$$
\text{Max } Z = 3.10 y_1 + 3.25 y_2 + 2.95 y_3 - (2.85 x_1 + 3.05 x_2 + 2.90 x_3)
$$

**Subject to:**

**Inventory Balance:**
1.  $I_1 = 1000 + x_1 - y_1$
2.  $I_2 = I_1 + x_2 - y_2$
3.  $I_3 = I_2 + x_3 - y_3 = 2000$

**Inventory Capacity:**
4.  $0 \le I_1 \le 5000$
5.  $0 \le I_2 \le 5000$
6.  $0 \le I_3 \le 5000$ (Note: $I_3=2000$, so this is automatically satisfied if $I_3 \ge 0$)

**Sales Constraints (Lag):**
7.  $y_1 \le 1000$  (Cannot sell more than initial stock)
8.  $y_2 \le I_1$
9.  $y_3 \le I_2$

**Cash Flow:**
10. $K_1 = 20000 - 2.85 x_1 + 3.10 y_1 \ge 0$
11. $K_2 = K_1 - 3.05 x_2 + 3.25 y_2 \ge 0$
12. $K_3 = K_2 - 2.90 x_3 + 2.95 y_3 \ge 0$

**Non-negativity:**
13. $x_1, x_2, x_3, y_1, y_2, y_3 \ge 0$
