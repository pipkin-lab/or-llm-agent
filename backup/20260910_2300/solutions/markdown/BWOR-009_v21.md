**Model Type:** MILP (Mixed Integer Linear Programming)
*(Note: While the optimal solution to this specific production planning problem will naturally be integer due to the integral data and network structure, variables are defined as integers to represent discrete physical units of equipment.)*

### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Sets:**
*   $T = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$: Set of quarters.

**Parameters (extracted from Table 3-30):**
*   $C_t$: Production capacity in quarter $t$ (units).
    *   $C_1 = 25, C_2 = 35, C_3 = 30, C_4 = 20$
*   $D_t$: Delivery quantity required at the end of quarter $t$ (units).
    *   $D_1 = 15, D_2 = 20, D_3 = 25, D_4 = 20$
*   $p_t$: Production cost per unit in quarter $t$ (thousand yuan).
    *   $p_1 = 12.0, p_2 = 11.0, p_3 = 11.5, p_4 = 12.5$
*   $h$: Holding cost per unit per quarter (thousand yuan).
    *   $h = 0.1$

**Derived Parameters:**
*   Total Demand $\sum_{t=1}^4 D_t = 15 + 20 + 25 + 20 = 80$ units.
*   Total Capacity $\sum_{t=1}^4 C_t = 25 + 35 + 30 + 20 = 110$ units.
*   Since Total Capacity > Total Demand, the problem is feasible regarding capacity.

### 2. Decision Variables

*   $x_t$: Quantity of equipment produced in quarter $t$.
    *   Domain: $x_t \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ (Non-negative integers)
*   $I_t$: Inventory level at the **end** of quarter $t$.
    *   Domain: $I_t \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ (Non-negative integers)
    *   Note: $I_0 = 0$ (Assuming no initial inventory is provided or allowed to carry over from a previous year in this specific annual planning context, standard for such problems unless stated otherwise).

### 3. Objective Function

Minimize the total annual cost, which includes production costs and holding costs.

$$
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{t=1}^{4} p_t x_t + \sum_{t=1}^{4} h I_t
$$

Substituting the parameters:
$$
Z = 12.0 x_1 + 11.0 x_2 + 11.5 x_3 + 12.5 x_4 + 0.1 I_1 + 0.1 I_2 + 0.1 I_3 + 0.1 I_4
$$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**1. Capacity Constraints:**
Production in any quarter cannot exceed the available capacity.
$$
x_t \le C_t, \quad \forall t \in T
$$
Specifically:
*   $x_1 \le 25$
*   $x_2 \le 35$
*   $x_3 \le 30$
*   $x_4 \le 20$

**2. Inventory Balance Constraints (Flow Balance):**
For each quarter $t$, the inventory at the end of the period equals the beginning inventory plus production minus the delivery required for that period.
$$
I_t = I_{t-1} + x_t - D_t, \quad \forall t \in T
$$
With $I_0 = 0$.

Specifically:
*   $I_1 = 0 + x_1 - 15 \implies x_1 - I_1 = 15$
*   $I_2 = I_1 + x_2 - 20 \implies -I_1 + x_2 - I_2 = 20$
*   $I_3 = I_2 + x_3 - 25 \implies -I_2 + x_3 - I_3 = 25$
*   $I_4 = I_3 + x_4 - 20 \implies -I_3 + x_4 - I_4 = 20$

**3. Non-negativity and Integrality:**
$$
x_t \ge 0, \quad I_t \ge 0, \quad \forall t \in T
$$
$$
x_t, I_t \in \mathbb{Z}
$$

**4. Backlog Prohibition (Implicit in $I_t \ge 0$):**
The constraint $I_t \ge 0$ ensures that we cannot deliver more than what is produced plus what is held in inventory. This satisfies the requirement to meet the delivery contract on time without backlogging.
