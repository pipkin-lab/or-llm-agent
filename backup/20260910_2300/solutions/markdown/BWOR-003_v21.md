**Model Type:** Linear Programming (LP)

### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Time Index:**
*   $t \in \{2003, 2004, 2005, 2006\}$: Years in the planning horizon.

**Loan Requirements (in thousands of dollars):**
*   $D_{2003} = 100$
*   $D_{2004} = 150$
*   $D_{2005} = 120$
*   $D_{2006} = 110$

**Investment Opportunities:**
*   **Bond A:**
    *   Purchase Time: Beginning of 2003
    *   Maturity Time: End of 2005 (3-year term: 2003, 2004, 2005)
    *   Return Factor: $1.40$ (140% of investment)
    *   Max Limit: $U_A = 60$
*   **Bond B:**
    *   Purchase Time: Beginning of 2003
    *   Maturity Time: End of 2004 (2-year term: 2003, 2004)
    *   Return Factor: $1.25$ (125% of investment)
    *   Max Limit: $U_B = 90$
*   **Bond C:**
    *   Purchase Time: Beginning of 2004
    *   Maturity Time: End of 2005 (2-year term: 2004, 2005)
    *   Return Factor: $1.30$ (130% of investment)
    *   Max Limit: $U_C = 50$
*   **Bank Account:**
    *   Purchase Time: Beginning of any year $t$
    *   Maturity Time: End of the same year $t$
    *   Return Factor: $1.04$ (4% annual interest)
    *   Max Limit: $[NOT\_PROVIDED]$ (Assumed unbounded or sufficiently large, denoted as $M$).

### 2. Decision Variables

Let the units be in thousands of dollars. All variables are continuous and non-negative.

*   $X_0$: Total amount of funds raised at the end of 2002 (Beginning of 2003).
*   $x_A$: Amount invested in Bond A at the beginning of 2003.
*   $x_B$: Amount invested in Bond B at the beginning of 2003.
*   $x_C$: Amount invested in Bond C at the beginning of 2004.
*   $b_{2003}$: Amount deposited in the bank at the beginning of 2003.
*   $b_{2004}$: Amount deposited in the bank at the beginning of 2004.
*   $b_{2005}$: Amount deposited in the bank at the beginning of 2005.
*   $b_{2006}$: Amount deposited in the bank at the beginning of 2006.
*   $s_t$: Surplus cash carried over from the end of year $t$ to the beginning of year $t+1$ (if not invested immediately). *Note: In this formulation, we will explicitly track cash flows at the beginning of each year to ensure all funds are allocated to either investment or immediate loan payment. To keep the model simple and rigorous, we define "Cash on Hand" at the beginning of each year after receiving returns but before making new investments/payments.*

Let's refine the variable definitions to be strictly "Cash on Hand" balances to avoid double counting.
*   $C_{2003}$: Cash available at the **beginning** of 2003, *after* raising $X_0$ and *before* making any investments or loan payments. Actually, it is cleaner to define the balance *after* investments.
    
    Let's use a standard cash balance approach:
    *   $Y_{2003}$: Cash remaining in the account (non-invested, non-loaned) at the **end** of 2003.
    *   $Y_{2004}$: Cash remaining in the account at the **end** of 2004.
    *   $Y_{2005}$: Cash remaining in the account at the **end** of 2005.
    *   $Y_{2006}$: Cash remaining in the account at the **end** of 2006.

    However, the problem states surplus funds can be invested. If funds are not invested or used for loans, they just sit there. The bank deposit is the only interest-bearing "idle" option. So, any cash not put into Bonds A, B, C, or used for the loan $D_t$ should be put into the Bank Deposit $b_t$ for that year.
    
    Let's stick to the investment variables defined above ($x_A, x_B, x_C, b_{2003}, b_{2004}, b_{2005}, b_{2006}$). We do not need explicit "cash on hand" variables if we enforce flow balance equalities.

    **Decision Variables Summary:**
    1.  $X_0 \ge 0$: Initial capital raised.
    2.  $x_A \ge 0$: Investment in Bond A (Year 1 start).
    3.  $x_B \ge 0$: Investment in Bond B (Year 1 start).
    4.  $x_C \ge 0$: Investment in Bond C (Year 2 start).
    5.  $b_{2003} \ge 0$: Bank deposit Year 1.
    6.  $b_{2004} \ge 0$: Bank deposit Year 2.
    7.  $b_{2005} \ge 0$: Bank deposit Year 3.
    8.  $b_{2006} \ge 0$: Bank deposit Year 4.

### 3. Objective Function

Minimize the total amount raised at the end of 2002.

$$ \text{Minimize } Z = X_0 $$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**Year 2003 (Beginning):**
The initial capital $X_0$ is available. It must cover the loan for 2003 ($D_{2003}$), the purchase of Bond A ($x_A$), the purchase of Bond B ($x_B$), and the bank deposit ($b_{2003}$). Any remaining cash is assumed to be held in the bank (since bank deposits are optional "any amount", we can assume all non-bond, non-loan cash goes to $b_{2003}$ to earn interest, or simply that the equality holds for the cash flow).
$$ X_0 = D_{2003} + x_A + x_B + b_{2003} $$
Substituting $D_{2003} = 100$:
$$ X_0 - x_A - x_B - b_{2003} = 100 \quad \text{(Constraint 1)} $$

**Year 2004 (Beginning):**
Funds available at the beginning of 2004 come from:
1.  Return from Bank deposit $b_{2003}$ (matures end of 2003, available start of 2004): $1.04 b_{2003}$.
2.  Note: Bond A and B are still running. Their returns come later.

These funds must cover:
1.  Loan for 2004 ($D_{2004} = 150$).
2.  Purchase of Bond C ($x_C$).
3.  Bank deposit for 2004 ($b_{2004}$).

$$ 1.04 b_{2003} = D_{2004} + x_C + b_{2004} $$
Substituting $D_{2004} = 150$:
$$ 1.04 b_{2003} - x_C - b_{2004} = 150 \quad \text{(Constraint 2)} $$

**Year 2005 (Beginning):**
Funds available at the beginning of 2005 come from:
1.  Return from Bank deposit $b_{2004}$: $1.04 b_{2004}$.
2.  Maturity of Bond B (2-year term starting 2003, ends end of 2004, available start of 2005): $1.25 x_B$.
3.  Maturity of Bond C (2-year term starting 2004, ends end of 2005? No, starts 2004, term 2 years -> ends end of 2005. Available start of 2006). So Bond C does NOT mature in 2005.
4.  Maturity of Bond A (3-year term starting 2003, ends end of 2005. Available start of 2006). So Bond A does NOT mature in 2005.

These funds must cover:
1.  Loan for 2005 ($D_{2005} = 120$).
2.  Bank deposit for 2005 ($b_{2005}$).

$$ 1.04 b_{2004} + 1.25 x_B = D_{2005} + b_{2005} $$
Substituting $D_{2005} = 120$:
$$ 1.04 b_{2004} + 1.25 x_B - b_{2005} = 120 \quad \text{(Constraint 3)} $$

**Year 2006 (Beginning):**
Funds available at the beginning of 2006 come from:
1.  Return from Bank deposit $b_{2005}$: $1.04 b_{2005}$.
2.  Maturity of Bond A (ends end of 2005): $1.40 x_A$.
3.  Maturity of Bond C (ends end of 2005): $1.30 x_C$.

These funds must cover:
1.  Loan for 2006 ($D_{2006} = 110$).
2.  Bank deposit for 2006 ($b_{2006}$).
*(Note: Since the project ends in 2006, any remaining cash after paying the final loan doesn't need to be invested in a bank account for future interest, but we can include $b_{2006}$ to balance the equation if we assume all cash is tracked. However, since we are minimizing $X_0$, we want to minimize initial cash. It is optimal to have $b_{2006} = 0$ because holding cash in 2006 yields no further benefit required by the problem, and any excess would just mean we raised too much. Thus, we can simply state that available funds must cover the loan, and any leftover is waste. To be mathematically precise with the equality structure, we include $b_{2006}$ and note it will be 0 in the optimal solution, or we use an inequality $\ge D_{2006}$. Given the "minimize raised funds" objective, an equality with a non-negative slack/bank variable is standard.)*

$$ 1.04 b_{2005} + 1.40 x_A + 1.30 x_C = D_{2006} + b_{2006} $$
Substituting $D_{2006} = 110$:
$$ 1.04 b_{2005} + 1.40 x_A + 1.30 x_C - b_{2006} = 110 \quad \text{(Constraint 4)} $$

**Investment Limits:**
*   Bond A: $x_A \le 60$
*   Bond B: $x_B \le 90$
*   Bond C: $x_C \le 50$
*   Bank Deposits: $b_{2003}, b_{2004}, b_{2005}, b_{2006} \ge 0$ (Unbounded above unless specified, assume sufficient).

**Non-negativity:**
*   $X_0, x_A, x_B, x_C, b_{2003}, b_{2004}, b_{2005}, b_{2006} \ge 0$

### Final Model Formulation

**Model Type:** LP

**Parameters:**
*   $D_{2003} = 100$
*   $D_{2004} = 150$
*   $D_{2005} = 120$
*   $D_{2006} = 110$
*   $r_{bank} = 1.04$
*   $R_A = 1.40, U_A = 60$
*   $R_B = 1.25, U_B = 90$
*   $R_C = 1.30, U_C = 50$

**Variables:**
*   $X_0 \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$
*   $x_A, x_B, x_C \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$
*   $b_{2003}, b_{2004}, b_{2005}, b_{2006} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$

**Objective:**
$$ \text{Minimize } X_0 $$

**Subject To:**

1.  **Year 2003 Cash Flow:**
    $$ X_0 - x_A - x_B - b_{2003} = 100 $$

2.  **Year 2004 Cash Flow:**
    $$ 1.04 b_{2003} - x_C - b_{2004} = 150 $$

3.  **Year 2005 Cash Flow:**
    $$ 1.04 b_{2004} + 1.25 x_B - b_{2005} = 120 $$

4.  **Year 2006 Cash Flow:**
    $$ 1.04 b_{2005} + 1.40 x_A + 1.30 x_C - b_{2006} = 110 $$

5.  **Investment Limits:**
    $$ x_A \le 60 $$
    $$ x_B \le 90 $$
    $$ x_C \le 50 $$

6.  **Non-negativity:**
    $$ X_0, x_A, x_B, x_C, b_{2003}, b_{2004}, b_{2005}, b_{2006} \ge 0 $$
