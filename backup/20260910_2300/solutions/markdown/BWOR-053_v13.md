To formulate this problem, we must define the sets, parameters, and decision variables that capture the scheduling constraints, wage costs, and labor requirements.

### 1. Model Type Declaration
**Model Type:** LP (Linear Programming)
*Note: Since work hours can be continuous (e.g., 0.5 hours) or integer depending on granularity, and the problem asks to minimize wages based on hours, we treat hours as continuous variables bounded by 0. However, given the discrete nature of "students on duty," if shifts must be contiguous integers, it could be MILP. Standard interpretation for "hours worked" in such lab contexts allows continuous time allocation unless specified otherwise. Given the small scale and specific daily caps, continuous variables $x_{i,d}$ are appropriate. If the problem implies discrete 1-hour blocks, they would be integers. We will use continuous variables $x_{i,d} \ge 0$ as is standard for time-allocation problems unless "shifts" are discrete units. Given the caps are integers, the solution will likely be integral or half-integer.*

### 2. Index Sets & Parameters

**Sets:**
*   $I$: Set of students, $I = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$.
    *   $I_{ug} \subseteq I$: Undergraduate students, $\{1, 2, 3, 4\}$.
    *   $I_{gr} \subseteq I$: Graduate students, $\{5, 6\}$.
*   $D$: Set of days, $D = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ corresponding to Monday through Friday.

**Parameters:**
*   $w_i$: Hourly wage for student $i$ (yuan/h).
*   $M_{i,d}$: Maximum available work hours for student $i$ on day $d$.
*   $H_{day}$: Total lab opening hours per day = 14 hours.

### 3. Decision Variables

Let $x_{i,d}$ be the number of hours student $i$ works on day $d$.
*   **Domain:** $x_{i,d} \ge 0$ for all $i \in I, d \in D$.
*   **Implicit Upper Bound:** $x_{i,d} \le M_{i,d}$ (enforced by constraint below).

### 4. Objective Function

Minimize the total weekly wage payment:

$$
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{i \in I} \sum_{d \in D} w_i x_{i,d}
$$

### 5. Operational Constraints

**A. Daily Staffing Requirement**
The lab must have exactly one student on duty at all times. Since the lab is open 14 hours/day, the total hours covered by all students on any given day must equal 14.

$$
\sum_{i \in I} x_{i,d} = 14 \quad \forall d \in D
$$

**B. Individual Daily Availability Constraints**
No student can work more than their maximum available hours on a specific day.

$$
x_{i,d} \le M_{i,d} \quad \forall i \in I, \forall d \in D
$$

**C. Minimum Weekly Hours for Undergraduates**
Each undergraduate student ($i \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$) must work at least 8 hours per week.

$$
\sum_{d \in D} x_{i,d} \ge 8 \quad \forall i \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}
$$

**D. Minimum Weekly Hours for Graduates**
Each graduate student ($i \in \{5, 6\}$) must work at least 7 hours per week.

$$
\sum_{d \in D} x_{i,d} \ge 7 \quad \forall i \in \{5, 6\}
$$

**E. Non-negativity**
$$
x_{i,d} \ge 0 \quad \forall i \in I, \forall d \in D
$$

**Total Demand:**

**Total Supply (Sum of Max Hours):**

Since $110 > 70$, the total capacity exceeds the demand.
