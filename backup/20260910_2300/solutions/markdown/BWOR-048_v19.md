# Operations Research Model: Engine Replacement and Repair Scheduling

## Model Type: LP (Linear Programming)

All decision variables are continuous (engines can be treated as divisible units in aggregate planning; if integer results are required, change to MILP).

## Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Set:**
- $t = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6$: half-year periods (each period is 6 months; total horizon = 3 years)

**Parameters (all costs in thousand yuan):**

| Parameter | Description | Value |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| $d_t$ | Engine replacement demand in period $t$ | $d_1=100,\; d_2=70,\; d_3=80,\; d_4=120,\; d_5=150,\; d_6=140$ |
| $c_N$ | Purchase cost per new engine | $10$ |
| $c_F$ | Fast repair cost per engine | $2$ |
| $c_S$ | Slow repair cost per engine | $10$ |
| $\tau_F$ | Fast repair lead time (periods) | $1$ (sent in period $t$, available in period $t+1$) |
| $\tau_S$ | Slow repair lead time (periods) | $2$ (sent in period $t$, available in period $t+2$) |

**Derived Quantity:**
- Total demand over horizon: $\sum_{t=1}^{6} d_t = 100+70+80+120+150+140 = 660$ engines

## Decision Variables

For each period $t = 1, 2, \dots, 6$:

| Variable | Description | Domain |
|----------|-------------|--------|
| $N_t$ | Number of **new engines purchased** in period $t$ | $N_t \geq 0,\; \text{continuous}$ |
| $F_t$ | Number of used engines **sent for fast repair** in period $t$ (available in period $t+1$) | $F_t \geq 0,\; \text{continuous}$ |
| $S_t$ | Number of used engines **sent for slow repair** in period $t$ (available in period $t+2$) | $S_t \geq 0,\; \text{continuous}$ |

> **Note on repair timing:** Engines sent for fast repair in period $t$ become available at the **start** of period $t+1$ and can satisfy demand in period $t+1$. Engines sent for slow repair in period $t$ become available at the **start** of period $t+2$ and can satisfy demand in period $t+2$. Repairs sent in periods 5 and 6 (fast) or periods 4–6 (slow) would yield engines available after the planning horizon ends (period 7 or later). Since the aircraft are decommissioned after 3 years and engines are scrapped, these late repairs would be wasteful. However, we include them in the model with the understanding that the optimizer will set them to zero (since they incur cost but cannot satisfy any in-horizon demand). For clarity, we note that $F_5, F_6, S_4, S_5, S_6$ will optimally be zero.

## Objective Function

Minimize total maintenance cost (in thousand yuan):

$$\min \; Z = \sum_{t=1}^{6} \left( c_N \cdot N_t + c_F \cdot F_t + c_S \cdot S_t \right)$$

Substituting parameter values:

$$\min \; Z = 10\sum_{t=1}^{6} N_t + 2\sum_{t=1}^{6} F_t + 10\sum_{t=1}^{6} S_t$$

## Operational Constraints

### 1. Demand Satisfaction Constraints (per period)

Each period's demand must be met by: new engines purchased in that period + fast-repaired engines arriving from the previous period + slow-repaired engines arriving from two periods ago.

$$N_t + F_{t-1} + S_{t-2} \geq d_t, \quad \forall\; t = 1, \dots, 6$$

where we define $F_0 = 0$ and $S_0 = S_{-1} = 0$ (no pre-existing repaired engines available at the start).

Expanded explicitly:

| Period $t$ | Constraint |
|:---:|:---|
| 1 | $N_1 \geq 100$ |
| 2 | $N_2 + F_1 \geq 70$ |
| 3 | $N_3 + F_2 + S_1 \geq 80$ |
| 4 | $N_4 + F_3 + S_2 \geq 120$ |
| 5 | $N_5 + F_4 + S_3 \geq 150$ |
| 6 | $N_6 + F_5 + S_4 \geq 140$ |

> **Note:** For periods 5 and 6, $F_5$ and $S_4$ appear on the LHS but $F_5$ (fast repair sent in period 5) arrives in period 6 — this is correctly captured. $S_4$ (slow repair sent in period 4) arrives in period 6 — also correctly captured. Variables $F_6$ and $S_5, S_6$ do not appear in any demand constraint (they arrive after the horizon) and will be driven to zero by the minimization objective.

### 2. Non-Negativity Bounds

$$N_t \geq 0, \quad F_t \geq 0, \quad S_t \geq 0, \quad \forall\; t = 1, \dots, 6$$

| Objective Parameter | Value | Source in Text |

| $c_N$ (new engine cost) | 10 | "purchase cost of each new engine is 10 thousand yuan" |
| $c_F$ (fast repair cost) | 2 | "Fast repair: 2 thousand yuan per engine" |
| $c_S$ (slow repair cost) | 10 | "Slow repair: 10,000 yuan per engine" → 10 thousand yuan |
| $d_1$–$d_6$ (demands) | 100, 70, 80, 120, 150, 140 | "demand…will be: 100, 70, 80, 120, 150, 140" |
| $\tau_F$ (fast lead time) | 1 period | "delivered in half a year (i.e., sent in the current period, ready in the next period)" |
| $\tau_S$ (slow lead time) | 2 periods | "takes one year for delivery (i.e., sent in the current period, ready in the period after next)" |

All parameters are explicitly provided. No `[NOT_PROVIDED]` values.

## Feasibility Sanity Check

- Total demand = 660 engines.
- New engines can satisfy all demand: $\sum N_t = 660$ with $N_t = d_t$ is feasible → cost = $660 \times 10 = 6600$ thousand yuan.
- Fast repair in period 1 can help period 2: send $F_1$ engines, cost $2F_1$, saves $10F_1$ in new purchases → net saving $8F_1$.
- Slow repair in period 1 can help period 3: send $S_1$ engines, cost $10S_1$, saves $10S_1$ → net saving $0S_1$. Slow repair cost equals new engine cost, so it offers no savings (but also no penalty).
- The problem is clearly feasible.

## Summary of Model Structure

- **Type:** LP (all variables continuous; integrality not strictly required for aggregate planning)
- **Variables:** 18 total ($N_1$–$N_6$, $F_1$–$F_6$, $S_1$–$S_6$)
- **Constraints:** 6 demand constraints + 18 non-negativity bounds
- **Objective:** Linear, minimize total cost in thousand yuan
