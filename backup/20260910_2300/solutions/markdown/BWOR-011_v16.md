**Model Type:** Linear Programming (LP)

### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
*   $I = \{1, 2\}$: Set of power stations (Station I, Station II).
*   $J = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of cities (City 1, City 2, City 3).

**Parameters:**
*   $C_{ij}$: Cost (Yuan per unit) of supplying electricity from station $i$ to city $j$.
    *   $C_{11} = 15$, $C_{12} = 18$, $C_{13} = 22$
    *   $C_{21} = 21$, $C_{22} = 25$, $C_{23} = 16$
*   $S_i$: Maximum supply capacity (units) of station $i$.
    *   $S_1 = 400$
    *   $S_2 = 450$
*   $D_j^{\min}$: Minimum required supply (units) for city $j$.
    *   $D_1^{\min} = 290$
    *   $D_2^{\min} = 250$
    *   $D_3^{\min} = 270$
*   $D_j^{\max}$: Maximum required supply (units) for city $j$.
    *   $D_1^{\max} = 320$
    *   $D_2^{\max} = 250$
    *   $D_3^{\max} = 350$

**Note on Total Supply vs. Demand:**
*   Total Supply Capacity = $400 + 450 = 850$ units.
*   Total Minimum Demand = $290 + 250 + 270 = 810$ units.
*   Total Maximum Demand = $320 + 250 + 350 = 920$ units.
*   The problem states: "uses up **all** available electricity from both stations". Therefore, the total amount supplied must equal the total capacity, 850 units. Since $810 \le 850 \le 920$, a feasible solution exists where cities 1 and/or 3 receive more than their minimum but less than their original peak demand, while City 2 is fixed.

### 2. Decision Variables

Let $x_{ij}$ be the amount of electricity (in units) supplied from power station $i$ to city $j$.

*   **Domain:** $x_{ij} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ for all $i \in I, j \in J$.
    *(Electricity is treated as a continuous resource in standard OR transportation models unless discrete units are explicitly mandated, but given the magnitudes, continuous is the standard interpretation for "units" of electricity. Even if discrete, the LP relaxation yields integer solutions for transportation problems with integer coefficients, but we define it as continuous non-negative real numbers for the LP formulation.)*

### 3. Objective Function

Minimize the total cost of electricity supply.

$$
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{i \in I} \sum_{j \in J} C_{ij} x_{ij}
$$

Expanded:
$$
Z = 15x_{11} + 18x_{12} + 22x_{13} + 21x_{21} + 25x_{22} + 16x_{23}
$$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**A. Supply Constraints (Flow Out of Stations)**
The problem states that **all** available electricity must be used. Therefore, the sum of electricity sent out from each station must equal its maximum capacity.

1.  Station I Supply:
    $$ x_{11} + x_{12} + x_{13} = 400 $$
2.  Station II Supply:
    $$ x_{21} + x_{22} + x_{23} = 450 $$

**B. Demand Constraints (Flow Into Cities)**
Each city has a specific range of acceptable supply.

1.  City 1 (Min 290, Max 320):
    $$ 290 \le x_{11} + x_{21} \le 320 $$
2.  City 2 (Fixed at 250):
    $$ x_{12} + x_{22} = 250 $$
3.  City 3 (Min 270, Max 350):
    $$ 270 \le x_{13} + x_{23} \le 350 $$

**C. Non-negativity Constraints**

$$ x_{ij} \ge 0 \quad \forall i \in \{1, 2\}, j \in \{1, 2, 3\} $$

---
**Feasibility Check:**
Sum of LHS of supply constraints = $400 + 450 = 850$.
Sum of LHS of demand constraints (using variables) = $(x_{11}+x_{21}) + (x_{12}+x_{22}) + (x_{13}+x_{23})$.
This must equal 850.
Let $D_1 = x_{11}+x_{21}$, $D_2 = 250$, $D_3 = x_{13}+x_{23}$.
$D_1 + 250 + D_3 = 850 \implies D_1 + D_3 = 600$.
Constraints on $D_1$: $[290, 320]$.
Constraints on $D_3$: $[270, 350]$.
Possible? Yes. E.g., if $D_1=300$, then $D_3=300$, which is within $[270, 350]$.
The model is feasible.
