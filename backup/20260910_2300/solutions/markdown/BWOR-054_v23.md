# Mathematical Model Formulation

## Model Type Declaration
**MILP** (Mixed-Integer Linear Program)
- Decision variables are binary (integer) indicating whether a student is assigned to a specific hour.
- Objective and constraints are linear.

## Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
- $S = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$: Set of students.
  - $S_{UG} = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$: Undergraduate students.
  - $S_{GR} = \{5, 6\}$: Graduate students.
- $D = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$: Set of days (Monday to Friday).
- $H = \{1, 2, \dots, 14\}$: Set of hours in a day.
  - Hour $h=1$ corresponds to 8:00-9:00, $h=2$ to 9:00-10:00, ..., $h=14$ to 22:00-23:00 (10:00 PM).
  - Note: The lab is open 8:00 to 10:00, which is 14 hours.

**Parameters:**
- $w_s$: Hourly wage (yuan/hour) for student $s \in S$.
  - $w_1 = 10.0$
  - $w_2 = 10.0$
  - $w_3 = 9.9$
  - $w_4 = 9.8$
  - $w_5 = 10.8$
  - $w_6 = 11.3$
- $M_{s,d}$: Maximum daily work hours for student $s \in S$ on day $d \in D$.
  - **Student 1**: $M_{1,1}=6, M_{1,2}=0, M_{1,3}=9, M_{1,4}=0, M_{1,5}=7$
  - **Student 2**: $M_{2,1}=0, M_{2,2}=9, M_{2,3}=0, M_{2,4}=6, M_{2,5}=0$
  - **Student 3**: $M_{3,1}=4, M_{3,2}=8, M_{3,3}=3, M_{3,4}=0, M_{3,5}=5$
  - **Student 4**: $M_{4,1}=5, M_{4,2}=5, M_{4,3}=6, M_{4,4}=0, M_{4,5}=4$
  - **Student 5**: $M_{5,1}=3, M_{5,2}=0, M_{5,3}=4, M_{5,4}=8, M_{5,5}=0$
  - **Student 6**: $M_{6,1}=0, M_{6,2}=9, M_{6,3}=0, M_{6,4}=6, M_{6,5}=3$
- $L_s$: Minimum weekly work hours for student $s \in S$.
  - For $s \in S_{UG}$ (1-4): $L_s = 8$
  - For $s \in S_{GR}$ (5-6): $L_s = 7$

## Decision Variables
- $x_{s,d,h}$: Binary variable.
  - $x_{s,d,h} = 1$ if student $s$ is scheduled on day $d$ during hour $h$.
  - $x_{s,d,h} = 0$ otherwise.
  - Domain: $x_{s,d,h} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall s \in S, d \in D, h \in H$.

## Objective Function
Minimize the total wage payment. Since each hour of work costs $w_s$ yuan:

$$
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{s \in S} \sum_{d \in D} \sum_{h \in H} w_s \cdot x_{s,d,h}
$$

## Operational Constraints

**1. Coverage Constraint (Exactly one student on duty at all times)**
For every day $d$ and every hour $h$, exactly one student must be assigned.

$$
\sum_{s \in S} x_{s,d,h} = 1 \quad \forall d \in D, h \in H
$$

**2. Maximum Daily Work Hours**
A student cannot exceed their maximum allowed hours on any given day.

$$
\sum_{h \in H} x_{s,d,h} \le M_{s,d} \quad \forall s \in S, d \in D
$$

**3. Minimum Weekly Work Hours**
Each student must work at least their minimum weekly requirement.

$$
\sum_{d \in D} \sum_{h \in H} x_{s,d,h} \ge L_s \quad \forall s \in S
$$

**4. Maximum Assignments per Week**
Each student can be assigned for duty no more than twice a week.
*Interpretation*: This constraint limits the number of days a student works. If a student is assigned to any hour on a day, that day counts as one assignment. We need to ensure that for each student, the number of days $d$ where $\sum_{h \in H} x_{s,d,h} > 0$ is at most 2.
To linearize this, we can introduce an auxiliary binary variable $y_{s,d}$:
- $y_{s,d} = 1$ if student $s$ works on day $d$, 0 otherwise.
- Domain: $y_{s,d} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall s \in S, d \in D$.

Constraints linking $y$ and $x$:
$$
\sum_{h \in H} x_{s,d,h} \le 14 \cdot y_{s,d} \quad \forall s \in S, d \in D
$$
(If $y_{s,d}=0$, all $x_{s,d,h}=0$. If $y_{s,d}=1$, $x$ can be non-zero up to 14 hours, but is also bounded by $M_{s,d}$).

Constraint on number of working days:
$$
\sum_{d \in D} y_{s,d} \le 2 \quad \forall s \in S
$$

**5. Maximum Students Per Day**
No more than 3 students can be scheduled for duty per day.
This means that for any day $d$, the number of distinct students working on that day must be $\le 3$.
Using the auxiliary variable $y_{s,d}$ defined above:
$$
\sum_{s \in S} y_{s,d} \le 3 \quad \forall d \in D
$$

**6. Non-negativity and Integrality**
$$
x_{s,d,h} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall s \in S, d \in D, h \in H
$$
$$
y_{s,d} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall s \in S, d \in D
$$

## Feasibility Sanity Check
- **Total Demand**: 5 days $\times$ 14 hours/day = 70 student-hours required per week.
- **Total Supply Capacity**:
  - Student 1: $6+0+9+0+7 = 22$ hrs (max 2 days, e.g., Wed 9h + Fri 7h = 16h? No, max 2 days. Best 2 days are Wed(9) and Fri(7) or Mon(6) and Wed(9). Max possible with 2 days is $9+7=16$ or $9+6=15$ or $7+6=13$. Let's look at max 2 days: Mon(6)+Wed(9)=15, Wed(9)+Fri(7)=16. So max 16h.)
  - Student 2: $0+9+0+6+0 = 15$ hrs (Max 2 days: Tue(9)+Thu(6)=15h).
  - Student 3: $4+8+3+0+5 = 20$ hrs (Max 2 days: Tue(8)+Thu(0)? No. Tue(8)+Fri(5)=13, Tue(8)+Mon(4)=12, Tue(8)+Wed(3)=11. Max 13h).
  - Student 4: $5+5+6+0+4 = 20$ hrs (Max 2 days: Wed(6)+Tue(5)=11, Wed(6)+Mon(5)=11, Wed(6)+Fri(4)=10. Max 11h).
  - Student 5: $3+0+4+8+0 = 15$ hrs (Max 2 days: Thu(8)+Wed(4)=12, Thu(8)+Mon(3)=11. Max 12h).
  - Student 6: $0+9+0+6+3 = 18$ hrs (Max 2 days: Tue(9)+Thu(6)=15, Tue(9)+Fri(3)=12. Max 15h).
  
  Sum of max possible hours (subject to 2-day limit):
  S1: 16, S2: 15, S3: 13, S4: 11, S5: 12, S6: 15.
  Total Max Supply = $16+15+13+11+12+15 = 82$ hours.
  Total Demand = 70 hours.
  $82 \ge 70$, so it is potentially feasible.

- **Per-Day Capacity**:
  - Mon: S1(6), S3(4), S4(5), S5(3). S2, S6 are 0. Max distinct students = 4. But constraint says $\le 3$ students per day.
    - Can we cover 14 hours on Mon with $\le 3$ students?
    - Max hours from top 3 students on Mon: S1(6) + S4(5) + S5(3) = 14. Exactly 14.
    - So Mon is tight but feasible if we use S1, S4, S5.
  - Tue: S2(9), S3(8), S4(5), S6(9). S1, S5 are 0.
    - Max hours from top 3: S2(9) + S6(9) + S3(8) = 26. Need 14. Feasible.
  - Wed: S1(9), S3(3), S4(6), S5(4). S2, S6 are 0.
    - Max hours from top 3: S1(9) + S4(6) + S5(4) = 19. Need 14. Feasible.
  - Thu: S2(6), S4(0 - wait, M4,4=0), S5(8), S6(6). S1, S3 are 0.
    - Students available: S2(6), S5(8), S6(6).
    - Max hours: $6+8+6 = 20$. Need 14. Feasible.
  - Fri: S1(7), S3(5), S4(4), S6(3). S2, S5 are 0.
    - Max hours from top 3: S1(7) + S3(5) + S4(4) = 16. Need 14. Feasible.

The model appears feasible.
