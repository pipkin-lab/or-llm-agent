# Mathematical Optimization Model: Production Scheduling with Inventory and Backlog

## 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
*   $P = \{I, II, III\}$: Set of products.
*   $T = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$: Set of quarters.
*   $P_{Q2} = \{II, III\}$: Set of products producible in Quarter 2 (Product I is excluded).
*   $P_{All} = \{I, II, III\}$: Set of all products.

**Parameters:**
*   $D_{p,t}$: Contracted demand for product $p$ in quarter $t$ (units).
    *   $D_{I,1}=1500, D_{I,2}=1000, D_{I,3}=2000, D_{I,4}=1200$
    *   $D_{II,1}=1500, D_{II,2}=1500, D_{II,3}=1200, D_{II,4}=1500$
    *   $D_{III,1}=1000, D_{III,2}=2000, D_{III,3}=1500, D_{III,4}=2500$
*   $H_p$: Holding cost per unit per quarter for product $p$.
    *   $H_I = 5, H_{II} = 5, H_{III} = 5$
*   $L_p$: Late delivery penalty per unit per quarter for product $p$.
    *   $L_I = 20, L_{II} = 20, L_{III} = 10$
*   $R_p$: Production rate (labor hours) per unit for product $p$.
    *   $R_I = 2, R_{II} = 4, R_{III} = 3$
*   $C_t$: Available labor hours in quarter $t$.
    *   $C_1 = 15000, C_2 = 15000, C_3 = 15000, C_4 = 15000$
*   $Inv_{p,0}$: Initial inventory for product $p$ at start of Q1.
    *   $Inv_{I,0} = 0, Inv_{II,0} = 0, Inv_{III,0} = 0$
*   $Back_{p,0}$: Initial backlog for product $p$ at start of Q1.
    *   $Back_{I,0} = 0, Inv_{II,0} = 0, Back_{III,0} = 0$
*   $Inv_{p,T}$: Required ending inventory for product $p$ at end of Q4.
    *   $Inv_{I,4} = 150, Inv_{II,4} = 150, Inv_{III,4} = 150$

## 2. Decision Variables

*   $x_{p,t} \ge 0$: Quantity of product $p$ produced in quarter $t$.
    *   Domain: Continuous (Large scale aggregate planning, $D > 1000$).
    *   Constraint: $x_{I,2} = 0$ (Product I cannot be produced in Q2).
*   $Inv_{p,t} \ge 0$: Inventory level of product $p$ at the end of quarter $t$.
*   $Back_{p,t} \ge 0$: Backlog (unmet demand) of product $p$ at the end of quarter $t$.

## 3. Objective Function

Minimize total cost (Holding Costs + Late Delivery Penalties) over all products and quarters.

$$
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{p \in P} \sum_{t=1}^{4} \left( H_p \cdot Inv_{p,t} + L_p \cdot Back_{p,t} \right)
$$

Expanding the costs based on parameters:
$$
Z = \sum_{t=1}^{4} \Big[ 5(Inv_{I,t} + Inv_{II,t} + Inv_{III,t}) + 20(Back_{I,t} + Back_{II,t}) + 10(Back_{III,t}) \Big]
$$

## 4. Operational Constraints

### A. Inventory Balance Constraints
For each product $p$ and quarter $t$, the inventory and backlog must balance.
The standard formulation for simultaneous inventory and backlog is:
$$
Inv_{p,t} - Back_{p,t} = Inv_{p,t-1} - Back_{p,t-1} + x_{p,t} - D_{p,t} \quad \forall p \in P, t \in T
$$
*(Note: Since $Inv_{p,t}$ and $Back_{p,t}$ are non-negative and their simultaneous positivity is suboptimal due to linear costs, this single equality correctly models the net position.)*

Explicitly for $t=1$ (using $Inv_{p,0}=0, Back_{p,0}=0$):
*   $Inv_{I,1} - Back_{I,1} = x_{I,1} - 1500$
*   $Inv_{II,1} - Back_{II,1} = x_{II,1} - 1500$
*   $Inv_{III,1} - Back_{III,1} = x_{III,1} - 1000$

For $t=2, 3, 4$:
*   $Inv_{p,t} - Back_{p,t} = Inv_{p,t-1} - Back_{p,t-1} + x_{p,t} - D_{p,t}$

### B. Labor Capacity Constraints
Total production hours in each quarter must not exceed available hours.
$$
\sum_{p \in P_{Allowed,t}} R_p \cdot x_{p,t} \le C_t \quad \forall t \in T
$$

Specifically:
*   **Q1:** $2x_{I,1} + 4x_{II,1} + 3x_{III,1} \le 15000$
*   **Q2:** $4x_{II,2} + 3x_{III,2} \le 15000$ (Product I production is 0)
*   **Q3:** $2x_{I,3} + 4x_{II,3} + 3x_{III,3} \le 15000$
*   **Q4:** $2x_{I,4} + 4x_{II,4} + 3x_{III,4} \le 15000$

### C. Ending Inventory Requirements
At the end of Q4, the inventory must be exactly 150 units for each product. Since ending backlog is typically undesirable or constrained to zero in final periods unless specified, and "inventory of 150" implies net positive stock, we enforce:
$$
Inv_{p,4} = 150 \quad \forall p \in P
$$
$$
Back_{p,4} = 0 \quad \forall p \in P
$$
*(Assumption: All backlogs must be cleared by end of Q4 to meet the specific inventory requirement of 150 positive units. If backlog remained, it would offset the inventory, but "inventory of 150" usually denotes physical stock on hand. Given the high penalty for late delivery, the model will likely clear backlogs anyway. To be precise, the balance equation $Inv_{p,4} - Back_{p,4} = 150$ is the strict mathematical interpretation, but given the problem asks for "inventory of 150", we fix $Inv_{p,4}=150$ and $Back_{p,4}=0$.)*

### D. Non-negativity and Production Restrictions
$$
x_{p,t} \ge 0 \quad \forall p, t
$$
$$
x_{I,2} = 0
$$
$$
Inv_{p,t} \ge 0, \quad Back_{p,t} \ge 0 \quad \forall p, t
$$
