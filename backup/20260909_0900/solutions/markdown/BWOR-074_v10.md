To minimize the total sorting workload, we must model the process of sequentially removing items of a specific grade from a mixed pile.

### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Sets:**
*   $G = \{A, B, C, D, E\}$: The set of grade labels.
*   $N = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$: The set of positions in the sorting sequence (1 being the first grade sorted, 5 being the last).

**Parameters:**
*   $V = 50,000$: Total number of participants (answer sheets).
*   $p_g$: The proportion of grade $g$ in the population, for $g \in G$.
    *   $p_A = 0.10$
    *   $p_B = 0.25$
    *   $p_C = 0.30$
    *   $p_D = 0.20$
    *   $p_E = 0.15$
*   $n_g$: The absolute count of answer sheets for grade $g$, calculated as $n_g = V \times p_g$.
    *   $n_A = 5,000$
    *   $n_B = 12,500$
    *   $n_C = 15,000$
    *   $n_D = 10,000$
    *   $n_E = 7,500$

### 2. Decision Variables

Let $x_{g, k}$ be a binary decision variable indicating whether grade $g$ is sorted at position $k$ in the sequence.
*   $x_{g, k} = 1$ if grade $g$ is assigned to the $k$-th position in the sorting order.
*   $x_{g, k} = 0$ otherwise.

**Domains:**
*   $x_{g, k} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall g \in G, k \in N$

### 3. Objective Function

**Workload Calculation Logic:**
When sorting grade $g$ at position $k$, the computer must scan through all answer sheets that have *not yet been removed*. These are:
1.  All sheets of grade $g$ itself (to identify and remove them).
2.  All sheets of grades $h$ that are scheduled to be sorted *after* position $k$ (i.e., at positions $j > k$).

Sheets of grades scheduled *before* position $k$ (i.e., at positions $j < k$) have already been removed and do not contribute to the workload at step $k$.

Let $W_k$ be the workload at step $k$.
The total workload $Z$ is the sum of workloads over all steps $k=1$ to $5$.

For a specific grade $g$ placed at position $k$, it contributes to the workload in two ways:
1.  **As the target:** It is scanned at step $k$. Contribution: $n_g$.
2.  **As a distractor:** It remains in the pile for all steps $j < k$ (steps before its removal). At each such step $j$, the machine scans the entire current pile, which includes $n_g$.
    *   Number of times $n_g$ is scanned as a distractor = $k - 1$.
    *   Total contribution of grade $g$ to the total workload = $n_g \times (1 + (k - 1)) = n_g \times k$.

Thus, the total sorting workload is:
$$ Z = \sum_{g \in G} \sum_{k \in N} n_g \cdot k \cdot x_{g, k} $$

**Objective:**
$$ \text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{g \in G} \sum_{k \in N} n_g \cdot k \cdot x_{g, k} $$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**1. Each grade is assigned to exactly one position:**
$$ \sum_{k \in N} x_{g, k} = 1 \quad \forall g \in G $$

**2. Each position in the sequence is assigned exactly one grade:**
$$ \sum_{g \in G} x_{g, k} = 1 \quad \forall k \in N $$

**3. Binary Constraints:**
$$ x_{g, k} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall g \in G, k \in N $$

---

### Logical Deduction for Optimal Solution

The objective function is a linear assignment problem. To minimize $\sum n_g \cdot k$, we should assign the largest $n_g$ to the smallest $k$ (earliest positions) and the smallest $n_g$ to the largest $k$ (latest positions). This is a classic Rearrangement Inequality application: to minimize the sum of products of two sequences, one should sort one sequence in ascending order and the other in descending order.

**Counts ($n_g$):**
*   $n_C = 15,000$
*   $n_B = 12,500$
*   $n_D = 10,000$
*   $n_A = 5,000$
*   $n_E = 7,500$

**Sorted Counts (Descending):**
1.  $15,000$ (C)
2.  $12,500$ (B)
3.  $10,000$ (D)
4.  $7,500$ (E)
5.  $5,000$ (A)

**Positions ($k$):**
1, 2, 3, 4, 5

**Optimal Assignment:**
*   Position 1 ($k=1$): Grade C ($n=15,000$)
*   Position 2 ($k=2$): Grade B ($n=12,500$)
*   Position 3 ($k=3$): Grade D ($n=10,000$)
*   Position 4 ($k=4$): Grade E ($n=7,500$)
*   Position 5 ($k=5$): Grade A ($n=5,000$)

**Verification of Workload:**
*   Step 1 (Sort C): Scan all 50,000. Remove 15,000. Remaining: 35,000.
*   Step 2 (Sort B): Scan remaining 35,000. Remove 12,500. Remaining: 22,500.
*   Step 3 (Sort D): Scan remaining 22,500. Remove 10,000. Remaining: 12,500.
*   Step 4 (Sort E): Scan remaining 12,500. Remove 7,500. Remaining: 5,000.
*   Step 5 (Sort A): Scan remaining 5,000. Remove 5,000. Remaining: 0.

Total Workload = $50,000 + 35,000 + 22,500 + 12,500 + 5,000 = 125,000$.

Using the formula:
$Z = 1(15,000) + 2(12,500) + 3(10,000) + 4(7,500) + 5(5,000)$
$Z = 15,000 + 25,000 + 30,000 + 30,000 + 25,000 = 125,000$.

### Final Answer

The optimal order to minimize total sorting workload is to sort the grades in **descending order of their frequency**.

**Optimal Sequence:**
1.  **C** (30%)
2.  **B** (25%)
3.  **D** (20%)
4.  **E** (15%)
5.  **A** (10%)

**Total Minimum Workload:** 125,000 scans.
