# Nurse Scheduling Optimization Model

## 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
*   $S = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$: The set of shift start times.
    *   $s=1$: 2:00
    *   $s=2$: 6:00
    *   $s=3$: 10:00
    *   $s=4$: 14:00
    *   $s=5$: 18:00
    *   $s=6$: 22:00
*   $T = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$: The set of time periods (4-hour blocks).
    *   $t=1$: 2:00–6:00
    *   $t=2$: 6:00–10:00
    *   $t=3$: 10:00–14:00
    *   $t=4$: 14:00–18:00
    *   $t=5$: 18:00–22:00
    *   $t=6$: 22:00–2:00

**Parameters:**
*   $R_t$: Required number of nurses for time period $t$.
    *   $R_1 = 10$
    *   $R_2 = 15$
    *   $R_3 = 25$
    *   $R_4 = 20$
    *   $R_5 = 18$
    *   $R_6 = 12$
*   $C_{FT}$: Cost per full-time nurse for an 8-hour shift.
    *   $C_{FT} = 10 \, \text{¥/hour} \times 8 \, \text{hours} = 80 \, \text{¥}$.
*   $C_{CT}$: Cost per contract nurse for an 8-hour shift.
    *   $C_{CT} = 15 \, \text{¥/hour} \times 8 \, \text{hours} = 120 \, \text{¥}$.
*   $A_{s,t}$: Incidence matrix indicating if a shift starting at $s$ covers time period $t$.
    *   A shift starting at time $s$ covers period $t$ and period $t+1$ (modulo 6).
    *   Specifically:
        *   Shift $s=1$ (2:00) covers $t=1$ (2-6) and $t=2$ (6-10).
        *   Shift $s=2$ (6:00) covers $t=2$ (6-10) and $t=3$ (10-14).
        *   Shift $s=3$ (10:00) covers $t=3$ (10-14) and $t=4$ (14-18).
        *   Shift $s=4$ (14:00) covers $t=4$ (14-18) and $t=5$ (18-22).
        *   Shift $s=5$ (18:00) covers $t=5$ (18-22) and $t=6$ (22-2).
        *   Shift $s=6$ (22:00) covers $t=6$ (22-2) and $t=1$ (2-6).

## 2. Decision Variables

Since the problem asks "how many should be hired", and nurses are discrete individuals, we use Integer variables. However, given the scale is small (requirements < 30), Integer Linear Programming (ILP) is appropriate.

*   $x_s$: Number of **full-time** nurses assigned to the shift starting at time $s$, for $s \in S$.
    *   Domain: $x_s \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$
*   $y_s$: Number of **contract** nurses assigned to the shift starting at time $s$, for $s \in S$.
    *   Domain: $y_s \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$

## 3. Objective Function

Minimize the total labor cost.

$$
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{s \in S} (C_{FT} \cdot x_s + C_{CT} \cdot y_s)
$$

Substituting costs:
$$
Z = \sum_{s=1}^{6} (80 x_s + 120 y_s)
$$

## 4. Operational Constraints

**Coverage Constraints:**
For each time period $t \in \{1, \dots, 6\}$, the total number of full-time and contract nurses working must meet or exceed the requirement $R_t$.

A shift starting at $s$ covers period $t$ if $t = s$ or $t = (s \pmod 6) + 1$ (with appropriate 1-based indexing logic). Explicitly:

1.  **Period 1 (2:00–6:00):** Covered by Shift 1 (2:00 start) and Shift 6 (22:00 start).
    $$ x_1 + y_1 + x_6 + y_6 \ge 10 $$

2.  **Period 2 (6:00–10:00):** Covered by Shift 1 (2:00 start) and Shift 2 (6:00 start).
    $$ x_1 + y_1 + x_2 + y_2 \ge 15 $$

3.  **Period 3 (10:00–14:00):** Covered by Shift 2 (6:00 start) and Shift 3 (10:00 start).
    $$ x_2 + y_2 + x_3 + y_3 \ge 25 $$

4.  **Period 4 (14:00–18:00):** Covered by Shift 3 (10:00 start) and Shift 4 (14:00 start).
    $$ x_3 + y_3 + x_4 + y_4 \ge 20 $$

5.  **Period 5 (18:00–22:00):** Covered by Shift 4 (14:00 start) and Shift 5 (18:00 start).
    $$ x_4 + y_4 + x_5 + y_5 \ge 18 $$

6.  **Period 6 (22:00–2:00):** Covered by Shift 5 (18:00 start) and Shift 6 (22:00 start).
    $$ x_5 + y_5 + x_6 + y_6 \ge 12 $$

**Non-negativity and Integrality:**
$$ x_s \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}, \quad \forall s \in \{1, \dots, 6\} $$
$$ y_s \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}, \quad \forall s \in \{1, \dots, 6\} $$

### Notes on Model Interpretation
*   **Cost Efficiency:** Since contract nurses are more expensive ($120/shift) than full-time nurses ($80/shift), the optimal solution will prioritize full-time nurses for all shifts. Contract nurses will only be used if there is a constraint limiting the total number of full-time nurses, which is not present in this problem description. Therefore, the optimal solution will likely have $y_s = 0$ for all $s$, and the problem reduces to finding the minimum cost mix of full-time nurses. If the question implies a fixed pool of full-time nurses is insufficient, contract nurses would fill the gap. Without a cap on full-time nurses, the answer to "Should the hospital hire contract nurses?" is **No**, it is cheaper to hire full-time nurses for all required slots.
*   **Solving Insight:** The problem is effectively a linear program (relaxed to integers) where we minimize cost subject to coverage. Since $C_{FT} < C_{CT}$, the solver will set $y_s = 0$ for all $s$ unless forced otherwise by other constraints (which do not exist here). The solution will be the standard minimum cost nurse scheduling solution using only full-time nurses.
