# Optimization Model: Factory Production and Inventory Planning

## 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
- $T = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$: Set of months (January to June).

**Parameters:**
- $D_t$: Delivery demand in month $t$ (in units of 100s).
  - From Table 7-5: $D_1=1, D_2=2, D_3=5, D_4=3, D_5=2, D_6=1$.
- $C_{cap}$: Monthly production capacity = 400 units = 4 (in units of 100s).
- $W_{cap}$: Warehouse storage capacity = 300 units = 3 (in units of 100s).
- $c_v$: Variable production cost = 10,000 yuan per 100 units.
- $c_f$: Fixed operating cost = 4,000 yuan (if production > 0).
- $h$: Holding cost = 1,000 yuan per 100 units per month.

**Unit Conversion Note:**
To simplify calculations and maintain consistency, all quantities are measured in **hundreds of units**.
- Capacity: 4
- Storage: 3
- Variable Cost: 10 (thousand yuan) per unit of 100s
- Fixed Cost: 4 (thousand yuan)
- Holding Cost: 1 (thousand yuan) per unit of 100s per month

## 2. Decision Variables

- $x_t$: Amount produced in month $t$ (in hundreds of units), $t \in T$.
  - **Domain**: $x_t \ge 0$ (Continuous).
  - *Justification*: The problem involves aggregate production planning for a factory with capacities in the hundreds. Standard LP relaxation is appropriate for this scale (BWOR-080 analysis). Integrality is not explicitly required, and fractional production in aggregate units (e.g., 250 units = 2.5 hundreds) is physically possible.
- $y_t$: Binary variable indicating whether production occurs in month $t$.
  - $y_t = 1$ if $x_t > 0$, else $0$.
  - $y_t \in \{0, 1\}$.
- $I_t$: Inventory level at the end of month $t$ (in hundreds of units).
  - $I_t \ge 0$.

## 3. Objective Function

Minimize Total Cost (in thousand yuan):

$$
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{t=1}^{6} \left( c_v x_t + c_f y_t + h I_t \right)
$$

Substituting parameter values:
$$
Z = \sum_{t=1}^{6} (10 x_t + 4 y_t + 1 I_t)
$$

*Note: The holding cost is incurred on the inventory held at the end of each month.*

## 4. Operational Constraints

**1. Inventory Balance Constraints:**
For each month $t \in T$:
$$
I_{t-1} + x_t - D_t = I_t
$$
With boundary conditions:
- $I_0 = 0$ (No initial inventory)
- $I_6 = 0$ (No remaining inventory after final delivery)

Explicitly:
- $t=1$: $x_1 - 1 = I_1$
- $t=2$: $I_1 + x_2 - 2 = I_2$
- $t=3$: $I_2 + x_3 - 5 = I_3$
- $t=4$: $I_3 + x_4 - 3 = I_4$
- $t=5$: $I_4 + x_5 - 2 = I_5$
- $t=6$: $I_5 + x_6 - 1 = 0 \implies x_6 = 1 - I_5$

**2. Production Capacity Constraints:**
Production in any month cannot exceed the monthly capacity.
$$
x_t \le C_{cap} \cdot y_t \quad \forall t \in T
$$
$$
x_t \le 4 y_t \quad \forall t \in T
$$
*(This constraint links the continuous production variable $x_t$ to the binary fixed-cost variable $y_t$. If $x_t > 0$, then $y_t$ must be 1. If $y_t=0$, then $x_t=0$.)*

**3. Storage Capacity Constraints:**
Inventory at the end of any month cannot exceed warehouse capacity.
$$
I_t \le W_{cap} \quad \forall t \in T
$$
$$
I_t \le 3 \quad \forall t \in T
$$

**4. Non-negativity and Domain Constraints:**
$$
x_t \ge 0 \quad \forall t \in T
$$
$$
I_t \ge 0 \quad \forall t \in T
$$
$$
y_t \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall t \in T
$$

## 5. Model Verification & Notes

1.  **Integrality Check**: The problem is a multi-period production planning problem. While individual units are discrete, the scale (hundreds of units) and standard OR practice for such aggregate problems suggest using continuous variables for production $x_t$ and inventory $I_t$. The binary variable $y_t$ handles the fixed cost structure. This results in a Mixed Integer Linear Program (MILP).
2.  **Fixed Cost Logic**: The constraint $x_t \le 4 y_t$ correctly enforces that the fixed cost of 4,000 yuan is incurred only if production is positive.
3.  **Holding Cost Timing**: The holding cost $h I_t$ is applied to the inventory $I_t$ at the end of month $t$. This is the standard convention for "storage cost per month" where the cost is accrued for the period the item is held.
4.  **Boundary Conditions**: The constraints $I_0=0$ and $I_6=0$ are strictly enforced by the balance equations and the explicit definition of $I_6$.
5.  **Data Completeness**: All demand values, capacities, and costs are explicitly provided in the problem statement. No assumptions about missing data are necessary.
