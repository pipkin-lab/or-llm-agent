# Mathematical Optimization Model for Hongyin Company Loan Fund Planning

## 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
- $t \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$: Time periods (Years 2003, 2004, 2005, 2006).
- $b \in \{A, B, C\}$: Specific investment bonds.

**Parameters (in thousand dollars):**
- $D_t$: Loan demand required at the beginning of year $t$.
  - $D_1 = 100$
  - $D_2 = 150$
  - $D_3 = 120$
  - $D_4 = 110$
- $r_{bank} = 0.04$: Annual bank interest rate (4%).
- $R_A = 1.40$: Total return factor for Bond A (140% of investment).
- $R_B = 1.25$: Total return factor for Bond B (125% of investment).
- $R_C = 1.30$: Total return factor for Bond C (130% of investment).
- $U_A = 60$: Maximum investment limit for Bond A.
- $U_B = 90$: Maximum investment limit for Bond B.
- $U_C = 50$: Maximum investment limit for Bond C.

**Maturity Timing Analysis:**
- **Bond A**: Invested at start of 2003 ($t=1$), matures after 3 years $\rightarrow$ Start of 2006 ($t=4$).
- **Bond B**: Invested at start of 2003 ($t=1$), matures after 2 years $\rightarrow$ Start of 2005 ($t=3$).
- **Bond C**: Invested at start of 2004 ($t=2$), matures after 2 years $\rightarrow$ Start of 2006 ($t=4$).
- **Bank Account**: Deposits at start of year $t$ yield $1.04$ times the deposit at the **end** of year $t$ (which is the start of year $t+1$). Note: The problem states "withdrawal at the end of the same year". Since loans are needed at the *beginning* of each year, funds from bank deposits made in year $t$ are available for the loan requirement in year $t+1$.

## 2. Decision Variables

Let $F$ be the total amount raised at the end of 2002 (thousand dollars).

**Investment Variables (continuous, non-negative):**
- $x_A$: Amount invested in Bond A (thousand dollars).
- $x_B$: Amount invested in Bond B (thousand dollars).
- $x_C$: Amount invested in Bond C (thousand dollars).
- $y_t$: Amount deposited in the bank at the beginning of year $t$ (thousand dollars), for $t=1, 2, 3, 4$.
  - *Note*: Deposits in year 4 are unnecessary as there are no future cash flows to fund, but we can define $y_4=0$ or simply omit it. We will include $y_t$ for $t=1, 2, 3$ as deposits in year 4 do not help meet any future obligations (the project ends after year 4).

**Auxiliary Flow Variables (continuous, non-negative):**
- $S_t$: Surplus funds available at the beginning of year $t$ **before** making investments and paying the loan $D_t$. This represents the total cash on hand entering period $t$.
  - Alternatively, and more directly, we can model the cash balance equation directly without explicit $S_t$ variables if we track the flow explicitly. Let's use a direct flow balance approach for clarity.

Let's refine the variables to ensure a clean LP:
- $F$: Total funds raised (Objective variable).
- $x_A, x_B, x_C$: Bond investments.
- $y_1, y_2, y_3$: Bank deposits at start of years 2003, 2004, 2005 respectively. (Deposit in 2006 is not useful).

**Implicit Cash Flows:**
- Cash available at start of 2003: $F$.
- Cash available at start of 2004: Maturity of Bank Deposit $y_1$ (which is $1.04 y_1$).
- Cash available at start of 2005: Maturity of Bond B ($1.25 x_B$) + Maturity of Bank Deposit $y_2$ ($1.04 y_2$).
- Cash available at start of 2006: Maturity of Bond A ($1.40 x_A$) + Maturity of Bond C ($1.30 x_C$) + Maturity of Bank Deposit $y_3$ ($1.04 y_3$).

## 3. Objective Function

Minimize the total amount raised at the end of 2002:

$$ \min Z = F $$

## 4. Operational Constraints

**1. Cash Balance / Flow Constraints:**
The funds available at the start of each year must cover the loan requirement for that year plus any new investments made at that start. Any remaining cash cannot be carried over to the next year *unless* it is explicitly invested (in bonds or bank). Since the problem implies we must "make full use" or at least allow surplus to be invested, and we are minimizing the initial raise, we assume all unused cash not invested is simply not there (or equivalently, we only raise what is needed). A standard way to model this is:
**Total Cash Inflow at Start of Year $t$ $\geq$ Loan Demand $D_t$ + New Investments in Year $t$**.

*Note on "Surplus":* If we raise $F$, and in Year 1 we spend $D_1 + x_A + x_B + y_1$, any leftover cash $F - (D_1 + x_A + x_B + y_1)$ is idle. To minimize $F$, we will push $F$ down until at least one constraint binds. It is safer to model this as an equality if we assume no cash is left uninvested/unutilized, OR an inequality if we assume we can hold cash (which yields 0% if not in the bank). Since the bank is available for "any amount of surplus", it is always optimal to put surplus in the bank (4% > 0%). Therefore, we can assume **all** surplus is deposited in the bank. Thus, we can use **Equality** constraints for the cash flow if we define $y_t$ as the *entirety* of the residual cash.

Let's define the constraints based on the source of funds for each year:

**Year 2003 (Start):**
Funds available: $F$
Uses: Loan $D_1$, Bond A ($x_A$), Bond B ($x_B$), Bank Deposit ($y_1$).
$$ F = 100 + x_A + x_B + y_1 \quad (\text{Eq. 1}) $$
*(Assumption: Any surplus not allocated to A, B, or Bank would yield 0%, so it's optimal to allocate it to the bank. Thus, the equation holds.)*

**Year 2004 (Start):**
Funds available: Return from Bank Deposit $y_1$ ($1.04 y_1$).
Uses: Loan $D_2$, Bond C ($x_C$), Bank Deposit ($y_2$).
$$ 1.04 y_1 = 150 + x_C + y_2 \quad (\text{Eq. 2}) $$

**Year 2005 (Start):**
Funds available: Return from Bond B ($1.25 x_B$) + Return from Bank Deposit $y_2$ ($1.04 y_2$).
Uses: Loan $D_3$, Bank Deposit ($y_3$).
*(No new bonds can be bought in 2005 per problem description: A & B in 2003, C in 2004.)*
$$ 1.25 x_B + 1.04 y_2 = 120 + y_3 \quad (\text{Eq. 3}) $$

**Year 2006 (Start):**
Funds available: Return from Bond A ($1.40 x_A$) + Return from Bond C ($1.30 x_C$) + Return from Bank Deposit $y_3$ ($1.04 y_3$).
Uses: Loan $D_4$.
*(No need to invest further.)*
$$ 1.40 x_A + 1.30 x_C + 1.04 y_3 = 110 \quad (\text{Eq. 4}) $$

**2. Investment Limit Constraints:**
$$ x_A \leq 60 $$
$$ x_B \leq 90 $$
$$ x_C \leq 50 $$

**3. Non-negativity Constraints:**
$$ F \geq 0 $$
$$ x_A, x_B, x_C \geq 0 $$
$$ y_1, y_2, y_3 \geq 0 $$

## Summary of the Model

**Variables:**
- Continuous, non-negative: $F, x_A, x_B, x_C, y_1, y_2, y_3$.

**Objective:**
$$ \min F $$

**Subject to:**
1.  $F - x_A - x_B - y_1 = 100$
2.  $1.04 y_1 - x_C - y_2 = 150$
3.  $1.25 x_B + 1.04 y_2 - y_3 = 120$
4.  $1.40 x_A + 1.30 x_C + 1.04 y_3 = 110$
5.  $x_A \leq 60$
6.  $x_B \leq 90$
7.  $x_C \leq 50$
8.  $F, x_A, x_B, x_C, y_1, y_2, y_3 \geq 0$

**Verification of Logic:**
- **Eq 1**: Initial capital covers 2003 loan and initial investments.
- **Eq 2**: 2003 bank earnings cover 2004 loan and 2004 investments.
- **Eq 3**: 2003 Bond B maturity + 2004 bank earnings cover 2005 loan and 2005 bank deposit.
- **Eq 4**: 2003 Bond A maturity + 2004 Bond C maturity + 2005 bank earnings cover 2006 loan.
- The model ensures that funds are self-sustaining after the initial raise $F$ by leveraging the returns of previous periods' investments to cover future demands and investments.
