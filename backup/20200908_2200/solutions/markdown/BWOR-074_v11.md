To minimize the total sorting workload (total number of comparisons/operations) when sorting items into distinct categories by repeatedly selecting one category, we must determine the optimal sequence of grade extraction.

### 1. Problem Analysis
*   **Total Items ($N$):** 50,000.
*   **Categories:** 5 grades (A, B, C, D, E).
*   **Counts ($n_i$):**
    *   $n_A = 0.10 \times 50,000 = 5,000$
    *   $n_B = 0.25 \times 50,000 = 12,500$
    *   $n_C = 0.30 \times 50,000 = 15,000$
    *   $n_D = 0.20 \times 50,000 = 10,000$
    *   $n_E = 0.15 \times 50,000 = 7,500$
*   **Process:** We sort by identifying one grade at a time. Let the sorting order be a permutation $\pi = (g_1, g_2, g_3, g_4, g_5)$.
*   **Workload Cost:**
    *   To extract grade $g_1$, we scan the entire unsorted pool of size $N$. Cost: $N$.
    *   To extract grade $g_2$, we scan the remaining pool of size $N - n_{g_1}$. Cost: $N - n_{g_1}$.
    *   To extract grade $g_3$, we scan the remaining pool of size $N - n_{g_1} - n_{g_2}$. Cost: $N - n_{g_1} - n_{g_2}$.
    *   To extract grade $g_4$, we scan the remaining pool of size $N - n_{g_1} - n_{g_2} - n_{g_3}$. Cost: $N - n_{g_1} - n_{g_2} - n_{g_3}$.
    *   The last grade $g_5$ does not require a scan to identify (it is the remainder), or if it does, it is trivially defined by elimination. In standard "selection sort" or "filtering" models, the cost is the sum of the sizes of the pools scanned. The last group is identified by default. Thus, there are $K-1$ scanning steps for $K$ groups.

    Let $S_k$ be the set of grades extracted in the first $k$ steps. The workload is:
    $$ W = \sum_{k=1}^{K-1} \left( \sum_{j \notin S_k} n_j \right) $$
    
    Alternatively, we can analyze the contribution of each individual item (or group) to the total cost. An item belonging to group $g$ is scanned in step $1$, step $2$, ..., up to the step immediately before its group is extracted. If group $g$ is extracted at position $p$ in the permutation $\pi$ (where $p=1$ is first, $p=5$ is last), it is scanned $p-1$ times.
    
    Total Workload:
    $$ W = \sum_{i=1}^{K} n_{\pi(i)} \times (i - 1) $$
    
    To **minimize** $W$, we must assign the largest weights (largest $n_i$) to the smallest coefficients $(i-1)$. This is a classic rearrangement inequality problem. We should sort the groups by **descending** size. The largest group should be extracted first (coefficient 0), the second largest second (coefficient 1), etc. The smallest group should be extracted last (coefficient 4).

### 2. Mathematical Formulation

#### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Sets:**
*   $G = \{A, B, C, D, E\}$: Set of grades.
*   $P = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$: Set of positions in the sorting sequence.

**Parameters:**
*   $n_g$: Number of answer sheets for grade $g$.
    *   $n_A = 5,000$
    *   $n_B = 12,500$
    *   $n_C = 15,000$
    *   $n_D = 10,000$
    *   $n_E = 7,500$
*   $K = 5$: Total number of grades.

#### 2. Decision Variables

*   $x_{g, p}$: Binary variable, where:
    *   $x_{g, p} = 1$ if grade $g$ is assigned to position $p$ in the sorting sequence.
    *   $x_{g, p} = 0$ otherwise.
    *   $g \in G, p \in P$.

#### 3. Objective Function

Minimize the total sorting workload $Z$:

$$ \text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{g \in G} \sum_{p \in P} n_g \cdot (p - 1) \cdot x_{g, p} $$

*Note: The term $(p-1)$ represents the number of times the sheets of grade $g$ are scanned before they are removed from the pool. If a grade is at position 1, it is scanned 0 times (removed immediately). If at position 5, it is scanned 4 times.*

#### 4. Operational Constraints

1.  **Each grade is assigned to exactly one position:**
    $$ \sum_{p \in P} x_{g, p} = 1 \quad \forall g \in G $$

2.  **Each position is occupied by exactly one grade:**
    $$ \sum_{g \in G} x_{g, p} = 1 \quad \forall p \in P $$

3.  **Binary constraints:**
    $$ x_{g, p} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall g \in G, p \in P $$

### 3. Analytical Solution

According to the rearrangement inequality, to minimize the sum of products of two sequences, one should sort them in opposite orders.
*   Sequence 1 (Weights): $(p-1)$ for $p=1..5$ is $\{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}$ (Ascending).
*   Sequence 2 (Counts): $\{n_A, n_B, n_C, n_D, n_E\}$.

To minimize the dot product, we must pair the smallest weights with the largest counts.
Therefore, the counts must be sorted in **Descending** order.

**Ranking of Grades by Count (Descending):**
1.  $C: 15,000$
2.  $B: 12,500$
3.  $D: 10,000$
4.  $E: 7,500$
5.  $A: 5,000$

**Optimal Sorting Order:**
1.  **C** (Position 1, Coeff 0)
2.  **B** (Position 2, Coeff 1)
3.  **D** (Position 3, Coeff 2)
4.  **E** (Position 4, Coeff 3)
5.  **A** (Position 5, Coeff 4)

**Calculation of Minimum Workload:**
$$ Z_{min} = (15,000 \times 0) + (12,500 \times 1) + (10,000 \times 2) + (7,500 \times 3) + (5,000 \times 4) $$
$$ Z_{min} = 0 + 12,500 + 20,000 + 22,500 + 20,000 $$
$$ Z_{min} = 75,000 \text{ scans} $$

### Final Answer
The optimal order of sorting to minimize the total workload is **C, B, D, E, A**.
