To formulate the mathematical model for selecting the basketball starting lineup, we define the index sets, parameters, decision variables, objective function, and constraints based on the problem description.

### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

*   **Set of Players** $I = \{1, 2, \dots, 8\}$: The set of all available players.
*   **Set of Centers** $C \subseteq I = \{1, 2\}$: Players whose preferred role is Center.
*   **Set of Forwards** $F \subseteq I = \{3, 4, 5\}$: Players whose preferred role is Forward.
*   **Set of Guards** $G \subseteq I = \{6, 7, 8\}$: Players whose preferred role is Guard.
*   **Parameter** $h_i$: The height of player $i$ in meters.
    *   $h_1 = 1.92, h_2 = 1.90, h_3 = 1.88, h_4 = 1.86, h_5 = 1.85, h_6 = 1.83, h_7 = 1.80, h_8 = 1.78$.
*   **Parameter** $N = 5$: The required number of players in the starting lineup.

### 2. Decision Variables

Let $x_i$ be a binary decision variable for each player $i \in I$:
$$
x_i = \begin{cases} 
1 & \text{if player } i \text{ is selected for the starting lineup} \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$
Domain: $x_i \in \{0, 1\}, \forall i \in I$.

### 3. Objective Function

The goal is to maximize the total height of the selected players.

$$
\text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{i \in I} h_i x_i
$$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**1. Lineup Size Constraint:**
Exactly 5 players must be selected.
$$
\sum_{i \in I} x_i = 5
$$

**2. Position Constraints:**
*   **Exactly one center:**
    $$
    \sum_{i \in C} x_i = 1
    $$
    (i.e., $x_1 + x_2 = 1$)

*   **At least one forward:**
    $$
    \sum_{i \in F} x_i \ge 1
    $$
    (i.e., $x_3 + x_4 + x_5 \ge 1$)

**3. Logical Implication Constraints (Player 1, 4, and 6):**
The problem states: "If Player 1 or Player 4 is selected, then Player 6 must also be selected."
*   $x_1 = 1 \implies x_6 = 1$
*   $x_4 = 1 \implies x_6 = 1$

These can be modeled as:
$$
x_1 \le x_6
$$
$$
x_4 \le x_6
$$

The problem also states: "Conversely, if Player 6 is selected, then neither Player 1 nor Player 4 can be selected."
*   $x_6 = 1 \implies x_1 = 0$
*   $x_6 = 1 \implies x_4 = 0$

These can be modeled as:
$$
x_6 + x_1 \le 1
$$
$$
x_6 + x_4 \le 1
$$

*Note: Combining $x_1 \le x_6$ and $x_6 + x_1 \le 1$ implies that $x_1$ and $x_6$ cannot both be 1. If $x_1=1$, then $x_6=1$ (from first inequality), but then $1+1 \le 1$ is violated. Thus, $x_1$ must be 0. Similarly, $x_4$ must be 0. Effectively, these constraints force $x_1=0, x_4=0$, and leave $x_6$ free (0 or 1). Let's verify the logic carefully.*

*Logic Check:*
- If $x_1=1$, then $x_6=1$. But if $x_6=1$, then $x_1=0$. Contradiction. So $x_1$ must be 0.
- If $x_4=1$, then $x_6=1$. But if $x_6=1$, then $x_4=0$. Contradiction. So $x_4$ must be 0.
- Therefore, $x_1=0$ and $x_4=0$ are forced. The variable $x_6$ is not forced to be 0 or 1 by these specific implications alone, but is constrained by the other logic. Wait, "If Player 1 or 4 is selected, 6 is selected" AND "If 6 is selected, 1 and 4 are not selected" creates a biconditional-like structure where 1/4 and 6 are mutually exclusive, and 1/4 implies 6. This is a logical contradiction if 1 or 4 is selected. Thus, 1 and 4 cannot be selected. The constraints $x_1 \le x_6$ and $x_1 + x_6 \le 1$ correctly capture this.

**4. Mutual Exclusion Constraint (Player 2 and 8):**
"At least one of Player 2 or Player 8 must not be selected" means they cannot both be selected.
$$
x_2 + x_8 \le 1
$$

### Summary of the Model

**Variables:**
$x_i \in \{0, 1\}$ for $i=1,\dots,8$

**Objective:**
$$ \text{Maximize } Z = 1.92x_1 + 1.90x_2 + 1.88x_3 + 1.86x_4 + 1.85x_5 + 1.83x_6 + 1.80x_7 + 1.78x_8 $$

**Subject to:**
1.  $\sum_{i=1}^8 x_i = 5$
2.  $x_1 + x_2 = 1$
3.  $x_3 + x_4 + x_5 \ge 1$
4.  $x_1 \le x_6$
5.  $x_4 \le x_6$
6.  $x_1 + x_6 \le 1$
7.  $x_4 + x_6 \le 1$
8.  $x_2 + x_8 \le 1$
