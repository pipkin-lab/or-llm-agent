### 2. Mathematical Model

#### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Sets:**
*   $P = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of products.
*   $T = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of planning stages (periods).

**Parameters:**
*   $D_{p,t}$: Demand for product $p$ in stage $t$.
    *   $D_{1,1}=500, D_{1,2}=680, D_{1,3}=800$
    *   $D_{2,1}=750, D_{2,2}=800, D_{2,3}=950$
    *   $D_{3,1}=900, D_{3,2}=800, D_{3,3}=1000$
*   $a_{p}$: Equipment hours required per unit of product $p$.
    *   $a_1 = 2.0, a_2 = 1.0, a_3 = 3.1$
*   $b_{p}$: Working capital required per unit of product $p$.
    *   $b_1 = 40, b_2 = 20, b_3 = 55$
*   $H$: Equipment hours limit per stage = 5000.
*   $C$: Working capital limit per stage = 93,000.
*   $w_p$: Weight for stockout of product $p$.
    *   $w_1 = 1, w_2 = 1, w_3 = 2$.

#### 2. Decision Variables

**Production and Inventory Variables (Continuous, Non-negative):**
*   $x_{p,t}$: Quantity of product $p$ produced in stage $t$.
*   $I_{p,t}$: Inventory of product $p$ at the end of stage $t$.
    *   Note: $I_{p,0} = 0$ for all $p$.
    *   Constraint: $I_{p,3} = 0$ for all $p$ (Final inventory zero).

**Deviational Variables (Non-negative):**
*   $u_{p,t}$: Unmet demand (stockout) for product $p$ in stage $t$.
*   $v_t^+$: Amount of equipment hour overloading in stage $t$.
*   $c_t^+$: Amount of working capital overloading in stage $t$.
*   $c_t^-$: Amount of working capital underutilization in stage $t$ (usually not penalized, but included for balance equation if using equality form).

#### 3. Objective Function

This is a **Preemptive Goal Programming** problem. The objective is lexicographically minimized:

$$ \min Z = P_1 \left( \sum_{t=1}^{3} \sum_{p=1}^{3} w_p u_{p,t} \right) + P_2 \left( \sum_{t=1}^{3} v_t^+ \right) + P_3 \left( \sum_{t=1}^{3} c_t^+ \right) $$

Where $P_1 \gg P_2 \gg P_3$.

*   **Priority 1 Term**: Minimizes weighted stockouts.
    *   $\sum_{t=1}^{3} (1 \cdot u_{1,t} + 1 \cdot u_{2,t} + 2 \cdot u_{3,t})$
*   **Priority 2 Term**: Minimizes total equipment overloading across all stages.
    *   $\sum_{t=1}^{3} v_t^+$
*   **Priority 3 Term**: Minimizes total working capital overloading across all stages.
    *   $\sum_{t=1}^{3} c_t^+$

#### 4. Operational Constraints

**A. Inventory Balance Constraints**
For each product $p \in P$ and stage $t \in T$:
$$ I_{p,t-1} + x_{p,t} + u_{p,t} = D_{p,t} + I_{p,t} $$
With boundary conditions:
$$ I_{p,0} = 0 \quad \forall p \in P $$
$$ I_{p,3} = 0 \quad \forall p \in P $$
*(Note: $I_{p,t}$ represents ending inventory. The equation balances: Beginning Inv + Production + Shortage = Demand + Ending Inv. If production is high, $I_{p,t}$ is positive. If demand is not met, $u_{p,t}$ is positive.)*

**B. Equipment Capacity Constraints**
For each stage $t \in T$:
$$ \sum_{p=1}^{3} a_p x_{p,t} + v_t^- - v_t^+ = H $$
Since we only care about overloading ($v_t^+$) in the objective, and underutilization is not penalized in P2, we can write:
$$ \sum_{p=1}^{3} a_p x_{p,t} \le H + v_t^+ $$
Or in goal programming equality form:
$$ \sum_{p=1}^{3} a_p x_{p,t} + v_t^- - v_t^+ = 5000 $$
$$ v_t^-, v_t^+ \ge 0 $$
*(The objective minimizes $v_t^+$, so $v_t^-$ will naturally go to 0 if capacity is tight, or remain 0 if we define the constraint as $\le H + v_t^+$. Using the inequality form $\sum a_p x_{p,t} - v_t^+ \le 5000$ is also valid, but standard GP uses equality with deviations. Let's use the standard equality form for clarity.)*

**C. Working Capital Constraints**
For each stage $t \in T$:
$$ \sum_{p=1}^{3} b_p x_{p,t} + c_t^- - c_t^+ = 93000 $$
$$ c_t^-, c_t^+ \ge 0 $$
*(The objective minimizes $c_t^+$, representing the amount by which capital usage exceeds the limit.)*

**D. Non-negativity Constraints**
$$ x_{p,t} \ge 0 \quad \forall p \in P, t \in T $$
$$ I_{p,t} \ge 0 \quad \forall p \in P, t \in T $$
$$ u_{p,t} \ge 0, v_t^+ \ge 0, c_t^+ \ge 0 \quad \forall p \in P, t \in T $$

### Summary of the Model for Solving

To solve this, one would typically use a lexicographic approach:
1.  **Step 1 (Priority 1)**: Minimize Total Weighted Stockouts subject to all constraints (including Capital and Equipment as hard constraints? No, in GP, they are soft).
    *   Actually, the standard way to solve Preemptive GP is to solve sequentially:
    1.  Solve LP minimizing $Z_1 = \sum w_p u_{p,t}$ subject to all structural constraints. Let the optimal value be $Z_1^*$.
    2.  Solve LP minimizing $Z_2 = \sum v_t^+$ subject to all structural constraints AND the constraint $Z_1 = Z_1^*$. Let the optimal value be $Z_2^*$.
    3.  Solve LP minimizing $Z_3 = \sum c_t^+$ subject to all structural constraints AND $Z_1 = Z_1^*$ AND $Z_2 = Z_2^*$.

**Final Check on "Ensure Capital Does Not Exceed"**:
If the problem strictly means "Capital is a hard constraint", then $c_t^+$ would be forced to 0, and the Priority 3 objective would be trivial (always 0). Given it is listed as a priority, it is highly probable that the model intends for capital to be a *soft* constraint, allowing violations if necessary to satisfy higher priorities (like reducing stockouts), but penalizing them in the lowest priority tier. The formulation above reflects this standard Goal Programming interpretation.
