### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Sets:**
- $t \in \{1, \dots, 6\}$: Time periods (months), where $t=1$ is January, $t=6$ is June.

**Parameters:**
- $D_t$: Demand in month $t$ (units).
  - $D_1=3000, D_2=3600, D_3=4000, D_4=4600, D_5=4800, D_6=5000$.
- $p$: Selling price per unit ($240).
- $c_{mat}$: Material cost per unit ($150).
- $h_{hold}$: Inventory holding cost per unit per month ($10).
- $w_{skill}$: Wage for skilled workers per month ($2,000).
- $w_{train}$: Allowance for trainees per month ($800).
- $H_{worker}$: Hours available per skilled worker per month (160).
- $h_{unit}$: Skilled labor hours required per unit (4).
- $H_{train}$: Skilled labor hours required to train one new worker (50).
- $r$: Proportional attrition rate of skilled workers per month (0.02).
- $I_0$: Initial inventory (400 units).
- $I_{end,max}$: Maximum ending inventory in June (100 units).
- $W_0$: Initial number of skilled workers (80).

### 2. Decision Variables

- $W_t$: Number of skilled workers available for production in month $t$. (Continuous, $W_t \ge 0$)
- $N_t$: Number of new trainees recruited at the beginning of month $t$. (Continuous, $N_t \ge 0$)
- $P_t$: Production quantity in month $t$. (Continuous, $P_t \ge 0$)
- $I_t$: Ending inventory level at the end of month $t$. (Continuous, $I_t \ge 0$)
  - *Note: The problem implies meeting demand exactly or allowing backlogs? "satisfies the monthly... inventory requirements" and "storage cost if not delivered in month of production". Usually, in such aggregate planning problems with specific monthly demands listed, it is assumed that demand must be met in the current period or via inventory. Given the "storage cost" and no mention of backlogging penalties or lost sales, we assume all demand is met, either by current production or prior inventory. If inventory runs out, the problem would need a backlog variable. However, with 80 workers initially (80*160/4 = 3200 units capacity) and demand starting at 3000, inventory is likely to build up or stay positive. We will model standard inventory balance. If $I_t$ becomes negative, it implies backlogging. Given the high initial workforce relative to initial demand, and increasing demand, let's check feasibility.
    - Cap Jan: $80 \times 160 / 4 = 3200$. Demand 3000. Surplus 200 + Init 400 = 600.
    - It is highly probable $I_t \ge 0$ for all $t$. We will enforce $I_t \ge 0$.

### 3. Objective Function

**Maximize Total Profit (Revenue - Costs)**

$$
\text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{t=1}^{6} \left( p P_t - c_{mat} P_t - h_{hold} I_t - w_{skill} W_t - w_{train} N_t \right)
$$

*Clarification on Revenue vs Profit:* The prompt asks to "maximize the total revenue". However, in OR problems involving costs (labor, material, inventory), "maximize revenue" often loosely means "maximize profit" (net benefit) because producing more than sold incurs inventory costs, and under-producing might miss sales (though here we assume demand is met). If we strictly maximize Revenue ($\sum p P_t$), we would produce as much as possible (limited by labor capacity) regardless of cost, which is illogical. Standard interpretation: Maximize Net Profit.
*Note on Inventory Cost:* The problem states "storage cost of 10 dollars per unit per month". This applies to inventory carried over. Inventory $I_t$ is the ending inventory of month $t$, which is held during month $t+1$ (except for $I_6$, which might be discounted or just a constraint). Usually, holding cost is applied to the inventory held during the period. Let's apply holding cost to $I_t$ for $t=1 \dots 5$ (inventory held at end of $t$ is held into $t+1$). Does $I_6$ incur holding cost? Usually, end-of-horizon inventory is liquidated or not held. However, the constraint says "ending inventory in June not exceed 100". It doesn't explicitly say it's zero. If it's not sold, it's still an asset, but the holding cost is usually incurred *during* the month. Let's assume holding cost is incurred for inventory held *during* the month.
- Inventory at end of Jan ($I_1$) is held during Feb. Cost incurred in Feb? Or Jan?
- Standard convention: Holding cost is applied to the average inventory or ending inventory of the period. Let's use ending inventory $I_t$ for $t=1 \dots 5$. For $t=6$, since it's the end of the planning horizon, holding cost is typically not incurred (or the item is just sitting there). I will include holding cost for $I_1$ through $I_5$.
- *Alternative Interpretation*: "incur a storage cost... if not delivered in the month of production". This confirms cost is for units not sold in production month. So $I_1$ (units from Jan not sold in Jan) incurs cost. $I_5$ incurs cost. $I_6$? If they are in inventory at the end of June, they were not delivered in their production month (which could be Jan-Jun). So yes, $I_6$ also represents units that incurred storage. However, usually, the cost is per month of holding.
    - Units in $I_1$ were held for 1 month (Jan->Feb).
    - Units in $I_2$ were held for 2 months? No, $I_t$ is the *level*.
    - Correct formulation: Holding cost is $h_{hold} \times I_t$ for each month $t$ that the unit is in inventory.
    - $I_1$ is held during Feb.
    - $I_2$ is held during Mar.
    - ...
    - $I_5$ is held during Jun.
    - $I_6$ is held after Jun (outside horizon).
    - So, $\sum_{t=1}^{5} h_{hold} I_t$.

Let's refine the objective:
$$
Z = \sum_{t=1}^{6} (240 P_t - 150 P_t) - \sum_{t=1}^{5} 10 I_t - \sum_{t=1}^{6} 2000 W_t - \sum_{t=1}^{6} 800 N_t
$$
$$
Z = \sum_{t=1}^{6} 90 P_t - 10 \sum_{t=1}^{5} I_t - 2000 \sum_{t=1}^{6} W_t - 800 \sum_{t=1}^{6} N_t
$$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**1. Labor Balance (Workforce Dynamics)**
The number of skilled workers in month $t$, $W_t$, depends on the previous month's workers, attrition, and new graduates from training.
- Start of Jan: $W_1 = 80$ (Given).
- For $t > 1$:
  Workers available in $t$ = (Workers from $t-1$ who stayed) + (Trainees from $t-1$ who finished).
  - Workers from $t-1$: $(1 - r) W_{t-1} = 0.98 W_{t-1}$.
  - Trainees from $t-1$: $N_{t-1}$.
  - Therefore, $W_t = 0.98 W_{t-1} + N_{t-1}$ for $t=2, \dots, 6$.
  
  *Constraint Formulation:*
  $$ W_1 = 80 $$
  $$ W_t - 0.98 W_{t-1} - N_{t-1} = 0, \quad \forall t \in \{2, \dots, 6\} $$

**2. Production Capacity**
Production is limited by the available skilled labor hours.
- Total hours available in month $t$: $W_t \times H_{worker} = 160 W_t$.
- Hours consumed by training: $N_t \times H_{train} = 50 N_t$.
- Hours consumed by production: $P_t \times h_{unit} = 4 P_t$.
- Constraint: Production hours + Training hours $\le$ Total available hours.
  $$ 4 P_t + 50 N_t \le 160 W_t, \quad \forall t \in \{1, \dots, 6\} $$
  Simplifying:
  $$ 2 P_t + 25 N_t \le 80 W_t, \quad \forall t \in \{1, \dots, 6\} $$
  *(Divided by 2 for simplicity)*

**3. Inventory Balance**
Inventory at end of $t$ = Inventory at end of $t-1$ + Production in $t$ - Demand in $t$.
- $I_0 = 400$.
  $$ I_t = I_{t-1} + P_t - D_t, \quad \forall t \in \{1, \dots, 6\} $$
  Rearranged for solver:
  $$ I_t - I_{t-1} - P_t = -D_t, \quad \forall t \in \{1, \dots, 6\} $$
  (With $I_0$ as a constant parameter).

**4. Inventory Bounds**
- Non-negativity (assuming no backlogging allowed, or that optimal solution will naturally have $I_t \ge 0$ given initial stock and capacity):
  $$ I_t \ge 0, \quad \forall t \in \{1, \dots, 6\} $$
- End-of-June Constraint:
  $$ I_6 \le 100 $$

**5. Variable Bounds**
- $W_t \ge 0, \forall t$
- $N_t \ge 0, \forall t$
- $P_t \ge 0, \forall t$
- $I_t \ge 0, \forall t$
