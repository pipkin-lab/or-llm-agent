### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
*   $I = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of supply origins (Rows I, II, III).
*   $J = \{A, B, C, D, E\}$: Set of demand destinations (Columns A, B, C, D, E).

**Parameters:**
*   $S_i$: Supply capacity at origin $i \in I$.
    *   $S_1 = 50$
    *   $S_2 = 100$
    *   $S_3 = 150$
*   $D_j$: Demand requirement at destination $j \in J$.
    *   $D_A = 25$
    *   $D_B = 115$
    *   $D_C = 60$
    *   $D_D = 30$
    *   $D_E = 70$
*   $C_{ij}$: Unit transportation cost from origin $i$ to destination $j$.

| $C_{ij}$ | A | B | C | D | E |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **I ($i=1$)** | 10 | 15 | 20 | 20 | 40 |
| **II ($i=2$)** | 20 | 10 | 15 | 30 | 30 |
| **III ($i=3$)** | 30 | 35 | 40 | 55 | 25 |

**Balance Check:**
*   Total Supply: $50 + 100 + 150 = 300$
*   Total Demand: $25 + 115 + 60 + 30 + 70 = 300$
*   The problem is **balanced** ($\sum S_i = \sum D_j$).

### 2. Decision Variables

Let $x_{ij}$ be the quantity of goods shipped from origin $i$ to destination $j$.

*   **Domain:** $x_{ij} \ge 0$ for all $i \in I, j \in J$.
*   **Integrality:** Since the supplies and demands are integers and the problem is a standard transportation LP with a balanced structure, the optimal solution will naturally have integer values due to the total unimodularity of the transportation constraint matrix. Therefore, continuous variables are sufficient.

### 3. Objective Function

Minimize the total transportation cost:

$$ \text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{i \in I} \sum_{j \in J} C_{ij} x_{ij} $$

Expanded form:
$$ Z = 10x_{1A} + 15x_{1B} + 20x_{1C} + 20x_{1D} + 40x_{1E} $$
$$ + 20x_{2A} + 10x_{2B} + 15x_{2C} + 30x_{2D} + 30x_{2E} $$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**Supply Constraints:**
The total amount shipped from each origin cannot exceed its supply capacity. Since the problem is balanced, these can be modeled as equalities.

$$ \sum_{j \in J} x_{ij} = S_i, \quad \forall i \in I $$

1.  **Origin I:** $x_{1A} + x_{1B} + x_{1C} + x_{1D} + x_{1E} = 50$
2.  **Origin II:** $x_{2A} + x_{2B} + x_{2C} + x_{2D} + x_{2E} = 100$
3.  **Origin III:** $x_{3A} + x_{3B} + x_{3C} + x_{3D} + x_{3E} = 150$

**Demand Constraints:**
The total amount received at each destination must satisfy its demand.

$$ \sum_{i \in I} x_{ij} = D_j, \quad \forall j \in J $$

1.  **Dest A:** $x_{1A} + x_{2A} + x_{3A} = 25$
2.  **Dest B:** $x_{1B} + x_{2B} + x_{3B} = 115$
3.  **Dest C:** $x_{1C} + x_{2C} + x_{3C} = 60$
4.  **Dest D:** $x_{1D} + x_{2D} + x_{3D} = 30$
5.  **Dest E:** $x_{1E} + x_{2E} + x_{3E} = 70$

**Non-negativity Constraints:**
$$ x_{ij} \ge 0, \quad \forall i \in I, j \in J $$
