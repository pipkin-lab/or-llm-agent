# Formal Optimization Model: Two-School Location Problem

## 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Sets:**
- $V = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8\}$: Set of villages.
- $K = \{1, 2\}$: Set of schools to be built (indexed by $k$).

**Parameters:**
- $x_i$: x-coordinate of village $i$ (given in Table 5-14).
- $y_i$: y-coordinate of village $i$ (given in Table 5-14).
- $n_i$: Number of students in village $i$ (given in Table 5-14).
- $d_{ij} = \sqrt{(x_i - x_j)^2 + (y_i - y_j)^2}$: Euclidean distance between village $i$ and village $j$.
- $M$: A sufficiently large constant (e.g., $M = 100$) for Big-M linearization.

**Pre-computed Data (Example):**
- $d_{1,2} = \sqrt{10^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{109} \approx 10.44$
- $d_{1,3} = \sqrt{12^2 + 15^2} = \sqrt{369} \approx 19.21$
- ... (all pairwise distances can be pre-computed).

## 2. Decision Variables

**Binary Variables:**
- $y_{ik} \in \{0, 1\}$ for all $i \in V, k \in K$:
  - $y_{ik} = 1$ if school $k$ is built in village $i$, 0 otherwise.
- $z_{ik} \in \{0, 1\}$ for all $i \in V, k \in K$:
  - $z_{ik} = 1$ if all students from village $i$ are assigned to school $k$, 0 otherwise.

*Note: Since the problem asks for the location of two schools and implicitly assumes students walk to the nearest school (or are assigned to a school), we model this as a capacitated facility location problem where each village is assigned to exactly one school, and each school is located in exactly one village.*

## 3. Objective Function

**Minimize** the total weighted walking distance:

$$
\min \sum_{i \in V} \sum_{j \in V} \sum_{k \in K} n_i \cdot d_{ij} \cdot z_{ij} \cdot y_{jk}
$$

*Linearized Objective Function:*
Since the product of three binary variables is non-linear, we introduce an auxiliary variable $w_{ijk}$ or simplify by noting that if school $k$ is at $j$ and village $i$ is assigned to $k$, the cost is $n_i d_{ij}$. A standard linear formulation uses the assignment directly linked to location:

Let's refine the variables to ensure linearity.
- $y_{jk} \in \{0,1\}$: School $k$ is at village $j$.
- $z_{ik} \in \{0,1\}$: Village $i$ is assigned to school $k$.

The cost for village $i$ assigned to school $k$ is $\sum_{j \in V} d_{ij} y_{jk}$.
Total Cost = $\sum_{i \in V} \sum_{k \in K} n_i \left( \sum_{j \in V} d_{ij} y_{jk} \right) z_{ik}$.

This is still non-linear ($y \cdot z$). To linearize, we can use the fact that for a fixed assignment $z_{ik}=1$, the cost is determined by the location $y_{jk}=1$.
Standard Linearization: Introduce variable $u_{ijk} \in \{0,1\}$ such that $u_{ijk} = 1$ iff $z_{ik}=1$ and $y_{jk}=1$.
However, a simpler approach for small $N=8$ is often used in textbooks: enumerate all pairs of schools? No, we need a general model.

Let's use the standard Big-M linearization for the product $y_{jk} z_{ik}$.
Actually, since each village is assigned to exactly one school, and each school is at exactly one village, we can simplify.
If we assume students always go to the closest school, the model is different. But the problem says "minimize total walking distance", implying an assignment decision.

Let's use the following linear structure:
Objective: $\min \sum_{i \in V} \sum_{j \in V} \sum_{k \in K} n_i d_{ij} u_{ijk}$

Where $u_{ijk}$ is a binary variable indicating that village $i$ is assigned to school $k$ located at village $j$.

## 4. Operational Constraints

**1. Each school is built in exactly one village:**
$$
\sum_{i \in V} y_{ik} = 1 \quad \forall k \in K
$$

**2. The two schools must be in different villages (optional, but usually implied if "one in each of two villages" means distinct locations. If same village is allowed, this constraint is omitted. Usually, building two schools in the same village is suboptimal or not intended. Let's assume distinct):**
$$
y_{ik} + y_{ik'} \le 1 \quad \forall i \in V, \forall k, k' \in K, k \neq k'
$$
*Alternatively, simpler:* The assignment logic below will naturally handle this if costs are positive, but explicit constraint ensures distinct sites.

**3. Each village is assigned to exactly one school:**
$$
\sum_{k \in K} z_{ik} = 1 \quad \forall i \in V
$$

**4. Linking Assignment to Location (Linearization):**
We define $u_{ijk}$ such that $u_{ijk} = 1$ if and only if village $i$ is assigned to school $k$ AND school $k$ is located at village $j$.
$$
u_{ijk} \le z_{ik} \quad \forall i, j, k
$$
$$
u_{ijk} \le y_{jk} \quad \forall i, j, k
$$
$$
u_{ijk} \ge z_{ik} + y_{jk} - 1 \quad \forall i, j, k
$$
$$
u_{ijk} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall i, j, k
$$

**5. Consistency of Assignment:**
The sum of $u_{ijk}$ over all locations $j$ for a fixed $i, k$ must equal $z_{ik}$:
$$
\sum_{j \in V} u_{ijk} = z_{ik} \quad \forall i \in V, k \in K
$$

**6. Consistency of Location:**
The sum of $u_{ijk}$ over all villages $i$ for a fixed $j, k$ is not directly constrained to 1, but follows from the above.

**Simplified Objective using $u_{ijk}$:**
$$
\min \sum_{i \in V} \sum_{j \in V} \sum_{k \in K} n_i d_{ij} u_{ijk}
$$

**Variables Summary:**
- $y_{jk} \in \{0,1\}$: School $k$ at village $j$.
- $z_{ik} \in \{0,1\}$: Village $i$ assigned to school $k$.
- $u_{ijk} \in \{0,1\}$: Village $i$ assigned to school $k$ located at village $j$.

**Constraints Summary:**
1. $\sum_{j} y_{jk} = 1, \forall k$
2. $\sum_{k} z_{ik} = 1, \forall i$
3. $u_{ijk} \le z_{ik}, \forall i,j,k$
4. $u_{ijk} \le y_{jk}, \forall i,j,k$
5. $u_{ijk} \ge z_{ik} + y_{jk} - 1, \forall i,j,k$
6. $\sum_{j} u_{ijk} = z_{ik}, \forall i,k$
7. $y_{ik} + y_{ik'} \le 1, \forall i, k \neq k'$ (Ensure distinct villages)

**Note on Integer vs Continuous:**
Since $N=8$ is small, this is a standard MILP. The variables are binary.
