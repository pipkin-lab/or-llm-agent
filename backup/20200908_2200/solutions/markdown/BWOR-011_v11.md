### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
*   $S = \{I, II\}$: Set of power stations. Let $s=1$ represent Station I and $s=2$ represent Station II.
*   $C = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of cities.

**Parameters:**
*   $Cap_s$: Maximum supply capacity of station $s$ (units).
    *   $Cap_1 = 400$
    *   $Cap_2 = 450$
*   $D_{min, c}$: Minimum required supply for city $c$ (units).
    *   $D_{min, 1} = 290$ (320 - 30 reduction allowed)
    *   $D_{min, 2} = 250$ (Fixed)
    *   $D_{min, 3} = 270$
*   $D_{max, c}$: Maximum required supply for city $c$ (units).
    *   $D_{max, 1} = 320$
    *   $D_{max, 2} = 250$
    *   $D_{max, 3} = 350$
*   $C_{s,c}$: Unit cost of supplying electricity from station $s$ to city $c$ (Yuan/unit).
    *   $C_{1,1} = 15, C_{1,2} = 18, C_{1,3} = 22$
    *   $C_{2,1} = 21, C_{2,2} = 25, C_{2,3} = 16$

**Derived Constants:**
*   Total Supply Capacity: $TotalCap = Cap_1 + Cap_2 = 400 + 450 = 850$.
*   Total Minimum Demand: $TotalD_{min} = 290 + 250 + 270 = 810$.
*   Total Maximum Demand: $TotalD_{max} = 320 + 250 + 350 = 920$.
*   Note: Since $TotalCap (850)$ is between $TotalD_{min} (810)$ and $TotalD_{max} (920)$, a feasible solution exists where all supply is used and minimum demands are met.

### 2. Decision Variables

*   $x_{s,c}$: Amount of electricity supplied from station $s$ to city $c$ (units).
    *   **Domain**: $x_{s,c} \ge 0$ for all $s \in S, c \in C$.
    *   **Integrality**: Continuous. Electricity is treated as a divisible resource in aggregate planning problems of this scale (hundreds/thousands of units). Standard LP relaxation is appropriate.

### 3. Objective Function

Minimize the total cost of electricity supply.

$$
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{s \in S} \sum_{c \in C} C_{s,c} x_{s,c}
$$

Explicitly:
$$
Z = 15 x_{1,1} + 18 x_{1,2} + 22 x_{1,3} + 21 x_{2,1} + 25 x_{2,2} + 16 x_{2,3}
$$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**1. Supply Capacity Constraints (Use up all available electricity):**
The problem states the plan must "use up all available electricity from both stations". This implies the sum of supplies from each station must equal its maximum capacity.

$$
\sum_{c \in C} x_{1,c} = 400
$$
$$
\sum_{c \in C} x_{2,c} = 450
$$

**2. City Demand Constraints:**
The supply to each city must meet the specified minimum and maximum bounds.

*   **City 1**: Supply must be between 290 and 320.
    $$
    290 \le \sum_{s \in S} x_{s,1} \le 320
    $$
    *Expanded:*
    $$
    x_{1,1} + x_{2,1} \ge 290
    $$
    $$
    x_{1,1} + x_{2,1} \le 320
    $$

*   **City 2**: Supply must be fixed at 250.
    $$
    \sum_{s \in S} x_{s,2} = 250
    $$
    *Expanded:*
    $$
    x_{1,2} + x_{2,2} = 250
    $$

*   **City 3**: Supply must be between 270 and 350.
    $$
    270 \le \sum_{s \in S} x_{s,3} \le 350
    $$
    *Expanded:*
    $$
    x_{1,3} + x_{2,3} \ge 270
    $$
    $$
    x_{1,3} + x_{2,3} \le 350
    $$

**3. Non-negativity Constraints:**
$$
x_{s,c} \ge 0 \quad \forall s \in S, c \in C
$$
