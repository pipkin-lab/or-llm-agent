## 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
*   $P = \{A, B, C, D\}$: Set of chemical plants.
*   $R = \{I, II, III, IV, V, VI\}$: Set of sales regions.

**Parameters:**
*   $S_p$: Supply capacity of plant $p$ (tons).
    *   $S_A = 200, S_B = 300, S_C = 400, S_D = 100$.
*   $D_r$: Demand of region $r$ (tons).
    *   $D_I = 200, D_{II} = 150, D_{III} = 400, D_{IV} = 100, D_V = 150, D_{VI} = 150$.
*   $c_p$: Unit production cost at plant $p$ (yuan/kg).
    *   $c_A = 1.2, c_B = 1.4, c_C = 1.1, c_D = 1.5$.
*   $s_r$: Unit sales price in region $r$ (yuan/kg).
    *   $s_I = 2.0, s_{II} = 2.4, s_{III} = 1.8, s_{IV} = 2.2, s_V = 1.6, s_{VI} = 2.0$.
*   $t_{p,r}$: Unit transportation cost from plant $p$ to region $r$ (yuan/kg).
    *   *Note on Units*: Production/Sales/Transport costs are per kg, while Supply/Demand are in tons. To maintain consistency, we convert Supply/Demand to kg (multiply by 1000) OR convert costs to per ton (multiply by 1000). Let's work in **kg** for flow variables to match cost units directly, or simply keep tons and multiply the objective coefficients by 1000.
    *   *Decision*: Let variables be in **tons**. The net revenue per ton is $(s_r - c_p - t_{p,r}) \times 1000$ Yuan/ton.
    *   Alternatively, define Net Profit Coefficient $\pi_{p,r} = (s_r - c_p - t_{p,r})$. The objective will be maximized in terms of Yuan, so we multiply the sum of $\pi_{p,r} \cdot x_{p,r}$ by 1000.

    **Transportation Cost Matrix ($t_{p,r}$):**
    | Plant \ Region | I | II | III | IV | V | VI |
    | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
    | **A** | 0.5 | 0.4 | 0.3 | 0.4 | 0.3 | 0.1 |
    | **B** | 0.3 | 0.8 | 0.9 | 0.5 | 0.6 | 0.2 |
    | **C** | 0.7 | 0.7 | 0.3 | 0.7 | 0.4 | 0.4 |
    | **D** | 0.6 | 0.4 | 0.2 | 0.6 | 0.5 | 0.8 |

**Derived Parameters (Net Profit per kg):**
$\pi_{p,r} = s_r - c_p - t_{p,r}$

*   **Plant A ($c_A=1.2$):**
    *   I: $2.0 - 1.2 - 0.5 = 0.3$
    *   II: $2.4 - 1.2 - 0.4 = 0.8$
    *   III: $1.8 - 1.2 - 0.3 = 0.3$
    *   IV: $2.2 - 1.2 - 0.4 = 0.6$
    *   V: $1.6 - 1.2 - 0.3 = 0.1$
    *   VI: $2.0 - 1.2 - 0.1 = 0.7$
*   **Plant B ($c_B=1.4$):**
    *   I: $2.0 - 1.4 - 0.3 = 0.3$
    *   II: $2.4 - 1.4 - 0.8 = 0.2$
    *   III: $1.8 - 1.4 - 0.9 = -0.5$ (Loss)
    *   IV: $2.2 - 1.4 - 0.5 = 0.3$
    *   V: $1.6 - 1.4 - 0.6 = -0.4$ (Loss)
    *   VI: $2.0 - 1.4 - 0.2 = 0.4$
*   **Plant C ($c_C=1.1$):**
    *   I: $2.0 - 1.1 - 0.7 = 0.2$
    *   II: $2.4 - 1.1 - 0.7 = 0.6$
    *   III: $1.8 - 1.1 - 0.3 = 0.4$
    *   IV: $2.2 - 1.1 - 0.7 = 0.4$
    *   V: $1.6 - 1.1 - 0.4 = 0.1$
    *   VI: $2.0 - 1.1 - 0.4 = 0.5$
*   **Plant D ($c_D=1.5$):**
    *   I: $2.0 - 1.5 - 0.6 = -0.1$ (Loss)
    *   II: $2.4 - 1.5 - 0.4 = 0.5$
    *   III: $1.8 - 1.5 - 0.2 = 0.1$
    *   IV: $2.2 - 1.5 - 0.6 = 0.1$
    *   V: $1.6 - 1.5 - 0.5 = -0.4$ (Loss)
    *   VI: $2.0 - 1.5 - 0.8 = -0.3$ (Loss)

**Total Supply:** $200 + 300 + 400 + 100 = 1000$ tons.
**Total Demand:** $200 + 150 + 400 + 100 + 150 + 150 = 1150$ tons.
Since Total Supply (1000) < Total Demand (1150), not all demand can be met. We must choose which regions to supply to maximize profit.

## 2. Decision Variables

Let $x_{p,r}$ be the amount of product (in tons) shipped from plant $p \in P$ to region $r \in R$.

**Domain:**
$x_{p,r} \ge 0, \quad \forall p \in P, r \in R$

*(Note: Since the scale is large (hundreds of tons) and the problem does not explicitly require integer units, continuous variables are appropriate for this LP formulation.)*

## 3. Objective Function

Maximize total profit. Profit per ton for route $(p,r)$ is $1000 \times \pi_{p,r}$ Yuan.
To simplify, we can maximize the sum of $\pi_{p,r} \cdot x_{p,r}$ and multiply the final result by 1000, or include the 1000 in the coefficients. Let's define the objective in **Yuan**.

$$
\text{Maximize } Z = 1000 \sum_{p \in P} \sum_{r \in R} \pi_{p,r} x_{p,r}
$$

Expanding with calculated $\pi_{p,r}$ values (Yuan/kg * 1000 kg/ton = Yuan/ton):

$$
\begin{aligned}
Z = 1000 \Big( & 0.3 x_{A,I} + 0.8 x_{A,II} + 0.3 x_{A,III} + 0.6 x_{A,IV} + 0.1 x_{A,V} + 0.7 x_{A,VI} \\
& + 0.3 x_{B,I} + 0.2 x_{B,II} - 0.5 x_{B,III} + 0.3 x_{B,IV} - 0.4 x_{B,V} + 0.4 x_{B,VI} \\
& + 0.2 x_{C,I} + 0.6 x_{C,II} + 0.4 x_{C,III} + 0.4 x_{C,IV} + 0.1 x_{C,V} + 0.5 x_{C,VI} \\
& - 0.1 x_{D,I} + 0.5 x_{D,II} + 0.1 x_{D,III} + 0.1 x_{D,IV} - 0.4 x_{D,V} - 0.3 x_{D,VI} \Big)
\end{aligned}
$$

*Note: Terms with negative profit (losses) will naturally be driven to 0 by the maximization solver unless required by constraints.*

## 4. Operational Constraints

**1. Plant Supply Capacity Constraints:**
The total shipment from each plant cannot exceed its production capacity.
$$
\sum_{r \in R} x_{p,r} \le S_p, \quad \forall p \in P
$$
Specifically:
*   $\sum_{r} x_{A,r} \le 200$
*   $\sum_{r} x_{B,r} \le 300$
*   $\sum_{r} x_{C,r} \le 400$
*   $\sum_{r} x_{D,r} \le 100$

**2. Region Demand Constraints:**
Since Supply < Demand, we cannot satisfy all demand. We ship *up to* the demand. However, specific constraints apply to Regions III and IV.

*   **General Regions (I, II, V, VI):** Shipment cannot exceed demand.
    $$
    \sum_{p \in P} x_{p,r} \le D_r, \quad \forall r \in \{I, II, V, VI\}
    $$
    *   $\sum_{p} x_{p,I} \le 200$
    *   $\sum_{p} x_{p,II} \le 150$
    *   $\sum_{p} x_{p,V} \le 150$
    *   $\sum_{p} x_{p,VI} \le 150$

*   **Region III Constraint:**
    The problem states "at least 100 tons must be supplied to Region III". It does *not* state that full demand (400 tons) must be met, only the minimum.
    $$
    100 \le \sum_{p \in P} x_{p,III} \le D_{III}
    $$
    $$
    100 \le \sum_{p} x_{p,III} \le 400
    $$
    *(Note: If the solver finds it profitable to ship more than 100, it will, up to the demand limit. If it's not profitable, it will stick to 100.)*

*   **Region IV Constraint:**
    The problem states "the demand in Region IV must be fully satisfied".
    $$
    \sum_{p \in P} x_{p,IV} = D_{IV}
    $$
    $$
    \sum_{p} x_{p,IV} = 100
    $$

**3. Non-negativity:**
$$
x_{p,r} \ge 0, \quad \forall p \in P, r \in R
$$

### Summary of Model Structure for Solver Implementation

*   **Variables:** 24 continuous variables $x_{p,r}$.
*   **Objective:** Maximize linear combination of $x_{p,r}$ with coefficients $1000 \cdot \pi_{p,r}$.
*   **Constraints:**
    *   4 Supply inequalities ($\le$).
    *   4 Demand inequalities for regions I, II, V, VI ($\le$).
    *   1 Two-sided bound for region III ($100 \le \dots \le 400$).
    *   1 Equality for region IV ($= 100$).
    *   24 Non-negativity bounds.
