# Operations Research Model: Production and Inventory Planning

## 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
*   $T = \{1, 2, \dots, 12\}$: Set of months (1=Jan, ..., 12=Dec).

**Parameters:**

**Demand ($D_{i,t}$ in units/month):**
*   Product I ($i=1$):
    *   $D_{1,t} = 10,000$ for $t \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$
    *   $D_{1,t} = 30,000$ for $t \in \{5, 6, 7, 8, 9\}$
    *   $D_{1,t} = 100,000$ for $t \in \{10, 11, 12\}$
*   Product II ($i=2$):
    *   $D_{2,t} = 15,000$ for $t \in \{3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9\}$
    *   $D_{2,t} = 50,000$ for $t \in \{1, 2, 10, 11, 12\}$

**Production Costs ($c_{i,t}$ in yuan/unit):**
*   Product I:
    *   $c_{1,t} = 5$ for $t \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$
    *   $c_{1,t} = 4.5$ for $t \in \{6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12\}$
*   Product II:
    *   $c_{2,t} = 8$ for $t \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$
    *   $c_{2,t} = 7$ for $t \in \{6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12\}$

**Capacity & Storage Parameters:**
*   $C_t = 120,000$: Total monthly production capacity (units) for all $t \in T$.
*   $v_1 = 0.2$: Volume per unit of Product I ($m^3$/unit).
*   $v_2 = 0.4$: Volume per unit of Product II ($m^3$/unit).
*   $V_{wh} = 15,000$: In-house warehouse volume capacity ($m^3$).
*   $h_{in} = 1$: In-house storage cost (yuan/$m^3$/month).
*   $h_{out} = 1.5$: External storage cost (yuan/$m^3$/month).
*   $I_{i,0} = 0$: Initial inventory for both products.

**Derived Parameters (Unit Storage Costs in yuan/unit/month):**
*   $h_{1,in} = h_{in} \times v_1 = 1 \times 0.2 = 0.2$ yuan/unit/month.
*   $h_{2,in} = h_{in} \times v_2 = 1 \times 0.4 = 0.4$ yuan/unit/month.
*   $h_{1,out} = h_{out} \times v_1 = 1.5 \times 0.2 = 0.3$ yuan/unit/month.
*   $h_{2,out} = h_{out} \times v_2 = 1.5 \times 0.4 = 0.6$ yuan/unit/month.

## 2. Decision Variables

Let $x_{i,t}$ be the production quantity of product $i$ in month $t$ (continuous, $\ge 0$).
Let $I_{i,t}$ be the ending inventory volume of product $i$ in month $t$ (continuous, $\ge 0$).
Let $E_{i,t}$ be the ending external rented inventory volume of product $i$ in month $t$ (continuous, $\ge 0$).

*Note: We model inventory in **units** for the balance equations, but convert to volume for capacity constraints and costs. Alternatively, we can model in volume directly. Given the costs are provided per $m^3$, modeling in units is more intuitive for production/demand, and we convert in constraints/objective.*

Let's define variables in **units**:
*   $x_{i,t} \in \mathbb{R}^+$: Production of product $i$ in month $t$.
*   $I_{i,t} \in \mathbb{R}^+$: Internal inventory of product $i$ at end of month $t$.
*   $E_{i,t} \in \mathbb{R}^+$: External inventory of product $i$ at end of month $t$.

## 3. Objective Function

Minimize total cost (Production + Internal Storage + External Storage).

$$
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{t \in T} \sum_{i \in \{1,2\}} c_{i,t} x_{i,t} + \sum_{t \in T} \sum_{i \in \{1,2\}} (h_{i,in} I_{i,t} + h_{i,out} E_{i,t})
$$

Substituting values:
$$
Z = \sum_{t=1}^{12} [5 x_{1,t} + 8 x_{2,t}]_{t \le 5} + \sum_{t=6}^{12} [4.5 x_{1,t} + 7 x_{2,t}] + \sum_{t=1}^{12} \sum_{i=1}^{2} (h_{i,in} I_{i,t} + h_{i,out} E_{i,t})
$$
*(Note: The production cost coefficients change based on $t$. It is cleaner to write the sum as $\sum_{t=1}^{12} \sum_{i=1}^{2} c_{i,t} x_{i,t}$ where $c_{i,t}$ is defined by the parameters above.)*

## 4. Operational Constraints

### A. Inventory Balance Constraints
For each product $i \in \{1, 2\}$ and month $t \in T$:
$$
I_{i,t} + E_{i,t} = I_{i,t-1} + E_{i,t-1} + x_{i,t} - D_{i,t}
$$
With initial conditions $I_{i,0} = 0$ and $E_{i,0} = 0$.
*This ensures that total available inventory (internal + external) plus production meets demand, with the remainder carried to the next month split between internal and external storage.*

### B. In-House Warehouse Capacity Constraint
The total volume of internal inventory at the end of each month cannot exceed the in-house warehouse capacity.
$$
0.2 I_{1,t} + 0.4 I_{2,t} \le 15,000 \quad \forall t \in T
$$

### C. Production Capacity Constraint
The total production volume (in units) for both products in any month cannot exceed the factory capacity.
$$
x_{1,t} + x_{2,t} \le 120,000 \quad \forall t \in T
$$

### D. Non-negativity Constraints
$$
x_{i,t} \ge 0, \quad I_{i,t} \ge 0, \quad E_{i,t} \ge 0 \quad \forall i \in \{1,2\}, t \in T
$$

### Part (a) Analysis: Infeasibility of the Original Problem

The original problem (without external storage) implies $E_{i,t} = 0$ for all $i, t$. Thus, all inventory must be stored in-house: $I_{i,t} \ge 0$ and $0.2 I_{1,t} + 0.4 I_{2,t} \le 15,000$.

To show infeasibility, we examine the peak demand periods versus capacity and storage limits.

1.  **Demand vs. Capacity Check:**
    *   **Oct-Dec ($t=10, 11, 12$):**
        *   $D_{1,t} = 100,000$
        *   $D_{2,t} = 50,000$
        *   Total Demand = $150,000$ units/month.
        *   Capacity = $120,000$ units/month.
        *   Shortfall = $30,000$ units/month.
    *   To meet demand in Oct, we must produce in prior months and store.
    *   **Storage Volume Check for Shortfall:**
        *   The shortfall in Oct must be stored from previous months.
        *   Let's look at the cumulative demand vs cumulative capacity.
        *   However, a simpler check is the **maximum possible storage**.
        *   Max internal volume = $15,000 m^3$.
        *   Max units of Product I that can be stored = $15,000 / 0.2 = 75,000$ units.
        *   Max units of Product II that can be stored = $15,000 / 0.4 = 37,500$ units.
        *   If we store a mix, the max total units is bounded by the volume.
        *   Consider the period **Oct**. Demand is 150k. Capacity is 120k. We need 30k units in inventory at the start of Oct (or built up before).
        *   Can we store 30,000 units before Oct?
            *   If all Product I: $30,000 \times 0.2 = 6,000 m^3$. This fits in 15,000 $m^3$.
            *   If all Product II: $30,000 \times 0.4 = 12,000 m^3$. This fits in 15,000 $m^3$.
            *   So, storing the *monthly* shortfall seems physically possible in terms of volume alone.
    
    *   **Let's check the cumulative constraint more carefully.**
        *   Demand in May ($t=5$): $D_{1,5}=30,000, D_{2,5}=15,000$. Total = 45,000.
        *   Demand in Jun ($t=6$): $D_{1,6}=30,000, D_{2,6}=15,000$. Total = 45,000.
        *   Demand in Jul ($t=7$): 45,000.
        *   Demand in Aug ($t=8$): 45,000.
        *   Demand in Sep ($t=9$): 45,000.
        *   Demand in Oct ($t=10$): 150,000.
        
        *   Let's look at **January**.
            *   $D_{1,1}=10,000, D_{2,1}=50,000$. Total = 60,000.
            *   Capacity = 120,000.
            *   Surplus capacity = 60,000 units.
            *   Can we store this surplus?
            *   Max storage volume = 15,000 $m^3$.
            *   If we produce 60,000 extra units in Jan, what is the minimum volume?
            *   Even if all are Product I (smallest volume), Volume = $60,000 \times 0.2 = 12,000 m^3$. This fits.
            
    *   **Let's look at the critical constraint: Can we meet the Oct-Dec demand given the storage limit?**
        *   Total Demand Oct-Dec = $150,000 \times 3 = 450,000$ units.
        *   Total Capacity Oct-Dec = $120,000 \times 3 = 360,000$ units.
        *   Total Shortfall Oct-Dec = $90,000$ units.
        *   These 90,000 units must be produced in months 1-9 and stored until Oct, Nov, or Dec.
        *   Specifically, the inventory at the end of September ($I_{1,9} + I_{2,9}$) must be sufficient to cover the cumulative shortfall if production in Oct-Dec is at max capacity.
        *   Actually, we can produce *more* than capacity in earlier months? No, capacity is a hard limit per month.
        *   Let's check if we can build up 90,000 units of inventory by the end of September.
        *   Max Inventory Volume = $15,000 m^3$.
        *   To minimize volume for 90,000 units, we should store as much Product I as possible (0.2 $m^3$/unit).
        *   If all 90,000 units were Product I, Volume = $90,000 \times 0.2 = 18,000 m^3$.
        *   $18,000 m^3 > 15,000 m^3$ (Max Warehouse Capacity).
        *   Therefore, it is **impossible** to store the required 90,000 units of inventory needed to cover the Oct-Dec shortfall, even if we produced and stored only the most volume-efficient product (Product I).
        *   Since Product II has a larger volume (0.4 $m^3$/unit), any mix involving Product II would require even more volume.
        
    **Conclusion for (a):**
    The cumulative demand for Oct-Dec exceeds the cumulative production capacity for Oct-Dec by 90,000 units. These units must be produced in months 1-9 and stored. The minimum required storage volume for 90,000 units (assuming all are Product I) is $90,000 \times 0.2 = 18,000 m^3$. This exceeds the in-house warehouse capacity of $15,000 m^3$. Thus, no feasible solution exists without external storage.

### Part (b) Model Formulation

The model defined in sections 1-4 above is the required LP for part (b). It allows external storage ($E_{i,t}$) which has no capacity limit (other than non-negativity and cost), thereby removing the infeasibility caused by the warehouse volume constraint. The optimization will decide how much to store in-house (cheaper, limited) vs. externally (expensive, unlimited) to minimize total cost.
