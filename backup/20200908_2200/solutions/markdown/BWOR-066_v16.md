# Mathematical Optimization Model: Warehouse Purchase & Sales Planning

## 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
- $T = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$: Set of months (January to April).

**Parameters:**
- $P_{buy, k}$: Buying price in month $k$ (from Table 7-6).
  - $P_{buy, 1} = 10$
  - $P_{buy, 2} = 9$
  - $P_{buy, 3} = 11$
  - $P_{buy, 4} = 15$
- $P_{sell, k}$: Selling price in month $k$ (from Table 7-6).
  - $P_{sell, 1} = 12$
  - $P_{sell, 2} = 9$
  - $P_{sell, 3} = 13$
  - $P_{sell, 4} = 17$
- $C_{max}$: Maximum warehouse capacity = 1,000 units.
- $I_{0}$: Initial inventory at the start of Month 1 = 500 units.
- $L$: A sufficiently large number for Big-M constraints (e.g., $L = C_{max}$ or higher depending on variable bounds).

**Derived Parameters (Profit Margins):**
- $m_k = P_{sell, k} - P_{buy, k}$: Potential profit margin if bought and sold in the same month (not directly used due to lag, but useful for intuition).
  - Note: Since goods bought in month $k$ are available in $k+1$, the effective profit for a unit bought in month $i$ and sold in month $j$ ($j > i$) is $P_{sell, j} - P_{buy, i}$.

## 2. Decision Variables

Given the problem states "product quantities are in integers", we use integer variables. However, since the scale is small (capacity 1000), integer constraints are appropriate.

- $x_k$: Number of units **purchased** in month $k$. ($x_k \ge 0$, integer)
  - *Note:* Purchases in month $k$ are available for sale starting month $k+1$.
- $s_k$: Number of units **sold** in month $k$. ($s_k \ge 0$, integer)
- $I_k$: Inventory level at the **end** of month $k$. ($I_k \ge 0$, integer)

## 3. Objective Function

Maximize total profit (Total Revenue - Total Cost).

$$
\text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{k=1}^{4} (P_{sell, k} \cdot s_k) - \sum_{k=1}^{4} (P_{buy, k} \cdot x_k)
$$

Substituting values:
$$
Z = 12 s_1 + 9 s_2 + 13 s_3 + 17 s_4 - (10 x_1 + 9 x_2 + 11 x_3 + 15 x_4)
$$

## 4. Operational Constraints

### A. Inventory Balance Constraints
The inventory at the end of month $k$ equals the inventory at the beginning of month $k$ plus purchases made in month $k$ (which are available from the *next* month, so they do NOT add to current month's sellable stock) minus sales in month $k$.

*Clarification on Timing:*
- "Goods purchased in a given month... available starting the following month."
- This means:
  - Inventory available for sale in Month 1 comes from $I_0$ (Initial). Purchases $x_1$ arrive at the start of Month 2.
  - Inventory available for sale in Month 2 comes from $I_1$ (End of M1) + $x_1$.
  - Generally, for month $k$, the inventory available for sale is $I_{k-1} + x_{k-1}$ (where $I_0$ is initial stock and $x_0=0$).
  - The inventory at the end of month $k$, denoted $I_k$, is:
    $$ I_k = I_{k-1} + x_{k-1} - s_k $$
    For $k=1$: $I_1 = I_0 + x_0 - s_1 = 500 - s_1$ (Since $x_0=0$).
    For $k=2$: $I_2 = I_1 + x_1 - s_2$.
    For $k=3$: $I_3 = I_2 + x_2 - s_3$.
    For $k=4$: $I_4 = I_3 + x_3 - s_4$.

### B. Warehouse Capacity Constraints
The inventory at the end of each month cannot exceed the warehouse capacity.
$$ I_k \le 1000, \quad \forall k \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\} $$
*Note:* It is also implicitly required that the inventory *during* the month (available for sale) does not exceed capacity. Since sales reduce inventory and purchases arrive at the start of the *next* month, the peak inventory in month $k$ is effectively the starting inventory $I_{k-1} + x_{k-1}$.
So, we must also enforce:
$$ I_{k-1} + x_{k-1} \le 1000, \quad \forall k \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\} $$
(Where $I_0 = 500, x_0 = 0$).

### C. Non-negativity and Integrality
$$ x_k \ge 0, \quad s_k \ge 0, \quad I_k \ge 0, \quad \forall k \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\} $$
$$ x_k, s_k, I_k \in \mathbb{Z}^+ \cup \{0\} $$

### D. Sales Limitation (Implicit)
The number of units sold in month $k$ ($s_k$) cannot exceed the inventory available at the start of month $k$ ($I_{k-1} + x_{k-1}$). This is already enforced by the inventory balance equation $I_k = I_{k-1} + x_{k-1} - s_k$ combined with the non-negativity constraint $I_k \ge 0$.

## Summary of Model Structure

**Sets:** $T = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$

**Parameters:**
- $P_{buy} = [10, 9, 11, 15]$
- $P_{sell} = [12, 9, 13, 17]$
- $I_0 = 500$
- $Cap = 1000$

**Variables:**
- $x_k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ for $k=1..4$ (Purchases in month $k$)
- $s_k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ for $k=1..4$ (Sales in month $k$)
- $I_k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ for $k=1..4$ (End-of-month inventory)

**Objective:**
$$ \text{Max } Z = \sum_{k=1}^4 P_{sell,k} s_k - \sum_{k=1}^4 P_{buy,k} x_k $$

**Constraints:**
1. **Inventory Balance:**
   - $I_1 = 500 - s_1$
   - $I_2 = I_1 + x_1 - s_2$
   - $I_3 = I_2 + x_2 - s_3$
   - $I_4 = I_3 + x_3 - s_4$
2. **Capacity (Start of Month / Max Available):**
   - $500 \le 1000$ (Always true)
   - $I_1 + x_1 \le 1000$
   - $I_2 + x_2 \le 1000$
   - $I_3 + x_3 \le 1000$
   - $I_4 + x_4 \le 1000$ (Note: $x_4$ arrives in Month 5, so it doesn't affect Month 4 sales, but it does occupy space if we consider the warehouse state. However, usually, capacity is checked on the stock *on hand*. Since $x_4$ is bought in Month 4, it is in the warehouse. So $I_4 + x_4 \le 1000$ is the correct capacity constraint for the end of Month 4 if we consider $I_4$ as stock before new arrival? No, $I_4$ is defined as end of month. Let's stick to the standard: Inventory on hand at any time $\le$ Capacity.
     - Start of M1: 500
     - End of M1 (Start of M2): $I_1$. Arrival $x_1$ happens at Start of M2. So Max in M2 is $I_1 + x_1$.
     - End of M2 (Start of M3): $I_2$. Arrival $x_2$ happens at Start of M3. Max in M3 is $I_2 + x_2$.
     - End of M3 (Start of M4): $I_3$. Arrival $x_3$ happens at Start of M4. Max in M4 is $I_3 + x_3$.
     - End of M4: $I_4$. Arrival $x_4$ happens at Start of M5. The problem ends at Month 4. Does $x_4$ count towards capacity in Month 4? Yes, if purchased in Month 4, it is in the warehouse. So the inventory at the very end of Month 4, after purchase, is $I_4 + x_4$?
     - Let's refine the balance.
       - Let $Inv_{start, k}$ be inventory at start of month $k$.
       - $Inv_{start, 1} = 500$.
       - $Inv_{start, 2} = Inv_{start, 1} - s_1 + x_1$.
       - $Inv_{start, 3} = Inv_{start, 2} - s_2 + x_2$.
       - $Inv_{start, 4} = Inv_{start, 3} - s_3 + x_3$.
       - Constraint: $Inv_{start, k} \le 1000$ for $k=1..4$.
       - What about $x_4$? It is bought in Month 4. It is available in Month 5. It occupies space in Month 4. So the inventory at the *end* of Month 4 is $Inv_{start, 4} - s_4 + x_4$.
       - Constraint: $Inv_{end, 4} \le 1000$.
       - Also, we must ensure we don't sell what we don't have. $s_k \le Inv_{start, k}$. This is covered by $Inv_{start, k+1} \ge 0$ if we define it properly, or explicit non-negativity of inventory.

**Refined Constraints using $I_k$ as End-of-Month Inventory:**
1. $I_1 = 500 - s_1 + x_1$ ? No. $x_1$ is bought in M1, available M2. So $x_1$ is NOT in $I_1$ if $I_1$ is "stock ready for M2 sales minus what was sold in M1"?
   Let's define $I_k$ as inventory **at the beginning of month k+1** (which is the end of month k).
   - $I_1$ (End of M1) $= 500 - s_1$. (Purchase $x_1$ is made during M1, arrives start of M2. So it is part of the stock at start of M2).
   - So, Start of M2 Inventory $= I_1 + x_1$.
   - $I_2$ (End of M2) $= (I_1 + x_1) - s_2$.
   - Start of M3 Inventory $= I_2 + x_2$.
   - $I_3$ (End of M3) $= (I_2 + x_2) - s_3$.
   - Start of M4 Inventory $= I_3 + x_3$.
   - $I_4$ (End of M4) $= (I_3 + x_3) - s_4$.
   - Start of M5 Inventory $= I_4 + x_4$. (Problem ends, but $x_4$ is a decision variable for M4 purchase).

   **Capacity Constraints:**
   - Start of M1: $500 \le 1000$
   - Start of M2: $I_1 + x_1 \le 1000$
   - Start of M3: $I_2 + x_2 \le 1000$
   - Start of M4: $I_3 + x_3 \le 1000$
   - End of M4 (or Start of M5): $I_4 + x_4 \le 1000$ (Assuming $x_4$ is stored in the warehouse in M4).

   **Non-negativity:**
   - $I_k \ge 0$ for $k=1..4$
   - $s_k \ge 0$
   - $x_k \ge 0$

   **Integrality:**
   - All variables are integers.
