### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
*   $P = \{1, 2\}$: Set of products (Product I, Product II).
*   $M = \{A, B\}$: Set of raw materials.

**Parameters (converted to consistent units):**
*   **Units:**
    *   Decision Variable Scale: Thousands of boxes.
    *   Material Consumption: kg per thousand boxes.
    *   Material Supply: tons (t) per month. Note: $1 \text{ t} = 1000 \text{ kg}$.
    *   Currency: Thousand Yuan.

*   **Material Consumption Coefficients ($a_{m,p}$ in kg/thousand boxes):**
    *   $a_{A,1} = 1$ (Product I uses 1 kg of A per 1000 boxes)
    *   $a_{A,2} = 2$ (Product II uses 2 kg of A per 1000 boxes)
    *   $a_{B,1} = 2$ (Product I uses 2 kg of B per 1000 boxes)
    *   $a_{B,2} = 1$ (Product II uses 1 kg of B per 1000 boxes)

*   **Material Supplies ($S_m$ in kg/month):**
    *   $S_A = 6 \text{ t} = 6000 \text{ kg}$
    *   $S_B = 8 \text{ t} = 8000 \text{ kg}$

*   **Wholesale Prices ($R_p$ in thousand yuan/thousand boxes):**
    *   $R_1 = 30$ (Product I)
    *   $R_2 = 20$ (Product II)

*   **Market Constraints:**
    *   Max Sales of Product II ($U_2$): $2$ (thousand boxes)
    *   Difference Limit ($D$): $1$ (thousand boxes)

### 2. Decision Variables

Let $x_p$ be the production/sales quantity of product $p$ (in **thousands of boxes**).

*   $x_1$: Quantity of Product I produced (thousands of boxes).
*   $x_2$: Quantity of Product II produced (thousands of boxes).

**Domains:**
*   $x_1 \ge 0$ (Continuous)
*   $x_2 \ge 0$ (Continuous)

*(Note: Since the problem asks for "maximum possible wholesale revenue" in an aggregate planning context with large scale capacities (thousands of units), continuous variables are appropriate. "1.5 thousand boxes" is physically realizable as 1500 boxes.)*

### 3. Objective Function

Maximize total wholesale revenue ($Z$) in thousand yuan.

$$ \text{Maximize } Z = 30 x_1 + 20 x_2 $$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**1. Raw Material A Availability:**
Total consumption of Material A must not exceed the monthly supply (6000 kg).
$$ 1 x_1 + 2 x_2 \le 6000 $$
*(Note: Since $x$ is in thousands of boxes and $a$ is kg/thousand boxes, the LHS is in kg.)*

**2. Raw Material B Availability:**
Total consumption of Material B must not exceed the monthly supply (8000 kg).
$$ 2 x_1 + 1 x_2 \le 8000 $$

**3. Market Constraint for Product II:**
Monthly sales of Product II do not exceed 2 thousand boxes.
$$ x_2 \le 2 $$

**4. Relative Sales Constraint:**
The sales volume of Product II does not exceed that of Product I by more than 1 thousand boxes.
$$ x_2 - x_1 \le 1 $$

**5. Non-negativity:**
$$ x_1 \ge 0 $$
$$ x_2 \ge 0 $$

### Model Verification & Sanity Check

Let's quickly analyze the feasibility and likely optimal point to ensure the model makes sense.

1.  **Constraint 3** limits $x_2 \le 2$.
2.  **Constraint 4** implies $x_1 \ge x_2 - 1$.
3.  **Constraint 1** ($x_1 + 2x_2 \le 6000$) and **Constraint 2** ($2x_1 + x_2 \le 8000$) are very loose compared to the market constraints (2 and 4).
    *   If $x_2 = 2$, max possible $x_1$ from Constraint 4 is unbounded upwards? No, wait.
    *   Let's check the material constraints again.
    *   Supply A = 6000 kg. Supply B = 8000 kg.
    *   If we produce large quantities, materials become binding.
    *   However, look at the market constraint: $x_2 \le 2$.
    *   Look at Constraint 4: $x_2 - x_1 \le 1 \implies x_1 \ge x_2 - 1$. This is a *lower* bound on $x_1$ relative to $x_2$. It does NOT limit $x_1$ from above.
    *   Does the problem imply a maximum demand for Product I? The text says: "The sales volume of Product II does not exceed that of Product I by more than 1 thousand boxes." It does **not** state an upper limit on Product I sales, only a relative difference and material limits.
    *   Therefore, $x_1$ can grow as long as materials allow.

    Let's test the material limits if $x_2$ is small.
    If $x_2 = 0$:
    $x_1 \le 6000$ (from Mat A)
    $2x_1 \le 8000 \implies x_1 \le 4000$ (from Mat B)
    So max $x_1 = 4000$.
    Revenue = $30(4000) + 0 = 120,000$.

    If $x_2 = 2$ (max allowed):
    Constraint 4: $2 - x_1 \le 1 \implies x_1 \ge 1$.
    Material A: $x_1 + 2(2) \le 6000 \implies x_1 \le 5996$.
    Material B: $2x_1 + 1(2) \le 8000 \implies 2x_1 \le 7998 \implies x_1 \le 3999$.
    So max $x_1 = 3999$ (constrained by Material B).
    Revenue = $30(3999) + 20(2) = 119,970 + 40 = 120,010$.

    Comparing $120,000$ vs $120,010$, producing some Product II yields slightly higher revenue because Product I has a higher margin per unit of limiting resource B?
    Let's check resource efficiency.
    Product I: Uses 2 kg B, earns 30k yuan. Ratio: 15 yuan/kg B.
    Product II: Uses 1 kg B, earns 20k yuan. Ratio: 20 yuan/kg B.
    Product II is more efficient with Material B.
    Product I: Uses 1 kg A, earns 30k yuan. Ratio: 30 yuan/kg A.
    Product II: Uses 2 kg A, earns 20k yuan. Ratio: 10 yuan/kg A.
    Product I is more efficient with Material A.

    Since Material B is the tighter constraint relative to the "market cap" on Product II?
    Let's look at the intersection of Material A and B constraints.
    $x_1 + 2x_2 = 6000$
    $2x_1 + x_2 = 8000$
    Multiply second by 2: $4x_1 + 2x_2 = 16000$.
    Subtract first: $3x_1 = 10000 \implies x_1 = 3333.33$.
    $x_2 = 8000 - 2(3333.33) = 1333.33$.
    Check Market Constraint 3: $x_2 \le 2$. Here $x_2 = 1333.33 > 2$. **Infeasible**.
    
    So the market constraint $x_2 \le 2$ is definitely binding or the material constraints are not simultaneously binding at the optimum because the "natural" production mix exceeds the market cap for Product II.
    
    Since Product II is capped at 2, we should produce as much of the *other* product (Product I) as possible, or check if increasing $x_2$ within the cap increases revenue.
    
    Let's evaluate the objective function along the feasible boundary.
    Feasible region for $x_1, x_2$:
    1. $x_2 \le 2$
    2. $x_2 - x_1 \le 1 \Rightarrow x_1 \ge x_2 - 1$
    3. $x_1 + 2x_2 \le 6000$
    4. $2x_1 + x_2 \le 8000$
    5. $x_1, x_2 \ge 0$

    Since $Z = 30x_1 + 20x_2$, and $x_1$ has a higher coefficient, we generally want to maximize $x_1$.
    However, $x_2$ uses fewer of the limiting resource (B) per unit revenue?
    Actually, let's just look at the vertices.
    
    Vertex 1: $x_2 = 0$.
    From Mat B: $2x_1 \le 8000 \Rightarrow x_1 \le 4000$.
    From Mat A: $x_1 \le 6000$.
    From Mkt 4: $-x_1 \le 1$ (satisfied for $x_1 \ge 0$).
    Point: $(4000, 0)$.
    $Z = 30(4000) = 120,000$.

    Vertex 2: $x_2 = 2$.
    From Mkt 4: $2 - x_1 \le 1 \Rightarrow x_1 \ge 1$.
    From Mat B: $2x_1 + 2 \le 8000 \Rightarrow 2x_1 \le 7998 \Rightarrow x_1 \le 3999$.
    From Mat A: $x_1 + 4 \le 6000 \Rightarrow x_1 \le 5996$.
    Max $x_1$ is 3999.
    Point: $(3999, 2)$.
    $Z = 30(3999) + 20(2) = 119,970 + 40 = 120,010$.

    Vertex 3: Intersection of $x_2=2$ and $x_1 = x_2 - 1$ (i.e., $x_1=1$).
    Point: $(1, 2)$.
    $Z = 30(1) + 20(2) = 70$. (Very low).

    Comparing 120,000 and 120,010, the maximum is 120,010.
    
    The model formulation is correct. The "trick" is ensuring the units are consistent (tons to kg) and interpreting the relative sales constraint correctly.

### Final Mathematical Model

**Sets and Parameters:**
*   $P = \{1, 2\}$
*   $a_{1,1}=1, a_{1,2}=2, a_{2,1}=2, a_{2,2}=1$ (kg/thousand boxes)
*   $S_1 = 6000, S_2 = 8000$ (kg)
*   $R_1 = 30, R_2 = 20$ (thousand yuan/thousand boxes)
*   $U_2 = 2$ (thousand boxes)
*   $D_{diff} = 1$ (thousand boxes)

**Variables:**
*   $x_1, x_2 \in \mathbb{R}^+$

**Objective:**
$$ \max Z = 30x_1 + 20x_2 $$

**Constraints:**
1.  $x_1 + 2x_2 \le 6000$
2.  $2x_1 + x_2 \le 8000$
3.  $x_2 \le 2$
4.  $x_2 - x_1 \le 1$
5.  $x_1 \ge 0$
6.  $x_2 \ge 0$
