### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Sets:**
*   $G = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of base liquor grades (Grade I, Grade II, Grade III).
*   $B = \{R, Y, B\}$: Set of branded liquor products (Red, Yellow, Blue).
*   $g \in G$: Index for base liquor grade.
*   $b \in B$: Index for branded product.

**Parameters:**
*   $S_g$: Daily supply limit of base liquor grade $g$ (kg).
    *   $S_1 = 1500$
    *   $S_2 = 2000$
    *   $S_3 = 1000$
*   $C_g$: Cost per kg of base liquor grade $g$ (Yuan/kg).
    *   $C_1 = 6.0$
    *   $C_2 = 4.5$
    *   $C_3 = 3.0$
*   $P_b$: Selling price per kg of branded product $b$ (Yuan/kg).
    *   $P_R = 5.5$
    *   $P_Y = 5.0$
    *   $P_B = 4.8$
*   $L_{g,b}$: Minimum required percentage of grade $g$ in product $b$.
*   $U_{g,b}$: Maximum allowed percentage of grade $g$ in product $b$.
*   $D_{min, b}$: Minimum daily production requirement for product $b$.
    *   $D_{min, R} = 2000$
    *   $D_{min, Y} = 0$
    *   $D_{min, B} = 0$

**Blending Constraints Data (converted to fractions):**
*   **Red ($R$):**
    *   Grade I: $> 50\% \Rightarrow L_{1,R} = 0.50, U_{1,R} = 1.0$
    *   Grade II: No specific bounds stated, implied by others. $L_{2,R} = 0, U_{2,R} = 1.0$
    *   Grade III: $< 10\% \Rightarrow L_{3,R} = 0, U_{3,R} = 0.10$
*   **Yellow ($Y$):**
    *   Grade I: $> 20\% \Rightarrow L_{1,Y} = 0.20, U_{1,Y} = 1.0$
    *   Grade II: No specific bounds stated. $L_{2,Y} = 0, U_{2,Y} = 1.0$
    *   Grade III: $< 70\% \Rightarrow L_{3,Y} = 0, U_{3,Y} = 0.70$
*   **Blue ($B$):**
    *   Grade I: $> 10\% \Rightarrow L_{1,B} = 0.10, U_{1,B} = 1.0$
    *   Grade II: No specific bounds stated. $L_{2,B} = 0, U_{2,B} = 1.0$
    *   Grade III: $< 50\% \Rightarrow L_{3,B} = 0, U_{3,B} = 0.50$

*Note on Strict Inequalities:* The problem states "strictly follow" and uses symbols $>$ and $<$. In linear programming, strict inequalities ($<$ or $>$) define open sets which are not closed. However, in practical optimization contexts involving continuous variables and "follow specified proportions", these are typically treated as $\ge$ and $\le$ constraints. If strictness is mathematically required, the problem is ill-posed for standard LP solvers (no optimal solution exists on the boundary). Given the context of standard OR problems, we interpret $> 50\%$ as $\ge 50\%$ and $< 10\%$ as $\le 10\%$. If the problem implies that the *exact* boundaries are forbidden, it is usually a trick question, but standard practice assumes closed intervals for solvability. We will use $\le$ and $\ge$.

### 2. Decision Variables

Let $x_{g,b}$ be the amount (in kg) of base liquor grade $g$ used in branded product $b$.
*   $x_{g,b} \in \mathbb{R}^+ \quad \forall g \in G, b \in B$

Let $P_b$ be the total production quantity (in kg) of branded product $b$.
*   $P_b \in \mathbb{R}^+ \quad \forall b \in B$

*Note: $P_b$ can be defined as $\sum_{g \in G} x_{g,b}$. To keep the model linear and explicit, we define it as a variable linked by equality constraints.*

### 3. Objective Function

**Goal:** Maximize Profit.
Profit = Total Revenue - Total Cost.

$$ \text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{b \in B} P_b P_b^{price} - \sum_{g \in G} \sum_{b \in B} C_g x_{g,b} $$

Substituting parameters:
$$ Z = 5.5 P_R + 5.0 P_Y + 4.8 P_B - (6.0 x_{1,R} + 4.5 x_{2,R} + 3.0 x_{3,R}) - (6.0 x_{1,Y} + 4.5 x_{2,Y} + 3.0 x_{3,Y}) - (6.0 x_{1,B} + 4.5 x_{2,B} + 3.0 x_{3,B}) $$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**1. Production Balance (Definition of Product Quantity)**
The total production of each brand is the sum of all base liquors used in it.
$$ P_b = \sum_{g \in G} x_{g,b} \quad \forall b \in B $$

**2. Raw Material Supply Constraints**
The total usage of each grade cannot exceed its daily supply.
$$ \sum_{b \in B} x_{g,b} \le S_g \quad \forall g \in G $$
Specifically:
*   Grade I: $x_{1,R} + x_{1,Y} + x_{1,B} \le 1500$
*   Grade II: $x_{2,R} + x_{2,Y} + x_{2,B} \le 2000$
*   Grade III: $x_{3,R} + x_{3,Y} + x_{3,B} \le 1000$

**3. Blending Proportion Constraints**
For each brand $b$ and grade $g$, the amount $x_{g,b}$ must satisfy the percentage requirements relative to the total production $P_b$.
$$ L_{g,b} P_b \le x_{g,b} \le U_{g,b} P_b \quad \forall g \in G, b \in B $$

These can be written as linear inequalities:
$$ x_{g,b} - L_{g,b} P_b \ge 0 $$
$$ x_{g,b} - U_{g,b} P_b \le 0 $$

**Detailed Blending Constraints:**

*   **For Red ($R$):**
    *   Grade I ($\ge 50\%$): $x_{1,R} \ge 0.50 P_R \Rightarrow x_{1,R} - 0.50 P_R \ge 0$
    *   Grade III ($\le 10\%$): $x_{3,R} \le 0.10 P_R \Rightarrow x_{3,R} - 0.10 P_R \le 0$
    *   Grade II: No explicit bounds, but implicitly $0 \le x_{2,R} \le P_R$ (handled by non-negativity and sum definition, plus the other constraints). Note that $x_{1,R} + x_{2,R} + x_{3,R} = P_R$ is already enforced.

*   **For Yellow ($Y$):**
    *   Grade I ($\ge 20\%$): $x_{1,Y} \ge 0.20 P_Y \Rightarrow x_{1,Y} - 0.20 P_Y \ge 0$
    *   Grade III ($\le 70\%$): $x_{3,Y} \le 0.70 P_Y \Rightarrow x_{3,Y} - 0.70 P_Y \le 0$

*   **For Blue ($B$):**
    *   Grade I ($\ge 10\%$): $x_{1,B} \ge 0.10 P_B \Rightarrow x_{1,B} - 0.10 P_B \ge 0$
    *   Grade III ($\le 50\%$): $x_{3,B} \le 0.50 P_B \Rightarrow x_{3,B} - 0.50 P_B \le 0$

**4. Minimum Production Requirement**
At least 2,000 kg of Red brand liquor must be produced.
$$ P_R \ge 2000 $$

**5. Non-negativity**
$$ x_{g,b} \ge 0 \quad \forall g \in G, b \in B $$
$$ P_b \ge 0 \quad \forall b \in B $$

---
**Model Verification Notes:**
1.  **Linearity:** All constraints and the objective function are linear in the variables $x_{g,b}$ and $P_b$. The blending constraints are linear because the percentages are constants multiplied by the variable $P_b$.
2.  **Integrality:** The problem involves "kg" of liquor. While individual bottles might be discrete, "kg" is a continuous unit. The scale (thousands of kg) is large. Standard LP relaxation is appropriate and expected. No integer constraints are necessary.
3.  **Feasibility:**
    *   Red requires $\ge 2000$ kg total.
    *   Red needs $\ge 50\%$ Grade I $\Rightarrow \ge 1000$ kg of Grade I.
    *   Red needs $\le 10\%$ Grade III $\Rightarrow \le 200$ kg of Grade III.
    *   The remaining $1000$ to $1800$ kg of Red can be Grade II.
    *   Grade I supply is 1500. If Red takes 1000, 500 remain for Yellow/Blue.
    *   This seems feasible.
4.  **Strict Inequalities:** As noted, $>$ and $<$ are modeled as $\ge$ and $\le$. If the problem strictly forbids the boundary values (e.g., exactly 50%), the feasible region is open, and a maximum profit might not be attained (supremum exists, but no optimal solution). However, in standard OR textbook problems, "proportions" usually define the allowable range including boundaries, or the "strictly follow" refers to adhering to the recipe limits which are typically inclusive in industrial specs. Given the instruction "strictly follow its specified blending proportions", and standard LP solvers, $\ge/\le$ is the standard interpretation.
