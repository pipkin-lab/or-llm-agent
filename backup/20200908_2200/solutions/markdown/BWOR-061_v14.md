This is a **sequential decision-making problem** under uncertainty, specifically an instance of the **Optimal Stopping Problem** (often related to the "Secretary Problem" or "Job Search Problem"). It is **not** a standard Linear Programming (Linear Programming) or Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) problem in the traditional sense of resource allocation, because the "variables" are decision rules (thresholds) based on observed states, and the objective is an expected value derived from a probability tree, not a linear sum of variables.

However, we can model this as a **Dynamic Programming** problem or formulate it as a **Mixed-Integer Linear Program (MILP)** if we discretize the decision rules. Given the small state space (3 candidates, 3 possible scores), the most rigorous mathematical formulation is a **Stochastic Dynamic Programming** model or a direct **Expected Value Maximization** over the finite set of possible strategies.

Since the prompt asks for a formal optimization model (LP/MILP) and the state space is tiny, we can model the **strategy** using binary variables that define the decision rule for each possible state.

### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Sets:**
- $C = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of candidate positions in the interview sequence.
- $S = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of possible interview scores (1=Unsatisfactory, 2=Satisfactory/Somewhat, 3=Satisfactory).

**Parameters:**
- $p_s$: Probability of score $s$ for any candidate.
  - $p_1 = 0.30$
  - $p_2 = 0.50$
  - $p_3 = 0.20$
- $V_t(s)$: The expected maximum future score obtainable from candidates $t+1$ to $3$, given that we are at step $t$ with current state $s$ (if we continue).
- $M$: A sufficiently large constant (Big-M) for linearization if needed, though not strictly necessary here due to the small scale.

### 2. Decision Variables

We need to define the **decision rule**. A strategy is defined by what we do when we observe a specific score at a specific stage.

Let $x_{t, s} \in \{0, 1\}$ be a binary decision variable:
- $x_{t, s} = 1$ if, at interview step $t$ (where $t \in \{1, 2\}$), upon observing score $s$, we decide to **HIRE** the current candidate.
- $x_{t, s} = 0$ if, at interview step $t$, upon observing score $s$, we decide to **REJECT** the current candidate and continue.

*Note:*
- At step $t=3$, the decision is forced: we must hire regardless of score. Thus, $x_{3, s}$ is not a decision variable; the action is fixed to "Hire".
- If we reach step 3, it implies steps 1 and 2 resulted in rejection.

### 3. Objective Function

The objective is to **MAXIMIZE** the expected score of the hired candidate.

Let $E$ be the expected score. We calculate $E$ by summing over all possible paths in the decision tree, weighted by their probabilities and the decision variables $x_{t,s}$.

The total expected score $Z$ is:

$$
\text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{t=1}^{2} \sum_{s \in S} \left( p_{\text{reach } t} \cdot p_s \cdot x_{t,s} \cdot s \right) + \sum_{s \in S} \left( p_{\text{reach } 3} \cdot p_s \cdot s \right)
$$

However, $p_{\text{reach } t}$ depends on previous decisions. To make this a linear function of $x$, we must expand the paths explicitly.

**Path Expansion:**

1.  **Hire at Step 1:**
    - Probability: $p_s$
    - Contribution: $p_s \cdot x_{1,s} \cdot s$

2.  **Hire at Step 2:**
    - To reach step 2, we must have rejected step 1.
    - Let $R_1$ be the event of rejecting step 1.
    - Probability of rejecting step 1 given score $s_1$ is $(1 - x_{1, s_1})$.
    - Probability of reaching step 2 and observing score $s_2$ is: $\sum_{s_1 \in S} p_{s_1} (1 - x_{1, s_1}) p_{s_2}$.
    - Contribution if we hire at step 2 with score $s_2$:
      $$ \left( \sum_{s_1 \in S} p_{s_1} (1 - x_{1, s_1}) \right) \cdot p_{s_2} \cdot x_{2, s_2} \cdot s_2 $$

3.  **Hire at Step 3 (Forced):**
    - To reach step 3, we must have rejected step 1 AND step 2.
    - Probability of rejecting step 1: $P(R_1) = \sum_{s_1} p_{s_1} (1 - x_{1, s_1})$
    - Probability of rejecting step 2 given we reached step 2: This is conditional.
    - Let's define the probability of reaching step 3, $P(R_3)$.
    - $P(R_3) = \sum_{s_1} p_{s_1} (1 - x_{1, s_1}) \sum_{s_2} p_{s_2} (1 - x_{2, s_2})$
    - Since the scores are independent, $P(R_3) = \left( \sum_{s_1 \in S} p_{s_1} (1 - x_{1, s_1}) \right) \left( \sum_{s_2 \in S} p_{s_2} (1 - x_{2, s_2}) \right)$.
    - The expected score of the 3rd candidate is $E[S_3] = \sum_{s_3} p_{s_3} s_3 = 0.3(1) + 0.5(2) + 0.2(3) = 0.3 + 1.0 + 0.6 = 1.9$.
    - Contribution: $P(R_3) \cdot 1.9$

**Linearizing the Objective Function:**

The term $P(R_3)$ involves products of terms $(1 - x)$. Specifically, $\left( \sum p_s (1-x_{1,s}) \right) \left( \sum p_s (1-x_{2,s}) \right)$ is non-linear (quadratic in $x$).

To strictly adhere to a **Linear** model, we must introduce auxiliary variables or recognize that the "strategy space" is small enough to enumerate, or use a **Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP)** formulation with auxiliary variables for the probabilities.

Let:
- $u_1 = \sum_{s \in S} p_s (1 - x_{1,s})$ : Probability of rejecting candidate 1.
- $u_2 = \sum_{s \in S} p_s (1 - x_{2,s})$ : Probability of rejecting candidate 2 (conditional on reaching it, but since the rejection rule is static based on score, the *unconditional* probability of rejecting step 2 given it is reached is determined by the mix of scores that led us there. Wait, the rejection decision at step 2 depends on the score *at step 2*. The probability of rejecting at step 2 is the probability that the score at step 2 is such that $x_{2,s}=0$.

Let's refine the variables to keep it linear.

**Revised Decision Variables:**
- $x_{1,1}, x_{1,2}, x_{1,3} \in \{0,1\}$: Hire candidate 1 if score is 1, 2, or 3.
- $x_{2,1}, x_{2,2}, x_{2,3} \in \{0,1\}$: Hire candidate 2 if score is 1, 2, or 3.

**Auxiliary Variables for Probabilities:**
- $q_1$: Probability that we proceed to candidate 2.
  - $q_1 = \sum_{s} p_s (1 - x_{1,s})$
  - Constraints:
    - $q_1 \ge 0.3(1 - x_{1,1})$
    - $q_1 \ge 0.5(1 - x_{1,2})$
    - $q_1 \ge 0.2(1 - x_{1,3})$
    - Actually, $q_1$ is exactly the sum. In MILP, we can define $q_1$ with equality if we linearize the product, but here $q_1$ is linear in $x$.
    - $q_1 = 0.3(1 - x_{1,1}) + 0.5(1 - x_{1,2}) + 0.2(1 - x_{1,3})$
    - This can be written as:
      - $q_1 + 0.3 x_{1,1} + 0.5 x_{1,2} + 0.2 x_{1,3} = 1.0$

- $q_2$: Probability that we proceed to candidate 3.
  - This is the probability that we rejected C1 AND rejected C2.
  - $P(\text{Reject C2} | \text{Reached C2}) = \frac{\sum_s p_s (1-x_{2,s})}{1}$? No.
  - The probability of reaching C2 is $q_1$.
  - Given we reached C2, the distribution of scores for C2 is still $p_s$ (independent).
  - The probability of rejecting C2 given we are at C2 is $r_2 = \sum_s p_s (1 - x_{2,s})$.
  - So, $q_2 = q_1 \cdot r_2$.
  - This is a **product of two variables** ($q_1$ and $r_2$), which makes the objective non-linear.

**Solution to Linearity:**
Since the state space is extremely small ($2^3 \times 2^3 = 64$ possible strategies), a pure LP/MILP formulation with linear constraints on the *expected value* is difficult without enumerating or using higher-order terms. However, we can formulate this as a **MILP** by introducing binary variables for each **complete path** or by using the fact that $q_2$ is a specific value determined by the $x$'s.

Actually, there is a simpler way. We can define the objective function directly in terms of the expected value, which is a quadratic function of $x$. Standard LP solvers cannot handle quadratic objectives. Therefore, this problem is best modeled as a **Quadratic Binary Optimization Problem** or solved via **Dynamic Programming**.

However, if we are forced to provide an LP/MILP structure, we can use the **Disjunctive Formulation** or simply state that the objective is non-linear. But usually, in such small problems, we can linearize by introducing variables for the joint probabilities.

**Alternative Approach: Dynamic Programming Backward Induction (Standard OR Solution)**

While the prompt asks for a mathematical model, the *correct* way to solve this is DP. We can present the DP formulation as the "model".

**Stage 3 (Last Candidate):**
- Must hire.
- Expected Value $V_3(s) = s$ for any score $s$ observed? No, $V_3$ is the value of the *process* ending at 3.
- Let $V_t$ be the expected optimal score from stage $t$ onwards, *before* observing the score at $t$.
- At $t=3$: We must hire. The expected score is $E[S] = 1.9$.
  - So, if we are forced to take C3, the value is $1.9$.
  - More precisely, let $f_t(s)$ be the expected future reward given that we are at stage $t$ and have just observed score $s$.
  - $f_3(s) = s$.
  - $V_3 = E[f_3(S_3)] = 1.9$.

**Stage 2:**
- We observe score $s_2$.
- Option 1: Hire. Reward = $s_2$.
- Option 2: Reject. Reward = $V_3 = 1.9$ (since C3 is independent and we must take it).
- Decision: Hire if $s_2 > 1.9$. Reject if $s_2 < 1.9$. If $s_2 = 1.9$, indifferent.
- Scores are 1, 2, 3.
  - If $s_2 = 1$: $1 < 1.9 \rightarrow$ Reject.
  - If $s_2 = 2$: $2 > 1.9 \rightarrow$ Hire.
  - If $s_2 = 3$: $3 > 1.9 \rightarrow$ Hire.
- So, at Stage 2, we hire if score $\ge 2$.
- Expected Value at Stage 2 (before observing score):
  - $V_2 = p_1 \cdot (\text{Value if reject}) + p_2 \cdot (\text{Value if hire}) + p_3 \cdot (\text{Value if hire})$
  - $V_2 = 0.3 \cdot 1.9 + 0.5 \cdot 2 + 0.2 \cdot 3$
  - $V_2 = 0.57 + 1.0 + 0.6 = 2.17$

**Stage 1:**
- We observe score $s_1$.
- Option 1: Hire. Reward = $s_1$.
- Option 2: Reject. Reward = $V_2 = 2.17$ (Expected value of optimal strategy from Stage 2 onwards).
- Decision: Hire if $s_1 > 2.17$. Reject if $s_1 < 2.17$.
- Scores are 1, 2, 3.
  - If $s_1 = 1$: $1 < 2.17 \rightarrow$ Reject.
  - If $s_1 = 2$: $2 < 2.17 \rightarrow$ Reject.
  - If $s_1 = 3$: $3 > 2.17 \rightarrow$ Hire.
- So, at Stage 1, we hire if score $= 3$.
- Expected Value at Stage 1 (before observing score):
  - $V_1 = p_1 \cdot (\text{Value if reject}) + p_2 \cdot (\text{Value if reject}) + p_3 \cdot (\text{Value if hire})$
  - $V_1 = 0.3 \cdot 2.17 + 0.5 \cdot 2.17 + 0.2 \cdot 3$
  - $V_1 = 0.8(2.17) + 0.6 = 1.736 + 0.6 = 2.336$

**Recommended Formulation for Implementation (MILP with Linear Objective via Path Enumeration):**

Since there are only $2^3=8$ possible decision rules for Stage 1 and $2^3=8$ for Stage 2 (total 64 strategies), we can define binary variables $y_m$ for each of the 64 possible complete strategies $m$.

**Sets:**
- $M$: Set of all 64 possible complete strategies.
  - A strategy $m$ is defined by a vector $(x_{1,1}, x_{1,2}, x_{1,3}, x_{2,1}, x_{2,2}, x_{2,3})$.

**Parameters:**
- $Z_m$: The expected score for strategy $m$, pre-calculated.
  - For a given strategy $m$, calculate $Z_m$ using the DP logic or direct path summation.

**Decision Variables:**
- $y_m \in \{0, 1\}$ for each $m \in M$.

**Objective:**
$$ \text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{m \in M} Z_m y_m $$

**Constraints:**
$$ \sum_{m \in M} y_m = 1 $$
$$ y_m \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall m \in M $$

This is a valid **Binary Linear Program**. The "model" is essentially selecting the optimal strategy from the finite set.
