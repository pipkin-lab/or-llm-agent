Based on the problem description, this is a **Knapsack Problem** (specifically a 0-1 or Unbounded Knapsack depending on availability, but typically in such textbook contexts with "types of products" and no explicit limit on production quantity, it implies an **Unbounded Knapsack** or **Integer Linear Programming** where you can produce multiple units of each product).

However, standard "production/shipping" problems where you decide *how many* of each product to ship usually assume you can produce any non-negative integer number of units. Since the weights are small integers (2, 3, 4) and the capacity is 10 tons, the total number of items is small. This is an **Integer Linear Programming (ILP)** problem.

Let's analyze the integrality requirement:
- The problem asks to ship products. You cannot ship 0.5 pieces of a product.
- Therefore, the decision variables must be **integers**.
- Scale check: Total capacity is 10 tons. Max items $\approx 5$. This is a small-scale problem, so MILP is appropriate and expected.

### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Sets:**
- $I = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of product types.

**Parameters:**
- $W_i$: Weight of one unit of product type $i$ (tons).
  - $W_1 = 2$
  - $W_2 = 3$
  - $W_3 = 4$
- $P_i$: Profit of one unit of product type $i$ (yuan).
  - $P_1 = 100$
  - $P_2 = 140$
  - $P_3 = 180$
- $C$: Total transportation capacity (tons).
  - $C = 10$

### 2. Decision Variables

Let $x_i$ be the number of units of product type $i$ to be shipped.

- **Domain**: $x_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ for all $i \in I$.
  - *Note*: We assume an unbounded supply of products (or sufficient production) such that the only constraint is the transportation capacity. If there were production limits, they would be specified.

### 3. Objective Function

Maximize the total profit:

$$ \text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{i \in I} P_i x_i $$

Substituting the values:

$$ \text{Maximize } Z = 100 x_1 + 140 x_2 + 180 x_3 $$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**1. Transportation Capacity Constraint:**
The total weight of the shipped products must not exceed the capacity.

$$ \sum_{i \in I} W_i x_i \le C $$

Substituting the values:

$$ 2 x_1 + 3 x_2 + 4 x_3 \le 10 $$

**2. Non-negativity and Integrality Constraints:**

$$ x_1, x_2, x_3 \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0} $$

### Final Mathematical Model

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Maximize} \quad & Z = 100 x_1 + 140 x_2 + 180 x_3 \\
\text{Subject to:} \quad & 2 x_1 + 3 x_2 + 4 x_3 \le 10 \\
& x_1, x_2, x_3 \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}
\end{aligned}
$$
