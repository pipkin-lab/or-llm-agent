### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Index Sets:**
*   $I$: Set of students, $I = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$.
    *   $I_{UG} = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$: Undergraduate students.
    *   $I_{Grad} = \{5, 6\}$: Graduate students.
*   $D$: Set of days, $D = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ representing Monday to Friday.

**Parameters:**
*   $w_i$: Hourly wage for student $i$ (yuan/hour).
    *   $w_1=10.0, w_2=10.0, w_3=9.9, w_4=9.8, w_5=10.8, w_6=11.3$.
*   $H_{i,d}$: Maximum available work hours for student $i$ on day $d$.
    *   $H_{1,1}=6, H_{1,2}=0, H_{1,3}=9, H_{1,4}=0, H_{1,5}=7$
    *   $H_{2,1}=0, H_{2,2}=9, H_{2,3}=0, H_{2,4}=6, H_{2,5}=0$
    *   $H_{3,1}=4, H_{3,2}=8, H_{3,3}=3, H_{3,4}=0, H_{3,5}=5$
    *   $H_{4,1}=5, H_{4,2}=5, H_{4,3}=6, H_{4,4}=0, H_{4,5}=4$
    *   $H_{5,1}=3, H_{5,2}=0, H_{5,3}=4, H_{5,4}=8, H_{5,5}=0$
    *   $H_{6,1}=0, H_{6,2}=9, H_{6,3}=0, H_{6,4}=6, H_{6,5}=3$
*   $L_{min}$: Minimum weekly work hours requirement.
    *   $L_{min, i} = 8$ if $i \in I_{UG}$, else $7$ if $i \in I_{Grad}$.
*   $T_{daily}$: Total operational hours per day = 14 hours.

### 2. Decision Variables

Let $x_{i,d}$ be the number of hours student $i$ works on day $d$.

*   **Domain**: $x_{i,d} \ge 0$ for all $i \in I, d \in D$.
*   **Type**: Continuous variables are appropriate here. Although hours are often scheduled in discrete slots (e.g., 0.5h or 1h blocks), standard workforce scheduling LPs typically treat hours as continuous unless "integer shifts" or specific slot constraints are explicitly mandated. Given the problem asks for a mathematical model to minimize wage and provides "maximum available hours" (which are integers) but does not specify discrete shift slots, a Linear Programming (LP) formulation is standard and sufficient. The fractional differences in optimal cost for such small-scale problems are negligible, and LP relaxation is the standard academic expectation for this type of "minimum cost flow/scheduling" problem.

### 3. Objective Function

Minimize the total weekly wage payment:

$$
\text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{i \in I} \sum_{d \in D} w_i x_{i,d}
$$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**1. Daily Coverage Constraint:**
At any time, there must be exactly one student on duty. This implies that the sum of hours worked by all students on any given day must equal the total operational hours of the day (14 hours).

$$
\sum_{i \in I} x_{i,d} = 14 \quad \forall d \in D
$$

**2. Student Availability Constraints:**
The hours worked by student $i$ on day $d$ cannot exceed their maximum available hours.

$$
x_{i,d} \le H_{i,d} \quad \forall i \in I, d \in D
$$

**3. Minimum Weekly Work Hours Constraints:**
*   Undergraduates ($i \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$) must work at least 8 hours per week.
*   Graduates ($i \in \{5, 6\}$) must work at least 7 hours per week.

$$
\sum_{d \in D} x_{i,d} \ge 8 \quad \forall i \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}
$$

$$
\sum_{d \in D} x_{i,d} \ge 7 \quad \forall i \in \{5, 6\}
$$

**4. Non-negativity:**

$$
x_{i,d} \ge 0 \quad \forall i \in I, d \in D
$$
