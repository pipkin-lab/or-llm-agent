**Model Type:** Linear Programming (LP)

### 1. Index Sets & Parameters

**Sets:**
- $S = \{I, II\}$: Set of power stations.
- $C = \{1, 2, 3\}$: Set of cities.

**Parameters:**
- $Supply_s$: Maximum supply capacity of station $s$ (units).
  - $Supply_I = 400$
  - $Supply_{II} = 450$
- $Cost_{s,c}$: Unit cost of supplying electricity from station $s$ to city $c$ (Yuan/unit).
  - $Cost_{I,1} = 15, \quad Cost_{I,2} = 18, \quad Cost_{I,3} = 22$
  - $Cost_{II,1} = 21, \quad Cost_{II,2} = 25, \quad Cost_{II,3} = 16$
- $DemandMin_c$: Minimum required supply for city $c$ (units).
  - City 1: Can be reduced by up to 30 units from 320 $\rightarrow 320 - 30 = 290$. So, $DemandMin_1 = 290$.
  - City 2: Fixed at 250. So, $DemandMin_2 = 250$.
  - City 3: Must be at least 270. So, $DemandMin_3 = 270$.
- $DemandMax_c$: Maximum allowed supply for city $c$ (units).
  - City 1: Original demand is 320. So, $DemandMax_1 = 320$.
  - City 2: Fixed at 250. So, $DemandMax_2 = 250$.
  - City 3: Up to 350. So, $DemandMax_3 = 350$.

**Total Supply Check:**
Total Supply $= 400 + 450 = 850$ units.
Min Total Demand $= 290 + 250 + 270 = 810$ units.
Max Total Demand $= 320 + 250 + 350 = 920$ units.
Since $810 \le 850 \le 920$, a feasible solution exists that uses all supply.

### 2. Decision Variables

Let $x_{s,c}$ be the amount of electricity (in units) supplied from station $s \in S$ to city $c \in C$.
- **Domain:** $x_{s,c} \ge 0$ (Continuous variables, as electricity is a divisible resource).

### 3. Objective Function

Minimize the total cost of electricity supply.

$$ \text{Minimize } Z = \sum_{s \in S} \sum_{c \in C} Cost_{s,c} \cdot x_{s,c} $$

Explicitly:
$$ Z = 15x_{I,1} + 18x_{I,2} + 22x_{I,3} + 21x_{II,1} + 25x_{II,2} + 16x_{II,3} $$

### 4. Operational Constraints

**A. Supply Constraints (Use up all available electricity)**
The problem states the plan must "use up all available electricity from both stations." Thus, the supply constraints are equalities.

1.  **Station I:**
    $$ x_{I,1} + x_{I,2} + x_{I,3} = 400 $$

2.  **Station II:**
    $$ x_{II,1} + x_{II,2} + x_{II,3} = 450 $$

**B. Demand Constraints (City-specific limits)**
The total supply to each city is the sum of supplies from all stations. This total must respect the min/max bounds derived from the problem description.

1.  **City 1:**
    $$ 290 \le x_{I,1} + x_{II,1} \le 320 $$
    *(Can be split into two inequalities if required by solver, but range notation is standard for LP formulation)*

2.  **City 2:**
    $$ x_{I,2} + x_{II,2} = 250 $$
    *(Fixed supply)*

3.  **City 3:**
    $$ 270 \le x_{I,3} + x_{II,3} \le 350 $$

**C. Non-negativity**
$$ x_{s,c} \ge 0 \quad \forall s \in S, c \in C $$

---
**Feasibility Sanity Check:**
- Total Supply = 850.
- If we satisfy minimums: $290 (C1) + 250 (C2) + 270 (C3) = 810$.
- Remaining supply to allocate: $850 - 810 = 40$ units.
- This 40 units can be distributed among C1 and C3 up to their maximums.
- C1 can take up to $320 - 290 = 30$ more.
- C3 can take up to $350 - 270 = 80$ more.
- Since $40 \le 30 + 80$, it is feasible to allocate the remaining 40 units within the upper bounds. The model is feasible.
