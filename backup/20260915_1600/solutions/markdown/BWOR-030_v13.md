### 3. Objective Function

The problem does not explicitly state "minimize cost" or "maximize profit". However, it is a trading company with a specific target inventory. Usually, such problems aim to **maximize profit** (or final capital) subject to the inventory target and capacity constraints. Alternatively, if the target inventory is a hard constraint, we maximize the net cash position at the end of the horizon.

Let's assume the goal is to **Maximize the final capital $K_3$** (or equivalently, maximize total profit).

$$ \text{MAXIMIZE } Z = K_3 $$

*Self-Correction/Refinement:* In many textbook formulations of this specific grain trading problem, the objective is often to maximize the total profit over the quarter. Since capital is conserved (cash in - cash out), maximizing $K_3$ is equivalent to maximizing Profit.

### 4. Operational Constraints

**A. Inventory Balance Constraints**
Inventory at end of month $t$ = Inventory at end of month $t-1$ + Purchases in month $t$ - Sales in month $t$.
*   End of Jan ($t=1$): $I_1 = I_0 + x_1 - y_1$
*   End of Feb ($t=2$): $I_2 = I_1 + x_2 - y_2$
*   End of Mar ($t=3$): $I_3 = I_2 + x_3 - y_3$

Substituting $I_0 = 1,000$:
1.  $I_1 = 1,000 + x_1 - y_1$
2.  $I_2 = I_1 + x_2 - y_2$
3.  $I_3 = I_2 + x_3 - y_3$

**B. Warehouse Capacity Constraints**
Inventory at the end of each month must not exceed warehouse capacity.

**C. Target Ending Inventory Constraint**

**D. Sales Timing Constraints**
Grain purchased in month $t$ cannot be sold until month $t+1$.
*   Sales in Month 1 ($y_1$): Can only come from Initial Stock ($I_0$).
    *   Constraint: $y_1 \le I_0 = 1,000$
    *   (This is implicitly handled by inventory balance if we ensure $I_1 \ge 0$, but explicit bounds on sales sources help clarity. However, standard inventory balance $I_t = I_{t-1} + x_t - y_t$ with $I_t \ge 0$ ensures we don't sell more than we have. But does it ensure we don't sell *new* purchases? Yes, because $x_t$ is added *after* sales in the standard "beginning inventory + purchases - sales = ending inventory" flow if sales happen before purchases or if we define the flow correctly.
    *   *Clarification on Timing:* "Grain arrives in the month it is purchased... can only be sold starting in the next month."

        *   In Month 1: Available for sale = $I_0$. ($x_1$ arrives too late to sell in M1). So $y_1 \le I_0$.
        *   In Month 2: Available for sale = $I_1$ (which includes $I_0$ and $x_1$). So $y_2 \le I_1$.
        *   In Month 3: Available for sale = $I_2$ (which includes $I_0, x_1, x_2$). So $y_3 \le I_2$.
    *   These are naturally satisfied by $I_t \ge 0$ if we define the balance as $I_t = I_{t-1} + x_t - y_t$ AND we interpret $y_t$ as sales from *available* stock. Wait.
    *   Let's check the balance equation again.
        *   $I_1 = I_0 + x_1 - y_1$. If $y_1 > I_0$, then $I_1 < x_1$. This doesn't violate $I_1 \ge 0$ necessarily.
        *   However, the physical constraint is $y_1$ cannot use $x_1$.
        *   Correct constraint for Month 1: $y_1 \le I_0$.
        *   Correct constraint for Month 2: $y_2 \le I_0 + x_1$ (i.e., $y_2 \le I_1$).
        *   Correct constraint for Month 3: $y_3 \le I_0 + x_1 + x_2$ (i.e., $y_3 \le I_2$).
    *   These constraints ensure that sales do not exceed the inventory available *at the beginning* of the month (which excludes current month purchases).
    *   8. $y_1 \le 1,000$
    *   9. $y_2 \le I_1$
    *   10. $y_3 \le I_2$
